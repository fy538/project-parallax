## Notes on Treatment Survivability Predictions

The highest-scoring assets share these traits:

**High Survivability (Scores 4-5 on Dimension 3):**
- Macro photography (circuits, pen tip, server racks) — extreme clarity survives desaturation well
- Industrial/architectural subjects — strong geometry unaffected by color removal
- Dramatic lighting with shadows — preserves tonal dynamic range post-duotone
- High-contrast equipment (servers, MRI chamber) — distinguishes elements via luminance

**Medium Survivability (Scores 3-4):**
- Well-lit interiors (dashboards, hospital bays) — readable but less dramatic
- Outdoor scenes with clear horizon — geographic context preserved
- Mixed materials (glass, metal, plastic) — different reflectances create some tonal separation

**Low Survivability (Scores 1-2):**
- Flat institutional lighting — everything compresses to single amber tone
- Pastel or washed-out sources — narrow tonal range → muddy duotone
- Bright daylight with blown highlights — detail loss in bright areas

This is why candidate #1 consistently ranks highest: architectural/macro subjects with strong geometry and directional lighting are inherently suited to the Parallax brand treatment.

## Archival Sourcing Guidance (beat2-fdr-1941)

**Primary source:** Library of Congress (www.loc.gov)
- Search: "Franklin Delano Roosevelt executive order" + "1941"
- Filter: Black and white photographs (pre-treated for duotone)
- Look for: Formal signing ceremony with FDR at desk, visible date/decree

**Alternative:** Wikimedia Commons (commons.wikimedia.org)
- Public domain category: "Franklin Delano Roosevelt photographs"
- Often linked to Library of Congress with attribution

**Why critical:** The script positions this as a historical echo — the visual must have authentic gravitas. A generic 1940s political portrait undermines the analytical frame. FDR's image is iconic and publicly recognizable; audience trust depends on accurate sourcing.

**If archival unavailable:** Note in production docs and consider alternative coverage (narrate over timeline graphic rather than photographic image). Do not substitute.
