# Cold Opens & Title Cards — Research Dossier

> Created: May 11, 2026. Research delegated to web-research agent; integrated and Parallax-aligned by claude. Update when new outlet conventions are observed.
>
> Not a per-template dossier — this is a cross-cutting research artifact covering episode-opening conventions across video-essay outlets. Read in conjunction with `POLISH.md` Editorial Doctrine, `motion-design.md`, and the `TitleTransition` / `KineticTypography` template dossiers when they exist.

## 1. The editorial purpose of the cold open and title card

The first 10–20 seconds of an editorial video do two things narration alone cannot:

1. **Promise the structure** — not the topic. The viewer needs to know within ~15 seconds *what kind of argument* they are about to watch (analytical essay, atmospheric meditation, deep-dive briefing). The cold open is a contract.
2. **Earn the title card.** A title that lands cold reads as a chapter heading; a title that lands *after* the viewer has already been pulled in reads as a confirmation of what they just felt.

The cold open is **not a teaser**. Teasers belong to broadcast television and trailer culture — they promise emotional payoff. Editorial cold opens promise *intellectual* payoff: a specific structural claim the rest of the video will examine. **The Economist's** "The World Ahead" video opens, **NYT Op-Docs**, **FT Film**, and **Pudding** video adaptations all converge on this distinction.

**The title card is not a TV bumper.** It is the moment the viewer registers "I am watching X, by Y." It earns its place by arriving *after* the viewer has been hooked, not before — and by holding long enough to be remembered (~1.5–2.5s on screen), then leaving without a sting.

## 2. Canonical cold-open patterns

### a. Hero stat or claim on paper
- **NYT The Daily** (audio) → video adaptations
- **FT Film** "How globalization broke" (2023) — opens with a single number on a near-empty frame

A single sentence or number sets on the page; narrator reads it once. The visual is austere, the claim is loaded.

*Works because:* trust accrues to the outlet that begins with substance, not setup. *Wrong when:* the claim is generic ("the world is changing") — the hero-claim form demands a specific, falsifiable opener.

### b. Atmospheric photo + literary quote
- **NYT Op-Docs** (recurring)
- **Retro Report**

An archival or atmospheric still holds for 4–8s while narrator reads a single sentence — often a quote, sometimes a literary citation, sometimes a deadpan observation. No music or very low bed.

*Works because:* the held image gives the viewer time to settle into the register. *Wrong when:* the image is decorative (stock photo of "Wall Street") rather than load-bearing (a specific archival moment).

### c. In medias res
- **Polymatter** "How Singapore got so rich" (2018) — opens mid-narration as if continuing
- **Wendover Productions** routinely

Narrator begins as if the viewer joined an essay already in progress. No setup. The orientation arrives 30–60s later when the title card lands.

*Works because:* signals intellectual confidence — "you can keep up." *Wrong when:* the viewer genuinely needs orientation; in medias res requires self-evident-enough subject matter.

### d. Question opener
- **Vox Atlas** "Why X" (recurring)
- **Kurzgesagt** routinely

Narrator poses a question; rest of the episode answers it. Often paired with a single iconic image.

*Works because:* primes a specific cognitive frame. *Wrong when:* the question is rhetorical-as-clickbait ("What if I told you...") rather than analytical-as-thesis.

### e. Archival photo with date-stamp
- **PBS Frontline** documentary opens
- **NYT Visual Investigations** routinely

A specific dated photograph or document fills the frame; the date stamp is part of the composition, not a caption. Narration anchors the historical moment.

*Works because:* establishes that the essay is *built on evidence* before it makes any analytical move. *Wrong when:* the photo is generic-period-stock rather than the actual document being discussed.

### f. Static text on paper (no image)
- **The Atlantic** video-essay adaptations
- **n+1** video collaborations

A single line of type held on a textured background. No image, no motion. Narrator reads.

*Works because:* maximum register signal — "this is a piece of writing, not a video." *Wrong when:* repeated across multiple episodes — once it's the channel's signature it becomes ritual, not opener.

## 3. Canonical title-card patterns

### a. Single hero word
- **NYT Magazine** video features
- **The Economist** "The World Ahead"

One word, display weight, paper background. Subtitle (if any) sits below in mono kicker.

*Works for:* episodes with a single thesis-word ("Decoupling," "Collapse," "Sovereignty"). *Wrong for:* episodes where the title is the *frame*, not the *subject*.

### b. Kicker + Title + Dek
- **The Atlantic** print → video adaptations
- **FT Weekend**

Three-line stack: small mono kicker (category / series), display-weight title, italic serif dek (one-line summary). Editorial-magazine convention transplanted to video.

*Works because:* gives the title room to be analytical rather than punchy — the dek does the work a punchy title can't. *Wrong when:* the dek restates the title rather than expanding it.

### c. Title over atmospheric image
- **Pudding** video essays
- **Bloomberg Originals**

Title sets over a treated photograph (often duotone, low contrast). Title type sits in a clear safe zone, not floating over busy detail.

*Works because:* establishes both subject and tone in one frame. *Wrong when:* the image fights the type — busy photographs need either heavy treatment or a different opener form.

### d. Title held in silence
- **Op-Docs** routinely
- **Retro Report**

Title appears with narration already concluded; held in silence for 1.5–2.5s before episode body begins. The silence is the punctuation.

*Works because:* in a dense narration register, *silence is rare and therefore loud.* *Wrong when:* paired with music — the silence is the move, music breaks it.

### e. Letter-by-letter kinetic
- **Vox** (recurring)
- **Johnny Harris** (when register is high-energy)

Title types itself onto the screen letter by letter, often with a typewriter or terminal aesthetic.

*Works for:* mystery / investigation / first-person reporting registers. *Wrong for:* analytical-essay register — the energy reads as YouTube-explainer, which is the register Parallax explicitly differentiates against.

## 4. Timing and rhythm conventions

**Cold-open runtime as a fraction of episode length:**

| Episode length | Cold open | Title card lands by |
|---|---|---|
| 15 min | 8–14s | 0:12–0:18 |
| 30 min | 15–25s | 0:20–0:30 |
| 60+ min | 30–45s (rare) | 0:40–1:00 |

**Hard ceiling: cold open should not exceed ~2% of total runtime.** Past that, the viewer is no longer being hooked — they're being delayed.

**Title-card hold duration:** 1.5–2.5s. Below 1.0s reads as a flash; above 3.0s reads as a held card the editor forgot to cut.

**Music entry:** **almost never during the cold open.** The Economist, NYT, FT, Atlantic, and Op-Docs all hold music until *after* the title card lands. This is the strongest cross-outlet convention in editorial video. Music entering during the cold open immediately signals YouTube-explainer or broadcast-news register.

**Sting culture:** the audio sting that traditional broadcast uses to punctuate a title card (BOOM → "TONIGHT") is **forbidden in editorial register**. The title earns its presence by being well-typeset and well-timed, not by a percussive cue.

## 5. Recommendation for Parallax

### Cold open: 10–14s, archival photo OR single Remotion artifact, no music

The Parallax cold open is a **single artifact + measured narrator cadence**. Either:

1. An archival photograph (treated to the brand duotone) holding for 6–10s with one or two narrated sentences
2. A single Remotion analytical artifact (a 2×2 matrix, a single data point on a timeline, a map with one marker) holding equally still

**No music during the cold open.** Music enters *after* the title card lands. This is the channel-defining move — every YouTube-explainer enters music in the first 3 seconds. Parallax's silence is the differentiation.

**The opener narrates a specific claim or question, never a setup.** No "in this video we'll explore..." No "have you ever wondered..." The first line is already substantive.

### Title card: Kicker + Title + Dek

The three-line stack is the editorial-magazine convention transplanted. Format:

```
PARALLAX · EPISODE 03                    [Plex Mono, ink @ 60%, small kicker]
The Prisoners' Dilemma                   [Plex Sans Display, ink, hero scale]
Why two rational actors choose the worst outcome — and what it takes to escape  [Plex Serif Italic, ink @ 80%, dek]
```

- **Background:** paper `#F5F0E8` (Light mode default). Dark mode uses ink `#1C1814` with bone `#F0E6D0` type.
- **No amber, no rust** on the title card itself. Amber is reserved for the ∴ brand mark in the corner; rust is reserved for analytical accents that haven't appeared yet.
- **Hold duration:** 2.0s on screen.
- **Music entry:** at the *exit* of the title card, not during. Music swells under the first body segment.

### The ∴ glyph

The ∴ mark appears on the title card in a fixed corner position (top-right or bottom-left, never both, never centered). Amber `#E5A544` at full saturation. It is the only element on the title card that uses an accent color.

### Cold-open → title-card transition

A clean cut. Not a wipe, not a crossfade, not a motion transition. The cold open ends, beat of black or paper, title card lands. The Economist and NYT both use the hard cut almost exclusively for this transition; the discipline of *cutting* rather than dissolving is part of the editorial register.

### Specific recommendation for the PD launch episode

**Cold open (12s):**
- Single artifact: 2×2 payoff matrix rendered as if on paper, no annotations, no Nash highlight yet — just the bare structure
- Narration: *"Two prisoners. Four outcomes. One of the most-cited thought experiments of the twentieth century — and a trap, if you take it too literally."*
- Held still, no motion on the matrix
- No music

**Title card (2s) at 0:12:**
```
PARALLAX · PHILOSOPHER'S LENS

The Prisoners' Dilemma

Why rational actors choose the worst outcome — and what it takes to escape
```
∴ glyph in amber, top-right corner.

**At 0:14 — music enters, first body segment begins.**

## 6. Current template alignment

Parallax has `TitleTransition` and `KineticTypography` templates. Neither is currently configured for the kicker+title+dek convention; both default to single-line hero titles.

**TitleTransition:**
- ✅ Holds title in centered safe zone
- ✅ Paper / ink default
- Diverges: no kicker field; no dek field; title scale is currently fixed (no automatic kicker stack)
- Diverges: enters with motion (slide/fade); canon for editorial is **fade-only with measured timing**, no slide
- **Missing:** the silence-hold before/after the title card

**KineticTypography:**
- Designed for kinetic emphasis sequences, not title cards. Correctly differentiated.

**No dedicated `ColdOpen` template exists.** Cold opens are currently assembled ad-hoc from `AnnotatedImage` / `StatReveal` / `TitleBlock` primitives. This is fine — cold opens are inherently bespoke — but a documented pattern (the 12s archival-photo-or-single-artifact opener) would standardize the production.

## 8. Failure mode flags (always catch in audit)

- **Music entering during the cold open.** Single most-frequent register-break in editorial video.
- **Cold open exceeding 20% of episode runtime** (15s on a 75s short, 90s on a 7-min episode).
- **Title-card hold below 1.0s** — flashes past, viewer can't read the dek.
- **Title-card hold above 3.0s** — reads as a held card the editor forgot to cut.
- **Setup-language opener** ("In this video we'll explore...", "Have you ever wondered...") — the first line must be substantive.
- **Motion transition between cold open and title card.** Editorial cuts. Wipes and dissolves break the register.
- **Audio sting on title card landing** — broadcast convention, not editorial.
- **Amber or rust on the title card type itself.** Accent colors are reserved for the ∴ glyph and for analytical artifacts; the title card is ink-on-paper.
- **Kinetic letter-by-letter title** — wrong register for analytical essay. Vox/Harris move; not Parallax.
- **Title that names the *topic* rather than the *structure*.** "The Prisoners' Dilemma Explained" is topic; "Why rational actors choose the worst outcome" is structure. The dek does the structural work, the title can be the canonical name.
- **Generic stock photograph as cold-open image.** Either the image is the actual document/moment being discussed, or it's a treated archival photo with editorial provenance. No "businessman silhouette at sunset."
- **No silence between cold open and music entry.** The silence after the title card lands is the move — music swelling immediately destroys the editorial register.

## TL;DR

**Parallax's opening register:** single artifact, measured narration, no music. Title card is Kicker + Title + Dek (Plex Mono / Sans Display / Serif Italic) on paper, held 2.0s, ∴ glyph in amber at top-right. Hard cut between cold open and title card. Music enters *after* the title card exits, not before.

**Default cold-open duration:** 10–14s for 15-min episodes, 15–25s for 30-min episodes. Hard ceiling at 2% of runtime.

**Forbidden patterns:** music during cold open, audio stings on title landing, motion transitions between open and title, setup-language openers, kinetic letter-by-letter titles, generic stock photography, accent colors on title-card type.
