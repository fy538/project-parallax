## SECTION 3 — CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTIONS (RANKED)

Eleven candidates investigated; ranked by *resonance* (how directly each illuminates the PD critique), not just empirical strength. ⭐ flags top-3 for likely script use.

### ⭐ #1 — THE FLOOD–DRESHER EXPERIMENT ITSELF AS THE FIRST CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION
**Resonance: very high. Empirical strength: very high (primary documents survive). Visual: payoff matrix, scoreboard, marginal commentary.**
The episode's most economical move is to use the PD's own birth experiment against it. The first-ever empirical run of the model contradicted it (60% mutual cooperation), and Nash's response was to discount the data rather than the theory. This is the rhyme: the framework was born *already failing*, and the response was to defend the framework. This is also the safest cross-domain move because the source is the architects themselves — no analogy break.

### ⭐ #2 — OSTROM'S COMMONS vs. HARDIN'S TRAGEDY (CONTEST OF METAPHORS)
**Resonance: very high. Empirical strength: very high. Visual: Spanish *huertas*, Swiss alpine meadows, Maine lobster fisheries map, Japanese irrigation.**
Garrett Hardin's 1968 *Science* essay framed shared resources as multi-player PDs; Ostrom's 30-year empirical program documented hundreds of communities that solved them. Her Spanish *huertas* (medieval irrigation cooperatives in Valencia and Murcia, still operating), Swiss alpine grazing commons (some with charters from the 13th century), Japanese *iriaichi* mountain commons, and Maine lobster gangs all violate PD predictions because they meet the eight design principles. The decoder line: *the tragedy of the commons isn't a discovery about resource use — it's the prediction of a model whose assumptions don't fit the cases it was applied to.* Caveats (be honest): Ostrom's cases are mostly small-to-medium scale, with identifiable users and clear boundaries; the global climate commons fails several principles (Section 8).

### ⭐ #3 — SKYRMS'S STAG HUNT AS THE SUPPRESSED CANONICAL GAME
**Resonance: very high (this is the deep philosophical move). Empirical strength: theoretical. Visual: Rousseau text overlay; payoff matrices side-by-side.**
Brian Skyrms, *The Stag Hunt and the Evolution of Social Structure* (Cambridge, 2004), argues that **Rousseau's stag hunt** (from *Discourse on Inequality*, 1755), not Hobbes's war of all against all, is the better canonical metaphor for cooperation problems. The stag hunt has *two* equilibria — both cooperate (high payoff, risky) or both defect (low payoff, safe) — and the strategic question is coordination, not domination. Crucially, in stag hunt **mutual cooperation is itself a Nash equilibrium**, so the puzzle is how to coordinate on it, not how to escape an iron logic of defection. Skyrms's claim: most of what we model as PD is actually stag hunt. The decoder line: *which game gets to be canonical is itself a political choice. Choosing PD makes cooperation a miracle; choosing stag hunt makes it a coordination problem.* This is the most powerful single move available to the script — it reframes the whole field by changing the default metaphor.

### #4 — Concert of Europe (1815–1853): four decades of great-power cooperation
After the Vienna Congress (1814–15), Castlereagh, Metternich, Talleyrand, and Tsar Alexander I built congresses (Aix-la-Chapelle 1818, Troppau 1820, Laibach 1821, Verona 1822) and an ambassadorial conference in Paris that managed crises through 1853 (Crimean War). What sustained it: shared trauma of revolutionary disorder, a tacit code that great powers made decisions collectively, asymmetric British naval/financial primacy underwriting the system (a Kindleberger setup), and what David Lindley calls "transparency-promoting" diplomatic infrastructure. PD-blind mechanisms: *legitimacy norms* (the Holy Alliance language about Christian monarchy was ideological glue), *repeated interaction with identifiable counterparties*, and *Castlereagh's explicit project* of building structures rather than relying on balance alone. Why it broke: nationalism (1848), Bismarckian unification, the rise of mass politics, and Britain's drift into "splendid isolation." Visual potential: high (maps, congress paintings). Resonance: high. Caveat: the system was hierarchical and deeply illiberal; "cooperation" included suppressing liberal revolutions. Don't romanticize.

### #5 — Axelrod's tournaments and what's been learned since
Axelrod's two iterated-PD computer tournaments (1980, 1981) and *Evolution of Cooperation* (1984): Anatol Rapoport's TIT-FOR-TAT won both. Four properties of successful strategies: *nice* (never first to defect), *retaliating, forgiving* (returns to cooperation quickly), *clear*. **Important post-1984 corrections:**
- **Pavlov / Win-Stay-Lose-Shift (Nowak & Sigmund, *Nature* 1993)** outperforms TFT in noisy, evolutionary settings: corrects mistakes, exploits unconditional cooperators. Not "nice" in Axelrod's sense.
- **Generous TFT** beats plain TFT in alternating-move settings (Nowak & Sigmund).
- **Press & Dyson (*PNAS* 2012)** discovered "zero-determinant" strategies, including extortionate strategies that unilaterally enforce a linear payoff relationship — a sentient ZD player can extract more than a fair share from any adaptive opponent. This was a real shock to the field: it showed that the iterated PD has an unexpected adversarial structure that Axelrod's tournaments missed. Stewart & Plotkin (2013) showed *generous* ZD strategies dominate evolutionarily, restoring some of Axelrod's optimism but on a more fragile basis.
- **Knight et al. (2015, PLOS One; Max Planck reanalysis 2024 with 195 strategies)** found that TFT's success is contingent on tournament design and payoff parameters; in diverse environments, "winning" strategies need to be cleverer, slightly envious, and adaptable. The script can use this as: *even within the iterated PD literature, the canonical answer turned out to be a feature of the test, not the world.*

Resonance: high. Visual: tournament leaderboards, payoff diagrams.

### #6 — Mechanism design success stories (the steelman in concrete form)
- **FCC spectrum auctions (1994–present)**, designed by Paul Milgrom and Robert Wilson with Preston McAfee — the simultaneous multiple-round auction (SMRA) and later combinatorial clock and incentive auctions. By 2014 the FCC alone had raised over $120 billion via this format; globally, spectrum auctions using descendants of SMRA have generated $200+ billion. The 2016 broadcast incentive auction generated $19.8 billion in forward auction revenue. Milgrom and Wilson received the 2020 Nobel.
- **Kidney exchange (Roth, Sönmez, Ünver)**, 2012 Nobel for Roth (with Lloyd Shapley). National Kidney Registry chains have produced thousands of transplants that wouldn't otherwise have happened.
- **School choice mechanisms** (Boston, NYC, redesigned by Roth/Pathak using deferred acceptance).

The interpretive move: *game theory works brilliantly where institutions can be designed to make the assumptions hold.* This is the steelman for game theory generally, but **it is a strong critique of using PD analytically for cases (geopolitics) where the assumptions cannot be designed in.* Resonance: high (especially because it lets the episode be fair).

### #7 — Evolutionary biology and "cooperative" organisms
Mycorrhizal networks (plant-fungus exchange, with biological-market dynamics — Kiers et al.; Bever 2009 on spatial structure); lichens; gut microbiome mutualism; cleaner fish (Bshary & Grutter); vampire bat blood-sharing (Wilkinson 1984; Carter 2013, 2020). Vampire bat case is particularly sharp: regurgitated blood-sharing depends roughly equally on relatedness and reciprocation index; bats track grooming relationships before food-sharing, building reputational scaffolding. Hamilton (kin selection), Trivers (reciprocal altruism), Maynard Smith (ESS), Nowak's five rules (kin selection, direct reciprocity, indirect reciprocity, network reciprocity, group selection) — all show that what looks like a PD at the gene/individual level is solved by structural features the PD doesn't model. Resonance: medium-high. Visual: network diagrams of mycorrhizal nets, vampire bat colonies. Caveat: these are not "PD with cooperation magically appearing" — they're systems with kin structure, repeated interaction, partner choice, and partner control that aren't PD-like at the mechanism level.

### #8 — Hanseatic League (~1356–1669)
Merchant cities (Lübeck, Hamburg, Bremen, Bruges, Novgorod, plus the London Steelyard) cooperating across centuries without a sovereign — Hansetage (diets) coordinating embargoes and naval response; *kontors* enforcing reputational discipline; the threat of revoking trading privileges as the central sanction. *Works in Progress* (2024) magazine's analysis is good on the actual fragility: enforcement was weak, free-riding common, and the League declined precisely when it could no longer hold coalitions. Resonance: medium-high. Useful counter-romance: cooperation worked because of reputational *and* coercive structure (privateer-suppressed piracy, joint warships, common law) — not because traders were nice.

### #9 — Islamic waqf institutions (~700s onward)
Endowment-based cooperation outside state enforcement, lasting centuries (Kuran, *The Long Divergence*, 2011; *Freedoms Delayed*, 2023). Founded under Islamic legal scaffolding that bound rulers' hands vis-à-vis pious endowments. Sustained provision of public goods (mosques, fountains, schools, soup kitchens) in the absence of corporations. **But Kuran's important twist:** the same rigidity that gave waqfs their cooperative durability eventually made them dysfunctional and politically weak. Useful for the episode because it complicates the Ostromian story: cooperation-without-state-enforcement isn't unalloyed good. Resonance: medium. Visual: Istanbul/Cairo waqf deeds, surviving institutions.

### #10 — Chinese strategic philosophy and 势 (shi)
François Jullien, *The Propensity of Things* (1995) and *A Treatise on Efficacy* (2004): classical Chinese strategic thought — Sun Zi, the *Sunzi bingfa*, and Daoist sources — operates on *shi* (势, situational propensity / strategic configuration), not on discrete actor-vs-actor decisions. The key contrast: von Neumann's actors choose moves from a payoff matrix; Sun Zi's strategist *positions* within an evolving configuration so that outcomes "take place of themselves." If this tradition is right that strategy is fundamentally relational and dispositional rather than transactional, then mapping Chinese diplomatic behavior onto a PD is not a model translation problem — it's a category error. Caveats: (a) Jullien's contrast is itself partly a French intellectual construction; Alastair Iain Johnston's *Cultural Realism* (1995) argues classical Chinese strategic thought contains plenty of force-and-deception-realpolitik, not a different ontology. (b) Risk of orientalist romanticism. Use carefully — the episode should *use* this tension, not resolve it. Resonance: very high if handled with care; serious risk of analogy-break if handled badly. **Recommendation: gesture to it, don't lean on it.**

### #11 — Other surfaced candidates (briefer)
- **Antarctic Treaty System (1959)**: 12 founding states, scientific cooperation framework, sovereignty claims "frozen." Joanne Yao (2021) gives the critical reading: cooperation worked partly because hierarchy was hard-coded into the formal exclusion structure, not despite it.
- **Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968→present)**: surprisingly successful cooperation; Belfer Center analyses note the puzzle of weak enforcement and weak verification yet remarkably few new nuclear states (9 vs. predictions in the dozens). Realist explanation: US/Soviet hegemonic enforcement plus extended deterrence umbrellas. Liberal explanation: norms and institutions. Probably both.
- **Hobbes vs. Rousseau as competing primal models**: covered structurally above via Skyrms.
- **Venice / Avogadori di Comun**: Diego Gambetta's work on trust networks, structured rivalry.
- **Open-source software, Wikipedia, Linux**: contemporary examples of large-scale cooperation under PD-like incentive structures, sustained by reputational mechanisms and intrinsic motivation. Benkler's *Wealth of Networks* (2006) is the key reference.
- **OPEC**: the *cleanest empirical case* where PD predicts well. OPEC has cheating-on-quotas problems repeatedly; cartel enforcement is genuinely fragile. The script should not pretend PD is wrong about *everything* — OPEC is where it's right (Section 5).
