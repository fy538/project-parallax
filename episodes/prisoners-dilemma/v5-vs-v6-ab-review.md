## Structural diff (objective metrics)

2 of 8 metrics show material delta:

- **Hook position (regex pivot detector):** v5 hits at 24.4s, v6 at 30.8s.
  This metric flagged v6 as *worse*, but the blind reviewer specifically
  noted v6 reaches the experimental result FASTER. The discrepancy: my
  regex matched a different pivot moment in v5 ("that's not what's going
  on" in the bridge between Beats 1 and 2) than the substantive
  misconception-first pivot. The metric is honest but mis-calibrated for
  this specific comparison. **Take-home: refine the pivot regex to weight
  the misconception-first signature higher than incidental contrast
  phrases.**
- **Pattern interrupts/min:** v5 at 8.9/min, v6 at 5.0/min. A real
  regression. v6's longer continuous takes (cold open is two extended
  text blocks + one long AI-GEN scene) cut visual density nearly in half.
  Research target is ~7.5/min. **Take-home: v6 could absorb 3-5 additional
  visual interrupts in Beats 1-2 without changing the narration.** Easy
  follow-up.

All other tracked metrics (word count, runtime, bridges, direct-address,
caveats, pivot detection) show parity or below-threshold deltas.

## Blind review verdict (qualitative)

Done by an independent agent with no access to version metadata. Labels
A/B were randomized; the agent never knew which was which.

The agent picked Script B (= v6) on every question:

1. **Cold open:** v6 stronger — "compresses the setup into a clean
   misconception-first pivot... reaches the 1950 experiment by its third
   paragraph." Open loops are "the kind of overt commitment device YouTube
   retention curves reward." v5's close ("once you see how — you can't
   unsee it") was "more atmospheric but less mechanical."
2. **Mid-section:** v6 less likely to lose viewers. v5's Schelling/
   Hamilton/Axelrod/Hofstadter lineage list "arrives as a five-name burst
   before the viewer has been told *why* it matters." v5's "Checkpoint"
   interrupt is "a tell that the author felt the beat was getting heavy."
3. **Named concept reveal:** v6 reveals "The Wrong Game" as a PAYOFF that
   closes the 6-minute open loop seeded in the cold open. v5 declares it
   "after one negotiation hypothetical with no prior promise that a named
   concept is coming." Same words, harder landing in v6.
4. **Bounded-analogy doctrine:** Both share the explicit caveat
   paragraphs verbatim. But v5 has flabbier signaling scaffolding
   ("The question this episode is about... has a specific answer. And that
   answer changes how you read every conflict playing out right now")
   that "reads as throat-clearing rather than confidence." v5 also crosses
   into oracle tone with "And right now, it is shaping every negotiation
   that will determine your future" — a line the bounded-analogy doctrine
   shouldn't permit. v6 removed it.
5. **Overall:** "B [v6] — same argument, better engineered for retention
   through loop discipline and a faster, more confident cold open."

**Notable independent finding:** the blind reviewer surfaced one editorial
problem in v5 that the audit didn't catch — v5's "every negotiation that
will determine your future" line crosses into oracle tone and violates
the bounded-analogy doctrine. v6's removal of it was the right move,
even though that wasn't the stated reason for the rewrite. This is the
exact value-add of the blind-review tool: catches things author + audit
both missed.

## The two regressions, ranked

1. **Pattern interrupts dropped from 8.9 to 5.0/min** — fixable without
   touching the script narration. Add 3-5 brief visual cuts in Beats 1+2
   inside the longer takes. Most directly: the 12s "Constructivist
   research office" AI-GEN scene in Beat 1 could be split into 2-3 micro-
   cuts (push-in + cut to detail + cut back to wide) without changing
   the editorial intent.
2. **Hook position metric says 30.8s but blind reviewer says faster** —
   probably a tool calibration issue (my pivot regex is too generous).
   Not actually a script issue. Future work: refine pivot detection to
   distinguish "incidental contrast phrase" from "misconception-first
   statement."

## Decision

**Keep v6.2 as canonical.** The blind qualitative win is decisive and
covers the dimensions that actually matter for retention. The structural
regressions are addressable (the pattern-interrupt fix is a half-hour
visual-spec edit, no script rewrite needed) and don't change the verdict.

Optional: bump v6.2 → v6.3 with the visual-density additions before
recording. Or: ship as-is, measure actual retention post-publish, then
decide whether the pattern-interrupt regression matters empirically.
