# prisoners-dilemma Angle Memo — The Prisoner's Dilemma Is Wrong About Almost Everything
**Storyline chosen:** A ("The Model That Failed Its Own First Test") + stag hunt as resolution from B

---

## Named Concept
**The Wrong Game** — the structural error of applying a special-case model (built for anonymous one-shot encounters) to situations where its assumptions don't hold — and the self-reinforcing cycle where acting as if you're in that game makes it become true. Breaks down when an actor successfully reframes (Reagan-Gorbachev), meaning the trap is strong but not permanent.

## Decoder Framing
The standard frame: the Prisoner's Dilemma explains why cooperation between nations is so hard. Our frame: the PD doesn't describe the game we're playing — it *creates* the game we're playing. It failed its own first experiment, conquered strategic thinking anyway, and the question is how.

## Cold Open Approach
**Stakes-shock (#1).**

**Primary:** "In January 1950, two researchers at the RAND Corporation ran the first-ever prisoner's dilemma experiment. The subjects cooperated sixty percent of the time. The theory predicted zero. John Nash read the results and said he would have thought them 'more rational.' That response — blame the players, not the model — set a template that's still running seventy-five years later."

**Backup:** "The most influential model in strategic thinking was contradicted by its own first experiment. It conquered the world anyway. And right now, it's shaping every negotiation that will determine your future."

## Cross-Domain Connections
1. **Flood-Dresher experiment (1950)** — The PD's first empirical run produced 60% cooperation; Nash's technically-valid-but-institutionally-consequential response established the template: ring-fence the model rather than revise it. Where it breaks: Nash was articulating the proto-Folk Theorem, not merely stonewalling.

2. **Ostrom's commons vs. Hardin's tragedy** — 800+ documented communities solved cooperation problems by building exactly the institutional features the PD assumes away. The model isn't wrong — it's irrelevant where its assumptions don't hold, which is almost everywhere that matters. Where it breaks: Ostrom's cases are community-scale; planetary commons still resist her design principles.

3. **Skyrms's stag hunt (the resolution)** — There was always another game available. In stag hunt, cooperation IS rational — the puzzle is coordination, not domination. Skyrms proves that an iterated PD is *mathematically* a stag hunt. Where it breaks: some problems (OPEC cheating, anonymous one-shots) really are PDs. The episode doesn't claim PD is always wrong — just that it's the wrong *default*.

## Emotional Arc
1. **Shock** (Cold open) — "It failed its own first test?"
2. **Fascination** (How it spread) — "How does a failed model conquer the world?" — RAND → Schelling → textbooks → biology → popular culture. The diffusion as a story of institutional momentum, not conspiracy.
3. **Growing unease** (The self-confirming mechanism) — "It's not just describing the world — it's *making* the world." The Schelling Trap reveal. Brief Black-Scholes parallel (one sentence: "the same thing happened in finance").
4. **Relief** (The way out) — "There was always another game." Stag hunt reframe + Ostrom as proof it works. The episode's constructive turn.
5. **Personal urgency** (Stakes return) — "This is shaping the room where your future gets decided — and now you can see it."

## Stakes Sentence
Every negotiation shaping your future is run by people trained on a model that failed its own first experiment — and now you can see the game they don't know they're playing.

## Visual Arc
**Motif:** A single equilibrium point (the PD's sole Nash equilibrium — mutual defection) that multiplies across the episode. Starts as one glowing dot in a payoff matrix. By Beat 3, it's replicated across maps (DC, Beijing, Geneva) — the same dot appearing in every negotiation room. In Beat 4, a second equilibrium point appears (the stag-hunt cooperative equilibrium) — and the viewer sees that two dots were always possible. Suggested template: GameBoard evolving across beats.

**Visual-first moments:**
- Beat 1: The Flood-Dresher scoreboard — 60 cooperation rounds highlighted in amber, 14 defections in muted grey — appears before narration explains the numbers. Viewer asks: "Why is cooperation so dominant if the model says it shouldn't exist?"
- Beat 4: A world map lights up with Ostrom's 800+ documented cases (Spanish huertas, Swiss alps, Japanese commons, Maine lobster) — before narration explains what they are. Viewer asks: "What are all these successful cases?"

**Counterpoint moment:**
- Beat 2: Narration describes the PD's rapid spread ("by 1975, over two thousand scholarly articles") while the visual shows the *narrowing conditions* where the model actually applies — anonymous, one-shot, no communication, no reputation — each condition eliminating another real-world scenario until almost nothing remains. The visual says "it spread everywhere, but applies almost nowhere."

## Shorts Beats (Design-Time)

Two design-time beats engineered as **shorts-native standalone moments**. Each passes the standalone test (structural claim + bounded clause inside 45–60s without requiring the long-form). Tagged in the script with `[SHORTS-BEAT: <series>]` for `shorts-adaptation` to pick up as primary candidates. Post-hoc extraction will yield 1–2 additional Shorts from Beats 2 (institutional momentum) and 3 (Schelling Trap) if they read standalone in review.

**Hedge-stripping self-check** — both beats keep the bounded clause inside the 45–60s window. Without it, Beat 1 would sound like "the textbook is just wrong" (Jiang/Whatifalthist register) and Beat 4 would sound like "the answer is always cooperation" (utopian register). Neither is what the long-form actually claims.

1. **Beat 1 — `history-rhymes`** (Flood-Dresher 1950 incident)
   - **Standalone claim:** *"In 1950, the Prisoner's Dilemma's very first experiment got 60% cooperation. The theory predicted mutual defection. John Nash blamed the players, not the model — and the field followed him for 75 years."*
   - **Bounded clause:** *"Nash was technically right about the math; a single Nash equilibrium is defection. But ring-fencing the result instead of revising the model set a template that's still running 75 years later — and you can hear it every time someone says 'players defect because they're rational.'"*
   - **Hook frame-1 visual:** Large bone-on-ink type: **"60%"** stacked over **"0%"** with captions "observed cooperation" / "predicted by model". Mid-century corporate-modernist register; sound-off legible.
   - **Wording note (hedge-strip self-check):** "predicted mutual defection" is the careful version. An earlier draft used "predicted zero" — game-theory critics would correctly note that PD's Nash analysis predicts an equilibrium strategy, not a literal rate. The long-form says *"mutual defection — the only outcome the model predicted — happened fourteen times out of a hundred,"* so this matches.

2. **Beat 4 — `framework-in-45s`** (stag-hunt reframe)
   - **Standalone claim:** *"The Prisoner's Dilemma has one equilibrium: defection. The Stag Hunt has two — and one of them is cooperation. Most situations people call 'prisoner's dilemmas' are actually stag hunts."*
   - **Bounded clause:** *"Some things really ARE prisoner's dilemmas — OPEC cheating, anonymous one-shots. The point isn't that PD is always wrong. The point is it's the wrong default — and defaulting to it locks in cooperation failure that wasn't structurally required."*
   - **Hook frame-1 visual:** SplitComposition — left half "PRISONER'S DILEMMA" with one glowing defection dot, ∴ divider, right half "STAG HUNT" with two glowing dots (cooperative one in amber). Single sentence below: "Most prisoner's dilemmas aren't."
   - **Wording note (hedge-strip self-check):** earlier draft phrased the difference as *"the PD assumes you can't talk. The Stag Hunt assumes you can."* That folksy compression misrepresents the long-form, which locates the difference in equilibrium structure (Skyrms's proof that iterated PD ≡ stag hunt is about equilibrium multiplicity, not communication). The equilibrium-count framing matches what the long-form actually argues AND lands geometrically on the dot-count visual.
