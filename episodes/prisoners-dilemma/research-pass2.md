# Pass 2 Cross-Domain Connections — "The Prisoner's Dilemma Is Wrong About Almost Everything"

**Research lead:** Pass 2 / Philosopher's Lens episode
**Status:** Five new candidates surveyed; two recommended for working set
**TL;DR:** The two strongest new additions are (1) **the Black–Scholes "volatility smile" and the performativity literature** (MacKenzie, Callon, Bamford), which is the cleanest non-PD case in the modern world of a model that *makes the world it claims to describe* until reality bites back, and (2) **Hutchinson's "Paradox of the Plankton"** (1961), which is the textbook cooperation-residual in ecology — competitive exclusion predicts homogenization, the ocean shows hundreds of coexisting species per liter, and ecology has spent six decades reframing the failure as scope clarification rather than refutation. Three further candidates (Imperial Keju, Major Evolutionary Transitions, DSGE macroeconomics) offer thematic depth but lower marginal novelty.

## CONNECTION 1 — THE BLACK-SCHOLES VOLATILITY SMILE AND THE PERFORMATIVITY OF FINANCE THEORY

**1. NAME — domain, period, specific case**
Quantitative finance / sociology of economics, 1973–present. The Black–Scholes–Merton option-pricing model (1973), its empirical validation in the 1976–1987 period, the October 1987 stock-market crash, and the persistent "volatility smile/skew" in implied-volatility surfaces from 1987 onward. Primary documentation in Donald MacKenzie, *An Engine, Not a Camera* (MIT Press, 2006); MacKenzie & Millo, "Constructing a Market, Performing Theory" (*AJS* 2003); Bamford & MacKenzie, "Counterperformativity" (*New Left Review* 113, 2018).

**2. STRUCTURAL CONCEPT MAPPING**
This is the densest single mapping available, hitting **three of the four** concepts at mechanism level.

- **Self-Confirming Strategic Framing (Concept 3) — exact mechanism match.** Before traders adopted Black–Scholes, market option prices systematically diverged from the model. As traders, exchanges, regulators, and clearing houses *adopted* the model — printing Fischer Black's price sheets, building it into risk-management software, using its Greeks to hedge — observed prices converged to the model's predictions. MacKenzie calls this "Barnesian performativity": the model didn't describe the world; the world reorganized itself around the model. The PD's "Schelling Trap" — assume the world is a PD, behave accordingly, observe the predicted defection, conclude the model was right — has the same generative structure: a descriptive framework that is true *because* it is believed.
- **Model Colonization (Concept 1).** Black–Scholes was derived under restrictive assumptions (log-normal returns, constant volatility, continuous hedging, no transaction costs, no jumps). Each violation, as it was discovered, was treated as a scope clarification — "stochastic volatility," "jump-diffusion," "local volatility" — generating a Lakatosian protective belt of auxiliary models (Heston, SABR, Dupire) whose entire purpose is to keep the BSM hard core load-bearing. The 1987 crash was the central anomaly; rather than abandoning the model, the field absorbed it as the "smile."
- **Cooperation Residual analog (inverted Concept 2).** The post-1987 volatility smile *is* a residual: the systematic, persistent deviation of market prices from BSM that the field treats as the puzzle requiring explanation, while the flat-volatility prediction is treated as the natural state. This is structurally identical to the cooperation-residual move: model says X, world shows not-X, field treats not-X as the anomaly rather than as evidence X is wrong.

**3. SURPRISE LEVEL — Very High.**
A college-educated viewer interested in geopolitics likely knows BSM as "the equation that won a Nobel" and may have heard of LTCM, but the *performativity* reading — that the equation became true because people believed it, and stopped being true the day they noticed it could fail — is a sociology-of-science argument almost no general audience has encountered. It is a near-perfect parallel to the PD's "the world acted like a PD because we treated it as one." The jump-cut from cold-war game theory to derivatives trading floors is the kind of pairing the episode is built on.

**4. ANALOGY BREAK-POINT (mandatory)**
- *Direction of the residual is opposite.* In the PD case, the cooperation residual is reality outperforming the model (more cooperation than predicted). In Black–Scholes, the residual is reality *under*performing the model in the tails (more crash risk than the lognormal predicts). The structural shape is the same — anomaly absorbed as scope — but the moral valence is reversed. Don't elide this.
- *No strategic agency on the "world" side.* The PD claims to describe the choices of reasoning agents; Black–Scholes describes price processes generated by aggregated reasoning agents. The performativity loop runs through traders' beliefs and hedging behaviors, but the underlying assets (corn, equities, currencies) have no preferences. The Schelling Trap analogy works at the level of *market participants*, not the underlyings.
- *Counterperformativity is identifiable.* MacKenzie documents (and Bamford & MacKenzie 2018 elaborate) that portfolio insurance — a direct application of BSM/Leland — *exacerbated* the 1987 crash, making the model less true through its use. The PD literature has no comparable instance of clean self-undermining; the analogous claim about realist IR theory remains contested. So the finance case is in some ways structurally *richer* than the PD — it includes both performative and counterperformative episodes.

**5. SOURCE QUALITY — Excellent.**
Primary academic sources: Donald MacKenzie, *An Engine, Not a Camera: How Financial Models Shape Markets* (MIT Press, 2006), particularly Ch. 5–7; MacKenzie & Millo, "Constructing a Market, Performing Theory: The Historical Sociology of a Financial Derivatives Exchange," *American Journal of Sociology* 109(1), 2003; Bamford & MacKenzie, "Counterperformativity," *New Left Review* 113 (Sept–Oct 2018); Michel Callon (ed.), *The Laws of the Markets* (Blackwell 1998) for the broader performativity framework. For the smile itself: Rubinstein, "Implied Binomial Trees," *Journal of Finance* 49 (1994); Jackwerth & Rubinstein, "Recovering Probability Distributions from Option Prices," *J. Finance* 51 (1996). MacKenzie won the Viviana Zelizer Prize for this work; it is mainstream economic sociology, not heterodox fringe.

**6. SCRIPT POTENTIAL**
Outstanding. Visuals: the BSM equation; a chart of implied volatility before and after October 19, 1987 (flat line → permanent smirk); footage/photos of Fischer Black's mimeographed price sheets on the CBOE floor; the metaphor of an "engine, not a camera" lends itself to a clean two-shot animation (camera turning into engine). One sentence: *"In 1973 Black and Scholes wrote down an equation about how options should be priced; by 1987 the market had rearranged itself to obey the equation, and on October 19th of that year it discovered what happens when reality refuses to keep cooperating."*

**7. RESONANCE SCORE — 9.5/10. Highest of the five.**
Surprise (very high) × structural depth (hits three of four concepts cleanly, with primary-source documentation of the mechanism) × source quality (peer-reviewed, prize-winning, interview-based) × visual potential (high). Toxin-line risk: low — this is mainstream sociology of finance, not conspiracy or heterodox economics.

## CONNECTION 2 — HUTCHINSON'S "PARADOX OF THE PLANKTON" AND THE COMPETITIVE EXCLUSION PRINCIPLE

**1. NAME — domain, period, specific case**
Theoretical ecology, 1932–present. G. F. Gause's *The Struggle for Existence* (1934) formalized the competitive exclusion principle (often called "Gause's law"); Hardin gave it canonical statement in *Science* (1960); G. E. Hutchinson, "The Paradox of the Plankton," *American Naturalist* 95(882): 137–145 (May–June 1961), gave the field its central anomaly. The "paradox" continues to drive theoretical ecology in 2024 (see Record, Pershing & Maps, *ICES Journal of Marine Science* 2014; *PNAS* / *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 2022 papers).

**2. STRUCTURAL CONCEPT MAPPING**

- **Cooperation Residual (Concept 2) — textbook case.** The competitive exclusion principle, derived from Lotka–Volterra dynamics, predicts that *n* species cannot coexist on fewer than *n* limiting resources; in equilibrium, the superior competitor on each resource drives the others extinct. Yet in a single liter of seawater, ~100 phytoplankton species coexist on roughly 3 limiting resources (light, nitrogen, phosphorus, plus iron/silicate). The 60+ years since Hutchinson's paper have produced what is essentially a Lakatosian protective belt: nonequilibrium dynamics, chaos in nutrient cycles, vertical light gradients, size-selective grazing, spatio-temporal heterogeneity, intransitive competition, neutral theory, micro-niche partitioning. Each "resolution" preserves the theoretical core (competitive dynamics on a Lotka–Volterra base) by relaxing an assumption. The pattern is unmistakable: the *prediction* (homogenization) is treated as the natural state; the *observation* (massive coexistence) is treated as the puzzle.
- **Model Colonization (Concept 1).** Lotka–Volterra and competitive exclusion remain the orienting framework in graduate ecology curricula and in the NPZ (Nutrient–Phytoplankton–Zooplankton) ocean models used for fisheries and climate work, despite being known to mispredict standing biodiversity. As Record et al. (2014) put it bluntly in *ICES J. Mar. Sci.*, "competitive exclusion is still the primary behaviour in ocean foodweb models."
- **Institutional Scaffolding parallel (Concept 4).** Many proposed resolutions amount to showing that real ecosystems contain "scaffolding" — environmental fluctuations, predator-mediated coexistence, spatial structure — that prevents the assumptions of competitive exclusion from holding. This is structurally identical to the PD argument: cooperation arises not by overcoming the model's logic but by being in a system where the model's assumptions don't apply.

**3. SURPRISE LEVEL — High.**
Most viewers know "survival of the fittest" as the popular gloss on natural selection. Almost none know that the formal version of this idea — Gause's law — is one of the most empirically embarrassed propositions in ecology, and that the field's response has been to keep the proposition and add epicycles. The specific phrase "paradox of the plankton" is a graduate-qualifying-exam staple but unknown to the public. The pairing of cold-war game theory and seawater biodiversity passes the "I never would have put those two things together" test.

**4. ANALOGY BREAK-POINT**
- *No strategic agency.* Plankton don't reason, anticipate defection, or update on shared norms. The mechanism producing the cooperation-residual is selectional and ecological, not deliberative. So the *form* of the residual (model predicts homogenization, world shows coexistence) maps cleanly, but the generative *mechanism* (chaos, predator switching, environmental fluctuation) is not the same as the institutional/communicative scaffolding that resolves PDs in human cooperation.
- *No "Schelling Trap."* Plankton can't be talked into believing the model and acting on that belief, so Concept 3 doesn't apply. This case is purely Concept 1 + 2.
- *Equilibrium framing.* Competitive exclusion is an equilibrium prediction; one of Hutchinson's own answers (since substantially confirmed) is that plankton communities are simply never in equilibrium. The PD literature has equivalents (folk theorem, evolutionary game dynamics) but the parallel is not exact: PD is fundamentally a static one-shot model, where competitive exclusion is a dynamical-systems result. Use carefully.

**5. SOURCE QUALITY — Excellent.**
Primary: Hutchinson (1961) is freely available (JSTOR 2458386); Gause (1934) *The Struggle for Existence*; Hardin, "The Competitive Exclusion Principle," *Science* 131 (1960). Modern review literature: Roy & Chattopadhyay, "Towards a resolution of 'the paradox of the plankton'," *Ecological Complexity* 4 (2007); Scheffer et al., "Why plankton communities have no equilibrium: solutions to the paradox," *Hydrobiologia* 491 (2003); Hening & Nguyen, "The competitive exclusion principle in stochastic environments" (arXiv 1810.00954); the 2022 *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* paper by Menden-Deuer et al. The point that this is treated as paradox-requiring-explanation rather than refutation-requiring-abandonment is explicit in the literature, not editorial.

**6. SCRIPT POTENTIAL**
Excellent. Visuals: high-magnification footage of mixed phytoplankton communities (publicly available from NOAA, Plankton Chronicles, Woods Hole), a simple Lotka–Volterra phase-plane animation showing the predicted collapse to one species, the same animation overlaid with the actual species count from a real bloom. One sentence: *"In 1961 the ecologist G. Evelyn Hutchinson pointed out that according to the dominant model of competition, the ocean should contain about three species; it contains about three thousand. Sixty years later, ecologists still call this 'the paradox' — not 'the refutation.'"*

**7. RESONANCE SCORE — 8.5/10.**
High surprise × strong structural depth on Concepts 1 and 2 × pristine sources × high visual potential. Marginally less rich than the Black–Scholes case because it doesn't hit Concept 3, but the cooperation-residual mapping is purer.

## CONNECTION 3 — THE IMPERIAL EXAMINATION SYSTEM (KEJU) AS MODEL COLONIZATION

**1. NAME — domain, period, specific case**
Chinese imperial history, Sui dynasty (587 CE introduction) through Qing abolition (1905), with particular weight on the Ming "eight-legged essay" (八股文, *bagu wen*) period (~1487–1905) and the Yuan-Ming canonization of Zhu Xi's Neo-Confucian commentaries as the only authorized interpretation. Primary modern analysis: Yasheng Huang, *The Rise and Fall of the EAST: How Exams, Autocracy, Stability, and Technology Brought China Success, and Why They Might Lead to Its Decline* (Yale UP, 2023); Benjamin Elman, *A Cultural History of Civil Examinations in Late Imperial China* (UC Press, 2000) and *Civil Examinations and Meritocracy in Late Imperial China* (Harvard UP, 2013).

**2. STRUCTURAL CONCEPT MAPPING**

- **Model Colonization (Concept 1) — almost the prototype non-Western case.** Keju began as a meritocratic recruitment device tested across multiple subject areas (the Sui-Tang "Five Studies" included military strategy, civil law, taxation, agriculture, geography). Over the second millennium it progressively narrowed: Song reforms emphasized the Confucian classics; the Yuan canonized Zhu Xi's Neo-Confucian commentaries as the only authorized interpretation; the Ming reduced acceptable form to the eight-legged essay. The model justified its continued use by reframing every governance failure (frontier wars, Ming fiscal collapse, 19th-century inability to respond to European technology) as a problem of insufficient classical learning or moral cultivation, never as evidence the curriculum had cut itself off from the empirical world. Huang (2023) documents this with a database of Chinese inventions: technological innovation is strongly anti-correlated with Keju intensity at the dynasty level.
- **Self-Confirming Framing (Concept 3, partial).** Because Keju was the gatekeeper for elite status, ambitious families optimized their sons' upbringing for it from age three. The "imprinting effect" Huang describes — generations of literati shaped by, and committed to, the very curriculum that excluded their later innovation — is the institutional analog of the Schelling Trap: the framework selected for participants who confirmed the framework.

**3. SURPRISE LEVEL — High.**
The general viewer knows "the Chinese had civil-service exams before Europe did" as a positive fact about meritocracy. The argument that the system *was* a millennium-long case of a special-purpose instrument colonizing the entire epistemic landscape — that it functioned exactly as the PD does in modern policy thinking, by canonizing a frame and absorbing failures as scope clarifications — is genuinely unfamiliar. The Yasheng Huang framing in particular (a Foreign Affairs "Best Book of 2023" reviewed in WSJ and FT) has not yet penetrated general audiences.

**4. ANALOGY BREAK-POINT**
- *Keju was not analytical, it was selective.* The PD claims to describe how rational actors will behave; Keju was a recruitment tournament. The "model" being colonized is not a *predictive* model in the same sense; it is a curriculum. Mapping requires care: Keju "predicts" who deserves authority, the way the PD "predicts" who will defect. Both reframe failures as scope.
- *Political coercion, not just intellectual capture.* The Ming-Qing emperors actively enforced curricular orthodoxy through punishment. PD's hold on Cold War strategic thinking was sociological and ideological, not coercive. The analogy works at the *systemic* level (a frame cannot be displaced because too much is staked on it) but the mechanisms differ.
- *Counterfactual harder to assess.* The Needham Question — why did China not develop industrial science despite a long lead — is still contested; some historians (Mark Elvin, Kenneth Pomeranz) argue the Keju explanation is overdetermined. The PD case has cleaner counterfactuals (Ostrom's commons, Ottoman millets) where you can point to specific arrangements that worked outside the model.

**5. SOURCE QUALITY — Strong, with one caveat.**
Excellent: Benjamin Elman is the dean of Anglophone Keju historiography — *A Cultural History of Civil Examinations in Late Imperial China* (2000) is unimpeachable. Yasheng Huang's 2023 book is empirically rich (uses a new database of inventions and political events) and published by Yale UP, but its causal thesis about Keju → stagnation is contested by China specialists; treat it as the strongest version of the case, not as settled. The Cambridge Element by Wang & Yang, *The Political Economy of China's Imperial Examination System* (2024), is a useful neutral synthesis. Caveat: avoid framing Keju as monolithic across 1300 years; Elman shows substantial variation. The model-colonization argument is strongest for the Ming-Qing period, weaker for Tang-early-Song.

**6. SCRIPT POTENTIAL**
Strong. Visuals: surviving examination cells (the *haowu*) at Nanjing's Jiangnan Examination Hall — about 20,000 cells in a grid the size of a stadium, where candidates wrote for nine days; reproduction eight-legged essays; Zhu Xi portraits. One sentence: *"For roughly thirteen hundred years, the most powerful selection mechanism in human history asked one question: how well can you write in a particular form about a particular set of texts? Every time the answers stopped solving China's problems, the response was to refine the test, never to question whether the test was the problem."*

**7. RESONANCE SCORE — 7.5/10.**
High surprise × good structural depth on Concept 1 × strong sources (with caveat on Huang) × good visual potential. Lower than #1 and #2 because the analogy requires more setup (the "model" is a curriculum, not a predictive equation), and because Pass 1 already covers institutional scaffolding via Hanseatic and waqf — the Keju case adds Concept 1 in a non-Western context, but it's a *thematic* extension rather than a sui generis mechanism.

## CONNECTION 4 — THE MAJOR EVOLUTIONARY TRANSITIONS AS COMPOUNDED COOPERATION RESIDUALS

**1. NAME — domain, period, specific case**
Evolutionary biology, 1990s–present, building on Leo Buss, *The Evolution of Individuality* (1987). Canonical statement: John Maynard Smith & Eörs Szathmáry, *The Major Transitions in Evolution* (W.H. Freeman, 1995); updated in Szathmáry, "Toward major evolutionary transitions theory 2.0," *PNAS* 112 (2015), and Bourke, *Principles of Social Evolution* (Oxford UP, 2011). The eight transitions: replicating molecules → chromosomes; independent replicators → RNA/DNA + protein; prokaryotes → eukaryotes; asexual → sexual reproduction; protists → multicellular organisms; solitary → colonial individuals; primate societies → human societies with language.

**2. STRUCTURAL CONCEPT MAPPING**

- **Cooperation Residual (Concept 2) — at every level of biological organization.** A strict gene-centered ("selfish gene") view predicts that lower-level replicators will defect from higher-level cooperative ventures because defection has higher local fitness. Yet the entire history of complex life is a stack of cases where exactly the opposite happened: replicators bound into chromosomes, prokaryotes into eukaryotes, cells into bodies, individuals into colonies. Each transition is a cooperation residual the simple model couldn't predict and now must explain post-hoc with auxiliary apparatus (kin selection, multilevel selection, policing mechanisms, enforcement of fair meiosis, etc.). The Maynard-Smith-Szathmáry program is the clearest case in biology of cooperation being treated as the puzzle requiring special explanation, with the prediction of conflict treated as the natural state.
- **Institutional Scaffolding (Concept 4).** Bourke's 2011 synthesis and the West, Fisher, Gardner & Kiers (2015) *PNAS* paper explicitly argue that what makes transitions possible is the *suppression of within-group conflict* — policing mechanisms, fair meiosis, monogamous mating in eusocial insects. These are biological "institutions" that transform the game from one with PD-like incentives into one where defection is mechanically suppressed. The parallel to assumption-satisfaction (repeated interaction, communication, identifiable actors) is direct.

**3. SURPRISE LEVEL — Medium to High.**
Educated viewers familiar with Dawkins know "the selfish gene." Most do not know that the *major problem* of evolutionary biology since 1995 has been explaining why cooperation, not selfishness, dominates the history of life — and that the field's flagship answer (Maynard Smith & Szathmáry's theory of transitions) is structurally a response to a model that kept predicting the wrong thing. The framing "the entire history of complex life is a refutation of the simple selfish-gene model" is accurate, well-documented, and unfamiliar.

**4. ANALOGY BREAK-POINT**
- *Pass 1 already covers evolutionary cooperation* (vampire bats, mycorrhizae, lichens, cleaner fish). The marginal value is the *meta-level*: those are individual cases of cooperation; the Major Transitions framing is the claim that *all* increases in biological complexity are cooperation-residuals stacked on each other. Frame as the deeper version, not as a duplicate.
- *No agent rationality.* Selection is not deliberation. The PD's "rational defection" maps to "fitness-maximizing defection," which is a different kind of claim — it's an outcome, not a choice.
- *Dawkins's defense.* Defenders of the selfish-gene framework (Dawkins, West, Gardner) argue that inclusive-fitness theory was always able to predict the transitions and that no separate framework is needed. This is a live dispute (see the Nowak-Tarnita-Wilson 2010 *Nature* paper and the storm of replies). The episode should not present "the selfish gene was wrong" as settled; the argument is "the simple gene-as-selfish-actor model needed substantial scaffolding to handle cooperation, and the scaffolding looks structurally identical to Lakatosian protective belt."

**5. SOURCE QUALITY — Excellent.**
Maynard Smith & Szathmáry (1995) is the canonical text; the 2015 *PNAS* update is open-access. Bourke's *Principles of Social Evolution* (2011) is the standard graduate text. Calcott & Sterelny (eds.), *The Major Transitions in Evolution Revisited* (MIT Press, 2011) collects the philosophical critique. Samir Okasha, *Evolution and the Levels of Selection* (Oxford UP, 2006) gives the philosophy-of-biology reading. All peer-reviewed, all primary.

**6. SCRIPT POTENTIAL**
Good. Visuals: classic stack diagram (replicators → chromosomes → cells → multicellular → colonies → societies) with each layer labeled "predicted to defect, observed to cooperate"; time-lapse of slime mold (*Dictyostelium*) aggregation; eusocial insect colony footage. One sentence: *"If the simple selfish-gene model were the whole story, life on Earth would still be a thin film of competing molecules. Every step up the ladder of complexity — from chromosomes to cells to bodies to societies — is a case of cooperation refusing to behave as predicted."*

**7. RESONANCE SCORE — 7/10.**
Medium-high surprise × strong structural depth on Concept 2 × pristine sources × moderate visual potential (mostly diagrammatic). Lower than #1 and #2 because Pass 1 already touches evolutionary cooperation and because the philosophical claim ("the simple model fails") has live academic defenders, requiring epistemic care.

## CONNECTION 5 — DSGE MACROECONOMICS AND THE LAKATOSIAN PROTECTIVE BELT POST-2008

**1. NAME — domain, period, specific case**
Macroeconomics, ~1982 (Kydland-Prescott "Time to Build") through 2008 financial crisis and after. Dynamic Stochastic General Equilibrium (DSGE) models — descendants of Real Business Cycle theory and New Keynesian synthesis (Smets-Wouters 2003, 2007; Christiano-Eichenbaum-Evans 2005) — became the workhorse of central banks (Fed, ECB, Bank of England, IMF) by the early 2000s. None of them predicted the 2008 crisis. The response of the discipline, documented in Joseph Stiglitz, "Where Modern Macroeconomics Went Wrong," *Oxford Review of Economic Policy* 34(1–2), 2018, and Servaas Storm, "Cordon of Conformity: Why DSGE Models Are Not the Future of Macroeconomics" (INET WP 148, 2021), has been to add ad-hoc frictions to preserve the framework rather than replace it.

**2. STRUCTURAL CONCEPT MAPPING**

- **Model Colonization (Concept 1) — explicit, contemporary, well-documented.** Storm (2021) labels the post-2008 response "a Ptolemaic proliferation of DSGE models," each accommodating the crash by adding a small idiosyncratic friction. Stiglitz's 2018 *OxREP* paper enumerates the failures: DSGE assumes representative agents (no debt or asymmetric information), efficient markets, exponential discounting, rational expectations — all directly contradicted by the 2008 evidence — and yet the response has been to layer on frictions while preserving the core. This is Lakatos's protective belt operating in real time, in policy-relevant economics.
- **Cooperation Residual / Stability Residual (Concept 2 inverted).** Where the PD has a cooperation-residual, DSGE has a *crisis residual*: the recurring failure of the models to generate or explain the deep downturns that actually happen. Each major crash (LTCM 1998, dotcom 2001, 2008, 2020) is treated as an exogenous shock requiring a new auxiliary mechanism rather than as evidence that the equilibrium framework is misspecified.
- **Self-Confirming Framing (Concept 3, soft).** Olivier Blanchard's now-infamous August 2008 NBER paper, "The State of Macro," declared that "the state of macro is good" weeks before Lehman's collapse. The discipline's confidence in its own framework was itself a contributor to the policy decisions (e.g., on financial deregulation, the "Great Moderation" narrative) that made the crisis worse. This is softer than the Black–Scholes case because the policy/world feedback loop is less direct, but it is real.

**3. SURPRISE LEVEL — Medium.**
Among economists and policy wonks this is well-known. For a general "geopolitics-interested" viewer, the specific Stiglitz/Storm critique — and the analogy to Ptolemaic epicycles — is novel. Bonus surprise: in the 2010 House Science Subcommittee hearings on macro modeling, *Robert Solow himself* (the Nobel laureate whose growth model is one of the founding documents of modern macro) testified that DSGE models "do not pass the smell test." That detail lands.

**4. ANALOGY BREAK-POINT**
- *DSGE has predictive ambitions; PD has descriptive ones.* DSGE models are evaluated on forecast accuracy in a way the PD is not. The model-colonization parallel is therefore tighter to *Black–Scholes than to PD itself* — DSGE plays the role in macro that BSM plays in derivatives. Use this case to illustrate the *general pattern* of model colonization, not as a direct PD analog.
- *The protective-belt move is institutionally explicit.* Central-bank research departments are organized around DSGE models; careers depend on them; the discipline's response is partly sociological-bureaucratic (Storm calls it the "cordon of conformity"), not purely epistemic. The PD's hold on Cold War strategic studies has similar institutional/career dynamics, but the parallel is sharper if you compare DSGE to RAND-era PD enthusiasm rather than to the PD itself.
- *Live, ongoing, contested.* Unlike Galenic medicine or pre-1543 anatomy, DSGE has not been displaced. The episode would be claiming a parallel that is still being adjudicated. This is a strength (relevance) and a risk (over-claim).

**5. SOURCE QUALITY — Strong.**
Stiglitz (2018) *Oxford Review of Economic Policy* is the headline citation; Storm (2021) is more polemical but well-sourced and published by INET. Blanchard (2008), "The State of Macro," NBER WP 14259, is the on-the-record pre-crisis confidence. The 2020 *OxREP* "Rebuilding Macroeconomic Theory" issue (Vines & Wills, eds.) is the establishment's effort to respond. Note: the strongest critics are heterodox or center-left mainstream (Stiglitz, Krugman, Romer's "The Trouble with Macroeconomics," 2016); the case requires presenting the orthodox defense as well (Christiano-Eichenbaum-Trabandt, "On DSGE Models," *JEP* 2018).

**6. SCRIPT POTENTIAL**
Moderate. Less visual than the others — equations, charts, central-bank press conferences. Best deployed as a brief reinforcing example rather than a full beat. One sentence: *"After 2008, when not a single DSGE model had predicted the crash, the response of the macroeconomics profession was to add small frictions to the same models — what one critic calls a 'Ptolemaic proliferation' designed to preserve the core assumption that markets clear rationally."*

**7. RESONANCE SCORE — 6.5/10.**
Medium surprise × good structural depth on Concept 1 × strong sources × moderate visual potential. Lower because the case is closer to the user's own world (most viewers know "economists missed the crash") than the others, and because it duplicates territory the Black–Scholes case already covers more vividly.

### Recommended for the script's working set: #1 (Black–Scholes / Volatility Smile) and #2 (Paradox of the Plankton).

Reasoning: These two cover the three structural concepts most clearly, sit in domains (modern finance, marine ecology) genuinely *underrepresented* in Pass 1, are sourced to peer-reviewed, prize-winning, primary scholarship, and offer strong visuals. They also pair well rhetorically: Black–Scholes is the case where humans make the model true (Concept 3), and the Plankton is the case where nature refuses to make the model true (Concepts 1+2). Together they bracket the PD: one shows the Schelling Trap mechanism; the other shows the cooperation residual mechanism. If forced to pick only one, take **Black–Scholes/Volatility Smile** — it's the cleanest non-PD instance of self-confirming strategic framing in the modern world, and the "Engine, Not a Camera" framing gives the episode a memorable rhetorical handle.

#3 (Keju) is the strongest alternate if the episode wants a non-Western, longue-durée case to balance the two modern-Western examples. #4 and #5 should be held as B-roll references or footnotes rather than full beats.

### Caveats for the writer

1. *MacKenzie's performativity argument has critics* — notably Daniel Beunza and Yuval Millo's later work emphasizes that performativity is partial and contingent, not total. Don't overstate ("the model became true") without the qualifier ("approximately, until 1987").
2. *The Keju–stagnation thesis is contested.* Use Huang as the strongest version; flag that Pomeranz, Elvin, and others offer different accounts of the Great Divergence.
3. *Major Transitions theory has a live internal dispute* (Nowak vs. Hamilton-Dawkins-West school). Frame as "the field's mainstream answer to a real problem," not as "the selfish gene was wrong."
