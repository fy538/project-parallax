## Summary

This script serves **Priya (Geopolitics Regular)** and **Sofia (Framework Thinker)** at a high level — the argument is novel, the evidence is genuine, the framework pivot from PD to stag hunt is intellectually honest, and the v5.1 reflection trigger gives them something portable. **Marcus (Algorithm Discovery)** is substantially better served than in v4: the curiosity-pivot seed at 7:30 arrives precisely at his decision-point and successfully reframes the episode's central question as a mystery rather than an argument, converting him from a likely 7-minute dropout to a probable finisher. **James (Tech Insider)** is engaging but somewhat miscast — this is a philosophy episode using nuclear strategy and climate governance as examples, not a tech episode, and James has no domain authority to validate the game theory claims he'd instinctively want verified. **Amara (Cross-Cultural Viewer)** gets a meaningfully better hook than in v4 (the pivot seed opens space for non-Western answers) but the intellectual framework gap persists in Beat 5: the "stag hunt reframe" draws on Rousseau, Skyrms, and Ostrom without ever examining whether actors from different strategic traditions might define "coordination" differently. The stag hunt reframe doesn't capture this. The script offers Reagan-Gorbachev as the hopeful precedent but the INF Treaty worked because both sides agreed on what nuclear weapons reduction *meant* in strategic terms. The AI governance problem may involve fundamental disagreement about the strategic frame itself.

The reflection trigger — "which game are they actually playing?" — is correct as a question but assumes the answer is "a stag hunt they haven't recognized yet." The harder possibility: they're playing games that don't share a coordinate system.

## Cross-Persona Analysis

### Best served
**Sofia and Priya.** Both come for intellectual rigor and novel frameworks; both leave with the stag hunt reframe, the Ostrom evidence, and a portable analytical question. The script was built for this audience and delivers.

### Underserved
**Amara** — the intellectual-tradition gap. The episode critiques a Western framework (PD) and replaces it with another Western framework (stag hunt). The non-Western actors it invokes (China, climate negotiators from developing countries) are treated as subjects of Western analytical frameworks, not sources of alternative analytical vocabularies. The script knows this gap exists at the institutional level (RAND funding) but doesn't extend the critique to its own proposed solution.

**James** — domain mismatch, not script failure. This is a philosophy episode; James's technical verification instincts fire without an outlet. The Skyrms citation gap is the one actionable fix.
