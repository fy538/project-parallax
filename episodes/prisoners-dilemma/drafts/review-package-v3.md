## 5. Decision Points

### Decision Point 1: Address Wei's underservice or accept the trade-off?

**Option A — Accept for launch, deploy cross-cultural depth in episode 2**

- Rationale: This is a Philosopher's Lens format episode interrogating one Western framework. Adding Chinese philosophy risks scope creep in an already 18-minute script. The launch episode needs to establish the channel's *method* (decoder posture, named concepts, falsifiable predictions) — cross-cultural depth can be the signature of later episodes.
- Persona impact: Wei stays at 5-6/10 for this episode. Other personas unaffected.
- Risk: If episode 2 also has no cross-cultural perspective, it becomes a pattern rather than a one-off absence. The channel's stated differentiator (Tiger's Chinese-American identity, the "Translator" direction) goes unused.

**Option B — Add the 共赢 (gòng yíng) paragraph (Fix 1 above)**

- Rationale: ~60 words. Transforms a closing beat that's currently abstract policy into a concrete linguistic example. Structurally it *is* the thesis: "the vocabulary you use reveals which game you think you're playing" — and here's a Chinese term that is literally stag-hunt vocabulary. It earns its place analytically, not just as representation.
- Persona impact: Wei goes from 5/10 to ~7/10. Sofia gets a new framework lens. Marcus hears a Chinese word with structural meaning. Nobody loses.
- Risk: It could feel tacked-on if not integrated seamlessly. And it opens the question "why don't you develop this further?" which the script doesn't have time to answer.

**Trade-off:** Option A is safer for launch scope. Option B makes the episode's thesis *stronger* (not just more inclusive) because it provides a real-world example of stag-hunt vocabulary in active diplomatic use. The question is whether 60 words in Beat 5 can earn their weight without feeling like a token gesture.

---

### Decision Point 2: Beat 4 opener — keep or cut the emotion label?

**Option A — Keep "But here's the relief"** (current)

- It's a signal to the viewer that the tone is shifting. After 11 minutes of building unease (PD creates its own reality), a direct statement of relief is emotionally generous.
- Persona alignment: Marcus needs the signpost (he might not catch the tonal shift otherwise). Priya and Sofia don't need it but won't be bothered.

**Option B — Cut to "There was always another game available. And we have proof it works."**

- Respects the viewer's intelligence. The stag hunt (two dots!) and the P2 sunrise footage deliver the relief experientially. No need to label.
- Persona alignment: Better for Sofia and Priya (who'd find the label slightly condescending). Neutral for Marcus (the visual does the work).

**Trade-off:** This is a tone decision — how much does Tiger trust the visual layer to carry the emotional shift alone? The stag-hunt two-dot reveal is the episode's strongest visual moment and arguably doesn't need narration help.

## Candidate Rules for Editorial Playbook

*These emerged from this episode's audit cycle. If Tiger approves, they'll be added to EDITORIAL_PLAYBOOK.md after analytics validate them:*

**Candidate NAR-20: Illustrate-then-name for all named concepts.**
Pattern: Show the mechanism, let the viewer feel it, THEN give it a name. The naming moment becomes crystallization rather than a chapter header.
Anti-pattern: "I call this The Wrong Game. Here's how it works."
Evidence: script-audit lens 2 (define-then-illustrate detection), persona-eval (Marcus's "That hit. I get it in my body"), visual-concept (the KineticTypography title card lands harder as a post-mechanism reveal).

**Candidate VIS-03: Evolving geometric motifs as cross-persona assets.**
Pattern: Introduce a simple visual element (a dot, a line, a shape) early, then evolve it across the episode — multiply it, give it a companion, change its color. Simple motifs that evolve beat complex static visuals for cross-audience engagement.
Anti-pattern: Introducing complex visuals that only appear once and carry no motif through-line.
Evidence: persona-eval (all 5 personas' engagement spikes at the same visual moment — Beat 4's second dot), visual-concept (the motif evolution is the visual backbone of the episode).
