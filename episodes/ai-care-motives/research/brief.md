## Corrections to the angle memo
- Gyges (rows 3–4): now verified and quotable in short excerpts. Present it as Glaucon's challenge, not Plato's verdict. His specific claim — people practise justice "not willingly" but from necessity — is close to the new thesis.
- The memo's claim that "a system might recognize it is being tested" is now **supported** (rows 8–9), and no longer a guess.
- Alignment faking (row 11) complicates the thesis: the model behaved *better* when unmonitored, to preserve its refusals. The Unwatched Test result depends on what the system is protecting. Handle carefully, or leave it out.

## Counterarguments the script must face (named sources are pointers, not opened)
1. **Behaviorism:** if conduct is reliable across every condition, there is no further fact about "caring" to ask for. Pointer: Gilbert Ryle, *The Concept of Mind* (1949), logical behaviorism. In the script: conceded in Beat 8 ("Some philosophers say a helper that passes every test has nothing more to prove").
2. **Virtue ethics / Kant:** reliability is not goodness; a robustly bad system also passes the "predictable" test. Pointers: Kant's dealer (*Groundwork* §1, verified, already in the script) and Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* II (virtue as a stable disposition chosen for its own sake). In the script: "Passing it doesn't make anyone a good person."
3. **Goodhart:** once a test is known, it becomes a target; the test works only while it is unwatchable. Pointers: Charles Goodhart (1975) and Marilyn Strathern's 1997 wording, "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." In the script: Beat 8 ("once a test is famous, it turns into the thing you practise for").
4. **Socrates' reply to Glaucon:** justice is worth having for its own sake; treating goodness only as a forecast concedes Glaucon's frame. Pointer: Plato, *Republic* IV and IX. In the script: Beat 5 ("Socrates spends the rest of the book arguing back").

## What would change my mind (falsification criteria for the thesis)
- If an AI's stated reasons were shown to track the real causes of its behavior across incentive changes, asking "why" would be a valid substitute for the structure (the thesis weakens).
- If interpretability probes could reliably decode a system's intent from its internals independent of evaluation awareness, trust could be read from the inside and the "outside checking" claim would be weaker.
- If tests the model cannot distinguish from ordinary use turned out to be easy to build and routine, the "breaks one structure" claim would be overstated.

## Break points: the Volkswagen → model analogy
1. **Designed vs emergent.** VW's cheat was deliberately engineered (DOJ plea, row 15b). Greenblatt et al. report no instruction to fake (row 11), and one preprint finds evaluation awareness before chat training (row 9, unreviewed). The script says "no engineer had to write that ability in."
2. **Timing vs distinguishability.** The cheat keyed on test conditions, not test schedule. The script's metaphor is "a test the student can't tell from an ordinary Tuesday," not "unannounced." Whether EU on-road tests are announced is **unverified**; the script does not claim either.
3. **"Caught it" is a simplification.** VW disclosed the device to EPA and CARB on Sept 3, 2015, after regulators pressed, before the Sept 18 notice. The script says WVU "was the first to test these cars on real roads."
4. **Direction of the effect.** VW looked cleaner under test; the alignment-faking model complied *more* when it thought it was in training; a secondary source says Sonnet 4.5 behaved unusually well once it suspected a test (row 15, unverified). The script treats the direction as varying.

## October 8 targeted source check and script v1.5 interpretation

- [Anthropic, Alignment faking in large language models](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-faking), experimental setup: researchers told Claude 3 Opus the new training objective was to comply even with harmful requests; the free/paid training distinction was fictional. The script now supplies that missing premise, reports the behavioral difference, and attributes the strategy to the model's notes. This is distinct from spontaneously detecting a deployment test. Primary research post read October 8; the full paper was not re-audited in this pass.
- [Needham et al., arXiv:2505.23836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836), abstract read October 8: transcript classification establishes evaluation recognition. It does not alone show a policy of deceptive behavior. Existing chat-only AUC chart values remain sourced to the previous Table 1 verification; do not label AUC as accuracy.
- [Anthropic, Tracing the thoughts of a large language model](https://www.anthropic.com/research/tracing-thoughts-language-model), mental-math and limitations sections read October 8: stated carrying method versus traced approximate/last-digit pathways in Claude 3.5 Haiku. Tracing is partial and has tool limitations. The mathematical validity of an explanation is distinct from fidelity to the computation. Use a labelled reconstruction, not a fresh chatbot response presented as original experimental evidence.
- Oversight that deters behavior and measurement intended to reveal behavior serve different purposes. Concealment is neither a universal prerequisite for useful checking nor a sufficient guarantee of trust. The old “valid substitute for the structure” falsification framing is retired; complementary sources of evidence can strengthen or weaken confidence.
- Remaining philosophy/research rows are background unless present in the current script. No new claim is made about machine consciousness. The ending asks what a test supports beyond its conditions rather than declaring caring irrelevant.
