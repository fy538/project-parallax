I need 3-4 strong historical analogies for resource weaponization between great powers. For each one, go deep:

- **What was the resource, who controlled it, what was the leverage mechanism, and how did it resolve?**

## 3. Philosophical Frameworks (the analytical spine — what makes this channel different)

This is the most important section. Don't treat these as afterthoughts — they're the *core value proposition* of the channel.

### Game Theory Lens
- How does the **security dilemma** apply? (US restricts chips for security → China accelerates domestic capability → US faces a more capable rival with no leverage → both worse off?)
- **Commitment problems**: Can the US credibly commit to a stable technology-sharing arrangement? Can China credibly commit to not weaponizing chip self-sufficiency?
- **Schelling points and red lines**: Where are the focal points for escalation/de-escalation? Are there natural "equilibria" or is this inherently unstable?
- What does the game-theoretic literature actually say about technology denial strategies between great powers?

### Complex Systems Lens
- **Feedback loops**: What are the reinforcing and balancing loops in the semiconductor ecosystem? (e.g., export controls → Chinese investment → talent poaching → Western countermeasures → ...)
- **Unintended consequences**: What second- and third-order effects of chip restrictions are already visible?
- **Tipping points**: Is there a threshold where China's domestic capability becomes self-sustaining? What would that look like?
- **Fragility vs. robustness**: How fragile is the current semiconductor supply chain? What are the single points of failure?

### Stoic Lens (human agency within structural forces)
- **Key decision-makers and their constraints**: Who are the individuals whose choices shaped this trajectory? (Think: Morris Chang, Jensen Huang, Xi Jinping's semiconductor advisors, the architects of US export control policy.) What were they actually choosing between?
- **Dichotomy of control**: What do each side's policymakers actually control vs. what are they trying to control but can't?
- **Virtue ethics dimension**: Are there moments where individual courage, prudence, or hubris visibly altered the trajectory?

### Bayesian Epistemology Lens
- **What should we update on?** Given the evidence from 2019-2026, what beliefs about the chip war should a rational observer have updated? What surprised the experts?
- **Prediction track records**: Who predicted the current situation correctly? Who got it wrong? Why?
- **Uncertainty mapping**: What are the key unknowns that would most change the analysis? What information would most shift a Bayesian prior about the outcome?
- **Epistemic humility**: What do we genuinely not know, and what would it be irresponsible to claim certainty about?
