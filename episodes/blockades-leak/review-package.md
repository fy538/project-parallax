## Priority Fix List (Ranked by Impact)

All fixes are narrative reshaping issues, not visual problems. The issues exist because the script tries to deliver too much analytical content without structural breathing room.

### Fix 1: Beat 1→2 Transition — Broken Logical Bridge (HIGH IMPACT)
**Source:** script-audit (Lens 1: Transitions)  
**Location:** End of Beat 1 → Start of Beat 2 (2:00 boundary)  
**Problem:** Viewer is promised a story about "whether the problem is solvable at all" (Beat 1 closing), then the script pivots to "here's a pattern that explains it" (Beat 2 opening). The handoff is abrupt. The promised question (solvability) doesn't match the actual move (structure explanation). Feels like narrative whiplash rather than logical continuation.

**Current text (Beat 1 end):**
> "This isn't a story about good enforcement versus bad enforcement. This is a story about whether the problem is solvable at all."

**Suggested fix:**
> "This isn't a story about good enforcement versus bad enforcement. It's a story about structure. And structure is harder to change than policy."

This reframes the closing question as structural (matching the Beat 2 pivot), making the framework explanation feel earned rather than introduced.

**Expected impact:** HIGH — This one-sentence change transforms the transition from abrupt to seamless. The viewer now *expects* an explanation of structural patterns instead of a solution exploration.

### Fix 3: Beat 2 Define-Then-Illustrate Lecture Mode — Switch to Decoder Posture (MEDIUM IMPACT)
**Source:** script-audit (Lens 2: Lecture Patterns, specifically Issue 2.1)  
**Location:** Beat 2, the Tragedy of the Commons introduction (approximately 2:15-4:00)  
**Problem:** The script defines tragedy of the commons *first* ("Imagine you're a herder...") then applies it to chips ("Now apply that to technology denial..."). This is explainer mode (define-then-apply). The decoder posture (Playbook: NAR-09) would show the mechanism *first in the chip context*, then name the framework after the viewer has recognized the pattern. This shifts the emotional contract from "let me teach you" to "watch what I'm seeing."

**Current structure:**
1. "Imagine you're a herder on a shared pasture..." [defines tragedy of commons in abstract]
2. "Now apply that to technology denial..." [applies to chips]
3. "In 1968, a biologist named Garrett Hardin named it..." [names the framework]

**Suggested rewrite — illustrate-then-name:**
> "When access to a strategic technology is restricted through international agreement, each nation faces a choice: cooperate and fall behind, or defect and gain a window of advantage. Breaking ranks means accessing forbidden chips undetected long enough to build capability. You get a hundred percent of the benefit. And the cost of regime failure? Shared across all who agreed to restrict.
>
> A biologist named Garrett Hardin named this problem in 1968 — the tragedy of the commons. He wasn't thinking about chips. He was thinking about a herder on a shared pasture. Your incentive is simple: add one more goat. You capture a hundred percent of the benefit. And the cost? Shared by all. So you add the goat. But when every herder does the same calculation, the pasture dies.
>
> That's called tragedy: the rational choice leads to ruin."

This flips the order: chip mechanism (what's happening now) → framework name (Hardin) → illustration (herders). The viewer discovers the structure *with you* in the chips context, then learns what it's called. This is decoder mode.

**Expected impact:** MEDIUM — Shifts the emotional register from lecture to co-investigation. The intellectual content is identical; only the framing changes. Maintains Playbook: NAR-09 (decoder posture) consistency.

### Fix 5: Beat 5 Case Study Repetition — Reduce Four Detailed Cases to Two Detailed + Two Brief (HIGH IMPACT)
**Source:** visual-concept (Lens 3: Visual Monotony, Issue Beat 5), script-audit (Lens 8: Connection Density)  
**Location:** Beat 5, approximately 14:30-17:00 (2:30 runtime)  
**Problem:** Four parallel case studies (Venice, fishing, NPT, OPEC), each with ~4-5 compositions, creates 18 MG compositions in 2.5 minutes. The repetition is a *narrative feature* (showing the pattern repeats across domains), but *visually* it becomes monotonous: case setup → framework comparison → case setup → framework comparison → case setup... Four times. By the second parallel, the visual pattern is clear; by the fourth, it feels rote rather than revelatory.

**Current structure (approximate composition count):**
- Venice: attribution (3s) + footage (6s) + comparison diagram (6s) = 3 comps
- Fishing: footage (4s) + chart (5s) + curve (5s) + comparison (5s) = 4 comps
- NPT: attribution (3s) + timeline (6s) + payoff matrix (6s) + footage (4s) = 4 comps
- OPEC: attribution (3s) + chart (6s) + curve (5s) + comparison (8s) = 4 comps
- Total: 15 compositions over ~150 words narration

**Suggested restructuring — two detailed + two brief:**
- **Venice (full treatment):** 3 compositions — attribution, glassblowing footage, Murano vs Modern comparison. Keep this as the iconic historical case (most parallel to chips, longest monopoly).
- **OPEC (full treatment):** 3 compositions — attribution, quota violations chart, UAE exit announcement. Keep this as the most recent, most vivid parallel (happening now, April 2026).
- **Fishing (brief callout):** 1 composition — show fishing quota chart as evidence. Narration carries the parallel without dwelling: "Fishing quotas show the same pattern since 1983..."
- **NPT (brief callout):** 1 composition — Khan network timeline. Narration: "Nuclear weapons treaties face the same defection pressure, as the 30-year-undetected Khan network showed."

The beat transforms from "four full case studies" to "two detailed cases, two evidence points supporting the structure." Reduces visual saturation from 18 MG in 2:30 to ~13 MG, allowing longer holds on the two powerful cases and faster movement through the supporting evidence.

**Narration restructure (sample):**
Instead of: "Here's Venice. [details]. Here's fishing. [details]. Here's NPT. [details]. Here's OPEC. [details]..."
Restructure to: "This pattern shows up everywhere. Venice's glass monopoly fell to defection and knowledge spread. OPEC's cartel is fragmenting from internal quota violations — the UAE just quit in April. Fishing quotas, nuclear treaties, technology denials: same structure, different domains. The pattern is universal."

This preserves the intellectual power (four domains prove universality) while dramatically improving visual rhythm. The move from "four detailed cases" to "two detailed + two compact evidence points" also strengthens the decoder posture: you're showing the pattern *and* proving it's structural across domains, not just collecting examples.

**Expected impact:** HIGH — Solves the visual fatigue problem in Beat 5 without losing the cross-domain proof. Transforms the beat from "monotonous case-study carousel" to "two deep cases + supporting evidence for universality." The narration is actually more efficient this way.
