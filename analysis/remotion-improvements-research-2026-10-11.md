> October 11, 2026. Repo audit by reading code, data and rendered QA stills (`remotion-templates/out/qa/ai-care-motives/`, five contact sheets and eight full frames); package use checked with grep and by loading the installed packages in Node; clip files inspected with `ffprobe`. Web research from text sources only (official Remotion docs, docs source and release notes on GitHub, the Remotion team's own agent-skill rule files, papers, design-system guidelines, practitioner write-ups), all read Oct 11, 2026 unless a publication date is given. Nothing was installed, rendered or edited, and no video was watched, so every statement about how the episode *moves* comes from code and stills. ### 1.2 Weaknesses confirmed in code

1. **Fonts are fetched from Google at render time, and 95% of the fetches are for fonts this episode never uses.** `fonts.ts` is imported once in `Root.tsx:13`, so every composition and every browser tab runs it. The loader (`node_modules/@remotion/google-fonts/dist/cjs/base.js`) builds one `FontFace` per family × style × weight × subset pointing at `fonts.gstatic.com` and loads each eagerly under its own `delayRender` (60 s handle, 18 s per attempt, three attempts). Counted from the package metadata: **685 font files per tab**, of which 652 are Noto Sans SC (202), Noto Serif SC (202) and Noto Sans JP (248), loaded with no `subsets` because the subset name fails. The episode's data files contain no CJK characters; it needs 20 files (Plex Sans, Serif, Mono, JetBrains Mono). The four calls without `subsets` (the three Noto families and Oswald) will throw in Remotion 5 ([font loading errors](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/troubleshooting/font-loading-errors), [loadFont](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/google-fonts/load-font)).
2. **Illustrations are baked into H.264 clips before Remotion sees them.** `tools/ai-video/render_kenburns.py` renders each still with ffmpeg `zoompan` (zoom linear in frame number). All 12 shot-list entries are `ken-burns`: 11 push in from 1.0 to 1.08–1.15, one pulls out. The result is 16 clip files used in 22 segments, 248 s or 37% of the runtime, played through `OffthreadVideo` under `BrandImage`. Consequences: every picture makes the same slow push; a reused still restarts the same move from frame 0 (six clip files are used twice; QA already flags `aigen-10` ×5 and `aigen-06` ×4 by still); the picture is encoded twice; and a re-time that lengthens a slot past the clip needs a new clip. The source stills are 6688×3764, enough for a 3.5× punch-in at full sharpness, and none of that range is used. `ImageComposite` already moves photographs natively with a CSS scale.
3. **No reveal follows the voice yet.** The manifest is `mode: "estimate"` with no narration file; 0 of 53 segments carry resolved `syncPoints`. About 35 start times are typed in seconds across the 28 data files (`atSec` 15, `appearAtSec` 9, `paragraphAtSec` 3, `statAtSec`, `rowAtSec`, `revealAtSec` 2 each, `conditionAtSec`, `branchesAtSec`). `ForegroundSegment` in `FullEpisode.tsx` neither passes the slot length as `durationSec` nor scales typed seconds, so points 5 and 6 of the Oct 10 design (A4) are still open. The positional-cue fix (`resolvedSyncPrefix`) is in.
4. **Every card cuts in empty and then fades up.** Inside an episode, templates no longer fade themselves in as a whole (seam ownership), but `TitleBlock` (used in 65 template files) still fades the title from frame 0, and content starts 0.3–1.2 s later (`AXIOMS_START_SEC`, `DEFAULT_FIRST_ROW_SEC`), or at the typed second: the first row of `who-checks-whom.json` arrives at 4.6 s. A cut to a card is therefore a cut to bare paper with a frame line and wordmark.
5. **One entrance for almost everything.** `fadeIn(` appears about 549 times and `slideIn(` 78 times, against 20 spring calls. Fade lengths cluster at 0.4 s (123 uses), 0.5 s (103), 0.3 s (63) and 0.6 s (46) whatever the element's size or travel; rises are 8–20 px upward. `AxiomList.tsx` and `PairedComparison.tsx` both use fade 0.6 s plus a 14 px rise for every row, with ordinal, text and gloss moving as one block.
6. **Timing and easing tokens exist but are not the vocabulary in use.** `timing.entrance|exit|stagger|hold` appears on 67 lines; `sec(` is called 1,359 times, mostly with literals. Three easing vocabularies coexist (`easing` beziers in `theme.ts`, the `CLAMP_*` poly presets, the `easings` role map in `animation.ts`), and doctrine disagrees with itself on overshoot: `theme.ts` and `remotion-templates/CLAUDE.md` forbid it, POLISH A2 asks for it, `heroSpring` (damping 12) produces it.
7. **Dissolve tails freeze the outgoing shot.** `HoldTail` wraps the segment in `<Freeze>` for the tail. Fine for a settled card; a moving picture stops dead when a dissolve or hold-under begins (in this episode the only moving picture given a tail is at `beat5-seg27 → 28`, where the hold is intended).
8. **Seam ownership is a module-level mutable flag** (`setSeamsOwnedByEpisode`, set during render in `FullEpisode`). It works because one composition renders per page; it is fragile if two compositions ever mount together (Studio thumbnails, a Player page).
9. **No premounting.** No `premountFor` anywhere in `src/`. Rendering is unaffected; Studio playback mounts each of the 53 segments at its first frame.
10. **Dead or duplicated helpers.** `morphPath` / `morphPathAnimated` (about 130 lines in `animation.ts`) have no callers and duplicate `interpolatePath` from `@remotion/paths`. `remotion-templates/CLAUDE.md` and LESSONS L44/L83 still describe `useEntrance`, which was removed (`src/hooks/index.ts:40`).

### 1.4 What the episode looks like (contact sheets and frames)

- Two worlds: dark, textured, painterly illustrations and bright cream cards with thin type. In beat 7 (7:47–9:41) the picture alternates between them six times. The QA report counts only card-to-card switches (4).
- Nearly all of the 28 template segments share one skeleton (the two dark title cards and the full-bleed Wolfsburg photograph are the exceptions): inset frame line, wordmark top left, title top left, content centred in the remaining area, source bottom right. Title and content sit on different alignment axes, and on sparse cards (`three-kinds-of-test`, `three-checks`, `checkpoint-three-claims`, `network-street`) the content occupies roughly a third of the frame with a wide gap under the title.
- Depth cues are mixed. Paper grain and hairlines say print; the `StatReveal` bar has a gradient and drop shadow, the hero number a glow, the `HorizontalTimeline` axis a soft red glow, and `ConditionalArgument` uses filled tick and cross badges, which say dashboard.
- Line breaks are whatever the box gives: the branch label in `free-tier-conditional` wraps to leave one word alone on the second line. `textWrap: "balance"` is used in two places, both in `HorizontalTimeline`.
- Holds are long: median segment 12 s, longest 29 s (`portrait-kant`), and the list cards do not change once their rows have arrived.

- **`@remotion/renderer`: a fix for progressive slowdown in long renders** (4.0.533, Oct 5, 2026, PR #11912). No figure is given; an 11-minute, 20,358-frame render is the case it describes. [v4.0.533](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/releases/tag/v4.0.533)

## 3. How larger Remotion projects are structured

**First-hand accounts from essay or explainer channels: none found.** Remotion's published success stories are all automation products (Submagic, Revid, Crayo, Typeframes, Shortvid, YARX, MakeStories). [Success stories](https://www.remotion.dev/success-stories). What exists first-hand is the Remotion team's own guidance and projects:

- **The team's agent-skill rule files** (the instructions they ship for agent-written projects): one substantial scene per component and file, each also registered as its own composition so it has its own timeline (their equivalent of a precomp); timing written on the JSX node with `fps` from `useVideoConfig()`; one second of premount on every timed item; `TransitionSeries` for consecutive scenes; assets in `public/` through `staticFile()`; a built-in effect preferred over a CSS approximation; and for narration, one audio file per scene with `calculateMetadata` deriving each scene's length from its audio. [SKILL.md](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/skills/skills/remotion-markup/SKILL.md), [multi-scene](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/skills/skills/remotion-markup/multi-scene-video.md), [connected compositions](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/skills/skills/remotion-markup/connected-compositions.md), [voiceover](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/skills/skills/remotion-markup/voiceover.md)
- **The files read contain no design guidance.** Nothing on layout, hierarchy, restraint or hold times; they cover determinism, editability and loading. Quality of look is left to the author.
- **Jonny Burger, React Summit Remote, May 14, 2021:** every frame must be a pure function of the frame number because tabs render in parallel; sequences exist so scenes can be built independently; raise spring damping to remove overshoot. [Talk](https://gitnation.com/contents/creating-videos-programmatically-in-react)
- **Burger on Syntax #1005, May 18, 2026:** code stays the source of truth even with visual editing, and the agent workflow still needs a person judging the output. [Transcript](https://www.syntax.fm/show/1005/programatic-and-skill-based-video-creation-with-remotion/transcript)
- **Remotion Recorder** (their video-production template): captions kept as editable JSON with a correction step, layout computed rather than placed by hand, chapters derived from scene data. [Recorder](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/recorder)
- **GitHub Unwrapped** (a public production template of theirs): video template and web app in separate folders, with scripts that precompute gradients and optimise SVGs before render. [Repo](https://github.com/remotion-dev/github-unwrapped)

**Against that, our structure is sound.** A manifest-driven timeline, a template registry, Zod schemas and per-template catalog compositions are the same ideas. The differences are the ones already listed: no premount, data through a generated import file, one narration file re-timed after the fact instead of a duration per scene, and picture motion precomputed outside Remotion in a way that removes control rather than cost.

## 4. Motion craft for information graphics, mapped to our code

| Principle and source | Where we follow it | Where we do not |
|---|---|---|
| **Easing and duration.** Enter on ease-out, leave on ease-in, never linear; duration grows with distance or size travelled; too long is the commoner fault. [NN/g, Laubheimer, Feb 9, 2020](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/), [IBM Carbon motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/). Slow-in, slow-out also makes a move predictable. [Heer & Robertson 2007](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jheer/files/2007-AnimatedTrans-InfoVis.pdf) | `fadeIn` on the decelerate curve; `easing` tokens; the `linear-ok` lint marker | One duration band for everything (1.2 item 5), so a hairline and a full-width bar take the same 0.4–0.5 s. Tokens unused; overshoot doctrine contradictory (item 6). Picture moves are linear and identical (item 2). A constant-speed move that runs from cut to cut is normal documentary practice; the fault is sameness, and no ease where a move starts or settles on screen |
| **Productive versus expressive motion.** Carbon keeps expressive motion for occasional, important moments: "The best interface motion may go unnoticed." [Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/) | Deprecated wipes and zooms, iris retired, the hold-motion register, `stillness` as an option | Every card drifts (scale 1.00 to 1.02) over animated grain and substrate wobble, so stillness is never available as contrast, and the one hero number per episode has nothing extra to spend |
| **Staging.** Present one idea so it cannot be missed (Thomas & Johnston, via [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation)); break complex changes into stages; group things that change together. [Heer & Robertson 2007](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jheer/files/2007-AnimatedTrans-InfoVis.pdf). Signalling aids learning (median effect about 0.4 across 28 tests, as summarised from Mayer's handbook chapter). [Cambridge Handbook](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/C98AB3A6CE760DD63C048936EA0B3B44) | POLISH A4 structure → data → labels; dim and blur of non-focused events in `HorizontalTimeline` and `NetworkDiagram`; the key-row rail | `AxiomList`, `PairedComparison` and `ConditionalArgument` have no focus shift: earlier rows stay at full strength while the narrator has moved on. Structure animates in after the cut instead of being there (item 4) |
| **Stagger is rhythm, not comprehension.** Controlled experiments found staggered starts had a negligible or negative effect on tracking, because common motion and predictability are lost. [Chevalier, Dragicevic & Franconeri, IEEE TVCG 2014](https://visualcognition.psych.northwestern.edu/publications/ChevalierDragicevicFranconeri2014.pdf) | Narration-paced reveals (`staggerSec: 3.3` in `three-kinds-of-test.json`) are sequencing by meaning, which is right | POLISH A3 asks for a 50–150 ms stagger on everything. Keep it for reading order; move together what is one idea |
| **Overlap and secondary action.** Parts of one object arrive at slightly different times; secondary motion supports the main action without competing. [Korenman, School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/animation-tricks-follow-through-after-effects), [Morningstar motion](https://design.morningstar.com/foundations/motion) | `PairedComparison`'s arrow draws for half a second and the right-hand cell lands as it completes; `StatReveal` scales the number, counts, then grows bars; `lockOnPulse` | A list row is one rigid block: ordinal, text, gloss and rail share one opacity and one translate (`AxiomRow`). Opacity and position always share start and length |
| **Motion should mean something.** Fade items in and out for consistency instead of movements that carry no meaning; animate between two graphics only when they share structure, otherwise cut or dissolve. [Heer & Robertson 2007](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jheer/files/2007-AnimatedTrans-InfoVis.pdf). Animation helps presentation and costs accuracy. [Robertson et al., IEEE TVCG 2008](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/papers/infovis08-anim.pdf) | Seam table in `TRANSITION_GRAMMAR.md`; connectors that draw from source to target | The universal upward rise says nothing about where an element comes from or what it belongs to |
| **Text.** No gimmicky or warped type. [Morningstar](https://design.morningstar.com/foundations/motion). Hold and redundancy rules are already ours (`project/VISUAL_QA.md`: 3 s + 0.25 s per word; Mayer redundancy) | Text-animation register with lints; measured title fit; measured highlight spans | Line breaks unmanaged (1.4); quotes arrive whole and sit (QA: `kinetic-glaucon-quote` static for 17 s) |
| **Depth in a flat style.** Overlap, layering and differential movement carry depth. **The tells, in this episode.** Practitioner write-ups name the same few: default easing, everything on one beat, every element given equal weight, entrance and timing changing without a system, and effects stacked everywhere. [Bartek, olafmotion, Mar 25, 2026](https://olafmotion.com/tips/how-to-make-motion-design-more-professional/), [Lieu, Apr 26, 2014](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2014/04/26/25-simple-tricks-for-better-motion-graphics). Ours are consistent rather than chaotic, which is the better problem, and they are specific: (1) every picture does the same slow push; (2) every card arrives blank and fills by fade-and-rise; (3) every card wears the same frame, wordmark and title position; (4) list cards do not move with the voice once built; (5) dashboard-style glows and shadows on print-style paper; (6) unbalanced line breaks.

### Attractive, not worth doing here

- **Moving `FullEpisode` to `<TransitionSeries>`.** It shortens the timeline by each overlap and forbids adjacent transitions; our times are absolute and locked to narration, on two layers, with hold-under. The resolver built on Oct 10 already draws the three transitions we allow.
- **The newer transition presentations** (zoom blur, film burn, cube and the rest). The grammar bans what they do.
- **Motion blur.** No fast motion to blur, at several times the render cost.
- **HTML-in-canvas effects for grain, paper or LUT.** Right idea, on a browser API the docs themselves call unstable. Grain plates first.
- **Lottie, Rive, three.js, Skia, GSAP.** No authored assets, no spatial argument and no choreography problem in this episode; each adds a second source format.
- **Restructuring for Studio write-back editing or connected compositions.** It needs inline `interpolate` on individual JSX nodes; our motion deliberately goes through shared helpers and a manifest.
- **A Player-based review page, or Lambda.** One reviewer, one machine.
- **A repo-wide migration of 1,359 `sec()` calls to tokens.** Polish the 13 templates this episode uses; the rest can wait for an episode that needs them.
- **Automatic depth parallax on the stills.** Banned by our own motion doctrine for flat illustration, for good reason.
