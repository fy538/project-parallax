# Philosophy visual style study — 22 September 2026

## 23 September review — working direction

The `continuity-record-study` is a **rejected production pattern**, although it remains a useful experiment. In the rendered screenshots, “Promise-maker” does not sit convincingly in its generated impression; the two matching forest circles look like repeated thumbnails; and the dossier/seal/paper stack makes a philosophical question feel like a period prop. The subsequent custom slide, masking, and timed reveals increased complexity without fixing that underlying visual idea. Stop refining that scene. The earlier positive motion assessment below records what we were testing, not the current recommendation.

**Proposed illustration language: a contemporary editorial field atlas of possible minds, obligations, and institutions.** “Editorial modernism” supplies strong asymmetrical composition, a visible grid, confident type, and a few large planar forms. “Scientific archive” supplies observational precision: traces, comparisons, uncertainty, source provenance, and measured annotation—not distressed paper or a claim that fiction is evidence. “Speculative institutional design” supplies the logic of an imagined world: a plausible rule, interface, place, or ordinary artifact that shows what a society *does* with an idea. It need not look like a government form. This is a working art direction, not a prescribed vintage effect on every shot.

The illustration itself should feel like a **diagrammatic editorial print**: a legible silhouette or spatial metaphor, four to six major masses, shallow layered depth, selected fine lines, quiet texture, generous negative space, and one purposeful accent. A path that forks, a threshold, two overlapping territories, an interrupted trace, or a cutaway of a place can embody an argument. Figures and architecture may appear when the thought experiment needs people or a lived environment. Avoid repeated circles, abstract chip/brain motifs, ornamental seals, faux aged files, pseudo-text, and unexplained arrows. The current reference library already has useful DNA in `r9_american_modernist_fortune.png` and `r15_neutral_default.png`: decisive geometry, restricted color, and spatial composition. The new philosophy register should keep those strengths while dropping default industrial and propaganda-poster imagery where it does not fit the subject. This would be a deliberate extension of the older constructivist-forward rules in `project/VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md`, which should be updated only after a pilot confirms the direction.

**Composition rule:** generate one unlettered illustration for one clear visual idea. Put all exact words, numbers, labels, axes, and source cues in stable Remotion layout regions *outside* the generated object. Never depend on a prompt to place a circle, blank space, or edge at pixel-exact coordinates, then align code text to it. For a true comparison, use a code-defined two-column layout with separate images or a single illustration that already communicates the comparison without labels. The lesson of the off-center label is a contract problem between probabilistic image composition and deterministic typesetting, not a request for another coordinate adjustment.

**Small transition vocabulary for the philosophy pilot:**

| Editorial move | Default treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Next claim or example in the same argument | Hard cut, preferably on a spoken beat | Gives the thought a clean step. |
| Same subject, later state or slower reflection | Brief dissolve or an in-place image change | Preserves continuity without implying a new chapter. |
| Major question, objection, or chapter | Held title/card with a deliberate pause; use black only if the passage needs it | Marks a real structural turn. |
| Thought carried across pictures | Narration or sound bridges the cut | Continuity can come from audio without moving the whole frame. |

No bespoke spatial match cut unless both endpoints are code-defined and the match itself explains the concept. Do not use a page slide, iris, wash, mask, or Ken Burns move to compensate for an unconvincing still. `project/TRANSITION_GRAMMAR.md` already offers the larger toolkit; this is a deliberately smaller selection for the new pilot. Remotion's [transition package](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transitions) handles scene-level presentations, while [J/L audio cuts](https://helpx.adobe.com/ie/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/trim-clips/perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts.html) can smooth a cut without visible effect. The tool gap is not the main problem here. The current `BRAND.md` cross-pillar motion signatures are a richer legacy option, not an obligation to animate every change of medium.

**Production limit for the next test:** sketch the frame with text and image regions first; generate at most two candidates for one hero illustration; view them at actual video size beside the real type; either select one or simplify/change the concept. Use one repeatable layout and no more than one meaningful move in the beat. If an asset requires pixel nudging of semantic labels or custom masks to become legible, reject the asset/layout. Test with scratch narration before judging the transition rhythm. This is a proposed time budget, not a measured production benchmark.

This direction draws on [Sutnar's information grid](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2013/10/02/sutnar-on-the-grid/) for active page structure and [Isotype's transformation of ideas into consistent visual forms](https://isotyperevisited.org/2012/08/introduction.php) for repeatability; neither implies copying their period look. [Scientific American's graphics editor](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/scientific-americans-senior-graphics-editor-on-the-power-of-a-good-science-illustration/) distinguishes metaphorical editorial illustration from literal explanation and describes concept-sketch → tight-sketch → final-art stages to prevent endless iteration. [Superflux's speculative projects](https://superflux.in/index.php/work/the-intersection/) are a useful reminder that institutional imagination is conveyed through coherent consequences and artifacts, not decorative bureaucratic styling.

## Proposed art direction: editorial modernism + scientific archive + speculative institutional design

This is a more useful umbrella than naming an image treatment such as risograph or engraving. Those are techniques within the system, not the channel's identity.

| Layer | Job in a frame | Existing Meridian foundation | Philosophy extension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Editorial modernism | Give the viewer a clear reading order | Warm paper/ink palette, disciplined grid, negative space, Plex type hierarchy | Let a philosophical question or contradiction lead; reduce decorative framing when it competes with the idea. |
| Scientific archive | Show how a claim is known and where uncertainty remains | Captions, labels, timelines, maps, source treatment | Use plate numbers, observations, marginal notes, and provenance only when they clarify evidence or the limits of an analogy. |
| Speculative institutional design | Make a thought experiment feel like a coherent possible world | Meridian's repeatable marks, diagram language, and ∴ signature | Invent a restrained visual vocabulary of records, branches, relations, and procedures; label imagined artifacts as fiction. |

The current Meridian pillars can be reinterpreted rather than replaced: **Cartograph** maps a conceptual or moral possibility space, **Dialectic** makes objections and competing interpretations visible, and **Tectonic** shows how a belief, identity, or institution changes through time. The old briefing-folder and war-room metaphors, crosshairs, geopolitical semantic colors, and literal hardware icons should not automatically carry into philosophy scenes; they can imply surveillance, conflict, or engineering where none is intended.

For production, generated illustrations supply *places, material, and atmosphere*. They should use a few legible masses, a controlled crop, and the Meridian palette. SVGs supply *reasoning and change*: a continuity trace, fork, boundary, relation, or revision should be understandable before the caption is read. Shared color, line weight, shape scale, and restraint make the two media belong together; imitating raster texture with many SVG paths is unlikely to help. The print/cut-paper comparison is the clearer default grammar, while engraving remains an occasional archive register.

Type should communicate roles. Plex Sans remains the functional channel voice; Plex Serif carries questions, narrative passages, and dialogue; Plex Mono is reserved for actual metadata such as dates, source IDs, measurements, and file codes. Newsreader is worth testing as a special editorial voice before making it a default. Fictional records need an explicit cue such as “thought experiment” or “imagined case”; the scientific-archive look must not make them appear to be empirical evidence.

That trade is acceptable for a recurring actor emblem, where recognition and mood matter; it is less acceptable for an explanatory diagram, where the viewer must understand the precise relation. ## A small grammar to test across the next two scenes

- **Shape:** compose images from a few readable masses; draw SVG actors as meaningful states and relationships, not generic icons. Prefer one clear change per beat.
- **Color:** use the shared Meridian palette (paper, sand, taupe, walnut, ink) with gold for one semantic thread or decision. Avoid adding a new accent merely to make a still vivid.
- **Material:** keep paper grain mostly in the generated illustration or surface. The SVG can echo cut edges, overlap, and small registration shifts, but should remain sharp enough to explain an argument at 1080p.
- **Type:** Plex Serif for the philosophical question, Plex Sans for agents and explanatory notes, Plex Mono for genuine metadata (episode number, source, date, measurement). The note “remembers the promise” is prose, not metadata. Newsreader is a candidate for fictional canonical texts or dialogue excerpts; test it with longer copy before adopting it broadly.
- **Consistency:** render the raster and SVG in the same frame at final viewing size. Compare the hierarchy and silhouette after a circular crop, not only the full-resolution generated asset.
