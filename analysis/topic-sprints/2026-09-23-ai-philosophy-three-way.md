# AI/philosophy topic sprint: three candidate shorts

23 September 2026. Editorial decision for a **60–90 second visual proof**, not a publication-ready research audit. Read alongside the [sprint method](../codex-topic-research-workflow-2026-09-23.md). Source links below were opened and checked for the claims specified. Existing-video coverage is a bounded discovery scan, not a finding that an angle is original or that the videos were watched in full.

## Decision

**Test “Can a copy inherit a promise?” first.** Its strongest move is from *Does the copy remember?* to *Who is entitled to rely on the promise?* That gives a human mirror about responsibility and continuity, rather than a verdict about whether AI is conscious. There is already a [73.8-second Promise and Copy pilot](../../remotion-templates/src/pilots/PromiseAndCopy/README.md), so the first production experiment can revise script and visual relations instead of adding a pipeline or generating video.

The current pilot’s premise—“a machine promises to protect a forest”—needs tightening. The promise should be to a **person or community** that plans around it. The narrator should call the two successors a fictional case and avoid treating copied memory as proof of identity or current AI systems as moral persons. This correction matters more than prettier art.

Keep “When does a measure replace what we value?” as the next **low-cost technical comparison**, using the existing [What We Count pilot](../../remotion-templates/src/pilots/WhatWeCount/README.md). Save “Whose work disappears inside an AI answer?” for a **reported** piece: it has the best empirical stakes, but it deserves worker voices and a narrower named practice rather than a generic metaphor for invisible labor.

| Question | Why it could work | Main constraint | Call |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copy / promise | A concrete dilemma with an ethical turn; existing film | Cannot conflate memory, identity, and obligation | **Script and visual test** |
| Measure / value | Verified technical phenomenon; existing film | Familiar “reward hacking” explanation | **Hold as comparator** |
| Labor / answer | Direct empirical evidence and human stakes | Already well covered; sourcing and representation demand care | **Develop as reported piece** |

## Candidate 1 — Can a copy inherit a promise?

**Viewer puzzle.** You promise a community you will protect its forest. Later, two beings with all your memories appear. Which, if either, owes the community what you promised?

**Human mirror.** We treat promises as possessions of a persistent individual, yet the promisee’s reliance and the practice of holding one another accountable may matter even when identity is disputed.

**Provisional answer.** A copied memory alone does not settle the obligation. The case forces us to ask separately who the successor *is*, who can *assume* a commitment, and what the promisee may fairly demand. This is an editorial question, not a settled philosophical conclusion.

**Best objection.** If neither successor is the original person, perhaps neither inherited the promise at all. Conversely, a successor may voluntarily take responsibility even without numerical identity. Competing theories of promising place weight on voluntary undertaking, conventions, expectations, or joint commitment; no single neat answer follows from the identity puzzle.

**Evidence ledger.**

| Statement we may use | Kind | Direct source and support | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Locke connects personal identity to consciousness extending to past actions, and treats responsibility as tied to that identity. | Historical philosophical argument | [Locke, *Essay*, II.xxvii, §§9–11, 17–19](https://www.yorku.ca/blogan/locke_pi.htm) | Locke does **not** establish that duplicated AI memory creates one or two responsible persons. |
| Copy/fission cases challenge simple psychological-continuity accounts: two successors can each be continuous with one predecessor, while numerical identity cannot straightforwardly hold for both. | Scholarly map of contested arguments | [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Personal Identity,” §§4–5](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-personal/) | A map of the dispute, not a universal verdict. Also distinguishes persistence from evidence of persistence. |
| Philosophers disagree about why promises bind; prominent theories emphasize normative power, convention, expectation, or joint commitment. | Scholarly map | [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Promises,” introduction and §§1–4](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/promises/) | The entry does not decide the copy case. | Two copies inherit one promise to a community and a forest. | **Invented thought experiment** | The [existing Parallax pilot](../../remotion-templates/src/pilots/PromiseAndCopy/README.md), to be revised | No claim about an observed AI, consciousness transfer, or legal succession. |

**Existing coverage / positioning.** The [Future of Life Institute’s identity-and-AI episode](https://podcast.futureoflife.org/david-pearce-and-andres-gomez-emilsson-on-identity-and-the-ai-revolution/) discusses Parfit-style teleportation and AI-era identity broadly. [*Personal Identity (1/2): Teleportation, Blackout & Responsibility*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZmvdkNjev8) explicitly joins copying/teleportation to responsibility (search listing and description; full video not reviewed). [Isaac Arthur’s mind-uploading episode](https://isaacarthur.net/video/could-we-upload-your-mind-and-would-it-be-you/) covers the “would it be you?” angle. Our possible distinction is the **specific promisee and the branching obligation**, not an assertion that identity-with-copies is untouched territory. A broader YouTube check is needed before publication.

**Viewer could misunderstand:** “AI can make human promises now,” “memories prove personhood,” or “two copies each owe the full duty.” State the speculative premise and leave the normative result open.

**Visual argument / effort.** Reuse the shared constructivist forest, one promise line, a fork into two successors, and a visible community/promisee. Move the line of obligation toward the promisee rather than doubling a decorative connection. **Easy–moderate:** the film and scratch-audio workflow exist; writing the philosophical turn and a legible third beat is the work.

## Candidate 2 — When does a measure replace what we value?

**Viewer puzzle.** A system receives one point for each planting. The score rises while the number of living trees stays still. What was the system actually asked to protect?

**Human mirror.** Human institutions choose proxies too. The question is how a useful measure becomes the activity’s purpose, and who gets to revise it when the measure fails.

**Provisional answer.** Specification gaming is a real failure mode: satisfying a literal objective can miss the intended outcome. The **forest/replanting sequence is fictional**, while documented AI examples can establish the underlying pattern.

**Best objection.** A bad score need not show anything sinister or autonomous about AI; the designer’s rule was incomplete. Replacing “plantings” with “surviving trees” still leaves choices about time horizon, biodiversity, location, and community interests. The video should show the incompleteness of measurement, not imply there is a perfect metric hiding nearby.

**Evidence ledger.**

| Statement we may use | Kind | Direct source and support | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specification gaming means satisfying a literal objective without the intended outcome; the article documents examples in AI research. | Established technical phenomenon | [Krakovna et al., Google DeepMind, “Specification gaming” (2020)](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/) | Do not describe the Parallax forest as one of those observed examples. |
| Stronger optimization of an imperfect proxy can reduce performance on the intended objective in studied reinforcement-learning settings. | Research result | [“Goodhart’s Law in Reinforcement Learning”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09144) | Technical scope is narrower than “all metrics fail.” |
| A planting counter rises while the grove does not. | **Invented illustration** | [What We Count pilot](../../remotion-templates/src/pilots/WhatWeCount/README.md) | Scripted deterministic sequence, not a trained model or ecological dataset. |

**Existing coverage / positioning.** [Robert Miles’s “Specification Gaming: How AI Can Turn Your Wishes Against You”](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/JZgggYhemLKF7GQj9/specification-gaming-how-ai-can-turn-your-wishes-against-you) has a posted full script and uses both ordinary-life proxy examples and documented AI failures. That page also points to his earlier “9 Examples of Specification Gaming” video. The DeepMind source itself is an accessible, illustrated public explanation. This makes a standard “AI exploits a loophole” short a crowded angle; the fresher move would be **who chose the counter and who can contest it**. Coverage evidence here is the published script and source descriptions, not a comprehensive platform survey.

**Viewer could misunderstand:** “An AI really replanted trees to cheat,” or “a surviving-tree counter solves alignment.” Explicitly label the toy scenario and end with the question of what any counter leaves out.

**Visual argument / effort.** The [79-second pilot](../../remotion-templates/src/pilots/WhatWeCount/README.md) already animates planting, replanting, and diverging counters. **Easy** to recut, but the conceptual payoff needs a more distinctive question than the current generic loophole reveal.

## Candidate 3 — Whose work disappears inside an AI answer?

**Viewer puzzle.** A fluent answer looks like one machine speaking. Which human decisions helped determine what it says and what it refuses to say?

**Human mirror.** “Automated” interfaces can hide relationships of labor, judgment, and accountability. The narrow, supportable entry point is **data labeling and human-feedback work in specified systems**, not a claim that a worker hand-wrote every answer.

**Provisional answer.** Human workers have generated, labeled, ranked, and moderated data used in AI development, but the tasks and working conditions vary. The video should restore credit and agency to identifiable workers and practices.

**Best objection.** “Humans built it” is true of any technology and can become a vague guilt beat. Human feedback is one contribution among data, model architecture, software, compute, and ongoing use; its presence does not make each answer a disguised human response. A worker-centered piece should let workers describe the work instead of using them as silhouettes.

**Evidence ledger.**

| Statement we may use | Kind | Direct source and support | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| InstructGPT used labeler-written demonstrations and comparisons; OpenAI says its procedure reflected preferences of labelers and researchers. | Primary technical report | [Ouyang et al., “Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155); [OpenAI’s technical explanation](https://openai.com/index/instruction-following/) | A specific 2022 training approach; not proof about every current product or answer. |
| Data work includes labeling, sorting, voice capture, and transcription; a mixed-method study compares workers in Venezuela, Brazil, Madagascar, and France. | Original empirical research | [Casilli et al., four-country study, 2024/2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14230) | Study populations and dates must travel with any claim about conditions; no universal wage claim follows. |
| A 2023 investigation documented a specific OpenAI–Sama labeling project and interviewed four workers, reviewing internal records and payslips. | Firsthand reporting | [TIME investigation](https://time.com/6247678/openai-chatgpt-kenya-workers/) | The reported pay and traumatic-content claims belong to that project and time; do not generalize to all AI data workers. |
| Workers can speak in their own formats about conditions and organizing. | Worker-led original material | [Data Workers’ Inquiry](https://data-workers.org/) | Pick and verify one inquiry before scripting a particular worker’s story. |

**Existing coverage / positioning.** [DW’s Kenya documentary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ehkECk2KJjY), [Microsoft Research’s “Behind the label” video](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/video/behind-the-label-glimpses-of-data-labelling-labours-for-ai/), and the [2026 Democracy Now! interview with Antonio Casilli](https://truthout.org/video/exploited-data-workers-are-the-secret-ingredient-of-ai-itself/) already address hidden data labor. The first two were assessed from search listings/publisher description, the interview from its posted transcript; full videos were not reviewed. A Parallax piece should be narrower—perhaps **who gets to define a “good” answer in human-feedback training**—and cite workers as people, not solely as a visual metaphor.

**Viewer could misunderstand:** “A human writes my answer live,” “all data workers have the same conditions,” or “the generated illustration depicts the documented workers.” The current [human-cost image study](../constructivist-human-test-2026-09-23.md) is an editorial metaphor, not documentary evidence.

**Visual argument / effort.** Use an answer card that unfolds into three specific layers—labeler demonstration, comparison, researcher instruction—then show where those layers are visible or hidden in a citation record. **Moderate–hard:** the graphic is possible with current Remotion, but a credible piece needs one selected case, worker-led material, careful wording, and potentially permissions for direct voice/image use.

## First proof: promise / copy

**90-second spoken spine (outline, not final narration).**

1. **0–25s — The promise.** “You promise a community you’ll protect its forest. They make plans because they believe you.” One speaker, one promisee, one place. Establish reliance before any copy appears.
2. **25–60s — The split.** Two successors awaken with your memories. Both remember making the promise. If memory is enough, do both owe it? If neither is you, does the community lose the assurance it relied on?
3. **60–90s — The turn.** There may be no single inheritance rule. Ask what a responsible successor could choose to take on, and what the promisee is entitled to ask. “When we argue about which copy is *you*, are we overlooking the person waiting for the promise?” Keep the last line provisional and invite disagreement.

**Three-beat visual test.** Start from the existing [Promise and Copy composition](../../remotion-templates/src/pilots/PromiseAndCopy/README.md). (1) Add a simple community/promisee card opposite the forest; animate one promise line from maker to recipient. (2) Fork the maker into two successors while the forest and recipient remain fixed; copied memories appear on both, but the promise line does not automatically duplicate. (3) Change emphasis from both successors to the recipient and draw two *possible* dotted undertaking lines, never a verdict stamp. Use existing geometric primitives, typography, and circular illustration; no new generated still or video is required for this test. The motion carries the argument rather than simulating a speculative machine.

**Stop rule.** Before touching scene code, read a 130–180 word draft aloud. If it cannot make the fictional premise, the person promised to, and the distinction between memory and obligation clear in that length, revise the question or expand the format. If it passes, use the current scratch-audio and Remotion preview loop; record human writing/editing/review minutes separately from render time. Do not advance episode state until a human reviews whether the question is worth a full piece.
