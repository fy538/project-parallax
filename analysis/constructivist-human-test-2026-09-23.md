# Parallax constructivist style: human/social range test

This is the second non-architectural test of the original Parallax constructivist visual language. The forest-score study showed an environmental reward-hacking scene. This one tests a human/social consequence: one person receives a small gold answer above a tilted work surface while another person visibly supports the heavy structure beneath it. The scene is an imagined editorial metaphor, not a claim about the labor behind a particular AI product.

Two images were made with the built-in image-generation tool: `remotion-templates/public/style-lab/constructivist-human-cost-v1.png` and the selected `constructivist-human-cost-v2.png`. Image 1 of the first generation used `tools/ai-video/style-references/r15_neutral_default.png` as a style reference only; image 2 used `remotion-templates/public/style-lab/constructivist-forest-score-v2.png` as a style reference only. The second generation edited v1 to clarify the lower worker and reduce incidental stacks and marks.

The chosen v2 is registered as `constructivist-human-study` in Remotion Studio. Source: `remotion-templates/src/experiments/ConstructivistHumanStudy.tsx`. Renders: `remotion-templates/out/style-lab/constructivist-human-study.png` (1920×1080) and `constructivist-human-study-thumbnail.png` (320×180). The same source registers `constructivist-range-board`, rendered at `remotion-templates/out/style-lab/constructivist-range-board.png`, to compare this scene with the forest study at equal size. The images contain no text; no narration or motion was tested.

**Reading.** The two scenes share layered angular space, warm print surfaces, cutaway depth, dark supporting planes, and one meaningful gold accent. They have different subjects and composition: the forest builds outward from a planted plot; the human scene divides visible benefit from hidden burden along one diagonal. This is stronger evidence of a flexible Parallax illustration language than the earlier flat-symbol board.

**Limits.** The small gold object can read as a paper plane or token rather than an “answer”; narration or adjacent type would need to establish that meaning. The lower worker is legible at thumbnail size but the specifics of their labor emerge only on a held frame. The social asymmetry is still a fairly direct editorial metaphor. Keep this as a visual-identity study, not a factual depiction of AI production.
