# Shared-code fixes outrank new AI tooling

**Parallax's Remotion output is held back by a handful of defects in shared code, not by missing capability or missing AI tooling, and fixing them is the shortest route to a shippable first episode.** The project is a 96-directory template library pinned to Remotion 4.0.457 with unusually thorough doctrine, yet `ai-care-motives` as it renders today shows dissolves that cut to blank cream paper, a dark shadow under every title on light cards, subtitles falling back to a Times-style serif, an internal slug and a "REC" timer in the chrome, and six unrelated image treatments. Five of the ten most visible weaknesses trace to five shared places: the transition wrapper, the theme hook, the title block, the chrome strips and the card background. Online, Remotion's official AI story has moved from an MCP server to 12 versioned agent skills and editor plugins, and the framework is 77 patch releases ahead of Parallax's pin; the skills assume the newest APIs, so installing them today would steer an agent toward functions this repo does not have. Practitioner write-ups and 2026 research agree that render-and-inspect loops earn most of their gain in the first round, get worse when run naively, and cannot judge timing or easing at all, while Parallax's schema-and-lint system is already stricter than any workflow the research found. Motion-design sources supply the numbers the fixes need: decelerating entrances of 200–500 ms, exits shorter than entrances, about one second per chart stage, hard cuts as the default seam, and sound that never leads picture. The recommended order is to record narration first (every duration in the manifest is still an estimate), spend roughly two days on the ranked shared-code and data fixes below, render once in BT.709 and watch it on a phone, and leave the Remotion upgrade, skills install, canvas effects and new lints until after the episode ships.

## The episode renders through 14 templates and a sliver of Remotion

Parallax's Remotion project is a manifest-driven renderer. A Python tool emits `assembly-manifest.json`, and `FullEpisode.tsx` places every segment in its own `<Sequence>` at an absolute, narration-aligned timestamp, each wrapped in a hand-written `TransitionWrapper` ([`FullEpisode.tsx:1056-1185`](../remotion-templates/src/templates/Episodes/FullEpisode.tsx)). For `ai-care-motives` that means **56 segments over 678.6 s (20,358 frames at 30 fps)**: 29 template segments spread across 14 components, and 27 footage segments made of 23 pre-rendered Ken Burns clips from AI stills, two stock clips and two unsourced placeholders ([manifest](../remotion-templates/data/episodes/ai-care-motives/assembly-manifest.json)). The library around the episode is far larger, with 96 template directories, 87 registered components and 328 visual-regression baselines, so the episode exercises about a sixth of what exists ([audit §1, §11](../research_notes/Remotion%20polish%20and%20AI%20tooling/current_state_audit.md)).

The Remotion surface in use is narrow. `package.json` pins the core packages to **4.0.457** exactly but leaves six `@remotion/*` helpers on caret ranges ([`package.json`](../remotion-templates/package.json)), a mix Remotion's docs warn against: every package should sit on one identical version without `^` ([Remotion docs](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/skia/skia.mdx)). On this episode's render path the effective surface is core `remotion`, `@remotion/google-fonts` and a single layout function, `measureText`; transitions, paths, shapes, noise and media-utils are installed but reach only prototypes or templates the episode does not use. The repo's own capability map puts usage at "roughly 25% of its surface" ([`REMOTION_CAPABILITY_MAP.md`](../remotion-templates/REMOTION_CAPABILITY_MAP.md)).

Motion is where design and code diverge most. A capable toolkit exists (eased presets, three springs, stagger, anticipatory reveals, eight drift presets), and the theme file states that "motion is NEVER linear", yet the house `fadeIn` helper is a bare linear interpolation ([`animation.ts:48-74`](../remotion-templates/src/utils/animation.ts)). The audit's grep counts find it called **518 times across 76 template directories**, against 15 spring uses in 8 directories and the newer timing tokens adopted in only 9 ([audit §2](../research_notes/Remotion%20polish%20and%20AI%20tooling/current_state_audit.md)). Three of the episode's templates, AxiomList, MathDerivation and SplitFrame, animate with linear opacity fades and nothing else. The shared composition hook compounds this: every card enters on an 8-frame linear fade and leaves on a 15-frame fade ([`useCompositionAnimation.ts:135-152`](../remotion-templates/src/hooks/useCompositionAnimation.ts)), so exits run nearly twice as long as entrances, the reverse of the convention described below.

Audio and narration sync are complete in code and empty in practice. The manifest cues six music beds and 63 sound-effect firings that resolve to 24 files, and **none of those files exists**; there is no narration recording, `narration.words` is empty, and the manifest runs in `estimate` mode ([audit §8–9](../research_notes/Remotion%20polish%20and%20AI%20tooling/current_state_audit.md)). The anticipatory-reveal mechanism that settles an element five frames before the narrator names it is wired into 65 template directories, but only 2 of the episode's 29 template segments name a sync word, and five of its templates ignore sync points altogether. Every reveal therefore fires on template defaults, and every hold length will move once real audio exists.

Rendering and QA are equally provisional. `remotion.config.ts` sets PNG frames, `yuv420p` and the ANGLE renderer but no colour space, CRF or concurrency ([`remotion.config.ts`](../remotion-templates/remotion.config.ts)); the only video output for this episode is a 15-second preview tagged `bt470bg`, and **no full-length render has been made**, so wall time and memory behaviour are unknown. The episode QA script samples one late frame per segment and reports 0 failures and 96 warnings: 46 for size, 42 for contrast, 8 for safe area ([QA report](../remotion-templates/out/qa/ai-care-motives/report.md)). Nothing tests seams, motion, font fallback, frame fill, colour consistency or audio, and the last stored unit-test run, from 19 May, shows 111 of 1,638 tests failing ([`test-results.json`](../remotion-templates/test-results.json)). The pattern across the codebase is that rules backed by a lint hold and rules without one are broken, while `POLISH.md` contradicts the theme or itself on springs, tracking, shadows, minimum type size and safe areas ([audit §12](../research_notes/Remotion%20polish%20and%20AI%20tooling/current_state_audit.md)).

The seam defect matters most because it is structural. Each segment computes its "in" over its own first frames and its "out" over its own last frames, above a full-frame paper surface, so a dissolve from a dark illustration to a light card is in fact a hard cut to empty cream followed by a fade-up. The preview clip confirms it at the one dissolve it contains: two consecutive frames at the `beat5-seg27` to `beat5-seg28` seam are entirely blank. The other ten dissolve and fade-ins share the code path and were not watched. The repo knows the limitation; `grammar.tsx` states that the wrapper "cannot truly OVERLAP two segments" and warns against swapping in `<TransitionSeries>` because sequential timing would drift off the narration-aligned timestamps ([`grammar.tsx:1-36`](../remotion-templates/src/transitions/grammar.tsx)). The iris ring sits at about 71% of the clip radius because CSS resolves a percentage `circle()` against roughly 1558 px at 1920×1080, by the audit's reading of the spec; the black corner wedges seen at near-full radius remain unexplained. Two further seam behaviours come mainly from reading the code: each card's own exit fade empties it to bare paper half a second before even a hard cut, and a one-second "film-burn" flash whose hue rotates through amber, rose and cyan fires at all seven beat boundaries, although the transition grammar never mentions it ([`FullEpisode.tsx:887-920`](../remotion-templates/src/templates/Episodes/FullEpisode.tsx)).

The finish defects are cheap and wide. There is no default font on the composition root, so any text node that omits `fontFamily` renders in Times and QA cannot tell; `fonts.ts` loads no italic face although italics are requested 54 times ([`fonts.ts`](../remotion-templates/src/design/fonts.ts)). The chrome was designed for the geopolitics "intelligence briefing" framing and now prints `AI-CARE-MOTIVES`, a timer that restarts on every card and the render date, which also makes two renders of the same commit differ in pixels; the project's QA spec records the chrome as an open design decision ([`VISUAL_QA.md`](../project/VISUAL_QA.md)). Whether natural colour on the AI stills is intended or an accident of the ignored ramp is recorded nowhere. Lower-priority items round out the picture: two 12-second placeholders, one still used five times with six clip files each reused in a second segment, and a 57-second cold open (8.4% of runtime) with a 7-second title card against doctrine that asks for at most 2% and 2 seconds.

## Remotion's AI tooling became skills while the platform ran 77 patches ahead

Remotion's official support for coding agents is now a maintained set of Agent Skills. The `remotion-dev/skills` repository was created on 19 January 2026 and had 5,004 stars on 10 October ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/remotion-dev/skills)); it holds **12 slash-skills** with `remotion-best-practices` as a router, each stamped with the framework version it was written for, currently 4.0.534 ([Agent Skills docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/skills), [remotion-markup skill](https://github.com/remotion-dev/skills/blob/main/skills/remotion-markup/SKILL.md)). Installation is `npx skills add remotion-dev/skills`, `npx remotion skills add`, or a Claude Code plugin that bundles the same skills ([Claude Code plugin docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/claude-code-plugin)). The hosted MCP server is deprecated in favour of a `/remotion-docs` skill, with shutdown promised no earlier than 31 August 2026 ([MCP docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/mcp)), and every docs page is served as Markdown for agents ([AI docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai)).

The skills carry less than their reputation suggests for a project like this one. Their timing rules are sound and specific: frame-driven animation only, explicit clamping, `Easing.bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` as the worked entrance curve, and `output: 'perceptual-scale'` on scale tweens ([timing rule](https://github.com/remotion-dev/skills/blob/main/skills/remotion-markup/timing.md)). The chart, text-animation and animation rule files that existed in March 2026 are absent from the current tree, which was fully rewritten in July ([skills repo](https://github.com/remotion-dev/skills/tree/main/skills), [release v4.0.501](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/releases/tag/v4.0.501)), so no official chart or kinetic-typography guidance exists today and Parallax's own registers are ahead in those areas. On review, the skills tell agents to open Studio and not to render unless asked ([best-practices skill](https://github.com/remotion-dev/skills/blob/main/skills/remotion-best-practices/SKILL.md)). Studio's new WebMCP tools let an agent read element bounding boxes and seek the timeline, but in the docs' words "only ChatGPT Codex supports WebMCP" ([WebMCP docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/webmcp)); the research found no official closed render-inspect-fix example for Claude Code. Remotion's Jonny Burger said in May that a big gap remains to a fast iterative workflow and that he has not found one-prompt-to-video comfortable ([Syntax.fm](https://www.syntax.fm/show/1005/programatic-and-skill-based-video-creation-with-remotion/transcript)).

The platform itself has moved quickly. The current stable release is **v4.0.534, published 7 October 2026**, with 5.0 still unreleased ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/remotion)); Parallax's pin was published on 4 May, 77 patch versions earlier. ## Review loops pay once, and craft sources supply the numbers

### Structured critique helps in round one; models cannot see timing

The baseline practitioner workflow is to keep Studio open, prompt, scrub and correct in sentences; one author reports that roughly one prompt in five needs a real correction for rushed timing, collisions or undersized type ([mejba.me](https://www.mejba.me/blog/claude-code-remotion-youtube-motion-graphics)). The projects that go further add a human-reviewed storyboard before code, a few approved style frames, a kit of tokenised primitives, one timeline file that picture and sound both read, mandatory frame inspection and an audio stage ([claude-motion](https://github.com/whaleyxbt/claude-motion), [Roboto Studio](https://robotostudio.com/blog/how-to-use-remotion-agent-skills-with-claude-code)). Roboto's audit before building its kit found same-role font sizes ranging from 14 to 19 px and five outlier easing curves across ten scenes. Parallax's Zod-validated manifest, registers and lints already cover the kit and the timeline more strictly than any project the research found, and nothing found justifies migrating to HyperFrames or another framework; that is the researchers' inference, since no neutral cross-framework benchmark exists.

The measured evidence on visual feedback loops is consistent. An Amazon study of render-screenshot-critique cycles reported a best-per-task gain of 10.8% with most of it arriving in the first cycle, while tokens rose from about 6.4k to about 73k ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)). A Manim system with a vision-feedback agent and a single repair round scored 0.763 on overlap against 0.628 for the base model alone, and removing the vision agent caused its largest drop ([arXiv 2605.15585](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15585)). Run naively, the same idea backfires: on one web-design benchmark **all 15 of 15 refined rounds from GPT-5.2 landed below the first draft**, because free-form critique says what is wrong without constraining what must stay; typed rubric items and exactly one repair target per round reversed the decline, in a result that is not yet peer reviewed ([RubSE](https://tyxiong23.github.io/rubse)). As judges, frontier models correlate only moderately with designers (Spearman 0.56 for Claude Opus 5) and localise poorly, with a region F1 of 36.7 ([arXiv 2609.34854](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34854)); on a five-point scale they agree exactly with humans about a third of the time ([arXiv 2604.25235](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25235v1)).

Motion is the hard limit. A Berkeley benchmark found models reproduce animation appearance well but score at most 0.31 on temporal similarity, barely changing between 2 and 24 sampled frames per second ([arXiv 2606.28593](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28593)), and on contrastive questions about speed and direction the best model reached 60.0% against 91.3% for humans ([arXiv 2609.09528](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09528)). Claude accepts images, not video, and a 1920×1080 still passes unscaled at 2,691 tokens ([Anthropic vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)); a 4×4 contact sheet shrinks each frame to about a third of linear scale by the researchers' arithmetic, which suits sequence and collision checks and defeats typography checks. The workable division of labour is deterministic measurement for geometry, full-resolution stills for hierarchy and legibility, and the human for timing and taste, capped at one or two rounds with one repair target each.

Practitioner lists of what marks agent-made Remotion video name linear easing, lone opacity fades, simultaneous entrances, missing exits, static pasted images, elements shrunk well below intended scale and text that collides mid-travel ([claude-remotion-skill](https://github.com/haidrrrry/claude-remotion-skill), [HeyGen benchmark](https://www.heygen.com/research/introducing-code2video-benchmark)). Parallax shows three of these by its own audit: linear fades, opacity-only entrances and underfilled frames. None of the lists has been validated against what viewers perceive.

### The numbers that apply to this episode's fixes

On easing and duration, design systems converge on three roles. IBM Carbon's expressive set gives `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.3, 1)` for entrances, `(0.4, 0.14, 1, 1)` for exits and `(0.4, 0.14, 0.3, 1)` for on-screen moves ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/)); Nielsen Norman Group puts most animations at 100–500 ms with entrances slightly longer than exits ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)). Data graphics run slower: Heer and Robertson recommend **about one full second per stage** with slow-in/slow-out, keeping axes fixed across states and staging any rescale separately ([Heer and Robertson 2007](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jheer/files/2007-AnimatedTrans-InfoVis.pdf)). Remotion's default spring overshoots and its canonical no-bounce setting is `damping: 200` ([TransitionSeries docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transitions/transitionseries)); Parallax's only spring in use, `heroSpring` at damping 12 and stiffness 100, has a damping ratio of 0.6 by the standard formula, so it overshoots, at odds with the theme's own no-overshoot rule. Stagger is a reading-order device, kept to 20 ms per item and 500 ms in total by Carbon ([Carbon choreography](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/choreography/)), and a controlled study found it does not help viewers track objects ([Chevalier et al. 2014](https://visualcognition.psych.northwestern.edu/publications/ChevalierDragicevicFranconeri2014.pdf)).

On seams, the hard cut is the default, and a dissolve asserts elapsed time or an association between two images ([NYFA](https://www.nyfa.edu/student-resources/use-dissolves/)). A traditional cross dissolve lasts 24–48 frames, and shorter ones behave like hard cuts ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/video-cross-dissolve)). The episode's sampled dissolve is 0.4 s between an unrelated illustration and a card, so a cut is the correct choice there even once the overlap bug is fixed.

On type, the one explicit 1080p video style guide found sets body text at no less than 22 px and captions at 28 px ([Towbook](https://styleguide.towbook.com/video/typography)). An angular-size legibility standard of 10 arc minutes minimum ([Extron](https://azwebdev.extron.com/article/videowallfontsize)) implies, by the researchers' estimate, about 38 px for a phone held upright with the video letterboxed. Parallax's QA floor of 18 px with a warning at 22 px therefore sits at the bare minimum for landscape viewing, and the 46 size warnings and the 10.9 px chrome are real phone-legibility problems, not noise. Mayer's redundancy principle holds that on-screen sentences duplicating narration hurt learning unless brief and placed beside what they describe ([Mayer summary](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/medixperience/scholarly-article/mayers-12-principles-of-multimedia-instruction/)); quote cards are the exception because the text is the evidence.

On texture and sound, a faint noise layer at 2–6% is the standard cure for 8-bit gradient banding ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/banding-issue-driving-me-insane/m-p/9511523/highlight/true)). Parallax already runs paper grain at about 7% opacity ([`EditorialSurface.tsx:69-130`](../remotion-templates/src/components/EditorialSurface.tsx)), and the audit attributes the banding to the blurred low-alpha colour clouds layered on top, an inference not yet confirmed by a test render; the fix to try is removing the clouds, not adding grain. Motion blur is contested for 2D type and Remotion's averaging blur is destructive to colour ([CameraMotionBlur docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/motion-blur/camera-motion-blur)). For audio, ITU-R BT.1359 puts detectability at about 45 ms when sound is early and 125 ms when it is late ([ITU-R BT.1359](https://itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1359-0-199802-S!!PDF-E.pdf)), which at 30 fps means hits land on the visual frame or one frame after, never before. The convention for YouTube is about −14 LUFS integrated with a −1 dBTP ceiling ([Youlean](https://youlean.co/?p=30813)) and music ducked 6–12 dB under speech ([CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/create/audio-ducking-for-clear-dialogue-in-video)). No study links cut rate or "change something every N seconds" to retention in video essays; those rules are unsourced.

## Narration first, two days of fixes, then one render

Because every duration is an estimate until narration is recorded, timing-independent fixes come first and anything tuned to the voice waits. Row 3 deserves a word because it replaces an engineering task with an editorial rule. The true fix is to let the outgoing segment run on under the incoming one while both keep their absolute start times; the audit judges this feasible in principle and nobody has built or tested it. Until then, cuts and ink washes are the two seam types that render as designed, and craft convention already says a cut is right between unrelated beats. One beat boundary should be previewed with `preview-range.mjs` before the final render, since the colour-rotating beat flash has never been seen in motion.

Three items in the table are decisions, not tasks, and only Tiger can take them: whether the briefing chrome survives the shift to philosophy and AI ethics, whether the illustrations stay in natural colour, and whether a 57-second cold open is this episode's choice or a doctrine violation. Every source that addresses direction says the same thing: tokens and loops secure consistency, and choice of direction stays with the human.

Several things remain unknown and could reorder the list. No full render has been timed. Entrances, the title card and colour-wash seams were judged from code and settled stills. The 48 data-lint errors reported across all data files were not broken down by episode. And the claim that the audio layer would fail without `noAudio` is read from code, not run.

## Conclusion

Parallax has the inverse of the problem the AI-video community is trying to solve. Other practitioners lack tokens, schemas and registers and are building them; Parallax has all three, and its visible defects sit underneath them, in the defaults that the tokens resolve to: a linear helper behind a no-linear rule, a dark shadow behind a light theme, a wrapper that cannot do what the transition grammar names. The lesson is to fix defaults before writing rules, because one corrected helper reaches hundreds of call sites while one more doctrine paragraph reaches none.

The research also redraws where outside help is worth taking. Remotion's official guidance has thinned in the areas where Parallax is strongest, charts and kinetic type, and thickened in primitives: colour management, effects, easing options, multi-frame stills. The useful import is therefore the primitives after an upgrade, not the instructions. And since no current model perceives timing, the final judge of motion is a person watching the render on a phone. A sensible habit follows for every episode after this one: harden only the templates the episode uses, so the library improves at the pace of shipping.
