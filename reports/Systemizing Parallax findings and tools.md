# Ship first; let use promote techniques

**Parallax should systemize the work of 7 to 11 October 2026, but only as far as a versioned catalogue with a status column, a one-page chooser and a pointer at the script stage. It should not build a stills register, a new `DIR:` directive or a new lint now, and before the first episode ships it should do no more than commit the work and make a handful of single-file edits.** The reason is cost set against evidence. Every fully shipped register in the repository took 20 to 43 files and 2,500 to 4,000 inserted lines, and even the carefully propagated iris retirement of 10 October left at least five stale references behind. Against that price, each of the 70 catalogued techniques has been seen once, in one take, on one episode's art, with cuts timed to a word-count estimate that the scratch-voice test showed to be 12 to 40% off, and Tiger has given no per-technique verdict. The engine pieces and the QA tool from the same four days are already wired in and documented; what no agent or pipeline step can reach is the catalogue, its twelve script and picture rules, `fal_edit.py`, the experiment components and most of the research conclusions. Outside practice points the same way from several independent traditions: write a catalogue and not a grammar, promote after about three real uses, keep a status ring and a dated decision record, and expect a template library to pull pictures toward sameness unless some beats stay bespoke. Anthropic's documentation adds the delivery rule (a few always-loaded lines saying the catalogue exists, the catalogue itself on demand, code only for what must hold every time), and the repository is already past the documented limits where it matters: `CLAUDE.md` is 180 lines but 42 KB, and the visual-spec skill asks an agent to hold about 717 KB before it chooses anything, none of it about how to treat a still. Tiger's two statements therefore point at different dates, not different directions: "I like having all of these options" is met by keeping every entry findable and versioned, and "push a complete first episode over the line" is met by deferring everything that costs more than one file until the episode has shipped and a second script asks for it.

## 1. A finding acts on production by three routes, and one register costs 20 to 43 files

### Seven layers, of which three act

The repository's knowledge system is a seven-layer stack. Context files sit on top: `CLAUDE.md` at 180 lines, `AGENTS.md` at 341 and `remotion-templates/CLAUDE.md` at 170. Below them `project/` holds **47 doctrine files and 15,079 lines**, including eight "registers" of 191 to 601 lines that each name a small set of canonical techniques, the editorial claim each makes, and a decision matrix. `remotion-templates/` adds 23 top-level documents and 7,839 lines of chooser material (the FT-modelled `PARALLAX_VISUAL_VOCABULARY.md`, five family selectors, a 1,170-line template picker) plus 79 research dossiers of 10,790 lines. Then come **25 skills totalling 7,978 lines**, seven slash commands and two subagents; a machine-readable layer (the concept registry, the shot-list and manifest schemas, an 87-value template enum, `palette.json`); lint and tests (eight linters in `tools/lint/` run from [`scripts/lint.sh`](../scripts/lint.sh), 75 Python test files, 91 TypeScript test files); and at the bottom the editorial playbook, a learning log with no entries because nothing has been published, and a six-file auto-memory folder ([repo audit §1](../research_notes/Systemizing%20Parallax%20findings%20and%20tools/repo_knowledge_system.md), P).

The audit's central inference is that a rule acts on production only when it reaches one of three places: the prompt text of a skill, a schema field or enum that `generate_manifest.py` writes and `FullEpisode.tsx` reads, or a lint rule. Everything else is reference text that works only if a session happens to open it (*Inference*, repo audit §1). Lint coverage shows the pattern. The four registers whose vocabulary became a schema enum (transitions, text animation, hold motion, math) have lint rules in [`manifest_lint.py`](../tools/lint/manifest_lint.py) and its siblings; the four that stayed prose (counterfactual, asset generation, soundscape, and pacing beyond checking that the token is valid) have none (repo audit §6, P). The house precedent for "enforced" is "has an enum".

Propagation cost scales with the number of places a rule is restated. The audit did not record hours for any of this; commit timestamps cluster within a day or two and say nothing about effort.

### A chart travels a validated path; a still's treatment is chosen in three disconnected places

A visual decision travels from the script row, through the optional visual-concept audit and the visual-spec skill, into `shot-list.json` and the template data files, through `generate_manifest.py` into `assembly-manifest.json`, and from there to `FullEpisode.tsx`. For code-drawn graphics every hop has vocabulary and a validator: mode tags and `DIR:` lines in the script, family selectors at the choice, Zod schemas on the data, lint on the manifest. The script-draft skill is told plainly where the choice happens: the picker documents are load-bearing "because `visual-spec` is mostly a transcriber of your choice" ([`skills/script-draft/SKILL.md`](../skills/script-draft/SKILL.md), P).

A generated still has no such path. In `ai-care-motives` its treatment is chosen in three places that do not talk to each other: free text in the script row ("quiet Ken Burns push"); a `motion_technique: "ken-burns"` key on all twelve assets in the shot list, one of 14 keys out of 23 that [`data/shot-list.schema.json`](../data/shot-list.schema.json) does not define; and a hand-written `framing` block on 22 manifest segments, which since decision D49 is the only one of the three that reaches the screen ([`shot-list.json`](../episodes/ai-care-motives/shot-list.json), [`assembly-manifest.json`](../remotion-templates/data/episodes/ai-care-motives/assembly-manifest.json), [`DECISIONS.md`](../project/DECISIONS.md), P). `generate_manifest.py` contains no handling of `framing`; visual-spec's Step 5 turns every `[AI-GEN:]` row into a brief for a video model; no `SKILL.md` mentions `StillFrame` or `framing`; and the five selectors cover maps, charts, diagrams, timelines and typography, with none for still pictures (repo audit §1, §3, §6, P). The step that was meant to decide still motion, [`MOTION_DIRECTOR_SPEC.md`](../project/MOTION_DIRECTOR_SPEC.md), is a design draft dated 23 May marked "Not built". In the audit's words, a generated still enters the system as an asset to be sourced, never as a thing with a treatment to be chosen (*Inference*).

The audit mapped the catalogue onto the fields that exist, and the mapping is the researcher's own and untested in the episode. Roughly a third of the techniques are expressible in the hand-wired manifest today with no new code: the slow push, framed details as consecutive segments on one still, insert and reverse angle, rapid-cut montage, a matched cut as a hard cut, a match dissolve, relight and soft state swap as a dissolve between two registered stills, and any AI clip as a footage segment. A second group has a template with a known limit (two-up through `SplitFrame`; labels and zoom stations through `ScrollyReveal`, `AnnotatedImage` and `AnnotatedZoom`, all of which force a duotone treatment). The rest has no place to be written down, and most of it is missing one of four data shapes: a second registered source on the same framing with a swap time, cut-out layers over a plate, a four-corner quad on a still, or a panel layout with per-panel crops (*Inference*, repo audit §3).

### The system is already past its own carrying capacity

To make one visual decision the visual-spec skill tells an agent to "familiarize yourself with" twelve documents of 7,201 lines and 523,630 bytes; with the 1,013-line skill, the 1,651-line schema reference and the family index, that is **10,270 lines and 717 KB before a selector or dossier is opened**, on the order of 180,000 tokens by the audit's arithmetic, and none of it covers how to treat a generated still (repo audit §6, P; the token figure is an estimate from byte counts). The script-draft skill's fixed reading list is about 5,526 lines on top of its own 482.

Staleness is equally measurable. Seventeen of the nineteen "doctrine splits" listed in the 11 October stills report are still true of the files, and the new engine added one: `POLISH.md` rule A1 says no linear interpolation, while `StillFrame.tsx` documents `linear` as its default ease and 18 of the episode's 22 framings use it ([`StillFrame.tsx`](../remotion-templates/src/components/StillFrame.tsx), repo audit §5, P). Three files give three different targets for AI-generated runtime (15–30% in the playbook's VIS-01, 10–20% in `VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md`, 5–15% in visual-spec) against a measured **45.6%**, and decision D3 still says clean motion graphics are primary and AI is "never" raw final output. The strategy documents predate the 7 October focus shift (`CONTENT_IDENTITY.md`, read by seven skills, mentions geopolitics 23 times and alignment never), and "geopolit" appears in 14 of 25 skills. Ten dated audit files in `project/` are read by no skill, context file or code. The audit's inference is that staleness clusters wherever a number or a tool name was restated in several files (repo audit §5).

Two structural facts sit underneath. The whole working tree since late May is uncommitted, so any adoption step taken today lands on an unversioned base and cannot be reviewed or reverted as a unit. And there are three physical copies of the skills: the repository's, symbolic links to them, and ten plugin installs that all differ from the repository and predate every October change (the plugin `visual-spec` is 135 lines dated 26 September against 1,013 in the repository), with nothing in the repository determining which copy a session invokes (repo audit §1, §4, P). The audit could not confirm that the pre-commit hooks are installed, did not run lint, and notes an earlier report's citation of a stored 19 May test run with 111 of 1,638 tests failing, which it did not verify.

## 3. Outside practice says catalogue, not grammar, and promotion after real use

### A working entry answers "when not" and "which sibling"; a working chooser asks four to nine questions

Mature design systems share a small skeleton. NN/g describes the layers as a style guide, a component library, a pattern library and the people who maintain them ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-systems-101/)); the systems the researchers surveyed add a fixed page per entry and a status with written entry criteria. The most reusable part is the page. GOV.UK's Accordion page runs, in order, when to use it, when not to use it, how to decide between it and its two siblings, how it works, the research behind it, known issues and gaps, and recent changes ([GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/accordion/)). Parallax's catalogue already has the equivalents of "known issues" (its faults table) and "research" (its first reads). It lacks "when not" and "decide between".

Choosers that last are entered by purpose and stay shallow. The FT Visual Vocabulary has nine categories, each defined by the relationship the reader should see, with chart types repeating across categories ([Financial Times](https://github.com/Financial-Times/chart-doctor/tree/main/visual-vocabulary)); From Data to Viz has six entry points leading to 39 chart types ([From Data to Viz](https://www.data-to-viz.com/)). The standing criticism is that one-page trees are underspecified and teach nothing. Stephen Few argues for a matrix of forms against purposes, with reasons attached, used as a reminder by someone who already understands the options ([Few](https://www.perceptualedge.com/blog/?p=2080), O), and Bertini and Desbarats add that purpose-keyed choosers need a finer second question and a scalability criterion ([Bertini](https://filwd.substack.com/p/choosing-chart-choosers-with-nick), O). Parallax's script-move table, keyed on what the sentence is doing, is the purpose-first design the critics prefer. At 35 rows in three tables over 15 families it is above the four-to-nine range for a first question, and its predictable weak point is several candidate techniques per sentence job with no tie-breaker (*Inference*, pattern note §2). Library science supplies the structural fix: one hierarchy for one dimension of classification, a grid for two, facets for three or more ([Denton](https://www.miskatonic.org/library/facet-web-howto.html)). Sentence job, family, cost and status are independent properties, so the families and the sentence jobs are two facets of one catalogue and neither should be the parent of the other (*Inference*).

The pattern-language record sets the ceiling on ambition. Dearden and Finlay's critical review finds that catalogues (a name, a rationale, an example, links) are achievable and useful; that generative languages in Alexander's sense almost never get built; that it could find no observational studies of practitioners benefiting from patterns; and that lookup time grows with the size of a collection, to the point that patterns help in live design only once internalised ([Dearden and Finlay 2006](https://research.cs.vt.edu/ns/cs5724papers/dearden-patterns-hci09.pdf)). The benefit most consistently claimed is vocabulary: a good name carries the design discussion. The nearest published precedents for a catalogue keyed on narrative function are research codings: Segel and Heer's seven genres and three groups of tactics from 58 examples ([Segel and Heer 2010](https://idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2010-Narrative-InfoVis.pdf)), Stolper's 20 techniques under four purposes ([Stolper et al. 2016](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/MSR-TR-2016-14-Storytelling-Techniques.pdf)), Bach's 18 narrative patterns, also issued as a card deck ([NAPA cards](http://napa-cards.net/)), and Amini's coding of 50 data videos by narrative role ([Amini et al. 2015](https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/assets/publication_files/DataVideoStorytellingCHI2015_Revision_Final.pdf)). All are descriptive. No source was found that documents a technique catalogue of this size for an essay channel, and none of the explainer studios examined publishes an internal visual system ([Kurzgesagt](https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/)).

### Promotion waits for about three real uses, and a negative result gets a ring of its own

Independent traditions converge on not standardising at first sight. The "rule of three" (tolerate duplication twice, abstract on the third occurrence) is attributed to Don Roberts through Fowler ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_three_(computer_programming)), S). Sandi Metz's first-hand account explains why: duplication is far cheaper than the wrong abstraction, which accretes parameters and conditionals until it is unreadable and held in place by sunk cost, and her remedy is to inline it back into each caller and look again ([Metz](https://sandimetz.com/blog/2016/1/20/the-wrong-abstraction)). Dan Mall applies the same count to design systems ([Mall](https://danmall.com/posts/three-times-is-a-pattern), S; the page returned 404 on 11 October), and Kent C. Dodds refuses a count altogether, saying only to wait until the abstraction is well-informed ([Dodds](https://kentcdodds.com/blog/aha-programming), S). Brad Frost's governance process gives explicit permission not to systemize: a one-off "snowflake" stays in the product, tracked, until it proves broadly useful ([Frost](https://bradfrost.com/blog/post/a-design-system-governance-process/)). GOV.UK splits the bar in two: "useful and unique" to propose an entry, "usable, consistent and versatile" to publish it ([GOV.UK contribution criteria](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/community/contribution-criteria/)).

Three instruments are small enough for one person to copy. Status ladders run three to five steps, for example Experimental, Ready and Deprecated at GitHub Primer ([Primer](https://primer.style/status), S), with a last-updated date under the title as Carbon prints it ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/overview/components/)). The Thoughtworks radar has four rings: Assess, Trial, Adopt, and a fourth for things that went badly, which its FAQ now calls Caution and most guides still call Hold; a blip reaches Trial only after production use ([Thoughtworks](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/faq)). Nygard's decision record is one or two pages under five headings, numbered, never edited, and superseded by a later record, on the argument that large documents are neither read nor kept current ([Nygard](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)). `project/DECISIONS.md` is already a log of this kind, and the catalogue's empty Verdict column is a status field waiting for a vocabulary. The researchers' translation for one person is that Assess means explored in a reel, Trial means used once in a real script, Adopt means used in three separate script situations and still liked, and Hold means tried and found to read badly or cost too much (*Inference*, pattern note §6). On that ladder nearly all 70 entries sit at Assess today, and the unit of demand is a real use, not a test reel.

### The measured benefit is speed on repeated tasks, and the costs do not shrink for one person

Two small studies measure benefit. In a Figma test designers finished 34% faster with a design system; it is a vendor study, the sample size is not stated, and its author calls the figure an upper bound ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/measuring-the-value-of-design-systems/), O). In an agency test eight developers' median time fell from 4.2 hours to 2 with IBM Carbon, with the from-scratch condition always run first ([Sparkbox](https://sparkbox.com/foundry/design_system_roi_impact_of_design_systems_business_value_carbon_design_system), S, O). The costs are better documented. Maintainers surveyed in 2022 named debt (43%), parity between design and code (37%) and adoption (36%) as their top challenges, and among users 39% called their system poorly documented and 35% could not tell what was old, broken or coming soon ([Sparkbox Design Systems Survey 2022](https://designsystemssurvey.seesparkbox.com/2022/)). NN/g says a full system needs a dedicated team and is unlikely to pay off for early work that is still likely to change ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-systems-101/)).

All of this evidence was collected on teams. The researchers' reading is that coordination benefits largely vanish for one person, repetition benefits remain only for techniques that actually recur, and debt, parity and unclear status stay at full size because there is no second person to notice a stale entry (*Inference*, pattern note §7). The doctrine splits described in section 1 are that cost, already being paid.

### Sameness is the documented side effect

On creativity the evidence concerns the form of a constraint more than its amount, and every item is known from summaries only. A review of 145 studies is reported as finding that moderate constraints help and only heavy ones stifle ([Acar et al.](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/23261/), S). Designers shown an example reproduce its features, including flawed ones, even when told to avoid them ([design fixation review](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_6a026941-2298-42fd-bf7a-dc08eaa73bd5), S), and one experiment found that teaching teams about fixation reduced it ([Howard et al.](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/79504), S). A computer-vision study of about 10,000 websites found layouts converging after 2007, with shared libraries among the suspected causes ([Goree et al. 2021](https://vision.soic.indiana.edu/papers/websimilarity2021chi.pdf), S). None of this was measured on video. The researchers infer that constraints on the frame (palette, type, durations, a small default set) are the helpful kind, and that a library of finished example frames is the kind most likely to produce copying; a catalogue that leads with the purpose and keeps the example one click away is the safer design (*Inference*, pattern note §7).

### The lightest forms survive because they are small or generated

Each minimal form in the survey is one artefact with one job: the FT's one-page poster; the BBC's `bbplot`, which reduces a house style to two functions and a cookbook and has 13 commits in total ([bbplot](https://github.com/bbc/bbplot)); a four-ring radar; a one-page decision record; a swipe file with a note on why each item was saved (O, S); the Magnum contact sheet, which shows the whole run of attempts beside the chosen frame ([Thames and Hudson](https://thamesandhudson.com.au/?p=70319), S); and a short checklist at one high-stakes stage, the form with the strongest outcome evidence, from surgery ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2009/01/surgical-safety-checklist-drops-deaths-and-complications-by-more-than-one-third/), S). Carbon's motion system is the model for the token layer: six durations and three easing curves in two registers, with choreography between components explicitly left to the product team ([Carbon motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/)). Parallax already owns most of these forms: tokens in `palette.json` and `theme.ts`, a decision log, a measured QA standard, and, in the seven reels and the gallery, a contact sheet that preserves all 70 attempts without endorsing any of them. That last property answers the fear that not systemizing something means losing it.

## 4. For agents: a few always-loaded lines, the catalogue on demand, code for what must hold

### Facts load always, procedures load on demand, guarantees run as code

Anthropic's documentation draws one line through its mechanisms. What loads into every request (`CLAUDE.md`, unscoped rules, skill descriptions) should be small and factual. Procedures and reference material belong in skills and their files, which load when used. Anything that must happen every time belongs in a hook, a permission, a lint or a script, which costs no context and does not depend on the model choosing to comply ([Claude Code docs: features overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/features-overview); [memory](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory); [hooks guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks-guide)). The same pages state the failure modes: longer always-loaded files reduce adherence, contradictory instructions are resolved arbitrarily, and vague or overlapping skill descriptions cause the wrong skill to load or none ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). The promotion path is given as triggers, not a pipeline: a mistake made twice becomes a `CLAUDE.md` line, a procedure pasted for the third time becomes a skill, something that must happen every time becomes a hook, and a line Claude already obeys without being told is deleted ([features overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/features-overview)).

Independent measurements support the direction without fixing thresholds for Claude Code. In Vercel's evaluations a compressed 8 KB index of documentation paths held in always-loaded context scored 100%, against 53% for an on-demand skill and 79% for a skill with explicit instructions, and the skill was never invoked in 56% of cases ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/agents-md-outperforms-skills-in-our-agent-evals), O). SkillsBench found that curated skills raised pass rates from 33.9% to 50.5% across 87 tasks and that focused skills of at most three modules beat exhaustive bundles ([SkillsBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670)). A study of repository context files found they generally did not improve task success while raising inference cost by over 20% ([Gloaguen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988)), and two earlier studies found performance falling as input length and instruction count grow ([Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot); [IFScale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538)). None of these tests Claude Code's harness on current models. Read together they suggest that a catalogue agents must consult unprompted needs a short always-loaded pointer, and that the pointer has to be paid for by removing something (*Inference*, agent note §3 and §5).

### A 70-entry catalogue belongs in reference files behind a short index

No official source recommends one large file, and none of the public examples reviewed embeds a catalogue in a `SKILL.md` body. The documented shapes are a short skill that says when to consult the material, with either one reference file per domain carrying a table of contents and grep hints, or a structured data file queried by a script whose output is all that enters context ([Anthropic: skill authoring best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices)). Vercel's react-best-practices skill is the public precedent for the second: one rule per file with an impact field, a short index, and a generated compiled document ([vercel-labs/agent-skills](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-skills/tree/main/skills/react-best-practices)). Two phrasings from the guidance apply directly to a chooser: provide "defaults, not menus", and tell the agent when to load each file instead of pointing at a folder ([agentskills.io best practices](https://agentskills.io/skill-creation/best-practices)). A flat menu of 70 equal options is the documented anti-pattern; a grid that resolves to "use this; if that condition holds, use the other" is the supported one.

The agent note infers that the house's own concept-registry pattern (a JSON file with a schema, a lookup CLI, a pre-commit validator) is the natural home for the catalogue. This report treats that as the right destination and the wrong first step. A registry with a schema, a CLI, tests and a generated document is a tool-sized or template-sized job by the house's own precedents, and it would fix field names for entries that have each been used zero times. A Markdown catalogue with one grid, a ring per row and a pointer costs one file and can be converted when entries reach Adopt (*Inference*, this report). No source justifies embeddings or a retrieval server at a scale of a few hundred entries in a local repository.

### Scripts beat servers for a fixed handful of paid calls, and evaluation should stay at two or three real prompts

For a local repository Anthropic's documentation calls CLI tools the most context-efficient way to reach an external service, and the skills guidance gives conventions for scripts an agent runs: no interactive prompts, a concise `--help`, structured output on stdout, meaningful exit codes, idempotent behaviour because agents retry, a `--dry-run` for stateful operations and an explicit confirmation flag for destructive ones ([Claude Code docs: best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices); [agentskills.io: using scripts](https://agentskills.io/skill-creation/using-scripts)). `fal_i2v.py` already writes a JSON sidecar with prompt and cost; neither fal script has tests, and no source the researchers found covers spend ceilings for paid APIs specifically. fal's hosted MCP server exposes nine tools over more than a thousand models ([fal](https://blog.fal.ai/connect-your-ai-to-1-000-models-with-the-fal-mcp-server), O); the agent note infers that it suits exploring new models and that the repository scripts, which encode the model choice, prompt structure, output location and record, suit the production path (*Inference*).

On evaluation the official starting size is two or three realistic prompts per skill, run with and without it, and Anthropic's own skill-creator says subjective skills such as writing style or visual design are better judged by human review than by quantitative evals ([agentskills.io: evaluating skills](https://agentskills.io/skill-creation/evaluating-skills); [skill-creator](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/skill-creator/SKILL.md)). The full description-tuning loop (about 20 queries, three runs each, up to five iterations) works out to roughly 300 model sessions per skill by the researcher's arithmetic ([agentskills.io: optimizing descriptions](https://agentskills.io/skill-creation/optimizing-descriptions)). The documentation also warns that a reviewer prompted to find gaps will usually report some, and that chasing every finding leads to over-engineering ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). That is Tiger's 7 October remark in official form. Picture work is the subjective case the guidance carves out, and rendering a still and looking at it is the review.

## 5. Practitioners pick the picture while writing the sentence, and fix only the container

### The picture is decided before the sentence is final

Across broadcast news, documentary and explainer practice the shared craft rule is that narration should add what the picture cannot show while landing at the same moment as the picture. A television-news tip sheet puts it as using words to add detail, context and meaning to what viewers are already looking at, never writing more words than there are pictures for, and never fighting the picture ([b-roll.net](https://b-roll.net/today/2015/02/more-tips-for-writing-tv-news/), O). The explainer practitioners with the most detailed accounts decide the picture while writing. Estelle Caswell of Vox says she knows exactly what she will show as she writes, edits sentence by sentence so the wording directs attention to the picture, requires a pitch to list its visual hooks and visual evidence before any script exists, and for abstract ideas borrows a familiar visual framework and teaches the viewer a small visual language ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/estelle-caswell-vox-podcast), O). Joss Fong says tight correspondence between what is seen and heard aids comprehension, that it is harder for topics that are less literal and physical, and that she plans one moment per episode that stays in the mind ([Video Consortium](https://videoconsortium.org/mag/episode-1-joss-fong), O). Kurzgesagt works the other way round, sketching only after about a dozen script drafts and using that phase to settle metaphors, transitions and which scenes deserve the most attention ([Kurzgesagt](https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/), O). The failure critics name is the one a script-first channel is most exposed to: video used as mere illustration of a finished text ([Film Studies in Motion](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/film-studies-in-motion/what-makes-a-video-essay-great-by-kevin-b-lee), O).

### Controlled evidence gives veto rules, not a mapping

The one body of controlled evidence is Mayer's multimedia-learning programme. Presenting narration and its matching picture together beat presenting them in sequence in 9 of 9 tests (median effect size 1.22); placing words beside what they describe won 22 of 22 (1.10); leaving out extraneous material won 23 of 23 (0.86); pictures with narration beat pictures with narration plus the same words on screen in 16 of 16 (0.86); and cueing the structure helped in 24 of 28, with a smaller effect of 0.41 ([Mayer and Fiorella 2014](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/CD5B7AE1279A9AB81F8EEBB53DBEC86E)). A 2025 pooled analysis of the same corpus puts the overall effect at g = 0.37 and finds results for animation inconsistent while static diagrams with words are reliable ([Cromley and Chen 2025](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10637927)); a commentary on it reports smaller benefits for liberal-arts content than for STEM ([Hendrick](https://carlhendrick.substack.com/p/which-multimedia-strategies-actually), O). These are learning outcomes, mostly on procedural material, not retention on a platform or persuasion. They serve as veto rules that apply to every technique. They say nothing about which treatment suits which kind of sentence.

That mapping exists only as description. The three academic frameworks that tie narrative function to visual treatment are codings of existing work that their authors present as vocabularies, and no study was found that tests a function-to-treatment mapping in narrated essay video (scripting note §3). One small study is instructive all the same: across 67 data stories the median story used three of Bach's eighteen patterns, and what authors said they used often diverged from what they did ([Blount et al. 2020](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2020/101216/101216.pdf)). The catalogue's "reach for first" column is therefore a set of hypotheses of the same standing, written from one take each. Tiger's second aim, to "know what type of visual strategy is good for which type of script", cannot be met by organising what exists; it needs episodes.

### Script formats keep the picture column coarse until late

The two-column audio-visual script is the standard carrier for narrated non-fiction, and in every published format the picture column holds a small closed vocabulary (a source type, a shot size, a duration), not prose description; detail arrives in later documents, the storyboard or shot list and then the animatic timed to scratch audio ([Chicago Media Works](https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3docedit_scriptmpl/3editing_docspaperedit.html); [Boords](https://boords.com/video-script-template/documentary-template), O). Two whole-piece planning tools come from animation and film theory: the colour script, a strip of crude colour studies of every scene made early and kept throughout ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/audio/playlist/192/2581)), and Bruce Block's principle that contrast between successive images raises intensity and affinity lowers it ([Block](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-visual-story/9781317745693/xhtml/Ch02.xhtml), O). No essayist or explainer studio was found publishing a visual beat map or a rule for how often a treatment may recur, and Caswell, asked about the conventional chain of mood boards, style frames and production boards, said she had never heard of it ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/estelle-caswell-vox-podcast), O). Small teams skip the ladder. Parallax's format already matches the convention (mode tags, a subject, `DIR:` lines), and a rendered preview against scratch narration, which `preview-range.mjs` and the 11 October voice test already produce, is an animatic.

### What is standardised is the container; the picture stays loose

The practitioners who publish their process agree on the split. Kurzgesagt keeps an illustration library and still redraws most assets to keep each video distinct ([transcript of its 2020 making-of](https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-english-online/courses/689474/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-120-4887128/), O). Caswell standardises the pitch format and editorial review and rejects a plug-and-play production model ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/estelle-caswell-vox-podcast), O). Fong says attempts to standardise episodes failed because each story has its own needs ([Video Consortium](https://videoconsortium.org/mag/episode-1-joss-fong), O). What they fix is the pitch, the review, the palette and type, the tools and the parameterised templates; what they leave open is the picture for each idea. The scripting note's inference is that a template library sits on the safe side when an entry encodes a form with open content, and tips into sameness when the metaphor itself is reused, and that a mapping from sentence function to technique can mechanise exactly the picture-illustrates-the-finished-sentence relation unless some beats are designed picture-first (*Inference*, scripting note §5 and §8). The guard practitioners describe is rationing: a few bespoke images per episode on a systemized base.

Published workflows for AI imagery agree on an order (lock the story, lock the references, write the shot list, generate frames, then video) and on generating a character sheet as one multi-panel image so that panels share a generation context ([invideo](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-use-ai-character-sheet-grid-panels-to-keep/), O). The catalogue reached the same conclusion from its own tests ("a sheet beats a chain"), which is two weak sources agreeing: a vendor FAQ and one take. The only discard figures found, roughly one generation kept in four, come from an unnamed production reported by a tool vendor and cannot be verified. On feedback, nothing was found tying retention to in-video visual treatments, and YouTube's testing tool covers thumbnails and titles only ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=187324)). The loop available to a small channel is observational, needs several episodes before a technique can be said to have earned its place, and is cheapest before publishing: a cold reader marking confusion.

For Parallax the transferable practice is small and sits entirely at the script stage (*Inference*, this report). The angle memo already has a "visual arc" item, which can carry the Vox-style list of visual hooks and two or three beats designed picture-first. The script-draft skill is where the choice is made, so the chooser belongs there and not in visual-spec. The catalogue's own six script rules (return to places, state the change as its own sentence, name what is in the picture, keep lists parallel and short, give analogies a returning phrase and a fixed order, put the number in its own clause) are the house's version of writing for pictures and the nearest thing in the repository to Tiger's third aim, thinking about the script from the side of visual strategy. They are unapproved and have no rule IDs.

If the answer is yes, the fix is the default in `StillFrame.tsx`, which reaches 18 framings at once, and not eighteen manifest edits or a new paragraph of doctrine. That is the 10 October report's lesson, that one corrected default reaches every call site while one more rule reaches none ([Remotion polish and AI tooling](Remotion%20polish%20and%20AI%20tooling.md), P).

On the grid, the outside evidence gives the shape: five to nine sentence jobs down the side, one discriminator across the top (whether there is a number, how many things, whether the place has been shown before), a default and at most two alternates in each cell, a "not when" line, and the fifteen families kept as the second facet for browsing. A technique may appear in more than one cell, as chart types do in the FT's poster. On the rings, the proposal to put to Tiger is that every row starts at Assess, that the four entries the catalogue itself reports as failing or unfixable (the stepped walk from edits, the AI camera arc, the AI transition between matched stills, depth-map parallax) go to Hold, and that the techniques the episode actually ships with enter at Trial once it is out: the slow push on 22 segments, type over a held picture on two, one layout, one AI clip.

The measure for episode two already exists. The QA tool's components table reports the share of runtime that is a still with a camera move and nothing else, **36.6%** in this episode, and the stills report calls that row the measure of whether the step did anything ([AI stills beyond Ken Burns](AI%20stills%20beyond%20Ken%20Burns.md), P). Whether a drafting session opens the grid without being told is the only trigger check the pointer needs, and it is observed during real work, not in an eval. With one video the retention data will yield hypotheses and not findings.

### What stays an entry, what becomes code, a rule or a lint, and what should not

Every technique stays a catalogue entry until a real episode uses it. That applies with most force to the group with no place to be written down (state swap inside one shot, accumulation build, cut-out moves, layered depth, type on a surface or in perspective, video inside a still, panel builds, panorama travel, the 3D model, the figure on green) and to the whole of the handmade-motion and other-model families, because each needs one of the four missing data shapes or per-picture measurement that belongs to stills the channel may not keep.

What should become code is short. The eased default in `StillFrame`, if Tiger rules that way. A spend preview on the two fal scripts, since Tiger's approval for the exploration carried a rule to state expected spend before each round and report it after, and a rule that must hold on every paid call is the documentation's definition of something to move out of prose (*Inference*, this report; a `--dry-run` that prints the resolved request and estimated cost is the form the script guidance describes). The generic pieces of the experiments, lifted on second use. One data shape at a time on `StillFrame`, at about six files with no registry changes, in preference to new templates at fifteen to eighteen.

What should become a rule goes through lanes that exist. The catalogue's twelve rules go to the playbook's pending lane for Tiger's approval, and two conflicts need a ruling before they can be approved at all. The catalogue's pose-to-pose, onion-skin, light-lapse and passing-light techniques need exactly what `ASSET_GENERATION_REGISTER.md` forbids (it requires figures in static poses and bans flickering light sources), and the script rule "return to places" runs against the QA rule that flags a still used more than twice, in an episode whose desk still appears five times. Rulings go to `DECISIONS.md` as new entries that supersede old ones.

What should become a lint is, for now, nothing new. The one candidate with a trust stake is VIS-12, the rule approved on 11 October that every exchange or transcript on screen carries one of three labels; it is mechanically checkable, and for one episode a look during the final QA pass is the proportionate check, with a lint worth writing when a second episode puts exchanges on screen (*Inference*, this report). A lint on technique choice has to wait for an enum, and the enum has to wait for adoption.

What should not be built: a stills register; a `DIR:` directive per technique; an enum of 70 values; a sixth selector document; a dedicated toolbox skill with its own description competing in an over-budget listing; evals for the catalogue; a retrieval server; fal's MCP server on the production path; a plugin package for a single repository.

## Conclusion

The four days changed something more basic than the size of the toolbox. `VISUAL_TOOLBOX.md` is the first document in the repository that treats a generated still as a thing with a treatment to be chosen, and the first whose unit is what the sentence is doing; every earlier chooser is organised by template family and treats a still as an asset to source. That reframing is the durable finding, and it costs one file to keep. The bottleneck it exposes is not a shortage of techniques, components or rules. It is the script-stage choice point, which today is fed about 5,500 lines of reading with selectors for charts, maps, diagrams, timelines and type, and none for pictures. A grid and a pointer address that directly; a register would add to the load that causes it.

The second implication concerns the house pattern itself. It has no status ladder: a capability is either unshipped or it is propagated to twenty or more files, and the iris residue shows what that all-or-nothing step leaks. Decision D49 quietly created the missing middle rung, because a hand-wired manifest lets a technique reach the screen with no vocabulary at all. Treating that rung as Trial, and reserving the full pattern for techniques that have reached Adopt, would make "enforced means it has an enum" a destination with a route to it, and would let the library improve at the pace of shipping. The test of whether any of this worked is concrete and close: the camera-move-only share of the second episode, and whether the session that drafts its script opens the grid without being told.
