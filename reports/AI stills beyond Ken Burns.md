# Free stills should cut, not drift

**Parallax gets the most out of free AI stills by spending several on each scene instead of one: registered states of a single frame (a pose changed, a figure removed, a detail reframed, a panel added) that cut on the narration, with code-drawn annotation on top, and AI video kept for the one technique that has passed a test on this art style, the pinned first and last frame.** The current episode shows what the alternative costs. `ai-care-motives` runs 11:19; 4:08 of it (36.6%) is a generated still with nothing but one linear zoom on it, against 0:10 (1.5%) of AI video; twelve stills carry 4:18, and the median Ken Burns hold is 12 seconds. The evidence for the shift is uneven, and this reference marks the grade of every claim. Film and learning research supports visual change that is tied to the sentence being spoken and warns against showing a finished picture and then talking over it, but no study measures picture-change rate in narrated essays. No source found, and no project test, has tried layered parallax, line boil, looping cinemagraphs, layer-splitting models or any video model other than MiniMax H3 Max on flat, faceless, constructivist illustration. The October 2026 toolset makes state-based work cheap (a targeted edit for $0.03–0.08, a layer split for $0.05, transparent PNGs, first-and-last-frame video from about $0.25 per five seconds), yet it still offers no alpha video from hosted flagships, no dependable loop switch and no benchmark of style retention. Reception points the same way for a different reason: a flat, clean, narrated explainer is now the look that viewers and YouTube's classifier associate with slop, and the documented defence is visible authorship, which layouts, annotation and deliberate cutting show and a slow push over one picture does not. Everything below builds at the pinned Remotion 4.0.457. The real obstacles are local: Ken Burns moves baked into MP4 files, picture templates that force a duotone the episode has switched off, a match-cut token that draws a plain cut, and doctrine that contradicts itself on parallax and has not absorbed the October tests.

The analysis draws four lessons that matter for every technique below. The model obeys statements of what stays still and the direction of motion, and it ignores amplitude and speed words. Whatever moves is redrawn in the model's smoother style while still regions keep the original texture, so style drift scales with how much of the frame moves. The drawn pose decides the motion: mid-stride figures walk and a standing figure glides. And both successes came from fixing the image first and asking the video model for less, which the file states as "edit the frame, then animate." The end frame was made with `fal-ai/nano-banana-2/edit` and composited back through a feathered mask so everything outside the window is pixel-identical; `fal-ai/object-removal/mask` was tried first and produced a blurred smear.

The analysis also reports defects shared across the clips: a visible pop between frame 0 and frame 1 (4.5 to 8.3 luma), a slow brightening over the clip (about 1 to 1.4 luma on the four harder tests, 2.6 on the first take, and close to steady on the widened retake), 24 fps output and a near-silent audio track. The used clip needed four manual fixes before it went into the timeline: its first three frames trimmed, a conform from 24 to 30 fps, the last frame held to fill the 10.2-second slot, and the audio stripped. A 3% slow push was then laid over the whole slot. Those steps are described in prose only, and the audit found no script that performs them and no saved prompt or parameters for the two image edits.

The limits of this evidence are specific. Each clip is one take with no seed, so nothing is known about take-to-take variance, and the "works" verdict rests on a single generation. The four-model comparison the project proposed (H3, Kling 3.0 Pro, Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash, Vidu Q4 Preview) was not run. The most reused still, the desk at night, has never been animated. No written verdict from the creator exists for any clip beyond the wiring of the one in use. No reference-image, multi-keyframe, video-to-video or motion-brush feature has been tried, and no 768P draft has been generated.

Three earlier project records complete the picture. On 23 September 2026 a Remotion test placed a transparent generated sapling cut-out over a generated ground plate and moved it by code; the creator rejected its motion and transition, and the file calls it "an experiment record, not an approved production approach" ([`illustrated-cutout-motion-2026-09-23.md`](../analysis/illustrated-cutout-motion-2026-09-23.md), P). The same day a hybrid-versus-code comparison concluded that "generated art can establish a situation, but it cannot silently become a precise data visualization" ([`image-remotion-synergy-2026-09-23.md`](../analysis/image-remotion-synergy-2026-09-23.md), P). On 24 September a documentation review, with no generation, proposed a four-panel storyboard with separate panel clips assembled in a code-controlled grid ([`illustration-to-video-workflows-2026-09-24.md`](../analysis/illustration-to-video-workflows-2026-09-24.md), P). The September analyses also proposed cutting between poses. Neither idea was built or tested.

The standing doctrine comes from May 2026 tests on Pika and Hailuo ([`CHAINED_STILL_LESSONS.md`](../project/CHAINED_STILL_LESSONS.md), P). Its empirical rules have held up: classify a shot as a reframe (the camera moved) or a transform (the scene changed) before choosing a tool; generate frame B by editing frame A; a few large flat shapes morph cleanly and many small details smear; a zoom is almost never a morph job; ground every motion prompt in the actual still, because anything named but absent gets hallucinated in. The same file holds a six-family taxonomy of single-still animation that already names two of the candidates in this report as allowed and unused: family B, the masked ambient loop, is "the underused safe tool", and family C, hand-layered 2.5D parallax, is "the one 'premium' move available", reserved for one or two hero shots per episode. Families E (auto-depth 2.5D) and F (an AI-hallucinated 3D camera) are blacklisted on flat art.

### Upscaling, zooming and single clips are scripted; edits and conforming are manual

Stills are made by hand in ChatGPT from a prompt document. Three steps after that are scripted in `tools/ai-video/`: `upscale.py` (Real-ESRGAN 4x, which took the twelve 1672×941 stills to 6688×3764), `render_kenburns.py` (one ffmpeg zoom per shot-list asset) and `fal_i2v.py` (one image-to-video clip per call, with an optional end image and a JSON sidecar recording prompt and cost). The 4K upscales give about **3.5 times the width of a 1920 frame**, so by the doctrine's own formula crops and pushes far tighter than the current 1.08 to 1.15 are available without softness (*inference* from the audit).

The gaps are equally concrete. `render_kenburns.py` reads only `assets`, not `ken_burns_variants`, so the four reuse variants were rendered by hand, and it cannot pan because it holds one focus point and changes only zoom. No tool exists for image edits or for conforming an i2v clip. `gen_depth.py` produces depth maps for a `DepthParallax` template and carries its own warning that depth estimation is unreliable on flat art. Two commands matter for trying anything new: `node remotion-templates/scripts/preview-range.mjs --episode=<slug> --segment=<id>` renders a stretch in context, and `npm run qa:episode -- --episode=<slug> --no-render --tag=components` regenerates the table above. The QA tool tells clip kinds apart by folder (`clips-i2v/` with a sidecar versus `clips-kenburns/`), so new clips must stay in those two folders or the report cannot classify them ([`VISUAL_QA.md`](../project/VISUAL_QA.md), P).

## The technique reference: correct names, recipes, costs and failure modes

Each entry gives the term to write in a production script, the standard name behind it (or a plain statement that none exists), how it is made, what it costs, how it fails on flat stylised art and when it earns its place. Unless a project test is cited, read every entry as *untested on flat art*.

### Raising pictures per minute: spend stills on more states of one scene

**What the numbers support.** Feature films fell from about 10 seconds per shot in the 1930s and 1940s to under 4 seconds after 2000 ([Cutting et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485803/)), and Bordwell puts the practical floor for a feature near 1.5 seconds ([Bordwell 2002](https://www.yumpu.com/en/document/view/36517206/intensified-continuity-visual-style-in-contemporary-american-film/2)). Documentaries had the fewest shots of any genre in one dataset, 491 per film ([Stephen Follows](https://stephenfollows.com/p/many-shots-average-movie)), roughly 11 seconds per shot if those films run about 90 minutes (*inference*; the source gives no runtime). *Astro Boy* used about 1,500 to 1,800 drawings per 25-minute episode ([Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/japan/anime-vs-cartoons/)), one fresh drawing every 0.8 to 1.0 seconds (*inference*). Kurzgesagt reports about 200 illustrated panels per video ([LingQ transcript of the channel's video](https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-english-online/courses/689474/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-120-4887128/)), about one every 3 seconds if a video runs ten minutes (*inference*; runtime unconfirmed). Parallax's manifest averages **12.8 seconds per segment**, and its own pacing register sets the analytical rate at a visual change every 4 to 6 seconds ([`PACING_REGISTER.md`](../project/PACING_REGISTER.md), P).

Two studies say what kind of change helps. Lang and colleagues found that as the rate of edits within one visual scene rose, arousal and memory rose with little added cognitive load ([Lang et al. 2000](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/the-effects-of-edits-on-arousal-attention-and-memory-for-televisi/)); the same research programme separates related from unrelated cuts and predicts an overload point ([LC4MP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LC4MP), S). Guo, Kim and Rubin's analysis of 6.9 million edX sessions found that continuously drawn tutorials held viewers 1.5 to 2 times as long as slides, and that building a slide and then explaining it for a while was the pattern where viewers stalled ([Guo et al. 2014](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf)). Against raw speed, MrBeast reported slowing his edits while views rose, and a spot check counted 38 cuts in a sampled minute before and 23 after, with the caution that the growth cannot be pinned on editing ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2024/03/04/mrbeast-editing-style-number-of-cuts-per-video/)).

**No study found varies picture-change rate in narrated essay video and measures retention or learning.** No source gives a tested limit on how long a still can hold under narration, and the "change something every N seconds" rules in circulation are unsourced vendor heuristics that disagree by a factor of ten: one says change something every 10 to 15 seconds early in a video ([monitoryt](https://monitoryt.com/blog/editing-for-retention), O), another asks for a pattern interrupt every 60 to 90 seconds past minute eight ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/advanced-retention-editing-cutting-patterns-that-keep-viewers-past-minute-8), O), and neither cites data. The researchers' working band, adopted here as a starting point and not as a finding, is a related change every 3 to 8 seconds during argument, 1 to 2 seconds for short bursts, and deliberate holds of 10 seconds or more on a summary or emotional frame. An edit inside one scene costs the viewer less than a jump to a new scene, so extra stills should go to more states of the same scene before they go to more scenes (*inference* from Lang's distinction).

**Rostrum move (the technique now in use).** "Ken Burns effect" was Apple's working title for iMovie's pan-and-zoom feature, shipped in 2003 with Burns's permission; the craft terms are pan and zoom on a still, rostrum move, push-in and pull-out, and the practice predates Burns in *City of Gold* (1957) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Burns_effect); [CineD](https://www.cined.com/?p=406363)). Write "slow push", "pull-out" or "pan to X" in scripts and keep "Ken Burns" for the family. Burns treats each photograph as a master shot containing long shots, close-ups, pans and reveals, insists that a person chooses the first and last frame of every move, and calls an unmotivated move "a zoom to nowhere" ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/?p=406363)). The standard motivated moves are a pan across a group that comes to rest on the person under discussion and a pull-out that reveals a second figure ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Burns_effect)). Sourced scale changes run from 2% to 30% ([Boinx forum](https://forum.boinx.com/t/setting-default-zoom-ratio-for-ken-burns-effect/8884); [LayerSlider docs](https://docs.webshopworks.com/layerslider/41-slide-settings/173-ken-burns-effect), S), and no source gives a speed rule. The failure on this project is editorial, not technical: one linear push per still, with the end frame not chosen for the sentence.

**Zoom-to-detail with stations.** No craft name exists beyond pan and zoom. The best published model is the New York Times "Close Read" series (2020), which walks one artwork through a sequence of framed details with a sentence per detail ([Tom Critchlow](https://tomcritchlow.com/2022/03/15/holding-scrolling-attention/), S). It is made by choosing three to five framings of one still and cutting or easing between them on the narration. It costs nothing, and the 4K upscales already supply the resolution: the tightest framing needs a source at least zoom factor × 1920 pixels wide (*inference*). It earns its place whenever a still contains more than one thing the narration names in turn, which is the case for the nine-figure row in aigen-06. Techniques that change framing also survive phone screens better than small internal motion (*inference*).

**Pose-to-pose sequence (the candidate called "sequence montage").** "Pose-to-pose" is a standard animation-planning term: draw the key poses first, then fill between them ([Toon Boom](https://learn.toonboom.com/modules/animation-principles/topic/straight-ahead-and-pose-to-pose-principle), S). The production tradition is limited animation, also called planned animation, which reuses frames, holds characters still and moves only a part ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_animation)). The film form is the still image film ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_image_film)), which *La Jetée* labels a photo-roman in its credits ([TCM](https://tcm.com/tcmdb/title/648488/wd), S). As an editing device on stills no single industry name exists; write "pose-to-pose sequence" for a figure changing pose inside one scene and "held pose" for the rest. It is made as three to five stills of one scene cut hard on sentence boundaries. The project's chain discipline is the method: one ChatGPT conversation per scene, frame 1 plus frame N−1 uploaded as anchors, camera position and the single change restated each time ([`CHAINED_STILL_LESSONS.md`](../project/CHAINED_STILL_LESSONS.md), P), or, more exactly, an edit of frame A composited back through a mask. Stills are free and an edit costs $0.03 to $0.08. Three failure modes apply. Independently generated stills drift, and OpenAI's own guide lists recurring-character consistency as a weak point ([OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). Still-image films draw the standing criticism that they are closer to a slideshow than to cinema ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_image_film)). And small wiggles added to a still tend to read as cheap, where a clean substitution of one strong pose for another does not (*inference* from a motion-comic review: [Comics Worth Reading](https://comicsworthreading.com/2009/03/01/watchmen-the-complete-motion-comic/), S·O). The episode's style block also forbids mid-stride and mid-gesture poses, so pose stills must be generated against that rule on purpose. It earns its place when the narration describes a sequence inside one scene. The closest high-craft precedents for a faceless flat figure style are UPA and Osamu Dezaki's held frames, not the photographic Ken Burns tradition (*inference*).

**State swap.** **No standard industry name exists.** The nearest named things are the [blink comparator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blink_comparator) in astronomy, the [flicker paradigm](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rensink/flicker/) in psychology, the [before/after slider](https://knightlab.northwestern.edu/2014/09/24/introducing-juxtaposejs-an-easy-way-to-compare-two-frames/index.html) in web journalism and [replacement animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppetoons) in stop motion (all S). "State swap" is a house term; use it, and write "pose swap" when a figure changes. It is made by editing frame A into frame B and compositing the edit back through a feathered mask so that everything else is pixel-identical, which is how the project made its empty-street keyframe. One pair already exists on disk: `stills/aigen-03-second-shopkeeper.png` and `keyframes/aigen-03-end-unwatched.png`. An edit costs $0.08 on fal's Nano Banana 2 endpoint ([fal schema](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/nano-banana-2/edit/llms.txt)). The firm rule comes from change-blindness research: cut or cross-fade directly between the two registered frames with nothing in between, because a blank of about 80 ms or more hides even a large change ([Rensink](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rensink/flicker/), S); and stay well under three large-area flashes per second, the accessibility threshold ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold), S). A dip through ink between the two states is therefore the wrong seam. The project's `match-cut-still` token, a 0.35-second opacity crossfade, was designed for this case and currently draws a cut. It earns its place when the argument is a difference, and this episode's thesis (watched versus unwatched) is one.

**Graphic match and match dissolve (the candidate called "match-cut pairs").** "Match cut" is the standard umbrella term. The sub-types are the graphic match (shape, colour or composition), the match on action, which is the same thing as cutting on action, and the sound bridge; a "match dissolve" is a graphic match executed as a dissolve ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/match-cuts-types-of-cuts/)). "Form cut" appears only in a vendor glossary ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/es/ai-glossary/match-cut), S·O), and "cut on shape" is not attested anywhere the researchers looked. The canon is the bone to the satellite in *2001*, the match to the sunrise in *Lawrence of Arabia* and the drain to the eye in *Psycho* ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/match-cuts-types-of-cuts/)). With AI stills it is made by generating still B with still A as the anchor so the shared shape sits in the same place, then cutting hard; no special transition is needed (*inference*). It costs nothing. The project's grammar names the failure: the two segments must share a real visual subject or the cut "reads as forced cleverness", and `match-cut-still` is "especially intolerant of composition drift" ([`TRANSITION_GRAMMAR.md`](../project/TRANSITION_GRAMMAR.md), P). The same file calls the match cut "the highest-leverage under-served transition in the channel", with zero production uses. It earns its place at analogy seams, where the bounded analogy is the argument.

**Montage.** The bare word is overloaded and should never appear alone in a script. Soviet montage makes meaning by colliding two shots ([Explaining History](https://explaininghistory.org/2025/11/19/the-politics-of-the-cut-how-soviet-montage-theory-revolutionized-cinema-and-challenged-hollywood/), S). The Hollywood montage sequence, identified with Slavko Vorkapich, compresses time with many short shots and optical effects ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavko_Vorkapich), S). Write "montage sequence" for time compression under one stretch of narration, "rapid-cut montage" when speed is the point and "juxtaposition cut" when two images collide (*inference*, suggested vocabulary). The pacing register sets a limit: a constructivist backdrop held for 2 to 3 seconds "reads as mood texture, not argument", acceptable in a rapid montage and not when the image has to deliver the beat's emotional meaning ([`PACING_REGISTER.md`](../project/PACING_REGISTER.md), P).

**Animatic.** The standard name is animatic, formerly "Leica reel": a storyboard given duration and sound, whose job is pacing ([Moonb](https://www.moonb.io/blog/how-to-make-an-animatic), S). YouTube's own trends reporting treats animatics as a finished format ([Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/distribution/youtube-animation-report-259897.html), S), and more than 4,000 fan videos for *EPIC: The Musical* were uploaded in the first half of 2025, hundreds of them animatics ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/09/youtube-culture-trends-original-animated-series-digital-circus-hazbin-hotel/)). Toon Boom's storyboard software gives each new panel one second by default ([Toon Boom docs](https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-25/storyboard/timing/about-timing.html), S). The useful part for Parallax is the method (time the stills to the recorded voice before polishing any of them), not the rough look. No canonical animatic-style essay channel was confirmed by a source.

**Faster transitions between illustrations or clips.** The project's grammar already answers this, and the evidence supports it. The default is a hard cut on a phrase boundary with narration leading and the incoming picture already moving. Illustration to illustration in the same scene is a straight cut, because "a dissolve would claim time has passed". A new scene or time takes a cut with a sound bridge or a 0.3 to 0.5 second dissolve. Wipes, iris, whip and zoom presets are out, and so are more than three transition types in one episode ([`TRANSITION_GRAMMAR.md`](../project/TRANSITION_GRAMMAR.md), P). No practitioner source gave frames of overlap for a match dissolve. The AI alternative, a first-and-last-frame clip used as the seam between two stills, is available on most video models, and PixVerse sells a dedicated two-image transition endpoint at $0.35 to $0.75 per five-second clip ([fal guide, March 2026](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/ai-image-to-video-generators)). The project's May tests are the warning: two independently generated frames are the worst case for interpolation, and "most 'morph' beats are dissolves in disguise" ([`CHAINED_STILL_LESSONS.md`](../project/CHAINED_STILL_LESSONS.md), P).

### More than one picture on screen: panels take turns and never peak together

**Names.** Strictly, a split screen has two images and anything more is a "multi-image screen" ([Kinolab](https://www.kinolab.org/browseTags.php?id=289), S). *American Cinematographer* in 1969 called it the "multiple-image technique" and its units "panels" ([ASC](https://theasc.com/article/multiple-image-technique-for-the-boston-strangler/)), and Pablo Ferro called them "multiples" ([Art of the Title](https://artofthetitle.com/title/the-thomas-crown-affair)). Picture-in-picture, or inset, is a small frame over a larger one ([Clueso glossary](https://www.clueso.io/glossary/picture-in-picture), S), and "Brady Bunch" is editors' slang, not a term ([WeVideo](https://blog.wevideo.com/news/the-brady-bunch-effect/), S). The suggested script vocabulary is "two-up", "three-up (triptych)", "four-up (quad)" and "inset" (*inference*).

**The rules, from the 1968 features.** *The Boston Strangler* is the best-documented case, and its makers' rules transfer almost directly to a build made from stills. Do not load one panel with too much or tell a complete story inside it. Never let two panels reach their climax at the same instant. Balance colour and density, because a brighter panel drags the eye. Keep the panel edge visible. Ask of every frame where the centre of interest is. One of the film's makers warned in the same article that when panels are animated around the frame for effect, the mechanics take over as the subject ([ASC](https://theasc.com/article/multiple-image-technique-for-the-boston-strangler/)). The technique fills about 35% of that film, with as many as 12 images at once by the ASC's account (a [rare-book listing](https://www.walterfilm.com/shop/film-category_all/crime-suspense-thriller/boston-strangler-the-1968-revised-second-draft-screenplay/) says seven, S), and panel shots usually ran one and a half to three feet of film, which is 1 to 2 seconds at 24 fps (*inference* from 16 frames per foot). *The Thomas Crown Affair* cut a fifteen-minute polo match to two minutes this way ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/split-screen-editing-and-composing/)). Multi-panel is the strongest time-compression device the research found, and Saul Bass came to think it suited abundance better than contemplation ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/split-screen-editing-and-composing/)).

**One moving element.** Three independent lines support letting only one panel change at a time: the animation principle of [staging](https://www.evl.uic.edu/ralph/508S99/staging.html), eye-tracking evidence that film viewers [look at the same place at the same time](https://doaj.org/article/ba3cabb1432346b98486af93cf52489e), and [tracking limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_object_tracking) of about four moving targets, falling to [one at high speed](https://visualcognition.psych.northwestern.edu/publications/AlvarezTracking2007.pdf) (all S). *Timecode* points with sound, raising the mix on whichever quadrant matters ([DVD Journal](https://www.dvdjournal.com/reviews/t/timecode.shtml), S), which implies that panel reveals should follow the order of the sentence (*inference*), in line with the channel's anticipatory-reveal rule. **No source gives a maximum number of simultaneously moving panels for video, a minimum panel size for phones, or a comprehension study of narrated multi-panel layouts.**

**Two-up and inset.** This is the standard split screen, already built as SplitFrame and used once. It has been used to show both ends of a call, reality beside desire, and simultaneity ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_screen_(video_production))). It costs nothing. The present use distinguishes Duty from Prudence by caption alone, on one identical still in both panes, which wastes the device.

**Four-up and the multi-panel build.** **No standard term exists for a frame that becomes two panels and then four**; practitioner sources say only that panels appear, multiply and change shape. Write "multi-panel build". The nearest film reference is *Hulk* (2003), where windows multiply, slide, and sometimes one expands to full frame ([Flicks](https://www.flicks.com.au/features/ang-lees-hulk-is-a-bad-movie-but-it-couldve-changed-cinema/), S); reviewers remain divided on whether those panels helped the film ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/reviews/the-hulk-20030620), S·O). The variant with AI video in each panel is made by generating four stills or states, animating at most one panel per moment, and assembling them in a code-controlled grid, which is what the project's 24 September analysis proposed. Stills are free, and each moving panel costs one clip, about $0.25 to $1. The failure modes are mostly reasoned, not measured. On an upright phone a quad gives panels of roughly 35 × 20 mm, enough for one bold flat shape and no in-panel text (*inference* by arithmetic). Because style drift scales with moving area, four moving panels multiply the exposure (*inference* from the October tests). The 28 px text floor and the safe area apply to every badge and caption ([`VISUAL_QA.md`](../project/VISUAL_QA.md), P). It earns its place when the argument is itself a grid, as with Duty and Prudence against watched and unwatched.

**Triptych.** Diptych, triptych and polyptych are standard art terms; a triptych is usually a dominant centre with two subordinate wings ([National Gallery](https://nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/glossary/triptych), S). "Polyvision" is Abel Gance's name for the three-screen finale of *Napoléon* (1927), where he used the panels as one panorama, as three contrasting actions and as mirrored sides framing a centre ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyvision); [Decent Films](https://decentfilms.com/reviews/napoleon1927), S). That gives a ready grammar for a bounded analogy: source case left, target case right, the claim or the break in the centre (*inference*). It costs three stills.

**Motion-comic panels.** "Motion comic" is the standard term, first used for Warner's *Watchmen: Motion Comic* (2008): panels expanded to full shots with voice, sound and limited movement on the original art ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_comic)). The form is criticised as cheesy when figures wiggle ([Comics Worth Reading](https://comicsworthreading.com/2009/03/01/watchmen-the-complete-motion-comic/), S·O) and for removing what comics rely on, the gap between panels and the reader's control of pace ([The Digital Review](https://thedigitalreview.com/issue01/timeframing/timeframing-script.pdf), S). McCloud's six panel-to-panel transitions (moment-to-moment, action-to-action, subject-to-subject, scene-to-scene, aspect-to-aspect, non-sequitur) are a usable vocabulary for what a cut between two stills is doing; they reached this report through secondary summaries only ([Middlebury MediaWiki](https://mediawiki.middlebury.edu/wiki/MIDDMedia/Closure_in_comics), S).

**Contact sheet or strip recap.** "Contact sheet" is a photography term for a same-size print of a whole roll ([Skylum](https://blog.skylum.com/contact-sheet-photography/), S), and it survives in post-production software as a review view ([Foundry Nuke docs](https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/16.0v1/content/timeline_environment/contact_sheet/contact_sheet_pickmode.html), S). **No citable source establishes "filmstrip recap" or "storyboard strip" as a named on-screen device**, and no named title sequence built on one was confirmed. The case for it is an inference from learning research: video content vanishes as new content arrives, which hurts novices unless it is segmented ([University of Reading briefing](https://research.reading.ac.uk/design-research-for-testing-diagnostics/wp-content/uploads/sites/178/2021/02/Research-Briefing_Instructional-videos_february-2021.pdf), S), and a layout that keeps earlier pictures on screen answers that directly. It costs nothing in generation. The repository's `build-contact-sheet.mjs` is a QA script, not a template.

### Motion inside a still: one technique has passed a test and the rest are unproven

**Pinned first and last frame.** Vendors call it first/last frame, start/end frame, tail image or keyframes; the generic craft phrase is keyframe-conditioned image-to-video. The project's September review warns against describing it as optical-flow interpolation ([`illustration-to-video-workflows-2026-09-24.md`](../analysis/illustration-to-video-workflows-2026-09-24.md), P). Reserve "morph" for a shot where the scene itself transforms. The recipe that worked: edit the start frame into the end frame, composite the edit through a feathered mask, write a prompt that leads with the camera state, names what moves and states in one sentence what does not change, then trim the first three frames, conform to 30 fps, hold the last frame and strip the audio. The six-second clip cost **$0.576 at a promotional rate**. On this art the documented failures are the frame-0 pop, a slow brightening, softer texture wherever something moves, and amplitude and speed words being ignored; the May tests add smearing of small countable details and failure on any zoom or dolly. It earns its place when the change is the argument, as when the street empties. It is the only AI-video technique with a passing test on this style, and that test is one take.

**Live still (AI subject micro-motion from one image).** "Live still" is the project's tag; the industry just says image-to-video, and the project's October research uses "living illustration" for the low-motion version. The four October tests are the evidence, and they are mixed: ambient wind animated the wrong thing and did not loop, a standing figure asked to walk glided, a turning head grew a nose ridge, and a two-figure pull held hands and faces but overshot until the start frame was widened and the prompt said what must not change. A five-second clip cost $0.48 at the promotional rate. Practitioners' keep rates for moving clips run from about 1 in 20 to 1 in 300 (see the reception section). It earns its place rarely: when one small region moving is the point of the shot and the drawn pose already implies the motion.

**Masked ambient loop (cinemagraph).** "Cinemagraph" was coined by Kevin Burg and Jamie Beck in 2011 for a still photograph in which one small movement repeats ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinemagraph)). Plotagraph and Flixel are products, not craft terms ([Photofocus](https://photofocus.com/software/creating-drifting-clouds-in-a-single-image-with-plotagraph-pro/); [Flixel](https://blog.flixel.com/trim-loop-crossfade-flixel-cinemagraph-pro-mac/), S). "Masked ambient loop" is the neutral name. Two routes exist. The procedural route clips a region of the still and drives a gradient, a glow or an opacity value from the frame number; it is deterministic and free, and the recipe is an untested sketch by the researcher. The video-sourced route needs a generated clip, freezes everything outside a mask and loops the rest. Loop type must match the motion: a bounce loop suits back-and-forth motion and a repeat loop suits one-way motion such as water or smoke ([Flixel](https://blog.flixel.com/trim-loop-crossfade-flixel-cinemagraph-pro-mac/), S). No hosted flagship video model documents a loop switch. The options are Midjourney's loop setting ([Midjourney updates](https://updates.midjourney.com/looping-and-end-frame-for-video-video-in-the-discord-bot/)), the loop flag on fal's RIFE interpolation endpoint ([fal schema](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/rife/video/llms.txt)), or setting the start frame equal to the end frame, which nobody has tested. The project's nearest test produced a single gust that does not loop, with a pop and a brightening. Further risks are reasoned: displacing a flat colour field shows nothing because only edges and stripes move; steam and dust sit close to the doctrine's "particles on light substrate" anti-pattern; and small internal motion can fall below what a phone shows (all *inference*). The stills were also generated by rule with nothing flickering. It earns its place as mood during narration-heavy holds, on one element per shot.

**Line boil.** "Line boil" (also boil or wiggle) is the standard term for the shimmer that appears when successive frames do not line up exactly ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/wiggle-text-line-boil/)). Squigglevision is one studio's method, a loop of five slightly different drawings ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squigglevision)). It is made either in code, with a displacement filter and a stepped seed, or by cycling three to five re-renders of one still. PremiumBeat's rates of 4 to 12 changes per second correspond to holding each variant for three or four frames at 30 fps (*inference*). Both routes cost nothing. The risks on flat art are reasoned: with large flat blocks the whole silhouette shimmers, and after YouTube compression a fine boil reads as codec noise (*inference*). The re-render route has a second problem: AI re-renders differ everywhere, not only at the edges (*inference*). The project has no doctrine on a deliberate boil, and its October pass criteria list "texture crawl" as a failure. If it is ever used, it is a texture choice made once per episode on one hero element, not per shot.

**Region-only AI motion.** Kling's Motion Brush and Runway's Multi-Motion Brush are described by resellers and reviewers and were not confirmed on the makers' pages ([Atlas Cloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/guides/kling-ai-motion-control), 3P). Wan VACE does masked video inpainting and is hosted on fal ([fal](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/wan-vace)), with one anecdotal user report of corruption outside the mask ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tezktt/snapshots/2026-05-22T22%3A42%3A24.48386Z), 3P). The more promising route is the project's own untried idea: composite only the moving region of a generated clip back over the original still, which follows from the finding that still regions keep their texture and from the feathered-mask method already used on a keyframe (*inference*, not tried).

**Mesh warp.** "Puppet tool" is Adobe's name for pin-driven mesh warping ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/animate-with-puppet-tools/animating-puppet-tools.html), S). It bends straight constructivist edges into rubber, which suits cloth and smoke and is wrong for rigid block figures and buildings (*inference*). No CSS equivalent exists. Leave it out.

### Depth and travel: author the layers and never estimate them

**Layered parallax (multiplane, 2.5D).** Multiplane names the rig, from Lotte Reiniger's 1923 glass layers to Disney's seven-plane camera of 1937, and parallax names the optical result ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplane_camera)). "2.5D parallax" is the motion-design term, nicknamed after *The Kid Stays in the Picture* (2002), whose directors split photographs into planes and blocked each like a set ([Documentary magazine](https://www.documentary.org/feature/kid-stays-picture-robert-evans-world-and-welcome-it)). The hand method on photographs is to cut each plane to its own layer, overlap the layers, fill the holes behind each cut, push the layers apart in depth and keyframe a small camera move, with at least three clearly separated layers ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/animating-parallax-images-in-after-effects-and-apple-motion/)). On flat art the stronger method is to author the layers and not derive them: a background plate without figures, a mid-ground, and the figures on transparent backgrounds (*inference*). The tools exist. OpenAI's 2.5 image models accept a transparent background ([OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)), Recraft removes a background for $0.01 ([Recraft pricing](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/pricing)), and Qwen-Image-Layered splits one image into 1 to 10 RGBA layers for $0.05 on fal ([fal schema](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/qwen-image-layered/llms.txt)). No source gives a percent-of-parallax standard. A defensible starting band is that the farthest layer moves one third to two thirds as far as the nearest (*inference* from a CSS perspective formula and one low-authority code snippet).

The failure modes are where this technique is least proven. ProVideo's image-selection rule is to avoid flat colour because each layer needs texture for its movement to show, so on this art depth has to come from overlapping silhouettes and edges (*inference*). A March 2026 paper argues that segmentation-style layer models do not match how illustrations are layered ([arXiv 2603.14925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14925v2)), and neither hosted layer model has been tried on faceted illustration. The project's one comparable test, a generated plate with a generated cut-out moved by code, was rejected by the creator on taste. Doctrine is split (see the doctrine section). It earns its place as punctuation for an arrival, at most twice per episode by the project's own cap.

**Depth-map parallax.** This is the family to avoid. Its names are product and paper names: "3D photo", LeiaPix or Immersity ([Immersity tutorial](https://leiapix.com/tutorial/how-to-use-leiapix-features), S), and "3D Ken Burns", the title of a 2019 Adobe Research paper ([arXiv 1909.05483](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05483v1), S). Adobe Research reported in November 2025 that monocular depth models are in effect trained to ignore flat media and get layer order wrong in illustrations, and proposed predicting a discrete layer index instead ([Illustrator's Depth](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17454v1)). That method is a paper with no hosted endpoint. No source tested any depth tool on flat vector-style illustration specifically, so the failure claim rests on the research papers and on the tools' own artefact documentation. The project's blacklist of family E stands on that basis.

**Panorama travel with stations.** No established motion-design term was found; "tableau pan" and "scroll-painting move" did not turn up as terms of art. The nearest documented craft language is scrollytelling's "panning and zooming" ([Oesch et al., Information Design Journal](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/idj.22005.oes), S) and the museums' "painting animation". The reference works are the National Palace Museum's animated handscrolls and the animated Qingming scroll at Shanghai Expo 2010 ([National Palace Museum](https://theme.npm.edu.tw/exh108/npmweiwuying/en/page-3.html), S). What separates them from a long slow pan is copyable (*inference*): the picture is composed as a sequence of scenes, time or state changes along the route, and the travel stops at stations where something is pointed out. A very wide still is one call on fal's Nano Banana 2 edit endpoint, which accepts 21:9, 4:1 and 8:1 ([fal schema](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/nano-banana-2/edit/llms.txt)), at $0.08 to $0.16; OpenAI's limit is 3:1 with no edge above 3,840 pixels ([OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). Remotion's `<Img>` inherits Chrome's maximum image size of 2^29 pixels ([Remotion docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/img)). `render_kenburns.py` cannot pan, so this needs the in-Remotion route.

**Push-through and zoom-through.** **No primary source was found** for doorway or portal transitions: no canonical example, timing or craft rule. The recipe in the notes is the researcher's unsourced sketch (scale the outgoing picture about the portal while the incoming picture grows from portal size, with scale exponential in time for constant perceived speed). It needs artwork drawn with a portal and enough resolution at the portal. The project's grammar lists `spatial-zoom` as deprecated and "zoom or whip presets" under "never", so a push-through needs an explicit ruling before anyone builds it.

### Graphics on the picture: the annotation layer carries the argument

**Annotation layer (annotated illustration).** "Annotation layer" is the newsroom term; Amanda Cox, then a New York Times graphics editor, is quoted second-hand as calling it the most important thing the desk does ([EU data visualisation guide](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/visual-annotations-introduction)). The parts have standard names: a note, a connector (line, elbow or curve), a connector end (arrow or dot) and a subject shape (circle, rectangle, threshold, badge) ([d3-annotation](https://d3-annotation.susielu.com/)). "Telestrator" was a device and is now a verb ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telestrator), S). It is made as an SVG overlay in image coordinates, with lines drawn on by stroke-dash animation and labels faded in. A spotlight, which dims everything but the subject, uses a mask hole. It costs nothing. Two project rules govern it: exact numbers, labels and source cues belong to code, and "if a label only works after several coordinate nudges over unpredictable artwork, change the layout or the image" ([`image-remotion-synergy-2026-09-23.md`](../analysis/image-remotion-synergy-2026-09-23.md), P). The reasoned risk on flat art is contrast, since saturated blocks compete with thin leader lines, and dimming the still solves it (*inference*); the QA contrast check is also weaker over busy images ([`VISUAL_QA.md`](../project/VISUAL_QA.md), P). Of all the techniques here it earns the most screen time, because it carries argument and not mood (*inference*). It also turns "show a complete picture, then talk over it" into a picture that builds with the voice.

**Pictogram nodes (spot illustrations as diagram nodes).** The named system is Isotype, by Otto Neurath and Gerd Arntz: flat pictograms with no perspective, where a larger quantity is shown by repeating a same-sized pictogram and never by enlarging one ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotype_(picture_language)), S). It is made by generating spot illustrations on transparent backgrounds and placing them as images in the existing diagram templates. Recraft generates native SVG for $0.08 and vectorises a raster for $0.01 ([Recraft pricing](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/pricing)). Two project findings cut against it. The asset register's rule 5 warns against literal symbol-icons, and the September SVG test was rejected because the style fell from a rich constructivist image to "generic triangle trees". Consistency across a set of spot illustrations is also unmeasured: no vendor documents multi-panel sheets or turnarounds as a feature, and the consistency figures quoted by third-party guides have no traceable method ([Invideo](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-create-a-character-reference-sheet-using-nano/), 3P). FrameworkDiagram's schema has an `icon` string whose meaning the audit did not check, and NetworkDiagram has no image field.

**Cut-out animation.** This is a standard term with a long flat-art history: Reiniger's silhouettes, Terry Gilliam's collage interstitials, the *South Park* pilot ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutout_animation)). Duik and Joysticks 'n Sliders are tool names ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/joystick-n-sliders), S). In code it is one image per body part with the transform origin at the joint, nested so the DOM hierarchy is the bone hierarchy, and rotations of a few degrees. It is native to flat art in principle, because faceless block figures split cleanly at the joints (*inference*). In practice the project tested the idea once and the creator rejected the motion, recorded as "taste, not a model limit". Gilliam's own test is the right one: the technique matters little next to the story, joke or idea it serves ([Make](https://makezine.com/article/craft/how-to_cut-out_animation_with/)).

**Integrated type and type over a held picture.** "Integrated typography" is the title-design idea, and David Fincher's question for the *Panic Room* titles is the test: does the type look projected onto the shot or physically present in it ([Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/panic-room/), S). In code it is text under a `matrix3d()` transform solved from the four corners of a wall or sign with a multiply blend (untested sketch). Text inside generated stills remains unreliable by OpenAI's own account, so type stays in code. The project already has the cheaper form, hold-under, where a quotation is typed over the previous picture held and dimmed; it works for KineticTypography only. ### Supporting tools are cheap; depth on flat art is the weak link

Frame interpolation with a loop flag costs $0.0013 per compute second on fal's RIFE endpoint ([fal schema](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/rife/video/llms.txt)). Background removal costs $0.01 at Recraft ([Recraft pricing](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/pricing)). Runway offers SAM 3 segmentation to transparent WebM and ProRes 4444 export ([Runway changelog](https://runway.com/changelog)). For parallax, the practical choice in October 2026 is a layer model, which also repaints occluded background, over a depth map plus displacement (*inference*). Whether interpolating a 24 fps clip to 30 fps introduces edge artefacts on this art is untested.

### What remains unreliable, and what nobody has measured

By the vendors' own admission and by test, the weak points are text placement, recurring-character consistency, layout and mask precision in images ([OpenAI guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)); physical plausibility of complex motion and stability with several interacting subjects in video ([ByteDance](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/one-take-creation-flexible-referencing-introducing-seedance-2-5)); and obedience to amplitude and speed words (P). A hands-on review of H3 reports texture instability and multi-shot continuity that breaks more often than Seedance's ([Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/minimaxh3-review), 3P). The first-frame colour shift is reported outside H3 as well, which suggests a general property of latent video models and argues for trimming two or three frames and colour-matching on every model until a test shows otherwise (*inference* from one anecdotal report).

**No published, controlled test of flat-illustration image-to-video exists for any current model.** Leaderboards do not measure style retention, reviews test photoreal and anime inputs, and praise for anime does not transfer automatically, because anime has outlines and trained-in conventions that faceted shapes without outlines lack (*inference*). The only direct evidence is the project's six clips on one model.

Two legal notes belong with the tool choice. fal states that H3 Max output may be used commercially ([fal guide](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/how-to-use-minimax-h3-max)), while the H3 open weights carry a licence whose territory excludes the United States and European Union ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/minimax-ships-h3-weights-with-the-us-and-eu-excluded/), 3P), so the hosted route is the safe one. The project's notes also record that MiniMax lost a motion to dismiss in Disney's suit on 22 May 2026 and advise against depending on it alone ([`ai-video-landscape-2026-10-10.md`](../analysis/ai-video-landscape-2026-10-10.md), P; underlying page: [Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/disney-enterprises-inc-v-minimax)).

## Reception and platform rules reward visible authorship

### The praised examples are AI-native storytellers, not essay channels

The verifiable, well-received users of AI imagery in narrated video are almost all AI-native creators. Neural Viz (Josh Wallace Kerrigan) writes a full script, storyboards every shot, renders one still per panel, maps his own performance onto characters and cuts conventionally; he unifies the look with a degraded broadcast grade and lists his tools on every post ([The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/neural-viz-ai-tv-universe/)). The Archive In Between pairs human-written narration with AI imagery generated in volume and culled, and lives on reader funding; it is the closest verified analogue to human narration over AI stills ([The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/how-ai-creators-make-videos/)). Alan Warburton's "The Wizard of AI" (2023) is the canonical praised visual essay: about 20 minutes, almost wholly AI-generated, assembled in After Effects, disclosed, with the AI look intentional because AI is the subject ([ODI](https://culture.theodi.org/the-wizard-of-ai/)). Raoul Peck cut an openly unreal AI sequence against real clips in *Orwell: 2+2=5* and reports that audiences laugh at it ([IDA Documentary](https://documentary.org/column/digital-title-synthesis-raoul-peck-using-ai-critique-authoritarianism-orwell-225)).

The criticised examples cluster elsewhere: photoreal fake history such as "On This Day… 1776" ([The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/on-this-day-1776-trailer-darren-aronofskys-ai-animated-revolution-series-drops-and-the-backlash-arrives-right-on-schedule-20260130/)), undisclosed images in *What Jennifer Did* ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/netflix-doc-what-jennifer-did-uses-ai-images-to-create-false-historical-record/)), and mass-produced narrated history ([Slashdot summary of 404 Media](https://news.slashdot.org/story/25/09/03/2028206/ai-generated-boring-history-videos-are-flooding-youtube-drowning-out-real-history)). The first wave of AI still slideshows drew millions of views from December 2022 and faded within about two months ([Tuber's Digest](https://tubersdigest.substack.com/p/a-brief-history-of-ai-videos-on-youtube)).

Three absences are findings. The researcher **could not verify a single established narrated-essay channel that openly builds its picture from AI stills**; targeted searches on eleven named channels found nothing citable, which is absence of indexed evidence and not proof. No write-up was found of an essay channel using split screens, panel grids, cut-out or multiplane with AI stills. And no documented workflow exists for a flat or constructivist style; every detailed account is for cinematic, horror or character-comedy looks. The project's own October research reached the same conclusion: no essay or philosophy channel was found documented as using AI animation well.

### Practitioners budget stills generously and clips sparingly

Documented pipelines converge on script, storyboard, one still per panel, selective image-to-video and a conventional edit. Self-reported discard ratios for moving clips are steep: 300 to 400 generations for 15 usable clips on the Kalshi ad ([Public Gaming](https://www.publicgaming.com/news-categories/advertising/14510-heres-the-2-000-fully-ai-generated-ad-that-aired-during-the-nba-finals)), about 1,000 clips for a three-minute Wall Street Journal film ([BGR](https://www.bgr.com/tech/you-wont-believe-this-movie-was-made-with-ai/)), and a 300:1 ratio on "Air Head" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/turns-out-the-viral-air-head-sora-video-wasnt-purely-the-work-of-ai-we-were-led-to-believe)). Neural Viz reports about 12 hours and roughly $100 a month in subscriptions for a two-to-three-minute episode ([The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/neural-viz-ai-tv-universe/)), which is 4 to 6 hours per finished minute (*inference*); Kurzgesagt reports about 1,200 hours per hand-made video ([LingQ transcript](https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-english-online/courses/689474/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-120-4887128/)). The still is the unit of consistency and the clip is a selective upgrade (*inference*), which matches the project's own ratio of six clips generated to one used. The Archival Producers Alliance recommends a cue sheet recording the prompt, software version, date and timecode of every generative element ([APA best practices](https://www.mishcon.com/download/apa-genai-best-practices)), a record that doubles as an appeal file if a classifier misfires (*inference*).

### Suspicion of AI lowers trust, and the flat explainer look now attracts it

The quantitative evidence is on text, brand video, news and static art, not on stylised illustration in long-form video; no study tests that case. Within those limits it is consistent. In a 3,000-person US survey, content that people suspected was AI-generated was rated 48% less trustworthy, and the penalty applied even to human-written content believed to be AI ([Raptive](https://raptive.com/blog/the-ai-stink-is-real-and-its-costing-brands/)). Identical paintings were rated lower when labelled AI-made, with perceived effort and narrative moderating the effect ([PsyPost on Bellaiche et al.](https://www.psypost.org/2023/08/new-psychology-research-reveals-why-people-prefer-human-created-artwork-to-ai-created-artwork-167429)). Across thirteen experiments, people who disclosed AI use were trusted less than those who did not ([Schilke and Reimann, 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v188y2025ics0749597825000172.html)). Yet every documented backlash that ended a project or forced an apology involved undisclosed or discovered AI, so disclosure is a cost paid to avoid a larger one (*inference*).

The incident closest to Parallax happened in July 2026. Kurzgesagt, whose videos are human-made, said YouTube's automated detection had classified its work as AI slop; the affected video was the channel's worst performer since 2013 despite above-average click-through and watch time ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/youtube-mistakenly-penalizes-popular-science-channel-kurzgesagt-for-ai-generated-slop-2000722702)). One trade site's explanation, its inference and not YouTube's, is that a flat, clean vector style with a calm narrator is the look slop channels copy ([Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/kurzgesagt-says-youtube-mistook-its-animation-for-ai-slop/)). The practical reading follows from the moderators in the painting study: authored structure (layout, annotation, a distinct recurring visual language) and a human voice signal effort, and a slow push over a generic still signals its absence (*inference*). AI as the essay's subject also gives licence, since the two best-received essay and documentary uses both use AI imagery to talk about AI and keep it visibly unreal (*inference*). That is this episode's situation.

### YouTube exempts stylised animation; the exposure is the slideshow rule and its classifier

YouTube requires disclosure for realistic altered or synthetic content and does not require it for clearly unrealistic content, including AI-generated animation, or for production help ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Since May 2026 YouTube applies labels automatically when its systems detect significant photorealistic AI use, keeps the label for animated content in the expanded description, and states that a label alone changes neither recommendation nor monetisation ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/)). Labels triggered by C2PA metadata cannot be removed by the creator ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). OpenAI images carry C2PA metadata and SynthID watermarks, according to a help article the researcher could read only as a title and summary ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/my-mm/articles/8912793-c2pa-and-synthid-in-openai-generated-images), S), and Google's carry SynthID ([Gemini image docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)). The expectation that a Remotion render does not carry a source still's C2PA manifest into the MP4 is an engineering inference that no source confirms, and no evidence was found that YouTube reads SynthID.

The real exposure is the monetisation policy renamed "inauthentic content" on 15 July 2025. It lists image slideshows and templated AI content that add little narrative, commentary or educational value as ineligible ([YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). An original 11-minute essay with human narration and bespoke graphics sits well inside the eligible examples, so the risk is the opaque classifier and not the rule (*inference*). Two specific moves change the picture: animating an archival photograph with image-to-video is what the Archival Producers Alliance warns against, and on YouTube it also turns an exempt stylised film into one that needs the disclosure (*inference*). The EU AI Act's transparency obligations applied from 2 August 2026 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content)); whether they bind a US solo creator's stylised illustration was not resolved, and the detailed readings come from law-firm commentary that was not checked against the Regulation.

Guidance on mixing media is consistent and comes from documentary practice by analogy: keep primary sources in their original form, signal generated material with a repeatable visual vocabulary, and make a change of medium an event ([APA best practices](https://www.mishcon.com/download/apa-genai-best-practices)). For this episode that means two registers that never blend: the Volkswagen photographs and the EPA notice shown as documents, and illustration that is unmistakably illustration (*inference*). The project already records the same practice in its own words.

## Every technique builds at Remotion 4.0.457; the blockers are in project plumbing

### What is installed and what the pin lacks

`remotion-templates/package.json` pins `remotion` at 4.0.457 with `@remotion/noise`, `@remotion/three`, `@remotion/paths`, `@remotion/shapes` and `@remotion/transitions` installed. `@remotion/effects` is not installed; it needs 4.0.464, and its `noiseDisplacement()` needs 4.0.474 ([Remotion docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/effects)). Remotion 5.0 was unreleased when checked ([v5 migration](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/5-0-migration)). One rule governs every recipe: all animation must be a pure function of `useCurrentFrame()`, with no CSS animations, no state carried between frames and no randomness other than Remotion's seeded `random()`; the same page advises against CSS `background-image` and `mask-image` because Remotion cannot know when they have loaded ([Remotion docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/flickering)).

Four implementation facts matter more than any recipe. First, `<OffthreadVideo>` is the conservative choice for a grid because it is frame-exact and Chrome does not throttle it, but it has no `loop` prop and holds its last frame; `@remotion/media`'s `<Video>` loops natively and renders fastest, and at the pin it lacks premounting and crop props ([Video vs OffthreadVideo](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/video-vs-offthreadvideo)). Second, render cost for a grid scales with tile count times source resolution; that is reasoning, not measurement, and no Remotion benchmark for N simultaneous videos was found. Third, every `TransitionSeries` transition shortens the timeline by its own length ([TransitionSeries](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transitions/transitionseries)), which a narration-locked essay cannot afford, so use plain cuts or the Overlay. Fourth, premounting has no effect at render time, so it improves Studio scrubbing only ([Premounting](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/player/premounting)). The render-time cost of full-frame SVG filters at 1080p was not found and needs a local timing test before a long segment depends on one.

The upgrade is not required for anything in the first-try list. What stands in the way is local and specific. The FOOTAGE path passes no trim, rate or loop. Moves are baked by ffmpeg, so each new crop means another hand-rendered file. Three annotation templates and PhotoMontage tint the still. Hold-under serves one template. The match-cut tokens draw a cut.

One further fact was checked for this report and is not in the research notes. The re-time tool moves segment boundaries and scales sound cues inside a segment, and its own report states that reveal timings inside a graphic are not moved by a re-time ([`retime_manifest.py`](../tools/assembly/retime_manifest.py), P). Narration is not yet recorded. A pose-to-pose sequence wired as consecutive manifest segments, each with its own narration reference, will therefore re-time cleanly, and one built as a single template with internal cue times will need checking by hand against the recording.

## Conclusion

The constraint that shaped limited animation has inverted. Studios in 1963 rationed drawings and spent craft on making a held pose acceptable; Parallax's drawings are free, and the scarce things are registration between two versions of a frame and evidence that a person arranged what is on screen. That makes edit-then-composite the core skill of this pipeline, and it is the same skill that produced the only AI clip good enough to use. The still techniques and the video technique recommended here are one move seen twice: fix the picture first, then ask the next tool for less. The practical unit of work stops being "a still and its zoom" and becomes "a frame and its states".

The largest unknown is also the cheapest to close. Nobody has published a test of any of these techniques on flat, faceless, constructivist art, and each test here costs between nothing and a dollar, with a QA report that already measures the result. The findings are worth more to the project than to anyone else, which argues for writing them into one current doctrine page that supersedes the May documents, with each prompt rule tied to its model and date, a ruling on layered parallax, and a rule on how much of an episode a single-move still may carry.
