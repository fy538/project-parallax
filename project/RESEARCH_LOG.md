## 1. Professor Jiang Xueqin Analysis

### Who He Is
- Chinese-Canadian educator, Yale undergrad, teaches at Moonshot Academy (high school in Beijing — NOT a professor)
- YouTube channel "Predictive History," 2M+ subscribers
- Uses structural historical analysis, game theory, and Asimov's "psychohistory" concept

### Why He Exploded
1. **Prediction-confirmation loop:** May 2024 "Iran Trap" lecture predicted Trump win, US-Iran war, US loss. First two came true. Video went from handful of views to 680K+. Gained 100K subs in 3 days.
2. **Psychological moment:** In chaotic geopolitical times, audiences crave narrative coherence. Jiang offers the structure that events are patterned and comprehensible.
3. **Cross-cultural arbitrage:** Chinese face, flawless American English, Yale credential, analyzing US decline from Beijing = inherently provocative and shareable from any political angle.
4. **Controversy flywheel:** Criticism generates more searches/views/debate.

### Controversies
- Weaves in Illuminati, Freemason, "Pax Judaica" conspiracy theories
- Critics argue he functions as CCP propaganda asset ("East Rising, West Declining" narrative)
- Mehdi Hasan interview (March 2026) pressed him on antisemitic undertones
- "Professor" title is misleading — he's a high school teacher
- Predictions rely on selective analogies and undemonstrated game theory

### Lessons for Our Project
- **Take:** Market exists for cross-domain pattern recognition content. Enormous appetite.
- **Avoid:** Hard predictions (fragile), conspiracy territory, declarative certainty, unearned academic credentials
- **Our edge:** Actual formal analytical frameworks (Bayesian, game theory) demonstrated visibly, not just name-dropped

## 3. Visual Strategy Research (CRITICAL FINDINGS)

### The Five Visual Styles in This Space
1. **Minimalist 2D (Kurzgesagt):** Clean vectors, bold colors. Signals "educational." 24M subs.
2. **Comic/Cartoon (OverSimplified):** Character-driven humor. 8M+ subs. Too comedic for our tone.
3. **3D Simulation (AiTelly/Kings and Generals):** Realistic environments. Labor-intensive. Signals "military/tactical."
4. **Map/Data Driven (CaspianReport/PolyMatter):** Animated maps, motion graphics, stock footage. The default in geopolitics.
5. **Cinematic AI Illustration (emerging):** AI-generated painterly images with subtle animation.

### Evidence-Based Verdict: AI Illustration is NOT Viable as Primary Visual

**Key data points:**
- Consumer trust drops ~50% when content is perceived as AI-generated
- Only 26% of consumers prefer AI-generated content (down from 60% in 2023)
- 70% lower audience retention for AI-heavy content vs human-fronted
- No successful educational channel found using AI illustration as primary visual
- AI illustration in geopolitics space is associated with disinformation networks (DFRLab found 20+ channels with 2B views mixing real news with fabricated events)
- YouTube terminated 16 major AI channels (35M combined subs) in Jan 2026
- "AI slop" was Merriam-Webster's 2025 Word of the Year

**What IS working:**
- Clean motion graphics + animated maps + stock footage (Wendover, PolyMatter, CaspianReport)
- Narrative-first, visuals-second approach (every successful channel)
- Vibrant illustration + data-driven content hybrid (Kurzgesagt)
- Educational content gets 42% retention — highest of any YouTube category — driven by storytelling quality, not visual spectacle

### Revised Visual Recommendation
- **Core:** Clean motion graphics, animated maps, data visualizations, stock footage integration
- **AI role:** Use as compositing tool (reference art, backgrounds, textures) — never raw AI output as final visual
- **Three-mode grammar:** Historical segments (warm tones), philosophical/analytical segments (clean/cool), contemporary segments (high-contrast modern)
- **Consistency:** Reusable templates, standardized color palette, recurring visual motifs

## 4. TTS/Narration Research

### Current State of TTS (2026)
- ElevenLabs v3 (Expressive) is market leader: natural pauses, breathing, emotion, zero tone drift
- Other strong options: Fish Audio (best value), Murf AI, Inworld TTS
- Voice cloning: can create custom voice from short sample, nearly indistinguishable from original speaker
- YouTube explicitly allows AI voiceovers if content meets originality/authenticity standards

### Evidence Against Pure AI Narration
- 35% viewer drop-off within 45 seconds for AI narration vs human
- Stock AI voices (e.g., ElevenLabs "Josh") increasingly recognizable — triggers "AI content" perception
- For authority-dependent content (philosophy, analysis), human voice carries trust signals AI doesn't replicate well

### Decision: Narrate Yourself
- Human narration is strongly supported by evidence for this content type
- Voice cloning remains an option for scaling later (clone your own voice, not a stock voice)
- Batch recording (3 episodes in one sitting) minimizes time investment
- Side benefit: improves speaking skills for future consulting/workshops

## 5. YouTube Policy on AI Content (2025-2026)

### Disclosure Requirements
- Mandatory since May 2025: must disclose "meaningfully altered or synthetically generated content" that appears realistic
- **Properly labeled AI content receives normal algorithmic distribution** — disclosure doesn't limit reach
- Undisclosed AI content faces reduced recommendations or removal from Partner Program

### Monetization
- July 2025: "inauthentic content" = mass-produced, template-driven, minimal human creative input
- AI-assisted content (research, thumbnails, audio cleanup, editing) IS allowed if meaningful human involvement

- Scripting: AI-assisted research/drafting, human-rewritten final scripts — no disclosure needed
- Narration: Human (Tiger) — no disclosure needed
- Visuals: AI-generated reference material composited into human-directed motion graphics — no disclosure needed as long as final output reflects human creative decisions

## 7. Growth Strategy Research (0 to 1K Subscribers)

### Timeline Expectations
- Average: 254 days (8-9 months) to 1K subscribers
- Optimistic: 4-6 months with aggressive optimization
- New geopolitics channels average ~4,066 subscribers/month (but high variance)
- First 15-20 videos are "practice" — expect lower metrics

### Key Strategies
1. **Shorts + long-form combined:** 41% faster growth than single-format. Shorts for discovery, long-form for conversion.
2. **70/30 evergreen/trending split:** Evergreen builds lasting library; trending catches algorithmic momentum.
3. **Cross-platform:** TikTok (2.8-3.15% engagement) -> YouTube Shorts -> YouTube long-form funnel.
4. **Reddit:** Engage genuinely in r/geopolitics, r/AskHistorians, etc. 9:1 participation-to-promotion ratio.
5. **Audio quality is non-negotiable:** 23% of viewers never return to channels with poor audio.
6. **Consistent schedule:** Inconsistent posting = 67% slower growth.

### Biggest Mistakes to Avoid
- Random/unfocused content (algorithm can't categorize you)
- Poor audio quality (more important than video quality)
- Not analyzing retention/drop-off data
- Ignoring Shorts funnel
- Expecting results too fast (first 15-20 videos are practice)

### First Video Strategy
- **Recommended approach:** Timely + evergreen hybrid
- Lead with current event hook (catches search traffic), explain deep historical pattern (provides lasting value)
- Target length: 10-13 minutes
- 60%+ audience retention is the key metric to optimize for
- First 8 seconds are make-or-break (55% of viewers drop in first 60 seconds)

### Subscription Triggers (Psychology)
- Future value signal ("this channel will teach me things I want to know")

- Trust in clarity ("I understand what this creator does")
- Explicit subscribe ask after delivering best insight (~70% through video)

## 10. Three Critical Content Gaps (Claude Research, April 2026)

### Gap 1: Philosophical/Analytical Frameworks Applied to Geopolitics
No established channel systematically applies Stoicism, Bayesian thinking, game theory, complex systems theory, or epistemological frameworks to current events. Jiang Xueqin's viral explosion (8M+ views from Tucker Carlson interview) proves massive demand. William Spaniel's surging growth with academic game theory confirms from a different angle. But Jiang is discredited by conspiracy theories; Spaniel is narrowly academic. The credible, rigorous, multi-framework version doesn't exist yet.

### Gap 2: Bilingual Content That Bridges Rather Than Translates
China-focused content is aggressively polarized: pro-CCP voices (often state-supported) vs anti-CCP channels (SerpentZA, Laowhy86). No prominent creator provides genuinely balanced, bilingual analysis synthesizing Western and Chinese analytical perspectives. Academic literature calls for "cross-civilizational negotiation between Western and Chinese epistemic perspectives" — incorporating Tianxia and Yin-yang dialectics alongside Western IR theory — but this exists only in journals, not accessible video.

### Gap 3: Epistemological Self-Awareness
Existing content routinely criticized for oversimplification and determinism (Zeihan's China-collapse predictions since 2010, CaspianReport as "too deterministic"). No channel teaches viewers how to evaluate geopolitical claims, recognize cognitive biases, or assess source quality. Tetlock's superforecasting literature has massive academic credibility but minimal YouTube presence.

### What Existing Content Does Poorly (Community Feedback)
- Too surface-level, too clickbait-driven
- Too Western-centric in framing
- Too reliant on geographic determinism
- Lacks explicit uncertainty modeling
- Lacks multiple analytical frameworks presented simultaneously

## 12. Chained Start+End-Frame Video Generation (May 2026 Field Report)

### Source
Full report: [`./research/2026-05-chained-video-generation.md`](./research/2026-05-chained-video-generation.md) — Claude Deep Research, May 2026. Triggered by the prisoners-dilemma "scattered postcard" problem and the broader pipeline question of how to plan multi-still continuous-feeling scenes from script-draft onward.

### Tool stack recommendation
- **Off-the-shelf for chained editorial illustration:** Vidu Q1 (7-image reference conditioning, anime/2D-tuned, ~$0.14 per 5s 1080p clip) + Kling 3.0 (10s start+end frame, best-in-class style preservation in published benchmarks).
- **Deep style lock (high-investment):** LTX-2 with custom LoRA fine-tuned on Parallax style sheet. Only path that genuinely solves drift rather than mitigating it.
- **Stills pipeline for chains:** Recraft V3 Style Lock as the style-anchor source → Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image, accepts up to 14 reference images) for the chain itself. Closest thing to a battle-tested multi-frame consistency pipeline as of May 2026.
- **Avoid for this use case:** Runway Gen-4 (no first/last frame on Gen-4; use Gen-3 Alpha Turbo if Runway), Sora 2 (start-only, no native last-frame, biased hard to photorealism).

### Channel survey finding (the unsettling one)
**No current YouTube essay channel sustains atmospheric illustrated time the way Parallax wants to.** Adam Curtis is the closest practitioner of long unbroken atmospheric stretches, but he uses archive footage, not generative illustration. Kurzgesagt rejects the postcard-vs-scene tradeoff by tying every frame to a mechanical exposition. Vox/Wendover/Polymatter/Real Life Lore/Johnny Harris all cap atmospheric stretches at 8–15s. **Parallax is in mostly uncharted territory.** Closest applicable rule (from Curtis): a sequence earns long breathing time when its purpose is emotional/inferential rather than informational.

### Pacing rule (synthesized — no single source for this exact question)
Atmospheric illustrated stretches up to ~60 seconds are defensible IF:
1. Narration continuously carries analytical content over them (the Curtis model — atmosphere amplifies argument, doesn't replace it).
2. Something visually changes every ~15–20s within the stretch (camera move, figure entry, palette shift).
3. The stretch ends on analytical re-engagement, not a fade.

Stretches over 60s without one of those guardrails go past where any published data backs you up. MrBeast's leaked memo treats any 30–60s stretch as retention risk; that's the wrong model for our content type but worth knowing as the dominant counter-position.

### Scripting conventions worth adopting (for extending the two-column AV script)
- **Alan Moore panel description.** Per-frame paragraph specs with composition, palette, figure pose, light direction. The script becomes the image-prompt sheet.
- **Lined script.** Vertical lines tying morph chains to narration timecodes ("MORPH 1A→1B→1C covers 02:14–02:59").
- **7-column shot list.** Per-frame mini-table: shot ID, framing, angle, movement, action, duration. Each row is simultaneously a still-prompt spec and a video-clip spec.
- **Pixar emotional beat outline.** One-line emotional-beat header per scene block ("dread → recognition → resignation"). Solves the question "is this middle frame doing real work or just interpolation?"
- **Celtx 3-column AV.** Visual / Audio / Image-thumb. Store the AI-generated still as the Image cell so the script document is the storyboard *and* the prompt sheet *and* the chaining plan in one artifact.

### Empirical gaps — Tiger must test on actual style sheet
The report flags four claims with no published study, all worth empirical testing:
1. Whether double-anchor referencing (frame 1 + frame N–1) actually beats single-anchor for chained stills on constructivist style.
2. Whether Vidu Q1's published anime/2D consistency claims hold for *Saul Bass-style angular vector* specifically.
3. Whether 30–60s sustained illustrated stretches retain Parallax's specific audience.
4. Whether Veo 3.1's "no radical morph" limitation can be worked around with LTX-2 LoRA.

**Recommended test protocol:** 5 stills × 3 morph clips × 2 candidate pipelines, ~60s of output, posted as unlisted A/B against an analytical-only control.

## 13. Documentary Techniques for YouTube Essays (May 2026)

### Source
Full report: [`./research/2026-05-documentary-techniques.md`](./research/2026-05-documentary-techniques.md). Catalogue of named editing/narration techniques (with originators) and how to encode them in our two-column production script.

### Technique vocabulary worth adopting
- **Eisenstein's five montage types** — *metric* (fixed-interval cuts), *rhythmic* (cuts on beat/action), *tonal* (shots sharing emotion), *overtonal* (synthesis of all three), *intellectual* (juxtaposing two shots to imply a third idea, e.g. Odessa Steps). Intellectual montage is the closest formal name for what a structural-analogy channel does visually.
- **Curtis Cut (Archival Collage)** — pivot voice-over with a connective phrase ("Meanwhile…") + jump-cut between disparate clips that share a concept. Music carries part of the argument; recurring motifs (Curtis's "dancers vs pigeons") thread across acts.
- **Errol Morris Interrotron / objective-truth ethic** — direct-eye interview framing; reenactment must be tagged. Translates to: any "[Dramatic Recon]" or simulated scene gets explicit labeling in script.
- **Herzog's "ecstatic truth" / "director as hornet"** — license to use metaphorical visuals that *feel* true without claiming literal accuracy; narrator takes a stance rather than observing. Compatible with Parallax's "structural resonance" framing if we hold the line at "this suggests" rather than "this proves."
- **Marker / Wiseman / Guzmán / Varda / Ken Burns** — essay-film associative montage; observational long takes (no VO) for variety; memory-and-myth juxtaposition (cosmic ↔ personal); reflexive "let's pause on this shot" beats; slow pan/zoom on stills.

### Priority reading list (added to project bookshelf candidate)
Walter Murch *In the Blink of an Eye* · Eisenstein *Film Form* · Bill Nichols *Introduction to Documentary* · Sidney Lumet *Making Movies* · Michael Rabiger *Directing the Documentary* · Stella Bruzzi *New Documentary*.

## 16. Audio Architecture — Two-Mode Score Design (May 2026)

### Source
Full report: [`./research/2026-05-audio-architecture.md`](./research/2026-05-audio-architecture.md). Operational spec for music + SFX once Parallax adds 30–60s atmospheric scenes alongside motion-graphics blocks.

### Core design principle
The score must run **two modes**:
- **Analytical mode** (motion graphics, charts, frameworks, quote reveals): music = floor, not ceiling. Low-melody, low-rhythm-change, soft drones / pulses / filtered piano. Drop the bed at the reveal sentence.
- **Atmospheric mode** (chained illustrations, civilizational-dread montage, decompression): music = co-narrator. Allowed to lead pacing. Best palette = "slow destabilizers" (low strings, processed choir, industrial pulses, detuned piano), not heroic trailer music.

Re-using one underscoring logic across both modes flattens the whole essay.

### Evidence base
- Background music's effect on learning is *inconclusive* across 30 studies (11 positive, 10 neutral, 9 negative). Operational rule: music is **task-matched**, not always-on.
- Curtis = high-confidence reference for atmospheric mode; Johnny Harris = bespoke-emotional-glue reference; Vox/Earworm = explainer that lets sound shape segmentation. Kurzgesagt / Wendover / Polymatter / Asianometry: report could not retrieve cue-by-cue evidence, so positioning is inferred.

### Sourcing stack recommendation
- **Primary library:** Epidemic Sound (~$5/episode at 24 eps/yr) for simplest workflow, OR Artlist Music+SFX Pro for more cinematic palette.
- **AI texture layer (atmospheric only):** Stable Audio for drones / pulses / archival textures.
- **Custom anchor:** one small theme pack ($500–$2,500) for theme + idents + 2–4 reusable dread cues. Highest-leverage custom spend.
- **Avoid as backbone:** Udio (export/download policy unstable as of 2026 label settlements). Suno only with copyright-protection caveat.
- AI music caveats: 97% of listeners can't tell AI from human in blind tests, but AI loses on deep emotional specificity, large-form architecture, and expressive intentionality. Use AI for atmosphere, humans/library for analytical anchors.

### SFX architecture (5-layer system)
- **Micro-confirmation** (UI ticks, typography landings) — chart point reveals, label pops.
- **Meso-transition** (whooshes, risers) — chapter / model / lens shifts only, NOT every cut.
- **Macro-emphasis** (low impacts, sub drops) — structural reveals, paired with bed withdrawal.
- **Atmospheric textures** (room tone, projector hum, tape flutter) — bridge category, polite under narration.
- **Signature idents** (one opening sting, one section break, one quote-reveal accent) — channel-brand assets, build intentionally.

### Three recurring cue families (the report's brand recommendation)
- **System** — minimal analytical cues (maps, charts, institutional logic).
- **Pressure** — dread/ambiguity cues (analogy setup, tension escalation).
- **Aftermath** — hollow, slower, reflective cues (consequence, moral framing).

### Pipeline integration points
- `AUDIO_DESIGN.md` — current 3-layer model (music bed / transition SFX / texture hits) is compatible but should be expanded to the 5-layer SFX system + 3 cue families. **STILL OUTSTANDING.**
- `assembly-manifest.json` — cue-family field on each segment so `generate_manifest.py` can pick the right bed type per mode.
- `VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md` pacing rule — atmospheric mode now has a sound-design rationale, not just a visual one.

## 17. Verification Architecture for AI-Assisted Research (May 2026)

### Source
Full report: [`./research/2026-05-verification-architecture.md`](./research/2026-05-verification-architecture.md). Empirical hallucination rates across frontier research tools + a 5-step audit protocol for the `research-audit` stage.

### Why this matters
Parallax's credibility depends on never narrating an invented quote, wrong date, or smoothed-over scholarly disagreement. The current `deep-research → research-audit → angle-memo` stage is well-shaped but the audit needs a narrower, harder definition.

### Hallucination-rate evidence (the headline numbers)
- **SourceCheckup (Nature Communications):** 50–90% of cited responses across 7 LLMs were not fully supported by the cited source. Even GPT-4o with web search left ~30% unsupported.
- **JMIR Mental Health (GPT-4o lit reviews):** 19.9% of citations fabricated; of the rest, 54.6% had bibliographic errors. ~⅔ fabricated or inaccurate overall.
- **Radiology comparative study:** Claude 3.5 Sonnet leads at 80.8% fully accurate references / 3.1% fabrication. Claude 3 Opus 59.6% / 18.3%. ChatGPT variants 5.6–7.3% / 27.7–52.9%. Gemini 1.5 Pro **2.7% / 60.6%**.
- **Tow / CJR news-citation study:** answer engines wrong >60% of the time on article-ID-from-quote. Perplexity best at 37% incorrect; Grok 3 worst at 94%.
- **BBC / EBU 2025:** 45–51% of AI news answers had significant issues; 13–20% had altered or non-present quotes; Gemini had 72% significant sourcing issues.
- **URL-validity preprint:** hallucinated URL rates 3–13%; non-resolving 5–18%. Claude 3.5 search at 3.0% lowest. Gemini 2.5 Pro Deep Research **13.3% highest**.
- **ReportBench / source-attribution AST:** "the link works" and "the page is on-topic" are weak tests. Best frontier models hit only 39–77% factual accuracy on claim-to-source. **More search depth reduced factual accuracy ~42% on average.**

### Practical model-trust ranking for citation-heavy work
1. **Claude (Sonnet / 4-class)** — strongest citation profile in the reviewed evidence; lowest URL hallucination; best for first-pass research and attached-document audits.
2. **ChatGPT Deep Research** — usable as brief writer + source collector; ~78.87% citation match on ReportBench, 3.5% hallucinated URLs.
3. **Perplexity Pro** — best answer engine in CJR test, but "best" = 37% wrong. Discovery layer only.
4. **Gemini Deep Research** — strong on parametric recall but weak in news-sourcing; highest URL-hallucination rate among deep-research tools.
5. **Discovery tools (Elicit, Consensus, Scite, Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar)** — no independent false-citation rate located; safest when used purely as search infrastructure.

### How public creators actually work (validation of our approach)
- **Ben Thompson (Stratechery):** reads the primary source first, then uses Deep Research to *substantiate* a thesis he already has. Angle-owning human, substantiating machine.
- **Noah Smith:** uses Deep Research because it gives "sources you can check yourself," not because the report itself is trustworthy.
- **Zeke Hausfather:** uses Claude Code with project plans + tests + careful review; explicitly says he'd spend more time on plan/uncertainty if it were a research paper.
- **Tyler Cowen:** AI as aggressive question-asker / fact-checker; refuses to let it "mess with the distinctiveness" of his writing.
- **Matt Yglesias:** AI for upstream synthesis/ideation only; human owns prose.

Common pattern: AI = upstream accelerator; human owns angle, voice, and final factual responsibility. Never a final citation authority.

### The 5-lens audit protocol (recommended for our `research-audit` stage)
1. **Citation existence** — every cited work resolves as a real object with matching title/author/year/venue/DOI/URL.
2. **Source support** — every paragraph's lead claim is supported by an *inspected* source, not just a citation that exists.
3. **Quote / date / number exactness** — auto-extract every direct quote, date, percentage, magnitude claim, superlative, timed causal sequence, and line-check each against the source.
4. **Disagreement handling** — every nontrivial interpretive claim is bucketed as *consensus* / *active dispute* / *unclear-mixed*. No flattening scholarly disagreement into faux singular view.
5. **Temporal freshness** — time-sensitive claims marked **verified as of [date]** in the memo itself.

Tiered application: **MVP** = quotes + dates + numbers + named papers/books + paragraph lead claims. **Standard** = add disagreement + freshness. **Flagship / controversial episodes** = private evidence appendix with source excerpts or page references for every nontrivial on-screen factual assertion.

### Newsroom/journal policies that transfer
AP, Reuters, NYT, Nature, Science, JAMA, ICMJE all converge on: **AI may assist; humans own factual responsibility, supervision, and disclosure.** No major news/research institution treats AI as final citation authority. AP allows experimentation only where a journalist starts and edits the work; Reuters discloses primary AI use; ICMJE forbids AI authorship and requires careful human review.

### Pipeline integration points
- `skills/research-audit/SKILL.md` — extend the audit checklist to the 5 lenses above. **STILL OUTSTANDING.**
- `EDITORIAL_PLAYBOOK.md` — codify "every quote, date, number, and named paper must be human-inspected" as a non-negotiable.
- `prompts/` — add a "claim + source text → does the source support this claim?" auditor prompt for second-pass verification.
- Episode template — add `verified-as-of: YYYY-MM-DD` field to every time-sensitive claim in the research memo.
