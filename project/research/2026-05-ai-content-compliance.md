# AI Content Compliance for an AI-Augmented Channel

**YouTube Policy:** YouTube’s Partner Program and Community Guidelines strictly forbid *“mass-produced, template-driven, repetitive content”*【52†L128-L136】【46†L89-L97】.  In practice, channels whose videos differ only superficially (e.g. same format, only names or words changed) are deemed **“inauthentic”** and lose monetization【52†L128-L136】【46†L89-L97】.  YouTube’s official line is that **AI isn’t banned per se**, but content created *entirely* by AI with *no human creative input* is treated as spam.  For example, YouTube terminated 11–16 high-subscriber channels (35 M total subs) in Jan 2026 for precisely this pattern【9†L51-L60】【30†L116-L125】 (AI-animated Dragon Ball stories, AI-voice biblical quizzes, etc.).  In these cases “AI handled every step — scripting, voice, visuals, publishing — with zero human editorial input”【30†L122-L126】.  YouTube confirmed these removals were enforcement of existing spam/inauthentic-content rules, not a new AI ban【9†L57-L60】【15†L270-L279】.  TeamYouTube even clarified that *“AI-enhanced videos remain eligible if properly disclosed”*【7†L15-L18】 (i.e. AI tools are fine so long as human creators genuinely shape the content).  **Bottom line:** if our videos are clearly Tiger’s original work (human-narrated, human-written script, unique editorial choices), and not just churned-out AI loops, we comply with YouTube’s “authentic content” policies【46†L89-L97】【52†L128-L136】.

**Recent Enforcement Patterns:** The January 2026 purge is well-documented【9†L51-L60】【30†L116-L125】.  It targeted very high-volume “AI slop” channels (e.g. *Cuentos Facinantes*, 5.95M subs) that published dozens of templated videos daily【30†L116-L124】.  All known cases shared *zero human final review*【30†L122-L126】.  By contrast, channels that use AI as *one tool* (with human oversight at each step) were not listed.  Note: investigative groups have also identified AI-driven *“war news”* channels (2B views) and AI-deepfake political content; some of those (e.g. Trump/Clinton voice fakes) have since been removed【24†L593-L602】.  It appears YouTube’s enforcement has primarily hit the **entertainment/“brainrot” AI channels**, but the same rules apply: any realistic misleading content (e.g. fake news using AI) would violate both inauthentic and misinformation policies【26†L275-L283】【24†L593-L602】.  We should assume YouTube will scrutinize any AI use that could be *deceptive or repetitive*, while **legitimate AI-assisted, human-driven work is allowed**【9†L57-L60】【42†L99-L101】.

## Disclosure & Labeling

Since May 2025 YouTube requires creators to label **“altered or synthetic content that appears realistic.”**  Crucially, *not all* AI use must be disclosed.  YouTube says **no disclosure is needed for “clearly unrealistic content, animated content, or AI used for production assistance”**【42†L99-L101】【40†L105-L114】.  Examples exempt from disclosure include color filters, CGI-style animation, or using AI to write a script or thumbnail【42†L128-L136】【40†L109-L117】.  In fact, the Help Center explicitly lists *“using generative AI tools to create or improve a video outline, script, thumbnail, title…”* as **not requiring disclosure**【40†L109-L117】, and likewise video/audio “repair or upscaling” or cloning your own voice is exempt【40†L112-L117】.  **We fall into these safe categories:** Tiger writes the script and narrates, so any AI research or idea-generation used is “production assistance”【40†L109-L117】.  Our Recraft illustrations (constructivist style) and Remotion graphics are stylized/artistic rather than photorealistic, so they are treated like “animation” – no disclosure needed【42†L128-L136】.  Even the chain-morph atmospheric scenes (if obviously graphics-like) would likely be exempt.  Likewise, AI-enhanced thumbnails (human photo plus AI stylization) count as “thumbnail creation” and need no label【40†L109-L117】.  In short, **only declare “Altered content: Yes” if a viewer could mistake something for real** (e.g. realistic deepfakes, realistic war scenes, a famous person “speaking” via AI)【42†L118-L126】【40†L129-L138】.  

When in doubt: YouTube’s guidance is, be honest but note that *disclosing has no penalty*.  Selecting the disclosure label will *not* reduce recommendations or monetization【40†L193-L202】.  YouTube explicitly states disclosure “won’t limit a video’s audience or impact its eligibility to earn money”【40†L193-L202】 (Confidence: high).  By contrast, failing to disclose required synthetic realistic content *could* lead to YouTube adding a label on their own or even demonetizing the video【40†L201-L209】.  To date, we have no documented cases of a creator losing reach simply by being overly cautious and checking “Yes” when not strictly needed; the risk is more from under-disclosing.  (Creators on forums worry about “over-labeling,” but YouTube’s documentation gives high confidence that only needed cases are flagged, and disclosure is meant to help transparency, not to punish the creator.) 

**Practical disclosure text:** YouTube’s form is a yes/no checkbox.  If “Yes,” YouTube will show a label like “this video has AI-generated content” to viewers.  There is no fixed text field for details.  As a courtesy, some creators add a note in the video or description (e.g. “*This video used AI for background art and research*”), but this is voluntary.  Focus on the checkbox: e.g. if we did **not** generate any realistic scenes or fake voices, we choose *No*.  If we ever did simulate something realistic via AI, we would mark *Yes*.  (Given our planned content, we expect almost all episodes will be *No*, with notes like “AI-assist” only in internal docs.) 

## FTC, Legal & Other Rules

- **FTC Endorsement Rules:** If an episode is sponsored or has affiliate marketing, standard FTC rules apply: disclose paid promotions (e.g. “Sponsored by X” or “#ad”). These rules do *not* require callers to mention the use of AI tools per se, only material connections.  There is no FTC mandate to say “this script was drafted by AI.” However, be aware FTC prohibits deceptive claims: e.g., using AI to generate fake customer testimonials or impersonate a person in an ad is illegal【37†L43-L52】 (False reviews/enforcement).  We simply shouldn’t misrepresent ourselves or a product in ads – standard practice.  

- **State/Federal Laws on Deepfakes:** Several jurisdictions ban deceptive synthetic media featuring real persons.  For example, Washington (Gov. 2026) now makes it unlawful to use AI “to create deceptive audio or video that imitates another person without consent”【39†L172-L181】.  California’s *Deepfake Deception Act* (AB 2655) requires platforms to label AI-generated political content by Jan 2027.  These laws primarily target misinformation and impersonation.  For us: **only use Tiger’s real voice and likeness**.  Don’t use AI to impersonate others.  Do not produce realistic fake news. (If we ever did a political or sensitive topic video with AI scenes, we’d have to consider CA/DC laws—though that is unlikely for a tech/creative channel.)  

- **International:** As a US channel, EU laws (e.g. AI Act, DSA) don’t directly impose new requirements on us now, but the trend is global.  The EU’s AI Act (phased 2026–27) will later require transparency for “synthetic media” in certain cases.  For completeness: if our audience is global, be prepared that very realistic AI-generated commentary (especially on politics) could be viewed unfavorably under those regimes.  But no immediate action needed beyond the YouTube rules above.  

## Retroactive Enforcement Risk

YouTube policies generally apply to **all content on a channel** (see Monetization policy: a violation can strip the whole channel【52†L128-L136】).  Historically YouTube has applied new rules to existing videos (e.g. recent tighter Partner requirements were retroactively enforced on all channels【48†L17-L23】).  That suggests: **comply today to be safe**.  In practice, there’s no public example of YouTube ex post demonetizing old videos just because a new disclosure rule came later.  But YouTube’s inauthentic-content policy is applied channel-wide at review time【52†L128-L136】.  

So: do we need to “over-disclose” now to hedge against future changes? Probably not necessary, given YouTube’s assurance that truthful disclosure isn’t penalized【40†L193-L202】.  Instead, the safe posture is to follow *current* guidance faithfully: if an element is really realistic (e.g. a deepfake mouth or real-looking destruction), mark it. Otherwise don’t. Over-labeling benign edits (like calling every color filter “AI-generated”) may be excessive and is not required.  In summary, **follow today’s policy**. If rules tighten later, it’s better that we were already clearly abiding by the old one. 

## Pre-Publish Checklist

Below is a per-video checklist Parallax will use before upload.  Each item is drawn from YouTube’s actual rules【46†L89-L97】【40†L105-L114】 (Confidence levels reflect documentation strength):

- **Script & Narration:**  
  - [ ] *Human Authorship:* Tiger writes the **final script in their own words**. Any AI research or prompt ideas were purely for brainstorming. (We ensure the content is *personality-driven and original*【46†L89-L97】 – **HIGH**.)  
  - [ ] *AI Editing:* We may use AI for fact-checking or outlines, but Tiger **fully edits and personalizes** the output. (Production assistance is allowed【40†L109-L117】 – **HIGH**.)  
  - [ ] *Disclosure:* Since the final script/audio is Tiger’s voice with AI only helping behind the scenes, choose “Altered content: **No**” in upload (no realistic synthetic person is shown or voiced)【40†L109-L117】【42†L99-L101】 – **HIGH**.

- **Visuals & Graphics:**  
  - [ ] *Illustrations:* All AI-generated illustrations are in a **stylized/Recraft constructivist** aesthetic. They are not photorealistic and are clearly artistic or cartoonish. (Animated/unrealistic content is exempt from labeling【42†L99-L101】【40†L105-L114】 – **HIGH**.)  
  - [ ] *Motion Graphics:* Remotion templates are used with data-driven animations (graphs, simple vector animations, etc.). Confirm none depict real events or places. (Abstract graphics do not require disclosure【42†L99-L101】【40†L105-L114】 – **HIGH**.)  
  - [ ] *Special Cases:* If we ever include an AI scene that looks real (e.g. a real city skyline with a tornado generated), we would mark “Yes” and disclose in text. Otherwise, we ensure every scene is obviously stylized.  
  - [ ] *Thumbnails:* Underlying photo is Tiger or stock; any enhancements (AI upscaling, colorization, or composite) are minor. (Thumbnail creation is production assistance – **no disclosure needed**【40†L109-L117】 – **HIGH**.)

- **Audio:**  
  - [ ] *Voiceover:* Use Tiger’s recorded voice for narration. If audio is cleaned or noise-reduced by AI tools, that’s fine (audio repair is exempt【40†L112-L117】 – **HIGH**).  
  - [ ] *No Cloned Voices:* We do not use AI to imitate any real person's voice other than Tiger's own. (Voice cloning of *others* would require disclosure or be disallowed【40†L129-L137】 – **HIGH**.)  

- **Metadata & Formatting:**  
  - [ ] *Original Titles/Descriptions:* Craft titles and descriptions describing content *accurately*, not clickbait. Avoid obviously “templated” wording (e.g. “Shocking News: X!” for every video). Ensure each video’s title uniquely reflects its subject. (This avoids “template repetition” flags【52†L128-L136】 – **HIGH**.)  
  - [ ] *Tags & SEO:* No keyword stuffing. Include a mix of topical and unique tags. Unique titles and thumbnails help show content is varied.  
  - [ ] *Chaptering & Comments:* Use chapters or timestamps for clarity. Encourage genuine engagement (no spammy auto-comments or misleading CTAs).  

- **Compliance Checks:**  
  - [ ] *Partner Program:* Ensure channel meets all monetization requirements (100K views or more, etc.) separately (irrelevant to AI policy but part of overall compliance).  
  - [ ] *Copyright & Fair Use:* Any third-party clips or images must be licensed or used under fair use (even if altered by AI, copyright rules still apply).  
  - [ ] *Child Safety:* If targeting kids, follow YouTube Kids guidelines (AI content rules are stricter there – likely not relevant for us now).

- **Documentation:**  
  - [ ] *Keep Records:* Archive scripts, screenshots of project files, and notes showing Tiger’s involvement. This is not required by policy, but **if flagged** we can demonstrate human input. (No direct source citation – prudent practice.)

## If YouTube Flags Your Content (Appendix)

1. **False-Positive AI Flag:** Suppose a video is demonetized or labeled “inauthentic” despite our compliance.  
   - *Action:* Immediately appeal via YouTube Studio’s feedback link or Partner Support. Provide context: “This video was 100% human-created. AI was only used for [research/illustration], which per YouTube does *not* require a disclosure【40†L109-L117】.”  
   - *Document:* Share evidence of human authorship (script drafts, narration audio, project notes). If needed, cite TeamYouTube’s guidance that AI-enhanced content with disclosure is fine【7†L15-L18】, and emphasize our genuine human creative process.  
   - *Follow-Up:* If a review is granted, clarify any misunderstandings. If removal was due to “spam,” argue our content is substantively unique each video (citing inauthentic policy: “each video differs by topic and content”【52†L128-L136】). (Confidence: Medium – no official playbook, but standard appeal practice.)

2. **Disclosure Dispute:** The system demands we check “Yes” on disclosure or applies a label we think is incorrect.  
   - *Scenario A:* We checked “No,” but YouTube believes our content should have been labeled. Or vice versa.  
   - *Action:* Review the flagged content. If it indeed contains realistic synthetic elements (e.g. a deepfake clip), check “Yes” on re-upload. If not, prepare an appeal or correction: explain that our AI use was limited to exempt categories (script/thumbnails/etc).  
   - *Communicate:* Use YouTube’s Help Community or Creator Support to explain. Reference their own examples: e.g. “According to YouTube, video outline/script assistance doesn’t require disclosure”【40†L109-L117】. Emphasize transparency: we will correct any mistakes.  
   - *Practice:* Generally, err on the side of disclosure if in doubt. The worst-case “over-label” merely adds a disclaimer, while under-labeling can lead to flagging or demonetization【40†L201-L209】. (Confidence: Medium.)

3. **Manual Review (Monetization/Channel):** A YouTube reviewer questions our channel’s eligibility (e.g. demonetizes or issues a strike).  
   - *Action:* Submit a formal review request. In the appeal, highlight our originality: every episode features Tiger’s on-camera or voice presence, original script, and unique AI-generated art – showing substantial human creative input (consistent with “allowed content” examples【52†L128-L136】).  
   - *Adjust:* If needed, revise future content to be more obviously varied and commentary-driven. Possibly add a short intro clip of Tiger speaking directly, to signal human presence.  
   - *Documentation:* Provide logs of the creative process (project files, timestamps of edits) if available.  
   - *Outcome:* If reinstated, continue with care. If not, consider removing the most “AI-heavy” content and reapplying for monetization. (Confidence: Low – actual appeals outcomes are opaque, but emphasizing human authorship aligns with documented policy.)
