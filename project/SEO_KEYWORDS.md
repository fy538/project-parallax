## Part 1: SEO Principles for Parallax

### The Core Tension: Depth vs. Discoverability

Parallax's differentiation is intellectual architecture — multi-framework analysis, historical analogy, epistemological transparency. But YouTube search rewards simplicity. Nobody searches for "Bayesian epistemological analysis of semiconductor geopolitics." They search for "chip war explained."

**The strategy:** Lead with high-search-volume keywords in titles and descriptions. Deliver the differentiated analysis inside the video. The door should be wide; the room should be deep.

### Title Architecture

Based on analysis of high-performing geopolitics titles (CaspianReport, PolyMatter, Asianometry, Wendover), the patterns that work:

**Pattern 1: "[Why/How] + [counterintuitive claim]"**
- "Why Blockades Always Fail"
- "How TSMC Outsmarted Both China and the US"
- "Why Export Controls Never Work"
- Best for: Detective, Philosopher's Lens formats

**Pattern 2: "[Thing] Explained" or "The [Thing] You've Never Heard Of"**
- "The Chip War Explained"
- "COCOM: The Export Control You've Never Heard Of"
- "The Prisoner's Dilemma Explained (With Real Geopolitics)"
- Best for: Evergreen content, Philosopher's Lens

**Pattern 3: "[Country/Actor] + [surprising action or state]"**
- "China Is Not Trying to Win the Chip War"
- "TSMC's Impossible Dilemma"
- "America's $280 Billion Bet That Might Backfire"
- Best for: Detective, Dialectic formats

**Pattern 4: "[Question everyone is asking]"**
- "Will China Invade Taiwan?"
- "Is the US Losing the Chip War?"
- "Can China Make Advanced Chips Without ASML?"
- Best for: Timely content, Dialectic format. NOTE: Use sparingly — these are high-search but competitive and potentially Yellow-flag-triggering.

**Pattern 5: "What [historical event] Tells Us About [current event]"**
- "What the Cold War Tells Us About the Chip War"
- "What Rome's Fall Tells Us About America"
- Best for: Time Collapse format. Bridges evergreen + timely.

### The 50-Character Rule

YouTube truncates titles at approximately 50-60 characters in most views (mobile, sidebar recommendations). Front-load the keyword and the hook before the truncation point.

"Why Technological Blockades Always Fail — COCOM, Semiconductors, and the Lessons of History" → Viewer sees: "Why Technological Blockades Always Fail — CO..."
The important part survives. The long tail helps SEO without hurting clickability.

### Description Strategy

YouTube's search algorithm indexes the full description. Use the first 2-3 lines for a compelling hook (visible without "Show more"), then include a keyword-rich paragraph below the fold.

**Template:**
```
[Hook — 1-2 sentences restating the video's central question]

[Brief preview of what the video covers — name the frameworks/history used]

— Topics covered —
[Keyword-rich list: "semiconductor export controls," "COCOM history," "US-China chip war," etc.]

— Sources —
[Links to key sources used in the video]

— About Parallax —
[Standard channel description with keywords]
```

### Tags Strategy

Tags have diminished SEO weight in 2026 (YouTube has confirmed they're a minor signal), but they still help with topic association. Use 10-15 tags per video mixing:
- Broad niche tags: "geopolitics," "history," "international relations," "political analysis"
- Topic-specific tags: "semiconductor," "chip war," "TSMC," "export controls"
- Framework tags: "game theory," "Stoicism," "Bayesian," "systems theory"
- Competitor association tags: "CaspianReport," "PolyMatter," "Asianometry" (helps YouTube associate your content with similar channels)

## Part 4: Ongoing Keyword Research Process

### Monthly Routine
1. **Check YouTube search suggestions.** Type your topic keywords into YouTube search and note autocomplete suggestions — these are real user searches.
2. **Monitor competitor titles.** Track what CaspianReport, PolyMatter, Asianometry, William Spaniel, and Zeihan are titling their videos. Note which get unusually high view counts relative to their channel size.
3. **Check Google Trends.** Compare keyword interest over time for candidate episode topics. Prioritize keywords with rising or stable interest over declining ones.
4. **Review your own analytics.** After 5+ episodes, YouTube Studio's search report shows which keywords are driving traffic to your videos. Double down on what's working.

### Tools (Free Tier Sufficient for Now)
- **YouTube search autocomplete** — free, real-time signal of what people search
- **Google Trends** (trends.google.com) — free, compare keyword interest over time, filter by YouTube search
- **vidIQ free tier** — shows keyword scores and competition for YouTube specifically
- **TubeBuddy free tier** — keyword explorer, tag suggestions, competitor analysis

### When to Do Dedicated Keyword Research
- Before starting each new arc (identify the keyword landscape for that macro topic)
- When titling a finished video (test 3-4 title variants against keyword data)
- When a breaking event creates a new search spike (rapid Shorts or Advisor Briefing opportunity)
