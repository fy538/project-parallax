# Parallax — Content Risk Playbook

## Purpose
Guidelines for navigating the specific risks that come with producing geopolitical analysis content on YouTube as a Chinese-American solo creator. This document covers platform demonetization triggers, the "historical analogy shield," propaganda accusation mitigation, and editorial red lines.

Created: April 26, 2026

## Part 1: YouTube Monetization Risk Map

### How YouTube's Ad System Works for Geopolitical Content

YouTube classifies every video into one of three monetization tiers:

- **Green (full monetization):** All advertisers can bid. Highest CPM. This is where you want to be.
- **Yellow (limited ads):** Some advertisers excluded. Reduced CPM, typically 30-70% lower. Common for geopolitical content flagged as "sensitive."
- **Red (no ads):** Violates community guidelines. No monetization.

**The key principle:** YouTube distinguishes between content that *exploits* sensitive topics and content that *addresses them with educational, documentary, or journalistic purpose.* As of January 2026, YouTube explicitly expanded full monetization to educational/documentary content covering controversial issues, violent events, and sensitive events — as long as the treatment is non-graphic and context-driven.

This is good news for Parallax. Educational analysis of geopolitics is exactly the category YouTube is now trying to protect, not suppress.

### Specific Demonetization Triggers to Avoid

**High-risk triggers (likely Yellow or Red):**

1. **Graphic imagery of conflict.** Even stock footage of military action can trigger Yellow. Use maps, diagrams, and motion graphics instead — which is already the production strategy.
2. **Incitement or calls to action.** Any language that could be interpreted as encouraging violence, even hypothetically ("Country X should invade...").
3. **Specific ongoing "sensitive events."** YouTube maintains an unpublished list of active sensitive events (ongoing wars, terrorist attacks, mass shootings). Content about these events faces heightened scrutiny in the first 48-72 hours. The Ukraine war content, for instance, was flagged for extended Yellow status.
4. **Election misinformation.** Any content that could be interpreted as claiming election fraud, challenging election results, or suppressing voting. Avoid entirely — not your lane anyway.
5. **Certain keywords in titles/descriptions.** Words like "war," "attack," "bomb," "terrorism," "kill" in titles can trigger automated Yellow flags. The automated system scans titles first, then reviews content if flagged.

**Medium-risk triggers (may trigger Yellow, recoverable on appeal):**

6. **Discussing specific military capabilities or weapons systems.** Educational context usually survives appeal, but automated systems flag it.
7. **Naming specific terrorist organizations.** Use them in analytical context, not as content focus.
8. **Content perceived as "taking sides" in active conflicts.** YouTube's system is more likely to flag content that appears partisan about ongoing wars.

**Low-risk (generally safe with educational framing):**

9. **Historical analysis of past conflicts.** Historical content is almost always Green.
10. **Philosophical and theoretical frameworks.** Game theory, Stoicism, systems theory — zero monetization risk.
11. **Economic and trade analysis.** Sanctions, export controls, trade wars — low risk unless tied to active sensitive events.

### Tactical Mitigations

**Title engineering:** Avoid trigger words in titles when possible. "Why Blockades Always Fail" is safer than "Will China's Military Blockade of Taiwan Succeed?" Both can cover the same analysis, but the first is less likely to be auto-flagged.

**First 30 seconds matter for review.** YouTube's automated system samples the opening of the video. Start with the analytical frame, not the most provocative claim.

**Self-certify accurately.** YouTube asks creators to self-certify whether content contains sensitive material. Be honest — over-certifying (claiming no sensitive content when there is some) can result in strikes. Under-certifying (flagging everything as sensitive) reduces your CPM unnecessarily.

**Appeal quickly.** Yellow flags from automated systems can be reversed through human review, usually within 24-48 hours. The first 48 hours of a video's life are the most valuable for the algorithm, so appeal immediately if you think a Yellow flag is incorrect.

**Lean on the "educational context" shield.** YouTube's own guidelines state: "If there's educational, documentary, scientific, or artistic value, that may change a video's suitability for advertisers." Your entire channel is educational by design.

## Part 2: The Historical Analogy Shield

### Concept

The "historical analogy shield" is a framing strategy: address sensitive contemporary topics *through* historical comparison rather than head-on. This serves both intellectual and platform risk purposes.

### How It Works

Instead of: "Will the US and China go to war over Taiwan?"
Frame as: "The last time a rising naval power challenged a dominant one over a strategically vital island, here's what happened — and here's what's different now."

Instead of: "Is the US using economic warfare against China?"
Frame as: "COCOM spent 40 years trying to deny the Soviet Union access to Western technology. Here's what worked, what didn't, and what the pattern tells us about today's export controls."

### Why This Works (Three Layers)

**Layer 1 — Platform safety.** Historical framing reduces automated trigger risk. "The Peloponnesian War" doesn't trigger content flags; "US-China military conflict" might.

**Layer 2 — Intellectual credibility.** Historical grounding makes your analysis more rigorous, not less. You're not speculating about the future — you're examining patterns that have played out before and asking whether the structural conditions recur.

**Layer 3 — Deniability without dishonesty.** If challenged ("Are you predicting war between the US and China?"), the honest answer is: "I'm examining historical patterns and noting where the structural conditions are similar. The viewer can draw their own conclusions." This is intellectually honest — it's exactly what you believe — and it's also the safest framing.

### When the Shield Doesn't Apply

Some topics require direct contemporary analysis (breaking events, the Advisor format, Wargame scenarios). In these cases:
- Use conditional and probabilistic language ("If X happens, then Y becomes more likely")
- Avoid declarative predictions ("X will happen")
- Frame scenario analysis explicitly as scenario analysis ("Let's map the decision tree")
- The Oracle layer helps here — stating explicit probabilities feels more responsible than vague claims

## Part 3: The Propaganda Accusation Problem

### Why This Risk Is Real and Specific to You

The research is clear: any Chinese-heritage creator producing geopolitical content that doesn't conform to the "China bad" framing of Western mainstream discourse will face propaganda accusations. This is not hypothetical — it's structural.

**The Jiang Xueqin precedent:** His rapid rise and subsequent criticism show exactly how this plays out. Chinese face + American English + geopolitical analysis = immediate suspicion of CCP alignment, regardless of actual content. The accusations against Jiang have *some* merit (conspiracy theories, "East Rising West Declining" narrative alignment), but the speed and ferocity of the backlash reflects a broader pattern that will apply to any Chinese-heritage analyst.

**The broader landscape:** YouTube is documented as a battleground for Chinese state influence operations. Multiple reports (ASPI, DFRLab) have catalogued CCP-affiliated content networks. This means the audience is primed to see Chinese-heritage creators through a propaganda lens.

### Preemptive Defenses (Build Before You Need Them)

**1. Epistemological transparency is your strongest shield.**
The single most effective defense against propaganda accusations is demonstrable intellectual honesty. Track predictions publicly. Show when you're wrong. Present multiple frameworks including ones that contradict your tentative conclusions. No propaganda operation does this. Your Oracle layer isn't just a content feature — it's a credibility infrastructure.

**2. Criticize all sides, including China.**
This doesn't mean forced "balance" or both-sidesism. It means: when your analysis leads to conclusions that are uncomfortable for Chinese strategic interests, don't flinch. The moment you consistently avoid criticizing China while freely criticizing the US, the propaganda accusation becomes impossible to refute. The Dialectic format helps here — steelmanning both positions means you're never exclusively aligned with one.

**3. Source transparency.**
Cite your sources visibly. Use Western academic sources, Chinese academic sources, and independent research. The breadth and quality of your sourcing is visible evidence of independence. Propaganda operations use selective sourcing — yours should be conspicuously broad.

**4. Distinguish "Chinese analytical frameworks" from "Chinese state narratives."**
There's a vast difference between applying Tianxia theory as an analytical lens and promoting Xi Jinping's "Community of Shared Future for Mankind." The former is intellectual; the latter is state rhetoric. Be explicit about this distinction. "Chinese strategic tradition suggests X — which is analytically interesting regardless of whether the current government is acting in good faith on it."

**5. Address it directly, once, early.**
Consider an early video or a channel trailer segment that names the elephant in the room: "I'm Chinese-American. I analyze geopolitics using frameworks from both traditions. That means I'll sometimes present analysis that Western audiences find uncomfortable, and sometimes analysis that Chinese audiences find uncomfortable. If either side is always happy with me, I'm doing it wrong." Say it once, cleanly, and let your track record speak after that.

**6. Never accept state-affiliated money or partnerships.**
This is an absolute red line. No Chinese state media partnerships, no CCP-affiliated event appearances, no Confucius Institute collaborations. Even if the opportunity seems benign, the association is permanent and impossible to explain away.

### Reactive Playbook (When Accusations Come)

**Don't over-respond.** A long defensive thread looks guilty. A short, factual response + pointing to your track record is sufficient.

**Point to specific examples of balanced analysis.** "Here's where I criticized Chinese industrial policy. Here's where I criticized US export controls. Here's my prediction record showing I get things wrong about both sides."

**Don't engage with bad-faith critics.** Some accusations will come from accounts that accuse *everyone* of propaganda. Engaging amplifies them. Ignore.

**Let the community defend you.** A loyal audience that has watched you criticize all sides is your best defense. The Discord community and engaged commenters will push back on bad-faith accusations faster and more credibly than you can.

## Part 4: Editorial Red Lines

These are absolute rules, not guidelines. Breaking them damages the brand permanently.

### Never Do

1. **Never make declarative predictions about specific events.** "China will invade Taiwan by 2027" is brand poison — fragile, irresponsible, and indistinguishable from propaganda. Probability estimates are fine. Certainty is not.

2. **Never attribute systemic outcomes to secret conspiracies.** No Illuminati, no Freemasons, no "they planned this all along." Focus on emergent systemic behavior from incentive structures — that provides the same analytical thrill without the intellectual dishonesty.

3. **Never use dehumanizing language about any population.** Criticize governments, institutions, and policies. Never peoples.

4. **Never present Chinese state narratives as your own analysis.** You can *analyze* why the CCP frames things a certain way. You cannot present their framing as if you independently arrived at it.

5. **Never present Western/US state narratives as your own analysis either.** Same rule applies symmetrically. State Department talking points are analytical inputs, not conclusions.

6. **Never use footage of real casualties or graphic violence.** Motion graphics and maps are both safer and more analytically useful.

7. **Never accept sponsored content from defense contractors, state-affiliated media, or political organizations.** These associations are permanent brand damage.

### Always Do

1. **Always acknowledge where analogies break down.** Every historical parallel has limits. Naming them is what separates you from lazy pundits.

2. **Always hedge appropriately.** "Structural resonance," "this suggests," "one reading of this is." Never "this proves," "this inevitably leads to."

3. **Always show your reasoning.** Don't just state conclusions — show how you got there. The process is the product.

4. **Always fact-check historical claims.** A single factual error in a historical claim undermines the entire analytical framework. The script-audit skill and Gate agent exist for this.

5. **Always disclose AI assistance where required by platform policy.** Currently, the production pipeline falls on the "AI-assisted" side of YouTube's line (D4), but policies evolve. Stay current.

## Part 6: Crisis Scenarios and Responses

### Scenario 1: Video incorrectly flagged Yellow
**Response:** Appeal immediately with a note explaining the educational/analytical context. Include timestamps showing the analysis is framework-based, not sensational. Typical turnaround: 24-48 hours.

### Scenario 2: Accused of being CCP propaganda
**Response:** One calm, factual statement pointing to your track record of multi-framework analysis including China-critical content. Do not engage further. Let community respond.

### Scenario 3: Accused of being anti-China / Western propaganda
**Response:** Same approach. Point to examples of analysis that challenges Western narratives. The symmetry of your approach means you'll face both accusations — which is actually a credibility signal.

### Scenario 4: A prediction turns out badly wrong
**Response:** Make it content. A "Was I Right?" episode where you publicly analyze what you got wrong is more valuable than a quiet correction. The Oracle layer was designed for this.

### Scenario 5: Breaking event makes a published video suddenly sensitive
**Response:** If a video about historical military conflict becomes sensitive due to a real-world escalation, add a pinned comment acknowledging the context. Do not delete the video unless YouTube requires it — deletion looks guilty.

### Scenario 6: A guest or collaborator becomes controversial
**Response:** Do not delete the collaboration. If asked, state that you engage with diverse perspectives and that featuring someone's analysis does not mean endorsing all their views.
