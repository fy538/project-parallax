> **The passage.** The opening of Beat 4, about 25 seconds: "Go back to the shop. Why does Kant's dealer charge everyone the same? Kant's word for him is prudent. Picture the street: other customers compare notes. Neighbors talk. A competitor would love the story. His honesty was never only a fact about his heart. It was a fact about his street."

## How the three pictures are used

The scene is built like a paper theatre, from three flat pieces stacked front to back.

| Piece | Picture | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Front | The counter and the two people | The transaction. Stays on screen throughout |
| Middle | The shop wall with its window | Closed, it makes the scene a shop. It swings away on "Picture the street" |
| Back | The street | Seen through the window at first, then in full |

Because the closed shop is the open street with the wall in front of it, the before and after match exactly. Nothing has to be redrawn between them, and the three pieces can move at different rates when the camera pulls back.

The diagram ("a fact about his street") needs no picture. It is typeset in Remotion over the street.

## Opening message (paste once, after the uploads)

```
I'm going to make 3 pictures that are parts of ONE stage set, like the flat pieces of a paper theatre. They will be stacked in an animation, so they must match each other.

I've uploaded two references:
- Image 1 is the LOOK. Match how it is painted: a few large flat areas of colour with hard edges, detail only on the person, a head built from painted planes with no facial parts.
- Image 2 is the HEAD of the main character, from five angles. Use the three-quarter views and the looking-down view. Do not use the straight-on front view.

Rules for all three pictures:

HOW IT IS PAINTED
- Flat, matte, opaque paint. Hard straight edges between areas.
- Walls, floor, sky, road and building fronts are each ONE plain field of colour, with only a little paper tooth and two or three dry-brush breaks. No wood grain, no brickwork, no patterned cloth, no leaf detail, no soft gradients, no glow.
- Detail goes on the people only: grouped hair strokes, a few seams, hand contours.
- Not photorealistic. Not a soft watercolour or a storybook illustration.

VIEW AND LIGHT (the same in all three)
- Seen straight on, at eye level, like a stage seen from the middle of the audience. No vanishing point and no receding walls: every surface faces the viewer.
- Light comes from the left. Shadows are flat shapes that fall to the right.

PALETTE
- Evening. Ink #1C1814, walnut #5C4A3D, umber #8B7355, amber #E5A544, bone #F0E6D0. No green, no blue, no grey.

PEOPLE
- Heads have NO facial parts, from any angle: no eyes, eyebrows or eye sockets; no nose, nostrils or nose ridge; no mouth or lips; no lines inside the ear. The front of a head is a clean angular contour with no bump where a nose or lips would be. A plain filled ear shape is fine.
- A large head is built from eight to ten unequal painted planes in bone, amber and walnut ochre, as in image 1. The planes describe the volume of a head, never features. A small, distant head is two or three flat planes.
- Bodies are anatomically coherent under the flat paint: shoulders, elbows, wrists and hands connect believably, and each hand can be traced to its own arm.

NEVER
- No lettering, numbers, signs or logos anywhere.
```

## Picture 1. The counter and the two people (`flat-1-counter.png`)

> The front piece. It is on screen for the whole passage and carries the close shot on "Go back to the shop."

```
Picture 1 of 3: the front piece of the set. 16:9. Only a shop counter and two people, on a transparent background. Nothing behind them: no wall, no floor, no shadow on the background.

The counter: a long plain shop counter runs across the right half of the picture, facing us. Its front is one flat walnut panel that runs off the bottom edge of the picture. Its top is a thin lighter band about two-thirds of the way down the picture. One round loaf sits on the counter near its middle.

The shopkeeper stands behind the counter, toward the right. He is the man from the head sheet: dark tousled hair, black long-sleeved top, bone apron. We see him in three-quarter view, turned toward the left, his head tilted down toward the child. His near arm reaches to the left across the counter top, hand open and palm up, with exactly two small coins lying on the palm. His other hand rests flat on the counter. The top of his head is about a quarter of the way down the picture.

The child stands on our side of the counter, at its left end, with her back to us. We see her from behind and a little to the side: dark bobbed hair, the back of one cheek, a pale bone hooded top. Her head comes a little above the counter top. She reaches up with one open hand, palm up, just below his hand. The two hands do not touch: there is clear empty space between them. The bottom edge of the picture cuts her off at the knees.

The left half of the picture is empty and transparent.
```

**Check before moving on**

- Both heads at full size: no nose bump on his contour, no marks where eyes or a mouth would be, nothing inside the ears.
- His face has several distinct planes and is not one flat patch.
- Two coins, two separate hands, a gap between them. Each hand leads back to its own arm.
- The counter front is one plain field with no wood grain.
- If the background is not transparent, keep the picture anyway. I can cut it out.

## If something does not match

The three pieces are separate layers, so I can move and scale each one. Position does not need to be exact. What I cannot fix afterwards is paint, palette or light that differs between them. If one picture comes back softer, greyer or lit from the other side, ask for it again in the same conversation:

```
Make that picture again. Keep the composition, but match picture 1 exactly in how it is painted: the same flat hard-edged fields, the same palette, the same light from the left.
```
