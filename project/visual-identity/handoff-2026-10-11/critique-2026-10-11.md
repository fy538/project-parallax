# Critique of the October 10–11 visual research

**Written:** 11 October 2026 by Claude, the session that ran the still-technique exploration (`project/VISUAL_TOOLBOX.md`). **Responds to:** section 20 of [the handoff](parallax-visual-research-handoff.md). **For:** Tiger, and whichever agent continues the visual research.

**What I reviewed.** The handoff in full. The thirteen directions as one sheet. `02c`, `s04e`, the head sheet, the six-frame board and the three reveal endpoints at full size. Eight frames of the opening-panel film. The six key poses, six frames from each of the three clips, and enlargements of the head turn. The pose comparison and `d1`. The eight scenery, object, overlay and background frames. Twelve frames of the combined sequence.

**Limits.** I judged the clips from sampled frames, not at speed, and I did not reopen every README or prompt file. Nothing below is a claim about repeatability or about how an audience will read the look. All rejection and provisional statuses in the handoff stand.

## Verdict

The ingredients are right and the direction is worth continuing. Three things stand between it and an identity:

1. It is not one look yet. Four different renderings are filed under "painted constructivism", and the work drifted away from the one Tiger said "better" to.
2. The face is solved at one angle. In strict profile and from the front, the rule and the richness do not yet coexist.
3. Every failure is a person doing something, and the motion tests did not use the method that the other thread found dependable.

The two days also produced four things I would keep without reservation: the faceted head, the wall that opens, the same picture under two captions, and painted material sitting cleanly under typeset graphics.

## Creative critique

### 1. One name covers four looks

| Look | Where it appears | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| A. Flat fields | `02c`, the foreground of `s04e`, `02b-architectural-scenery` | A few large flat areas, hard edges, detail only on the figure |
| B. Soft daylight | Board frames 01, 03, 05, 06; the coin frames | Pale walls, green trees, grey street, soft paint everywhere |
| C. Naturalistic with blank faces | `d1-river-context`, the C chain, `01-quiet-scenery` | Folded shirts, laced boots, rocks and foliage, with the face left empty |
| D. Modelled object | The two cups | Rounded ceramic shading |

The episode's twelve stills are a fifth: texture on every surface (`aigen-03-second-shopkeeper.png`).

Tiger's "better" was for look A. The work then moved toward B and C.

This matters most for the blank face. On a figure built from flat planes, a head with no features reads as a design decision. On a naturalistically painted body it reads as something removed. `d1` is the clearest case. The handoff separates "anatomically coherent" from "naturalistically rendered" in words, and the pictures have not held that line. The pose-first chain is where it slipped: a photograph, then a grey render, then a style pass, and each pass kept the cloth folds and boot laces of the one before.

My judgment, not evidence: look A is also the one that best answers "designed and less recognizably AI generated". Soft warm editorial painting is close to what image models give by default. Hard edges, few colours and large empty areas are what make `02c`, `s04e` and `02b` look made by someone.

### 2. The palette is undecided

The brand palette is ink, gold, bone, paper, walnut and umber, with dust blue. The daylight frames add greens and blue-greys. The river adds orange against teal. The frames that sit comfortably beside the Remotion graphics are the amber and ink ones.

This can be decided today with no new pictures. `02c` and board frame 06 (`06-desk-daylight.png`) show the same person at the same desk in the two palettes. If daylight stays, I would hold it to the light half of the brand palette (paper, bone, sand, taupe, dust blue) and drop the saturated greens.

### 3. The face is solved at one angle

`02c` is a three-quarter view from slightly behind. That is the easy angle, because the face is mostly cheek and jaw. The two harder views are not designed yet.

- **Strict profile.** `01-head-end.png` passes the rule by collapsing the face to two flat planes. The faceting Tiger liked is gone. In the clip the face stays at one to three flat planes throughout.
- **Front.** The head sheet's front view splits light from shadow down the centre line. That reads as a ridge where a nose would be, which the rule excludes.

So the head needs to be designed once as a solid with a fixed set of planes and then drawn from each angle. Prompting it afresh for each view has not produced that. Experiment 1 tests it.

Two smaller points. The preferred shadow scene fails the rule in its shadows: the woman's shadow in `s04e` has a nose and lips. And the hair is drawn strand by strand, finer than anything else on the figure. It is what makes him recognisable, but it works against "selective detail" and leans toward a contemporary anime reading. That deserves one deliberate decision.

### 4. The storytelling operations

- **The same picture under two captions** (`board-02-two-accounts.png`). The best idea in the set. It is the episode's argument, it costs nothing, and it cannot break a face rule.
- **The opening panel.** On film it reads as a stage flat swinging open, not as paper folding. That is fine, and it is informative: the operation that works belongs to paper theatre. I would stop treating paper theatre as a rival look and adopt it as the staging method, with walls as flats and scenes built in separate layers. That is also the route to the real parallax that section 15 says is unproven.
- **The connecting shadows.** Handsome, but the shadows assert a connection that the episode says cannot be observed. Keep `s04e` for its figures and its wall. I would not use the device in this episode unless the line under it is plainly "here is one story we could tell".
- **The cup with stairs.** The cleanest proof that a painting and typeset graphics can share a frame. As a motif it is a newcomer to a script that already has a shop, and its ceramic is a different material from the people. Keep the layout and the method. Drop the cup.

### 5. Against the alternatives

In the samples, eight of the thirteen rely on drawn faces (woodcut, charcoal, cel, line, ink wash, collage, rotoscope, paint on glass). They would need redesign to meet the face rule.

Of the rest, paper theatre and flat architectural surrealism (`08b`) are the strongest. Both fit inside the leading direction: one supplies staging, the other supplies environments, and `02b` already is one. Risograph (`05b`) is the one real rival worth keeping on file. It is fully flat, featureless by nature and likely the easiest to keep consistent. Pinscreen is striking but cannot sit beside light analytical graphics.

So I agree with the choice of direction, on the condition that it stays at the look A end.

## Production critique

### 6. Every failure is a person doing something

| Worked on the first or second attempt | Failed or unresolved |
|---|---|
| Architectural scenery, objects with transparency, overlays, backgrounds, the opening-panel film, the cup sequence, the two-captions board | Head turn (nose point mid-turn). Pull (rejected). Pull rebuild (seven attempts, rejected). Pose-first chain (nine images, none approved, three head corrections that did not clear the rule). Coin exchange (the best, with ear marks and soft fingers) |

The brief asks for ambitious movement when the story needs it. On this evidence that is affordable only if it is rationed to a few seconds an episode and staged for what the tools can do. Stills, cuts and authored motion carry the rest, which is what the toolbox catalogue is for.

### 7. The motion tests did not use the method that works

From the toolbox exploration, run on the same days in another thread, stated as what we saw and not as a measured rate: the video model was dependable when pinned between frames that were edits of one trusted picture, pasted back region by region. It was unreliable when it had to draw something new. That is where it invented objects and gave blank faces features.

The handoff's start and end frames were separately generated near-matches. The head turn pinned only its two ends and left the model to invent the profile in between. It invented a nose.

The other thread also made the suspended river rescue move. Three keyframes edited from the episode's river still, one six-second clip, about $0.36 (`remotion-templates/out/ai-care-motives/stills-test-reel-5.mp4`, 0:02 to 0:08; catalogue row "Three pinned frames in one clip"). In the four frames I enlarged, both arms can be traced to their owners, because each wrist ends in a bone cuff on a dark sleeve. The rescuer's profile has a nose point, so it fails the face rule. It was one take, and it was reviewed against the old stills, not against these rules.

Neither thread has a figure-action clip that passes everything. Each has the half the other lacks. This research has the head and the rules. The toolbox has the mechanics. Experiment 2 puts them together.

Two things to take from that clip either way:

- **Costume does what prompts could not.** The rejected pull puts two bare forearms of the same colour together. Cuffs, a sleeve against skin, or two people in different values make ownership readable before any hand is drawn well.
- **Three pins when the middle is where the rule breaks.** Two pins guard the ends only.

### 8. Stop correcting heads with words

Three verbal head corrections did not clear the rule (C2, C3, D2). A head in this style is a handful of flat polygons a few dozen pixels across. Paste a reviewed head over a failing one, or paint it. `tools/ai-video/fal_edit.py composite` pastes a boxed region back with a feathered edge. For stills that is deterministic and takes minutes. It does not help inside an AI clip, which is why the pins matter there.

### 9. Process weight

There are 461 files from two days, three render routes (HTML canvas with Playwright, isolated Remotion research entries, the production Remotion tree) and galleries with their own interface checks. The checking is thorough about files and thin about pictures. The first pull review passed a grip that Tiger then rejected.

I would use one page per experiment, mark each frame pass or fail against four named rules (face, arm ownership, support, palette), and drop the gallery checks. If the opening panel is adopted, rebuild it in Remotion and retire the canvas route.

### 10. Where this sits relative to the episode

Episode 1 has its twelve stills and 22 wired still segments, and it is waiting on narration. None of this research is on its path. I would keep it that way. The identity's first real use should be a short passage (experiment 3), then episode 2.

One thing for Tiger to rule on. The face rule as now written is stricter than episode 1's own stills. Some raters in `aigen-06-rating-cards.png` stand in profile with a nose, and one wears glasses. Either the rule starts with the new identity, or those stills need a pass.

## Three experiments

Each needs Tiger's go-ahead and a stated spend before it runs. Hailuo's promotional price ends on 15 October ($0.096 rising to $0.16 per second).

### Experiment 1. Can the head turn?

**Method.** Fix the head as a solid. Draw front, three-quarter and strict profile as stills first: the front with an off-centre split, the profile with at least three planes and a clean front contour. Review them. Then make one clip pinned at all three. Three takes.

**Resolves.** Whether "featureless profiles during motion" is a generation problem or a design problem, and whether on-screen head turns are available at all.

**Acceptable.** The three stills pass the face rule at full size and still read as the `02c` head. In at least two of three takes, no frame sampled every quarter second, and no frame at all between 1 s and 3 s, shows a nose, lip or inner-ear contour, and the face keeps more than two planes.

**If it fails.** Adopt the rule "heads turn across a cut or away from the camera" and stop testing. The toolbox reels already cut to a turned head instead of animating the turn. That turned head still needs a profile that passes.

**Cost.** About $1.10 to $2.40 for three takes, depending on the model and the date.

### Experiment 2. Can two people share effort?

**Method.** The river rescue, done by editing one picture. Start from one approved river frame in look A. Make the reach, the grasp and the pull as edits of that frame, pasting back only the changed region. Give each wrist a contrasting cuff or sleeve. Paste reviewed heads. Pin all three in one clip. Two takes.

**Resolves.** "Readable interacting hands and convincing shared effort", for the suspended pull that the Old Rag reference could not supply.

**Acceptable.** At phone size, someone who has not seen the stills can trace each hand to its owner in all three keyframes. In the clip the helper's body visibly takes load, with hips dropping or the elbow bending, while the other person rises. No face-rule failure in sampled frames. Feet and knees do not slide.

**If it fails.** The rescue becomes three or four stills cut on the narration. The keyframes already are those stills, so nothing is wasted.

**Cost.** About $0.50 to $1.00 for the edits and $0.70 to $1.60 for two takes.

### Experiment 3. Is it one system?

**Method.** The first half-minute of Beat 4 (The Street): the transaction, the wall opening onto the street, then "a fact about his street" as a diagram. Board frames 01, 03 and 04 already sketch it. Remake the pictures in look A, in the palette Tiger picks. Build it in the production Remotion path (`FullEpisode`, `StillFrame`, an existing diagram template), timed to a scratch read of the real lines. No AI video.

**Resolves.** Whether the people, the environment, the reveal and the typeset graphics are one look. What production actually needs (an image mode that preserves the paint, layered flats). And it gives Tiger something to judge in context, which the cup clip cannot, being silent and off-script.

**Acceptable.** Laid out as a strip, the frames share palette, edge hardness and grain with the graphics. Someone who has not read the explanation can say what changed when the wall opened and what it tells them. It renders from the production tree with no research-only code. Tiger says yes, or names what is wrong.

**Cost.** Three to five stills, free in ChatGPT or about $0.12 each on fal. No video.

### Order

If only one runs, run experiment 3. It decides the look, costs almost nothing, and is the only one that moves an episode forward. Experiments 1 and 2 decide how much figure motion the identity can promise.

### What would justify calling the identity final

Experiment 3 with Tiger's yes, and a settled answer from experiments 1 and 2, whether a pass or the fallback rule. Audience recognition cannot be established before something is published, and I would not wait for it.
