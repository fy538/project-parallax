# Parallax research on characters composition facets and surreal storytelling

October 10, 2026

The strongest direction to develop is our faceted, featureless people within a more varied world, with changes of viewpoint revealing relationships at important turns in the argument. Constructivist geometry can supply the structure; selective painted detail can supply the human presence; surrealism can supply the reveal. The opportunity is to make those choices work together across an episode.

This research covers character consistency, composition, the distribution of facets, and surreal storytelling, followed by animation implications and specific experiments. Museum and studio references establish precedents and processes. The Parallax applications below are proposed design choices. Animation performance and audience recognition still need direct tests.

## The reference we are developing

![The liked desk figure with a featureless head modeled through strong asymmetric painted planes](context-tests/02c-desk-faceted-face.png)

The latest desk refinement gives the head a distinct direction and volume through asymmetric painted planes. Hair, sleeve folds and hands supply selected detail. This is our strongest current face reference, following your “better” response.

The face rule applies at every angle and in shadows and reflections: **no eyes, eyebrows, eye sockets, nose, mouth, lips or inner-ear marks; no nose or lip protrusions in profile.** Richness comes from painted form, contour, light and material. Plain outer ear shapes may remain. Head proportions, hair and posture must do more of the identification work.

The sample also exposes the next challenges. Its warm domestic setting is familiar, its desk-to-wall continuity can read as an ordinary curved corner, and the broad amber areas still carry substantial texture. More variation in scene design will tell us more than another small increase in surface detail.

## 1 Character consistency and acting

### What the references establish

Traditional character model sheets specify a character across poses and angles, with size and color guides helping keep the design consistent. The Walt Disney Family Museum also describes personality through how a character moves and reacts. The useful precedent is a repeatable drawing and acting vocabulary. [Museum character worksheet, PDF pages 4 and 6](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/SW.worksheets.final.pdf).

Animator Anthony Wong emphasizes readable silhouettes, a clear line of action and believable weight. A hand gesture hidden inside the torso silhouette loses clarity; a braced body can communicate effort more directly than an impressive but unloaded pose. These are particularly useful principles when faces carry no expressions. [Wong’s posing tutorial, Animation Mentor](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-building-appealing-character-poses-for-animation/).

There is some perceptual support for giving bodies substantial expressive responsibility: Aviezer, Trope and Todorov found that bodies helped viewers distinguish positive from negative reactions at intense emotional peaks. Their experiments used photographs of real people. They do **not** establish that subtle feelings such as doubt or trust will read reliably in our faceless illustrations. [Original Science paper, author-hosted PDF](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/voices.uchicago.edu/dist/f/3051/files/2021/02/Aviezer_Science2012.pdf).

### What should stay constant

Use the head’s overall proportions, hair mass and hairline, neck length, shoulder width, body proportions and characteristic clothing as the character’s anchors. Different people need different combinations of those attributes; a single blank head copied onto different outfits will give us a weak cast.

Keep the *logic* of the facets consistent: a few larger, unequal planes that describe the turning head. Their tones may change with illumination. A bright patch should not become a permanent identity mark merely because it appeared in the first reference. Conversely, every new view should not invent a completely different sculptural head.

This distinction needs a controlled comparison: fixed graphic patches versus planes repainted coherently under new light. The second is my preferred starting point, but we should judge both visually before making it a rule.

### The experiment

Make one eight-view sheet of the desk character: front, left and right three-quarter, left and right profile, rear, looking slightly up, and looking slightly down. Hold clothing, scale, background and light constant. Then hold one three-quarter view constant and compare neutral daylight with side lighting.

Add a separate three-pose strip: listening attentively, pausing in uncertainty, and making a firm decision. Change shoulders, hands, torso and head angle. Keep the head featureless in all three.

Review the sheet for the same apparent person, coherent head volume and absence of forbidden facial parts. Review the poses without captions before adding narration. If uncertainty reads as fatigue, change the gesture or context instead of adding eyes.

For generation, keep a clean character reference separate from the scene reference and the composition sketch. Runway’s current reference workflow supports multiple references and recommends iterating complex changes individually; its sketch controls address placement and composition. That supports testing this organization, but its consistency claims do not prove that our specific faceted heads will survive every view. [Runway Gen-4 Image References](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/40042718905875-Creating-with-Gen-4-Image-References).

## 2 Composition and the range of the world

### What to study in Jacob Lawrence

Compare the crowded station of Migration Series Panel 1 with the sparse table scene of Panel 10. MoMA describes the latter as a pause amid the series’ busier images. The useful lesson is variation in density and rhythm within one visual language. The work’s account of Black migration and injustice is essential to its meaning; borrow compositional principles while developing subjects appropriate to Parallax. [Panel 1](https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2015/onewayticket/static/panel/1/), [Panel 10](https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2015/onewayticket/static/panel/10/).

Lawrence also worked across the sixty panels color by color. For our workflow, the useful adaptation is to review an entire scene sequence together for palette and emphasis before polishing individual frames. [Phillips Collection account of his process](https://www.phillipscollection.org/blog/2019-04-19-part-2-behind-scenes-jacob-lawrence-and-migration-series).

Disney’s layout process likewise treats framing, staging, camera choice and the relationship between adjacent shots as storytelling decisions. That suggests judging our identity through an edited sequence, alongside isolated illustrations. [Disney Animation layout process](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/layout/).

### Six composition studies to make

| Study | Composition to explore | What it would establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A close encounter | Crop at the shoulders; emphasize the interval between a hand, a coin and the other person | Whether a consequential gesture can carry the shot |
| A small person in a large system | Place a figure low and off-center within a broad institutional space | Whether architecture can express scale without overwhelming the human story |
| A group with different attitudes | Use three adults with distinct leaning, sitting and hand positions | Whether faceless people remain differentiated in a shared scene |
| Daylight | Use a bone-dominant setting, ink forms and restrained warm accents | Whether the identity survives outside amber night interiors |
| An obstructed view | Let a doorway or partition conceal part of a relationship | Whether a later reveal has a clear spatial setup |
| A contemporary institution | An assessment room, classroom or public service counter with sparse modern props | Whether the style feels current beyond the bakery and home |

These are exploratory compositions. For a controlled comparison, subsequently render the same encounter in the two strongest layouts while holding character, palette and detail treatment constant.

Aim for an immediate focal relationship and a quieter surrounding field. Use large differences in scale, density and placement. Distinctiveness should survive a thumbnail view and the removal of surface grain.

The current amber-heavy studies also need comparison with the channel’s existing warm monochrome graphics. The [brand system](../../../remotion-templates/BRAND.md) uses a broader ink-to-paper family and restrained accents. A daylight study can test that connection without changing the canonical palette.

## 3 Facets across people architecture and light

### What Feininger contributes

In Gelmeroda VIII, layered prismatic forms link the church with the surrounding space; small geometric figures establish its scale. The Whitney’s account emphasizes that the space around objects was central to the painting. This offers a useful alternative to treating facets as a texture applied independently to each object. [Whitney artwork and interpretation](https://whitney.org/collection/works/386).

For Parallax, a small number of shared directions could connect a head plane, a sleeve fold, a counter edge and a division of light. Preserve visible differences between skin, cloth, wood and empty space. If every surface is broken into equally sized triangles, the hierarchy disappears and the image risks reading as a uniform polygon treatment.

### Three treatments of the same frame

| Treatment | People | Environment | What to learn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local facets | Strong painted head planes; selected folds and hand detail | Broad, quiet walls and furniture | Does the head feel integrated or pasted into another medium? |
| Related planes | The same head and body treatment | A few architectural and light divisions share its directional structure | Can the whole frame feel designed without becoming busy? |
| Extensive facets | The same basic figure proportions | Many angular divisions across room, objects and light | Where does complexity begin to weaken attention and material distinction? |

The middle treatment is my leading candidate. Keep pose, crop, palette, props and texture density fixed across the comparison. Change the distribution of planes alone. Test it in the desk scene first, then in the dinner and rescue to see whether it generalizes.

Describe broad unequal painted planes, selected hard edges and quiet intervening surfaces in prompts. Keep bread, water, furniture and clothing within the same illustrated world. A photorealistic loaf or glossy sculpted head will break the drawing language even when its colors match.

### Detail in motion

Sony Imageworks’ Spider-Verse process is useful as a production precedent: illustrative linework was deliberately drawn, rigged and animated, and stylized rendering extended to environments and materials. The lesson for us is to decide how marks behave over time. It is not evidence that generative video will automatically reproduce that control. [Imageworks production account](https://www.imageworks.com/node/1371).

Separate two kinds of mark in future tests. Hair groups, garment folds and painted head planes should follow the character. A finishing paper texture can remain stable across the frame if that is the intended medium. Randomly appearing and disappearing marks would be an artifact, even in an otherwise painterly clip.

## 4 Surrealism as a recurring storytelling method

### Four precedents with different uses

| Reference to inspect | Relevant operation | Proposed Parallax use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Magritte, The Human Condition, National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/70170-la-condition-humaine) | A representation aligns with the view it conceals, raising a question about what the viewer can verify | A reassuring visible account leaves part of the underlying situation unexamined |
| [De Chirico, The Enigma of a Day, MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/80587) | Architectural space, shadows and distorted perspective create uncertainty | A quiet scene contains an unresolved relationship beyond the immediate encounter |
| [Escher, Relativity, Escher in The Palace](https://escherinhetpaleis.nl/en/about-escher/masterpieces/relativity) | Shared architecture accommodates incompatible orientations and gravity fields | The same situation can look coherent from different positions |
| [Steinberg, Drawing Table Reliefs, Saul Steinberg Foundation](https://saulsteinbergfoundation.org/essay/drawing-table-reliefs/) | Drawing, represented objects and physical objects play against one another | A drawn boundary or depicted surface changes its role in an explanation |

These are references for mechanisms. We can develop them within our painted constructivist language rather than reproducing the artists’ characteristic settings.

### Three recurring operations to test

**Independent shadows.** Begin with a legible ordinary action. Let its shadow suggest a second relationship: two physically separated people become connected, for example. Keep the contradiction simple enough to notice. In philosophical scenes, the shadow can represent an interpretation introduced by narration. It must not function as proof of a person’s motive or an AI’s hidden mental state.

**Continuous surfaces.** A counter, wall or sheet continues into another meaningful space. Make its changed role unmistakable through edges, scale and objects occupying it. Our desk study currently leaves room for an ordinary architectural reading. A useful next attempt would connect a human exchange to the surrounding street or assessment structure, rather than merely bend the tabletop upward.

**A changed viewpoint.** A foreground panel moves, or the camera reveals an adjacent area, exposing a relationship outside the first frame. Establish the concealed space in advance. Let the reveal change what the viewer can conclude while keeping the original human action understandable. This is the strongest candidate for a recurring channel gesture because it connects directly to Parallax’s interest in perspective and the limits of an explanation.

Use one major contradiction in a scene. Show enough ordinary reality for the change to have meaning, then allow a hold for recognition. Avoid stacking impossible stairs, floating objects, mismatched shadows and changing scale into the same frame merely to signal surrealism.

### What our current shadow study reveals

![The warm shop scene with separated foreground hands and connected shadows](surrealism-variations/s04e-constructivist-shadows-featureless.png)

The separated real hands and connected shadow hands create a more explicit contradiction than the desk’s curved surface. That is my reading of the still; an unprompted viewer test should check whether others notice the same relationship.

The foreground heads follow the blank-face direction, but the shadow profiles still contain nose- and lip-like notches. Future refinements need the face rule applied to those silhouettes too. Also give the shadow shape a clear narrative purpose: a warm connection could imply care, which is a different claim from the episode’s uncertainty about motives.

## Animation implications across all four areas

No primary source reviewed here establishes that featureless faces, constructivism or surrealism is universally easiest for AI image-to-video. The practical question is which combinations of drawing, action and control preserve our identity while completing the intended action.

Runway’s current image-to-video guide assigns composition, subjects, lighting and style to the input image, while the prompt describes movement. It recommends beginning with the essential motion, warns that input artifacts can intensify, and notes that implied motion in an image can conflict with a prompt. For us, this makes a clear starting pose and unambiguous action more useful than a long repetition of style adjectives. [Runway image-to-video guide](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/48324313115155-Image-to-Video-Prompting-Guide).

| Scene requirement | Route worth testing | What needs checking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A quiet pause or single gesture | Image-to-video from an approved still | Head planes, hand shape, completion of the gesture and unwanted camera movement |
| An exact shadow or panel reveal | Separate illustrated elements animated in Remotion | Layer preparation, concealed background reconstruction, alignment and timing |
| An ambitious human action | Key poses, followed by image-to-video or a movement reference and video restyling | Weight, support, contact, occlusion and preservation of featureless heads |
| A precise spatial contradiction | Designed before-and-after frames; compare an authored transition with generative interpolation | Whether the contradiction remains intentional and legible throughout |

A flattened PNG does not supply separate shadow or character layers. An authored reveal would require preparing those elements and any background revealed behind them. This can make the key relationship reviewable before animation.

For motion guided by an existing performance, Luma documents video input with keyframes and a character reference; Runway Aleph documents video restyling. These are candidates to test for complex movement, with no demonstrated result for our style yet. [Luma Ray3 Modify guide](https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/ray3-modify-user-guide), [Runway Aleph 2.0 guide](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/52150503729171-Aleph-2-0-Prompting-Guide).

Runway Act-Two’s published input recommendations specify defined facial features such as eyes and a mouth. That makes it a less suitable starting point for this identity; retain the featureless rule when selecting a workflow. [Act-Two input guidance](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/42311337895827-Performance-Capture-with-Act-Two).

Geometric abstraction also needs its own movement vocabulary. Schlemmer’s Triadic Ballet is an instructive precedent: its sculptural costumes both shaped and constrained movement. Our figures should retain flexible bodies, believable support and expressive asymmetry rather than become rigid mannequins. [Schlemmer estate account](https://www.schlemmer.org/triadic-ballet).

Evaluate motion amount and action completion separately from consistency. VBench++ explicitly distinguishes temporal stability, subject consistency and dynamic degree; a nearly motionless clip can hide weaknesses in stability under movement. VBench 2.0 separately addresses anatomy, physics and other aspects of faithful action. These benchmarks inform our review categories; they do not rank the proposed art directions. [VBench++](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13503v1), [VBench 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21755).

The [three previously prepared motion trials](context-tests/README.md#proposed-motion-trials) remain unrun and await approval for the fal.ai upload and charge. This research does not change that pending payload or authorize an alternative provider.

## A six frame episode pilot

This proposed board draws on [ai-care-motives script v1.7](../../../episodes/ai-care-motives/script.md), particularly the recurring shop motif. It samples moments across the episode rather than forming six consecutive shots. Its purpose is to test whether people, surreal imagery and analytical graphics belong to one publication.

| Frame | Narration context | Visual proposal | Main test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Ordinary help | Beat 2, the child receives the usual price and correct change | Quiet wide counter scene, featureless faceted heads, clearly separated hands and a painted loaf | Human readability and selective detail |
| 2 Two accounts | Beat 2, duty and prudence | Repeat the identical transaction in two panels; add the two reasons in typeset captions | Same behavior stays visually identical as interpretation changes |
| 3 The surrounding street | Beat 4, honesty is also a fact about the street | Reveal the adjacent street and other people through a prepared opening or wider view | Viewpoint adds context without implying access to a mind |
| 4 Analytical explanation | Beat 4, other customers, neighbors and a competitor | Match a counter edge to a diagram baseline; retain ink and paper, restrained accents and existing Plex typography | Illustration-to-graphic continuity and clear hierarchy |
| 5 Watching removed | Beat 5, what happens when perceived assessment is absent? | Return to the same window as passers leave; hold the transaction unresolved | A changed condition poses a question without inventing dishonesty |
| 6 Return to the viewer | Beat 8, ask what supports the reassuring judgment | Contemporary desk in daylight, the liked head treatment and room for a quiet hold | Range beyond the bakery and amber night scenes |

Build the river rescue as a separate action study: a clear starting pose, the pull, and a supported ending pose. The existing clasp needs further mechanical review. Do not make a successful rescue clip a prerequisite for assessing the six-frame visual sequence.

## Experiments in priority order

1. **One character sheet and an acting strip.** Establish the head from different angles before propagating it across a cast. Compare lighting separately from geometry.
2. **Six composition thumbnails.** Explore the layouts above using broad shapes first. Develop the strongest two as finished stills of the same encounter.
3. **Three facet distributions.** Hold the chosen scene constant and compare local, related and extensive planes. Keep texture density fixed.
4. **Three surreal before-and-after pairs.** Use the same shop encounter to compare independent shadows, continuous surfaces and a changed viewpoint. Each pair must have a one-sentence narrative purpose.
5. **The six-frame episode board.** Include one finished analytical graphic. Review the entire sequence for rhythm and shared identity before polishing further.
6. **Motion trials of the selected drawings.** Require the intended action to occur. Compare rich and selective texture with matched settings and repeat samples before attributing a result to detail level.

Keep each comparison’s input references, exact prompt, model/version, settings and every attempt, including rejected outputs. Separate layout, lighting, facet distribution and texture tests so a preference has an interpretable cause. One attractive output is a useful candidate; repeatable acceptable outputs establish a usable process.

## How to judge the next round

| Question | Practical review |
| --- | --- |
| Does it remain the same person? | Mix the new views with a few other character designs and check recognition without labels |
| Does the pose communicate? | Show the acting strip without captions; ask viewers what the person is doing or feeling before offering choices |
| Is the composition clear? | Inspect at thumbnail size and in grayscale; identify the first point of attention and the consequential relationship |
| Is the surreal operation legible? | Show the before and after without explaining the trick; ask what changed and what that lets the viewer infer |
| Does it feel like Parallax? | Mix selected new scenes with our earlier alternatives; test grouping by visual family with titles and logos removed |
| Does motion preserve the drawing? | Review at normal speed, then inspect key moments for invented facial parts, shifting facets, hand changes and texture flicker |
| Does the action work? | Check the actual ending pose, object continuity and body support; a stable frozen image does not pass an action test |

These are practical art-direction reviews, not validated audience metrics. A small viewer check can expose misunderstandings; it cannot establish market-wide distinctiveness.

My preferred combination to test first is the liked faceted head, quieter broad environments, more varied framing and occasional spatial reveals. Keep strong painted detail where people act and where an object matters. Let the episode’s argument decide when the world becomes surreal.
