## Contemporary desk with rich texture

The desk continues upward into an amber architectural plane. Dense printed marks carry the richness of the bakery reference into a contemporary setting.

## Contemporary desk with selective texture

The same figure, pose, furniture and lighting, with fewer fine marks on the desktop and dark wall. Hair, garment folds and hand contours retain detail. The amber vertical plane remains visibly textured, so this is a selective reduction rather than a fully flat treatment.

![Same contemporary desk composition with quieter painted surfaces](02-desk-selective.png)

The continuity of desk and wall is visible, although it can also read as a conventional curved corner. A future architectural reveal should make the change of spatial meaning more explicit.

## River rescue

The braced body, connected hands and diagonal bank establish urgency. The final face correction removes the swimmer's nose and mouth contour. The painted water and large figure shapes keep the scene within the illustrated world.

![Featureless rescuer on a riverbank holding a swimmer's hand with a strong diagonal between their bodies](04-river-rescue.png)

The generated contact is closer to a clasp around the hands and wrist than the reciprocal forearm grip requested in the original prompt. The grip and body mechanics need scrutiny in motion before this becomes a production shot. The exterior also retains more small landscape marks than the sparse setting proposed in the prompt.

## Drawing rules carried forward

- Faces have no eyes, eyebrows, eye sockets, nose, mouth, lips or inner-ear lines. Form comes from a few broad painted planes and a simple head contour.
- Clothing, grouped hair shapes, hands and consequential objects receive selected detail.
- Ink, amber, bone and walnut remain the main palette; the river uses muted grey-green.
- Surreal relationships belong in deliberately chosen spatial or shadow changes. Ordinary human scenes remain available alongside them.

These are stills made with the built-in image tool. The texture pair is an edit-based comparison; the other scenes test how the identity transfers across subjects.
