# Parallax Logo — Round 3 (Composition Exploration)

> Builds on Round 2 survivors (B1, B3, E1) and explores six "slightly more complicated" directions with 2 prompts each. 12 prompts total.

## What's different from Round 2

Round 2 was *two primitives + offset*. Round 3 is *one concept, but more elements doing the work*. Each direction asks the viewer to do slightly more cognitive work to complete the read, in exchange for more visual richness.

Same locked constraints:
- Two colors max + bone background
- Bauhaus geometric register
- No typography, no figurative reference, no decorative outline
- Square 1:1 aspect ratio

## Paste this primer once before the first prompt

```
I'm designing a logo mark for Parallax, an editorial geopolitics YouTube channel. The brand register is mid-century corporate-modernist — Paul Rand for IBM, Massimo Vignelli, Josef Albers, Bauhaus geometric precision. Editorial publication-mark energy, NOT consumer-tech or crypto. Restrict to two flat colors on a bone background (no third color, no gradients, no drop shadows, no 3D, no outlines, no text). Each prompt produces one 1024×1024 square image.
```

## Direction 1 — Modular Grid (one cell shifted)

The mark is a grid where most cells are perfectly aligned and one is offset. Brand reads as "the channel that notices what's out of place."

### G1 — 3×3 grid, center cell offset and accented

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A perfectly regular 3×3 grid of nine small solid squares, all identical size, arranged with consistent gaps between cells (the gap is 30% of one cell's width). The whole 3×3 grid occupies the central 45% of the frame. Eight of the nine cells are perfectly axis-aligned in their grid positions. The center cell (position row 2, column 2) is offset 35% of one cell's width to the right of its grid position — clearly out of alignment.

Style: Bauhaus modular grid, Massimo Vignelli register, flat vector illustration. Mid-century editorial mark.

Color: The eight aligned cells are solid ink (#1C1814). The center (offset) cell is solid amber gold (#E5A544). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: All cells are perfect squares — no rotation, no taper, no rounded corners. The offset of the center cell is the entire visual concept, and must be clearly visible at thumbnail size. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, outlines, or ornament. Do not add text. Do not use green, blue, purple, walnut, umber.
```

### G2 — 4×4 grid, missing cell with displaced mark below

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A perfectly regular 4×4 grid of sixteen small solid squares, all identical size, arranged with consistent gaps between cells (gap is 25% of one cell's width). The whole 4×4 grid occupies the central 55% of the frame. Fifteen of the sixteen cells are filled. The cell at row 2, column 2 is empty (background shows through). Directly below the empty cell, in the otherwise blank space between the empty cell's grid row and the row below it, sits a smaller solid square — 50% the size of a grid cell, axis-aligned, centered under the empty position — as if the missing cell has fallen out of the grid and landed below.

Style: Bauhaus modular grid, MIT Media Lab algorithmic identity register, flat vector illustration.

Color: The fifteen aligned cells are solid ink (#1C1814). The smaller displaced cell is solid amber gold (#E5A544). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: The grid alignment must be perfect except for the single empty position and the single displaced smaller cell. Do not round any corners. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, outlines, or ornament. Do not use green, blue, purple, walnut, umber.
```

## Direction 2 — Frame + Offset Mark

A clean geometric frame contains a smaller mark, but the inner mark is shifted off-center. Editorial publication-mark register.

### F1 — Square border with offset internal triangle

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A thin axis-aligned square border occupying the central 60% of the frame, centered. The border line weight is 3% of the frame width. Inside the border, in the lower-right quadrant of the interior space (clearly off-center), sits a smaller solid equilateral triangle pointing upward. The triangle's size is 30% of the inner space's width. Generous negative space inside the frame in the upper and left areas.

Style: Mid-century editorial publication mark, the kind seen on a serious quarterly's masthead. Bauhaus geometric precision, flat vector illustration.

Color: The square border is solid ink (#1C1814). The triangle inside is solid amber gold (#E5A544). Background (both outside the frame and inside the empty regions of the frame) is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: The border is a clean stroke, not a filled square with a smaller bone square inside. The triangle is fully contained within the border, never touching it. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, decorative corners, or ornament. Do not add text. Do not use green, blue, purple, walnut, umber.
```

### F2 — Two concentric rings with offset accent dot

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: Two thin concentric circular rings, sharing the same center, the outer ring occupying 55% of the frame's diameter and the inner ring 35%. Both rings are stroke-only (not filled), with line weight 3% of the frame width. Between the two rings, in the upper-right region of the annular space (clearly off-center, not on the cardinal axes), sits a small solid dot. The dot's diameter is roughly 8% of the frame width.

Style: Editorial publication mark with surveying/astronomical instrument overtones, mid-century corporate-modernist register. Flat vector illustration.

Color: Both rings are solid ink (#1C1814). The dot is solid amber gold (#E5A544). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: Rings are perfect circles, perfectly concentric. The dot is positioned between the two rings (not inside the inner ring, not outside the outer ring), and not on the cardinal axes (12, 3, 6, or 9 o'clock). Do not add tick marks, compass directions, or scale labels. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, or ornament. Do not use green, blue, purple, walnut, umber.
```

## Direction 3 — Negative-Space Cut-Out

A solid form with a smaller form punched through it. Negative space carries the parallax concept.

### N1 — Solid circle with offset circular hole (crescent)

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A single solid circle of diameter equal to 60% of the frame width, centered in the frame. A smaller circular hole is punched out of it — the hole's diameter is 45% of the larger circle's diameter, and the hole is offset 25% of the larger circle's radius to the upper-right of the larger circle's center. The result is a crescent shape with a thick part on the lower-left and a thin part on the upper-right.

Style: Bauhaus geometric precision, flat vector illustration. Mid-century editorial mark in the "smart logo" register — viewers feel clever for noticing the negative-space construction. WWF / FedEx-arrow lineage.

Color: The solid (non-hole) portion is solid amber gold (#E5A544). The hole shows the background through it. Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly two colors total.

Constraints: The hole is a perfect circle, cleanly punched, no soft edges. Do not render the crescent as a moon with craters or texture. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, outlines, or ornament. Do not add text. Do not reference astronomy, weather, or religious symbolism. Do not use green, blue, purple, oxblood, walnut, umber.
```

### N2 — Solid square with offset square hole

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A single solid axis-aligned square of side length equal to 55% of the frame width, centered. A smaller axis-aligned square hole is punched out of it — the hole's side length is 40% of the larger square's side length, and the hole is offset such that its center sits at the position 75% along the diagonal from the larger square's center toward the lower-right corner. The result is an asymmetric "C-shape" or "bracket" form with the hole near one corner.

Style: Bauhaus geometric precision, Josef Albers register (specifically his negative-space studies), flat vector illustration.

Color: The solid (non-hole) portion is solid amber gold (#E5A544). The hole shows the background through it. Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly two colors total.

Constraints: Both the outer square and the inner hole are perfectly axis-aligned (no rotation). Edges are clean. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, outlines, or ornament. Do not round any corners. Do not add text. Do not use green, blue, purple, oxblood, walnut, umber.
```

## Direction 4 — Implied Form (Kanizsa-Style)

Discrete small shapes whose arrangement makes the eye complete a larger form between them.

### K1 — Three Pac-Man wedges, implied triangle

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: Three identical small solid "Pac-Man wedges" — each is a circle with a triangular sector (60-degree wedge) removed, leaving three-quarters of a circle. The three wedges are positioned at the corners of an implied equilateral triangle: one at top center, one at lower left, one at lower right. Each wedge's missing sector points inward toward the center of the implied triangle, so the viewer's eye naturally completes a clean white equilateral triangle in the negative space between the three wedges. The implied triangle occupies the central 40% of the frame.

Style: Kanizsa illusion applied to brand mark. Bauhaus geometric precision, flat vector illustration. The mark IS the conceptual move of the channel — pattern recognition across separated data points.

Color: All three wedges are solid amber gold (#E5A544). Background (and the implied interior triangle) is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly two colors total.

Constraints: The three wedges must be identical in size and the wedge angle must be exactly the right amount so the implied triangle reads cleanly. Each wedge's missing sector is precisely aimed at the implied triangle's center. Do not draw any actual triangle outline. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, or ornament. Do not use green, blue, purple, oxblood, walnut, umber.
```

### K2 — Four right-angle brackets, implied square, one shifted

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: Four identical small solid right-angle brackets (each bracket is an L-shape: two short perpendicular bars meeting at a corner, like a printer's crop mark). The four brackets are positioned at the corners of an implied square: each bracket sits at one corner with its inner angle pointing toward the implied square's center. The implied square would occupy the central 45% of the frame. Three of the four brackets are in their exact corner positions. The fourth bracket — the upper-right one — is offset 8% inward (toward the center) from where it should be, breaking the implied square's perfect symmetry.

Style: Photographic crop marks elevated to brand mark, mid-century editorial register, flat vector illustration. Reads as "almost a square, but the upper-right corner is slightly closer than expected."

Color: All four brackets are solid ink (#1C1814). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). The implied square interior is bone (negative space). Exactly two colors total.

Constraints: The brackets are equal in arm length and line weight. The fourth bracket's offset is along the diagonal toward the center, not laterally. Do not draw any actual square outline. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, or ornament. Do not use green, blue, purple, walnut, umber.
```

## Direction 5 — Composite Primitives (small forms building a larger one)

A larger gestalt form constructed from many smaller modular pieces. Paul Rand IBM lineage.

### C1 — Triangle built from horizontal bars, one bar accented

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A larger upward-pointing equilateral triangle built from nine horizontal solid bars stacked vertically with small consistent gaps between them. The bottom bar is the widest (full base of the triangle); each successive bar above is shorter than the one below it, tapering toward the apex; the top bar is the shortest (near the apex). All bars share the same center x-axis. The triangular silhouette of the assembly is clearly readable. The whole triangle occupies the central 55% of the frame.

Style: Paul Rand IBM 8-bar logo register applied to a triangular gestalt, Bauhaus geometric precision, flat vector illustration.

Color: Eight of the nine bars are solid ink (#1C1814). The fifth bar from the bottom (the middle bar) is solid amber gold (#E5A544). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: All bars perfectly horizontal, axis-aligned. Bars taper in width following the triangle's slope cleanly. Gaps between bars are equal. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, or ornament. Do not round bar ends. Do not add text. Do not use green, blue, purple, oxblood, walnut, umber.
```

### C2 — Circle built from radial spokes, one accented and elongated

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A larger circle built from twelve solid radial spokes (short straight bars) arranged like clock-hour marks around a common center point. Each spoke is a thin rectangle pointing outward from the center. Eleven of the twelve spokes are identical in length (each is 15% of the frame width) and arranged at perfectly regular 30-degree intervals around the center. The twelfth spoke — at the 2 o'clock position — is 35% longer than the others, clearly breaking the otherwise uniform clock-face pattern. The overall circular silhouette of the assembly occupies the central 55% of the frame.

Style: Modular composite mark, mid-century editorial register, Bauhaus geometric precision, flat vector illustration. Reads as a clock face or compass rose where one direction is emphasized.

Color: The eleven uniform spokes are solid ink (#1C1814). The twelfth (elongated) spoke is solid amber gold (#E5A544). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: Spokes are perfectly straight thin rectangles. Angular intervals between spokes are exactly 30 degrees. Do not draw a central hub circle. Do not draw an outer circular ring. Do not add numerals, ticks, or cardinal directions. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, or ornament. Do not use green, blue, purple, oxblood, walnut, umber.
```

## Direction 6 — Asymmetric Punctuation (large form + small accent)

A primary geometric form plus a single small accent element. The accent makes it dynamic.

### A1 — Large ink triangle with single amber dot

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A single large solid upward-pointing equilateral triangle, occupying the central 60% of the frame, centered. Inside the triangle, near (but not at) the upper apex — roughly 25% of the way down from the apex along the triangle's vertical centerline, then offset 8% to the right of that centerline — sits a single small solid dot. The dot's diameter is 6% of the triangle's height. The dot's placement is deliberately off-center, like a deliberate mark or pin placed by a careful observer.

Style: Massimo Vignelli register, the kind of asymmetric punctuation seen in mid-century editorial marks. Flat vector illustration, Bauhaus geometric precision.

Color: The triangle is solid ink (#1C1814). The dot is solid amber gold (#E5A544). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: The triangle is a perfect equilateral, point up, axis-aligned. The dot is a perfect circle, fully contained inside the triangle, clearly off the triangle's centerline. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, outlines, or ornament. Do not add text or letters. Do not use green, blue, purple, oxblood, walnut, umber.
```

### A2 — Large amber square with external corner bracket

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A single large solid axis-aligned square, occupying the central 55% of the frame, centered. Touching the square's upper-right corner externally (outside the square, but with its inner angle exactly meeting the square's corner) sits a single small right-angle bracket — an L-shape, two short perpendicular bars meeting at a right angle. The bracket's arms each extend outward from the square's corner by 12% of the square's side length, in the up and right directions respectively. The bracket reads as an editor's revision mark, a crop mark, or a punctuation gesture pointing at one corner.

Style: Mid-century editorial mark with the energy of a magazine cover where a small typographic gesture punctuates a larger field of color. Bauhaus geometric precision, flat vector illustration.

Color: The square is solid amber gold (#E5A544). The bracket is solid ink (#1C1814). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: The square is perfectly axis-aligned. The bracket sits exactly at the upper-right corner, no gap. Bracket line weight is consistent in both arms. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, outlines around the square, or ornament. Do not add text. Do not use green, blue, purple, oxblood, walnut, umber.
```

## After you generate

Same playbook as Round 2:

1. **Iterate within chat with single-instruction edits** rather than re-prompting. "Make the offset 50% larger." "Move the dot to the lower-left corner instead." "Increase the gap between cells."
2. **Batch variants of a strong candidate** in a 2×2 grid: *"Generate four variants of this composition with the offset distance at 5%, 15%, 25%, 35%, laid out as a 2×2 grid."*
3. **Cross-direction comparison** when finalists emerge: paste 3-4 finalists back in one message and ask GPT to assess (32px legibility / brand register match / distinctiveness on a YouTube thumbnail grid).

## What to watch for in this round

These compositions ask the viewer to do more cognitive work than the Round 2 marks did. That's the gamble — more sophisticated reads can either elevate the brand or confuse the viewer.

For each finalist, run the **30-second test**: imagine someone seeing the mark for the first time on a YouTube avatar. Can they describe the mark to a friend in one sentence after looking at it for 30 seconds?

- "Three Pac-Man shapes that make a triangle" ✓
- "A square with a square hole punched out of one corner" ✓
- "A grid where one piece is offset" ✓
- "A triangle with a dot in it" — possibly too plain, ambiguous
- "Some shapes and stuff" ✗

If the one-sentence description survives, the mark is doing real work. If it doesn't, it's an art piece, not a logo.
