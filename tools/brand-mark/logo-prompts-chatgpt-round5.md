# Parallax Logo — Round 5 (Compound Glyph Marks)

> Builds on the typographic-symbol vocabulary surfaced in the Round 4 explorations (Λ, /, =, ), bent line, bisected circle, Y, II). **Treats the logo as a custom punctuation mark** — a single fused glyph that combines a triangle or square outline with one secondary geometric element. Returns to the bone background (the brand-canonical light-mode surface).

## Why this direction is promising

The candidates that emerged from the ChatGPT runs revealed something the earlier rounds were dancing around: the strongest marks read as **invented punctuation, not as illustrations**. Like a custom glyph in a typeface — a single character the channel uses to sign its name.

The mechanic for this round:

- **Two elements per mark, both at typographic scale** — sized like glyphs in a row, not like a shape with an annotation
- **Hairline stroke weight** — outlines, not fills. Roughly 0.7–1.0% of frame width (much thinner than Round 4)
- **Bone background** — Meridian light-mode primary, the established brand surface
- **One-or-two-color discipline maintained** — ink for the structural element, amber as accent

Same hard constraints as prior rounds:
- No typography, no figurative reference, no decorative ornament
- Bauhaus geometric precision throughout
- Square 1:1 aspect ratio

## T-Family — Triangle as Anchor

### T-A — "Λ/" compound (triangle and slash side-by-side)

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: Two glyph-scale elements placed side by side as if on a typographic baseline, occupying the central 50% of the frame. On the left: a triangle rendered as a thin "Λ" outline — two diagonal lines meeting at an apex point, no base line drawn, hairline stroke. On the right, immediately adjacent (very small gap, like a tight ligature): a single diagonal slash line at roughly 70 degrees from horizontal, of the same height as the Λ. The two elements together read as a single compound symbol — an invented piece of punctuation.

Style: Custom typographic glyph in the Bauhaus / Plex Mono lineage. Mid-century editorial mark, drafting precision. Hairline stroke weight throughout — both elements rendered as the channel's signature compound punctuation mark.

Color: The Λ is solid ink (#1C1814). The slash is solid amber gold (#E5A544). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: Both elements at exactly the same height, sharing the same baseline. Hairline stroke weight (approximately 0.8% of frame width) — thin and precise, not heavy. The Λ has no base/horizontal line drawn — it is an open inverted-V shape, two strokes only. The slash is a single straight line, not a fraction divider. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, or ornament. Do not add text or other letters. Do not use green, blue, purple, oxblood, walnut, umber.
```

## What's different about this round

The previous rounds were *forms*. This round is *glyphs*. Three structural shifts:

1. **Both elements at typographic scale.** Both pieces of the compound mark are sized like glyphs on a baseline, not like a shape with an annotation. The triangle and the slash in T-A are sibling characters, not figure and ornament.

2. **Hairline stroke weight.** ~0.8% of frame width (≈8 pixels at 1024×1024) — much thinner than Round 4's 1.5%. This is the difference between "a logo made of geometric shapes" and "a custom glyph in a typeface."

3. **Open Λ instead of filled triangle.** The triangle becomes the two diagonal strokes meeting at an apex, with no base line — like the Greek capital lambda or the Plex Mono caret character. This is a deliberate inheritance from your ChatGPT-rendered candidates that were doing this exact move.

## My favorite picks within this round

If I were running these and triaging fast, the order I'd test:

1. **T-F (Λ with apex dot)** — single most minimal and distinctive. The amber dot on the apex reads as a directional marker, a summit pin, or a typographic accent. Would survive at 32px better than any other in this round.

2. **T-D (Λ pierced by =)** — closest extension of what Round 4 was reaching for, now in the refined glyph register. The double-bar pierce is more distinctive than a single line.

3. **T-A (Λ/ compound)** — invented punctuation mark. Highest "looks like a custom typeface" energy.

4. **S-B (square pierced by diagonal slash)** — pure modernist. Could be a Massimo Vignelli sketch.

5. **T-E or S-D (inscribed in circle, ink-only)** — the two-color variants. Most editorial / publication-brand register. Most restrained.

## Iteration tactics

Same playbook — single-instruction edits within the same chat:

- *"Make the slash thinner — match the triangle's stroke weight exactly."*
- *"Move the dot up so it sits on top of the apex, not above it."*
- *"Increase the size of the equals sign so it matches the Λ's width exactly."*
- *"Try the compound symbol with the slash on the LEFT of the triangle instead of the right."*
- *"Render this same composition with the amber and ink swapped."*

When you have 3-4 finalists across all five rounds, paste them back together and ask GPT to critique by:
1. Legibility at 32×32 favicon size
2. Brand register (publication mark vs. tech-startup vs. crypto)
3. Distinctiveness on a YouTube thumbnail grid
4. Survives one-color (amber-only or ink-only) reduction

The 30-second test still applies: if someone can describe the mark to a friend in one sentence after 30 seconds of looking, it's a real logo.
