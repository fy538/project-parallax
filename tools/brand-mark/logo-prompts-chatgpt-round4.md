# Parallax Logo — Round 4 (Primitive + Thin Geometric Lines)

> Combines the Round 1/2 survivors (triangle, square) with **thin geometric lines** — drafting marks, registration crosses, plumb references, inscribed-circle construction. Pulls in the channel's existing "looking carefully at the structure" thesis via instrument-of-observation visual language.

## Why this direction is promising

The Round 1–3 marks were all *forms without scaffolding*. Lines add what the editorial-analytical register has always quietly wanted:

- **Measurement / surveying overtone.** Crosshair, plumb line, tick mark, registration cross — these are the visual language of the channel's actual practice (carefully reading structure).
- **Geometric construction visible.** Classical drafting — the inscribed circle, the bisecting line, the radial axis — gives the mark a "this was designed, not generated" feel.
- **Asymmetric balance.** A line cutting through a primitive can punctuate it in ways pure overlap can't.
- **Editorial layout DNA.** Rule lines, crop marks, registration marks are the printer's language — directly aligned to "publication brand" energy.

Same locked constraints:
- Two colors max + bone background
- Bauhaus / draftsman geometric precision
- Lines are *thin* — 1-2% of frame width is the target weight
- No typography, no figurative reference, no decorative outline
- Square 1:1 aspect ratio

## T-Family — Triangle plus Lines

### T1 — Triangle pierced by horizontal line (the horizon cut)

```
Draw a logo mark, square 1:1 aspect ratio.

Composition: A single solid equilateral triangle, point up, occupying the central 50% of the frame, centered. A single thin straight horizontal line passes directly through the triangle at the height roughly 60% from the apex down (i.e., crossing through the lower portion of the triangle's interior). The line extends beyond both sides of the triangle, reaching out to approximately 80% of the frame's width, well past the triangle's edges. The line is thin — about 1.5% of the frame width in stroke thickness.

Style: Bauhaus geometric precision, drafting-line register, mid-century editorial mark. The composition reads as "a horizon line cutting through a structure" — measurement plus form.

Color: The triangle is solid amber gold (#E5A544). The horizontal line is solid ink (#1C1814). Background is solid bone (#F0E6D0). Exactly three colors.

Constraints: The line is perfectly horizontal, perfectly straight, of uniform thickness end-to-end. The line passes in front of the triangle (visible over the amber). Do not add tick marks, arrowheads, or end caps. Do not add gradients, drop shadows, glow, 3D, or ornament. Do not add text. Do not use green, blue, purple, oxblood, walnut, umber.
```

## What to watch for in Round 4

The "primitive + line" pattern produces marks that read very differently depending on **line position** and **line extension**. Things to look for:

- **Line extension length.** Lines that extend just a little past the primitive feel cramped. Lines that extend a lot feel sprawling. Aim for the line being ~20–30% wider/taller than the primitive — enough to read as "passing through," not as "tangent to."
- **Line weight.** Too thin and the mark loses presence at thumbnail size. Too thick and the line competes with the primitive. ~1.5% of frame width (15 pixels at 1024×1024) is the target — if a generation comes back too thin, iterate with: *"Increase line weight by 50%."*
- **Off-center placement.** The horizontal-line marks (T1, S5) are deliberately at non-center positions (60% down for T1, 1/3 down for S5). That asymmetry is editorially deliberate — a centered line cuts the form in half visually, which reads as "split"; an off-center line reads as "axis of analysis." Don't let the model default to centering everything.
- **Surveying register vs gunsight register.** The crosshair marks (T2, S2) could read as "theodolite" or as "rifle scope" depending on stroke and proportions. Keep ticks off, keep the cross thin, and the register lands on surveying/observation.

## Iteration tactics

Same as previous rounds — single-instruction edits beat re-prompting:

- *"Move the horizontal line 10% higher."*
- *"Make the inscribed circle 30% larger relative to the triangle."*
- *"Reduce the line weight by 30%."*
- *"Extend the diagonal line so it reaches the frame's edges."*
- *"Try the same composition with oxblood as the primitive and amber as the line."*

When you have 3-4 finalists across all four rounds, paste them back together and ask GPT to critique by:
1. Legibility at 32×32 favicon size
2. Whether the mark reads as "publication brand" or as "tech startup"
3. Distinctiveness on a YouTube thumbnail grid

The 30-second test from Round 3 still applies: if you can describe the mark to a friend in one sentence after looking at it for 30 seconds, it's a real logo. If you can't, it's an exercise.
