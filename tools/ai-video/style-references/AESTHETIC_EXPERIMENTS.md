# Parallax — Grounding Register Aesthetic Experiments

> Created: May 4, 2026
> Purpose: Test alternatives to the photoreal-mannequin convention for Register 3 (Grounding) before locking the channel's house style.
> Context: The mannequin convention is becoming generic across AI-geopolitics-explainer channels. These six directions are candidates for a more distinctive Parallax-specific aesthetic.

## Why this exists

The mannequin convention solves three editorial problems: (1) honesty signal — "this isn't documentary footage," (2) depersonalization — "this is The Engineer, not a specific person," (3) quality safeguard — AI can't do faces well. Any alternative aesthetic must solve all three. These six directions all do.

The lock-in cost right now is essentially zero — no episode has shipped using mannequin yet. After EP01 ships, switching gets expensive. So this is the right moment to test alternatives.

## Test methodology

1. **Run the cleanroom prompt through all six directions.** This is your apples-to-apples control. Same scene, six aesthetic worlds. Judge which one feels most like Parallax.
2. **Test the top 2-3 winners on the secondary scenes.** The cleanroom is forgiving (technical environment, masked workers). Historical reconstruction (Direction 1's secondary), corridor/abstract space (Direction 2's secondary), and the trap-encirclement metaphor are harder. The aesthetic that holds across all three is the keeper.
3. **Generate at 16:9 aspect ratio (1920×1080 or 1792×1024 in DALL-E).** Match the channel's delivery spec.
4. **For ChatGPT specifically:** DALL-E 3 / GPT Image 2 follow positive vocabulary much better than negative prompts. The prompts below are heavily positive-loaded for that reason — short negative blocks at the end only for the most predictable failure modes.

## Direction 1: Constructivist figures

**One-line:** Drop photoreal entirely. Render people in the same Soviet-constructivist illustration language as Register 2's atmospheric backgrounds.

### Scene A — Cleanroom (control)

```
Soviet constructivist illustration in the style of Alexander Rodchenko,
El Lissitzky's photomontage, and Frans Masereel's industrial woodcuts.
Interior of a semiconductor fabrication cleanroom rendered as a
constructivist composition: angular workstations, stacked geometric
machinery, FOUP carriers on automated tracks. Three heroic monumentalist
worker figures in white bunny suits with full-face shields, faces shown
as flat color planes — simplified geometric features, no realistic
detail. Low horizon line, monumentalist scale, bold compositional
confidence. Restricted palette: deep ink (#1C1814), walnut (#5C4A3D),
burnt amber and gold (#C4A747), rust (#A64D46), and bone (#F0E6D0) on
paper (#F5F0E8) background. Color-blocked forms, no soft shading, no
gradients. Editorial weight, propaganda-poster dynamism. 16:9 aspect
ratio.

Avoid: photorealistic rendering, 3D render look, cool blue or teal,
Adobe stock aesthetic, gradient-heavy fills, Memphis design.
```

### Scene B — 1941 historical reconstruction

```
Soviet constructivist illustration in the style of Rodchenko and
El Lissitzky's photomontage tradition. A presidential office in
December 1941. A seated figure in a dark wool suit at a heavy wooden
desk, hand poised over a document. Period-accurate brass desk lamp,
dark wood paneling, side window with low-angle warm tungsten light
streaming in. The figure rendered with simplified planar features —
flat color planes suggesting facial structure without realistic
detail. Other suited figures standing at the edge of frame, faces
equally simplified. Bold geometric forms, monumentalist composition,
low horizon. Restricted palette: deep ink, walnut, umber, burnt amber,
rust, bone on paper background. Color-blocked, no shading, no
gradients. Mood: civilizational stakes, historical gravity,
propaganda-poster weight.

Avoid: photorealistic, cartoon, 3D render, cool tones.
```

## Direction 2: Silhouettes / shadow figures

**One-line:** Photoreal warm environments, fully opaque solid-black silhouette figures. Saul Bass title-sequence energy.

### Scene A — Cleanroom (control)

```
Editorial photography composition: interior of a semiconductor
fabrication cleanroom rendered photorealistically with warm amber
lithography lighting. Three figures rendered as pure black silhouettes
— hard-edged, full-body, fully opaque, no facial or clothing detail
whatsoever — operating wafer-handling equipment in the foreground. The
silhouettes are graphic forms in space, not portraits. The environment
is fully detailed and photoreal: yellow lithography light, FOUP carriers,
equipment, stainless steel surfaces, all rendered with deep umber
shadows giving way to amber highlights. Saul Bass title-sequence
aesthetic, Otto Preminger graphic style. Heavy chiaroscuro, dramatic
backlight rim defining the silhouettes against the warm environment.
16:9 cinematic composition, shallow depth of field. Mood: editorial,
intellectually severe, contemplative weight.

Critical: figures must be fully opaque solid black silhouettes — NOT
translucent, NOT semi-transparent, NOT shadow-like, NOT detailed.
```

### Scene B — Corridor / abstract space

```
Editorial photography composition: a long industrial corridor receding
into deep perspective, vanishing point centered. Warm tungsten lighting
from above, deep umber shadows along the walls, polished concrete floor
reflecting amber highlights. A single full-body human silhouette walking
away from camera in the middle distance, rendered as a pure opaque black
shape — hard-edged, no detail whatsoever. The silhouette is the only
figure in the frame. Saul Bass aesthetic. Mood: monumental,
contemplative, lonely. Photorealistic environment, graphic silhouette
figure. 16:9 cinematic composition.

Critical: figure must be fully opaque solid black form — NOT
translucent silhouette, NOT shadow on wall.
```

## Direction 3: Woodcut / linocut

**One-line:** Bold black ink line work, single warm accent color, 1920s political-illustration gravity. Frans Masereel reference.

### Scene A — Cleanroom (control)

```
Linocut woodcut illustration in the tradition of Frans Masereel's
*The City* (1925) and Lynd Ward's wordless graphic novels. Interior of
a semiconductor fabrication cleanroom rendered entirely in bold black
ink lines with stark white-bone (#F0E6D0) negative space and a single
warm amber accent color (#C4A747) used sparingly — only on the
lithography light source and equipment indicator lights. Three figures
in protective bunny suits operating equipment, rendered with primitive
graphic confidence: hatched and cross-hatched textures, no shading, no
gradients. Strong angular composition, high contrast. Every surface
texture is suggested through linocut hatching. 1920s political-
illustration tradition, industrial-modernist gravity. 16:9 aspect ratio.

Avoid: photorealistic, 3D, full color palette, soft shading.
Restricted to black ink + bone background + sparse amber accent only.
```

### Scene B — 1941 historical reconstruction

```
Linocut woodcut in the tradition of Frans Masereel and Lynd Ward. A
presidential office, December 1941. A figure in a dark wool suit
seated at a heavy wooden desk, hand on a document. The scene rendered
in bold black ink with hatched and cross-hatched textures — wood
paneling, brass lamp, leather blotter, side window — all suggested
through linocut texture, no photorealistic detail. Single warm amber
accent (#C4A747) on the lamp glow and the document, rest of the scene
black ink on bone (#F0E6D0) background. Mood: historical weight,
civilizational stakes, 1920s political-illustration gravity. 16:9
aspect ratio.

Avoid: photorealistic, cartoon, full color, soft shading. Restricted
to black ink + bone background + single amber accent.
```

## Direction 4: Photomontage / cutout collage

**One-line:** Photoreal environments + visibly pasted-in cutout figures. John Heartfield 1930s political-montage tradition.

### Scene A — Cleanroom (control)

```
Political photomontage in the tradition of John Heartfield and
Hannah Höch. Interior of a semiconductor fabrication cleanroom rendered
photorealistically as the background — warm amber lithography lighting,
equipment, FOUP carriers, all photoreal. Three human figures pasted
into the scene as visible cutout silhouettes: each figure is a hard-
edged paper-cutout shape sourced from a different photograph, with
visible ragged paper edges and slight tonal mismatch between figure
and background. The figures are recognizably human but clearly *placed*
into the scene, not native to it. The seam is the editorial mark.
1930s political photomontage aesthetic. Warm umber-amber palette
throughout. 16:9 composition.

Critical: figures must be visibly pasted-in collage elements with
paper-texture edges and tonal mismatch — NOT seamless integration,
NOT photorealistic figures inside the scene.
```

### Scene B — 1941 historical reconstruction

```
John Heartfield 1930s political photomontage. A presidential office,
December 1941, photoreal background — wood paneling, heavy wooden
desk, brass lamp, side window with warm tungsten light streaming in.
The seated figure and standing observers are visible paper cutouts
pasted into the scene: hard-edged silhouettes from different
photographic sources, with ragged paper edges and slight tonal
mismatch. The seam between figures and environment is deliberately
visible. The office is photoreal; the people are constructed. Mood:
historical, political, deliberately arch. Warm umber-amber palette.

Critical: the figures must read as cutout collage elements pasted
into a photoreal scene — NOT seamless, NOT integrated.
```

## Direction 5: Cropped / body-language figures

**One-line:** Photoreal everything, but every figure cropped above the chin. Faces never appear because the camera is positioned to exclude them.

### Scene A — Cleanroom (control)

```
Editorial photography composition: interior of a semiconductor
fabrication cleanroom, warm amber lithography lighting, photorealistic.
Three workers in white bunny suits, all framed below the shoulders —
hands gloved and gesturing over wafer-handling equipment, torsos and
arms in detail, faces and heads cropped above the frame edge. The
cropping is deliberate and consistent: the camera is positioned to
never include any figure's face. Edward Hopper composition discipline,
editorial documentary photography (Vanity Fair, Fortune magazine).
Heavy chiaroscuro, deep umber shadows, amber midtones, bone highlights.
Shot on 35mm film, shallow depth of field. 16:9 cinematic composition.
The cropping itself is the editorial mark.

Critical: heads must be cropped out of frame entirely — NOT mannequin
faces visible, NOT featureless smooth heads. Faces simply not in frame.
```

### Scene B — Diplomatic meeting

```
Editorial documentary photography. A 1980s-era diplomatic meeting in
a wood-paneled conference room with warm tungsten lighting from a
side window. Five figures seated around a heavy wooden table, all
cropped from the chest down or chest up — hands gesturing over papers,
body postures conveying tension, gestures conveying disagreement, but
no faces visible in the frame. The camera angle deliberately positioned
to crop above the chin or below the eyes. Vanity Fair power-portrait
composition. Heavy chiaroscuro, deep umber shadows, amber-warm light
from window. 35mm film, shallow depth of field. The body language
carries the entire emotional weight of the scene.

Critical: heads must be cropped out entirely or replaced by paperwork
held up — NOT faces visible, NOT featureless heads.
```

## Direction 6: Diegetic face obscuring

**One-line:** Photoreal everything, but every face is naturally hidden by something belonging to the scene — face shields, helmets, ceremonial masks, equipment glare.

### Scene A — Cleanroom (control)

```
Editorial photography. Interior of a semiconductor fabrication
cleanroom, warm amber lithography lighting, photorealistic. Three
workers in full bunny suits with reflective face shields — the shields
catch the amber overhead light and reflect it back as bright glare,
completely obscuring the faces behind them. The figures are realistic,
the equipment is realistic, but the face shields' reflections naturally
hide every face. The obscuring is part of the scene, not a stylization.
Heavy chiaroscuro, deep umber shadows, amber highlights, bone
reflections on the shields. 35mm film, shallow depth of field. Mood:
documentary reconstruction, technical environment, contemplative weight.
The face shields' glare is the editorial mark — figures are present
but their identities are naturally hidden by the equipment they wear.
```

### Scene B — Military command center

```
Editorial photography. Dim military command center interior, blue-screen
ambient light from monitors mixed with warm amber accent lighting from
overhead fixtures. Three operators in tactical helmets with mounted
optics that obscure their eyes, headsets covering the lower half of
their faces. The helmets and gear are diegetic — period-accurate,
contextually correct — and naturally hide the operators' identities
without any stylization. Heavy chiaroscuro, predominantly cool but with
warm amber accents from monitor edges. Photorealistic, shot on 35mm
film, shallow depth of field. Mood: contemplative, contained,
intelligence-operations weight.
```

## How to judge the results

After running all six on the cleanroom scene, ask three questions per output:

1. **Does it look like Parallax?** Could this scene appear in a video that also has Remotion analytical templates and constructivist atmospheric backgrounds, and feel like the same channel? If it clashes, it's wrong.

2. **Could ten other AI-geopolitics channels produce this aesthetic?** If yes, it's not differentiated enough. The whole point of this exercise is to escape the genre default.

3. **Does it solve all three editorial problems?** Honesty signal (not documentary), depersonalization (role not person), quality safeguard (no AI-face failure mode). If any of the three fails, it's not viable as a primary aesthetic.

The aesthetic that wins on all three questions becomes the new Register 3. The runner-up may still be useful as a sub-register for specific moods (e.g., woodcut for historical, silhouette for abstract). The losers get banked as references for what *not* to do.

If a winner emerges: I can update the `recraft.py` grounding preamble in 5 minutes, update VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md and AI_VIDEO_PIPELINE.md to match, and the change propagates through every future shot list automatically. The lock-in cost stays low until EP01 ships.

## Quick adaptation guide

If you want to test a different scene, the structure for each direction is consistent:

- **Direction 1 (constructivist):** swap subject, keep "Soviet constructivist illustration in the style of Rodchenko and El Lissitzky" + the palette block + "color-blocked forms, no soft shading"
- **Direction 2 (silhouette):** swap environment, keep "fully opaque solid black silhouettes" + "Saul Bass aesthetic" + the photoreal warm environment description
- **Direction 3 (woodcut):** swap subject, keep "Linocut woodcut in the tradition of Frans Masereel" + "black ink + bone background + single amber accent"
- **Direction 4 (photomontage):** swap environment, keep "John Heartfield political photomontage" + "visibly pasted-in cutout figures with ragged paper edges"
- **Direction 5 (cropped):** swap context, keep "all figures framed below the shoulders, faces cropped above frame edge" + the photoreal environment
- **Direction 6 (diegetic):** swap context, the obscuring element MUST be diegetically appropriate — masks for medical, helmets for military, ceremonial robes for religious, paperwork held up for office. Doesn't generalize to all scenes.
