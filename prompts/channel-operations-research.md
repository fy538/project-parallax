## Prompt 1: Tier A — Launch Operations for a Credibility-Driven YouTube Channel (May 2026)

This research is for a pre-launch YouTube channel in the analytical-geopolitics niche (similar in positioning to CaspianReport, Asianometry, PolyMatter, Hindsight, Wendover, William Spaniel). The channel produces 15–22 minute analytical video essays at biweekly cadence, with a strong visual identity, AI-augmented research/production pipeline, and a single solo creator. The launch decision is imminent.

**Applicable comparables:** CaspianReport (launched 2010, ~7 years to 100K, ~1.81M by Dec 2025), Asianometry (Jon Y posting since 2017, slow growth from ~300–500 views/video before later inflection, ~931K by May 2026), PolyMatter, Hindsight (per public profile, joined July 2024, ~373K by May 2026 — verify and use as most-recent comparable in this lane), Like Stories of Old, William Spaniel (educational content from 2009 onward), Kraut. Recent 2024–2026 launches in the credibility-niche are highest-priority data; back-fill historical creator interviews and retrospectives where launch-era data isn't directly available.

**Filter out:** Mr Beast / high-volume creators, news-briefing daily channels (Reuters, Bloomberg), reaction channels, gaming/lifestyle content. Reject any source whose register includes "go viral in 30 days," "hack the algorithm," "overnight success," "explode your channel" — that's the high-volume creator-economy literature and it does not transfer.

**Questions:**

1. **Pre-launch buffer.** For credibility-driven channels in 2024–2026, what's the median number of completed episodes "in the can" before episode 1 publishes? Distinguish between "fully complete and ready to publish" vs "in production." Is there empirical evidence (case studies, creator interviews, retrospectives) that a deeper buffer (4–6 episodes) produces better cold-start outcomes than a thin buffer (1–2)? What's the trade-off between buffer depth and time-to-launch?

2. **Launch sequence.** Single-video drop, batched launch (3–5 videos same day or same week), or trickle (1 video per week or 2 weeks for the first month)? What does evidence from the Hindsight launch (July 2024) and other 2024–2026 credibility-niche launches show? Hypothesis to test: batched launches give the algorithm a better signal-to-noise ratio for genre classification, but trickle launches accumulate more total watch time per video at the per-video level — which actually wins on first-90-days subscriber growth?

3. **First-week algorithmic dynamics in 2026 (a) — what the algorithm does to new channels.** What's actually known about session-based ranking, browse vs suggested vs search distribution for new channels, and the cold-start problem? Specifically: how does YouTube classify a new channel's content/audience before it has watch-history data? What's the documented "test audience" mechanism — initial impression batch size, signals it measures, expansion thresholds? What does YouTube Creator Insider, the YouTube Blog, Tubefilter, and vidIQ say about new-channel dynamics in 2025–2026 specifically (not generic algorithm changes)? What's the realistic 0→1K timeline distribution given current dynamics?

4. **First-week algorithmic dynamics in 2026 (b) — has "first 24 hours is everything" survived?** The 2018–2022 framing was that the first 24 hours of a video's life were decisive for its long-term distribution. Has this been updated by 2024–2026 changes — Made For Kids reform, AI content policy (May 2025 disclosure, July 2025 inauthentic content), multi-language audio rollout, the decoupling of Shorts and long-form recommendations, and the shift toward satisfaction-survey signals over raw watch time? What's the current operational rule of thumb — is it 24 hours, 48 hours, 7 days, or longer? Are there documented cases of late-bloomer videos succeeding under the 2025–2026 algorithm that would have been written off under 2020 dynamics?

5. **Channel-level setup before episode 1.** What does every first-time visitor see when they click your channel? Specifically: channel trailer (should there be one before episode 1, or is "no trailer until you have a body of work" the right call?), channel art / banner, About page copy length and structure, featured video logic for non-subscribers vs subscribers, playlist organization at launch (single playlist? thematic playlists from day one?), and the use of Channel Sections / Featured Channels. Evidence from 2024–2026 credibility-niche launches and from YouTube's documented best-practice updates.

6. **Title and thumbnail iteration economics.** When does a sub-1K channel retitle/rethumb based on first-week data? What's the actual playbook — at what view threshold does iteration produce reliable signal vs noise? Coverage of YouTube's "Test & Compare" feature for titles and thumbnails as it currently exists (rolled out globally to creators with Advanced Features in late 2025): how does it work in practice, what's the minimum view threshold for it to produce meaningful signal, what's the documented test duration, and what does YouTube actually decide on (watch time, click-through, both)? Are there documented case studies of retitle/rethumb interventions on credibility-niche channels?

7. **Launch-week comment management specifically.** The first 24–72 hours after publishing have unique algorithmic and audience dynamics distinct from steady-state comment ops. What's the documented best practice for the first-week comment workflow — first reply within X hours, pinning a comment with intent, hearting threshold, the role of the creator's own first comment as a pinned anchor? Evidence on whether early comment engagement actually shifts the first-week distribution decision, vs whether it's cargo-culted from high-engagement creator advice.

**Output format:** An actionable playbook with explicit decisions for each question, with confidence ratings (High/Medium/Low) for each recommendation given the available evidence. Where evidence is sparse, say so directly rather than extrapolating from high-volume-channel data. Target depth: 15–20 pages, ~30 min reading. The deliverable should produce concrete decisions for: (a) how many episodes to bank before launch, (b) launch sequence, (c) channel-page setup checklist, (d) first-week comment workflow, (e) when/how to use Test & Compare. Each decision should be tied to the specific evidence underpinning it.

## Prompt 2: Tier B — First 12 Months Operations for a Credibility-Driven YouTube Channel (May 2026)

This research is for the first 12 months post-launch of an analytical-geopolitics YouTube channel (15–22 min essays, biweekly cadence, solo creator, AI-augmented pipeline, positioned in the CaspianReport / Asianometry / Hindsight / Wendover lane). Assumes Tier A launch decisions are made.

**Applicable comparables:** Same as Tier A. Plus, for newsletter operations: Stratechery (Ben Thompson), Noahpinion (Noah Smith), Slow Boring (Matt Yglesias), Astral Codex Ten, Construction Physics. For sponsorship benchmarks: Wendover Productions, RealLifeLore, CaspianReport, William Spaniel.

**Filter out:** High-volume newsletter operators (Morning Brew, The Hustle), high-volume YouTube channels, lifestyle/gaming creators. Sponsorship advice calibrated to 1M+ sub channels does not transfer. Reject "go viral / hack the algorithm / overnight success" register entirely.

**Questions:**

1. **Cadence vs quality at sub-1K scale.** Does the algorithm actually punish irregular biweekly cadence for sub-1K credibility channels, or is that bro-science from high-volume niches? Specifically: do channels in the credibility-niche cohort (CaspianReport, Asianometry, PolyMatter, Kraut, Hindsight, Like Stories of Old) show retention/growth differences correlated with cadence consistency vs cadence frequency? What's the actual trade-off curve between video frequency (weekly / biweekly / monthly) and per-video quality at sub-1K, sub-10K, and sub-100K scales? Is there a documented inflection point where consistency starts to matter more than peak quality?

2. **Comment and Community Tab management at steady-state.** What's the actual ROI on ongoing comment engagement (post-launch-week) for credibility channels? How much time per episode do depth-channel creators (Asianometry, CaspianReport, William Spaniel) actually spend on comments? At what subscriber thresholds does the time cost stop scaling linearly, and what's the operational adjustment (delegate, batch, AI-assist, ignore)? Community Tab specifically: what's documented on whether posts and polls move retention or sub-conversion for credibility channels, vs being a time sink that produces vanity engagement? (Note: launch-week comment ops are owned by Prompt 1; this question covers steady-state only.)

3. **Newsletter / email list strategy.** When does a credibility-driven YouTube creator actually start a newsletter — alongside launch, after 1K subs, after 10K, never? Platform comparison (Substack vs Beehiiv vs ConvertKit vs Ghost) limited to fees, YouTube creator integration, and migration risk — skip generic newsletter-platform reviews. What role does email play *alongside* Patreon/Memberships rather than replacing it? Are there documented operational lessons from email-list-first creators (Stratechery, Noah Smith, Matt Yglesias) whose ops differ usefully from YouTube-first creators trying to bolt on newsletter? Specifically: realistic YouTube → newsletter conversion rates, time cost of running both, and the failure-mode patterns (newsletter cannibalizing YouTube, or vice versa).

4. **Shorts-from-longform pipeline AND cross-platform seeding.** This is one combined question because the workflow is the same. Concrete workflow from a 15-minute analytical essay to 2–3 shorts that actually drive long-form watch time, *plus* the cross-platform extension to TikTok and Instagram Reels. Tools, time costs, success metrics. Does the "subscribe rate from shorts" actually convert to long-form watch time for credibility channels, or do shorts-subs stay in shorts? Should TikTok/Reels exist before, during, or after the YouTube long-form launch? What 2024–2026 case studies exist on credibility-niche cross-platform funnels? Documented best practice for cross-promo (link in shorts description, pinned comment, end card)? Realistic time-to-make-a-short at Tiger's pipeline maturity (script + visual layer already exist; AI tools available)?

5. **Membership and Patreon operations.** When does a credibility-driven channel actually start in-house monetization (YouTube Memberships, Patreon, both)? What's the documented conversion rate from sub → Patreon at sub-10K, 10–50K, 50–100K scales for analytical/educational content? What tier structures and reward models work for depth content (vs cosmetic perks that work for high-volume creators)? Specifically: do "early access," "ad-free," and "behind the scenes" tiers actually convert for analytical channels, or do they require something more substantive (research notes, source bibliographies, member-only Q&A)? Time cost of running a Patreon at the analytical depth Patrons would expect.

6. **Sponsorship readiness.** At what subscriber count and watch-hour count does a credibility channel become *actually* sponsorable — not just "could theoretically book a Brilliant.org sponsorship"? What rates are realistic at sub-50K, 50–100K, 100–250K scales for analytical/educational content? Cold outreach vs inbound sponsor requests vs platforms (Patreon Concierge, NebulaTalent, Standard, ThoughtLeaders, VeryGoodAds). Disclosure operations and FTC compliance for analytical-content channels specifically. Realistic month-9 to month-18 sponsorship trajectory for a sub-50K channel in this niche.

**Output format:** A 12-month operational playbook with month-by-month or milestone-by-milestone recommendations, and explicit "wait until [milestone] to do X" gating for each question. Include realistic time-cost estimates per workflow item — Tiger has a 5–10 hours/week budget that already produces episodes, so any new operational workflow has to fit inside that envelope or displace something. Target depth: 25–30 pages, ~45 min reading. The deliverable should make it possible to slot operational tasks into a weekly schedule without guessing.

## Prompt 3: AI Content Compliance for an AI-Augmented YouTube Channel (May 2026)

This research is for a pre-launch AI-augmented (not AI-generated) YouTube channel. The creator writes scripts, narrates, and makes editorial decisions; AI assists with research, illustration (Recraft constructivist style), motion-graphics generation (Remotion templates with AI-assisted data fills), and video generation (Pika 2.5 chained morphs for atmospheric scenes). The channel is philosophically on the right side of YouTube's "inauthentic content" line, but the *practical compliance layer* is unclear.

**Background facts (already established):**
- January 2026: YouTube terminated 16 major AI channels with 35M combined subs.
- July 2025: YouTube updated "inauthentic content" policy — defined as "mass-produced, template-driven, minimal human creative input."
- May 2025: Mandatory disclosure for "altered or synthetic content that appears realistic."
- Properly disclosed AI content reportedly receives normal algorithmic distribution; undisclosed AI content faces reduced recommendations or Partner Program removal.

**Filter out:** General "is AI content allowed" debates. Speculation about future policy. Reddit takes from sub-1K creators. Focus on documented enforcement actions and first-party YouTube guidance.

(Note: account security and backup channel ops are owned by Prompt 4, not this prompt. This prompt is content-compliance specific.)

**Questions:**

1. **What did the 16 terminated channels actually do?** Document the patterns — was it AI narration, AI thumbnails, AI scripts, all of the above, or operator-volume (multiple channels by one entity)? Was the trigger thinness of human touch, scale of output, deceptive framing (fake news), or something else? Are there public statements from YouTube about specific failure modes vs the general policy? Distinguish AI war coverage (DFRLab's reported 20+ channels with 2B views) from analytical AI channels — were both classes terminated, or is the policy narrower than press coverage suggests? (Verify the "16 channels, 35M combined subs" figure from RESEARCH_LOG §3 — note any drift in the numbers as more enforcement happened across 2026.)

2. **The safe-harbor checklist for AI-augmented channels.** What specific operational practices distinguish "AI-assisted with meaningful human creative input" (allowed) from "AI-generated with thin human framing" (penalized) in the eyes of YouTube's enforcement? Is there documented enforcement vs guidance? Specifically, does each of these require disclosure or risk enforcement: AI-assisted research where a human writes the final script; AI-generated illustration in a stylized aesthetic; AI-generated motion graphics; a single AI-generated animation scene inside an otherwise human-narrated video; AI-assisted thumbnails (when the underlying photography is human); AI-cleaned audio?

3. **Disclosure operations.** When YouTube requires "altered or synthetic content" disclosure (May 2025 onward), what counts in practice? How are creators actually filling out the disclosure form? Is there evidence of distribution penalty for over-disclosing (flagging things that don't legally need disclosure) vs under-disclosing? Are there documented cases where the disclosure decision affected reach? What's the practical disclosure text creators are using for AI-assisted research, AI-generated illustration, and AI-generated motion graphics?

4. **FTC and broader disclosure obligations.** What's the parallel obligation under FTC rules for AI-augmented content, especially when sponsored? Does the AI augmentation itself require any disclosure beyond YouTube's own form? State-level obligations (California's AI transparency law in particular)? Tiger is US-based (NYU graduate, NYC-area implied) — calibrate to US obligations and flag if international compliance (EU AI Act, etc.) would meaningfully differ for a US creator distributing globally.

5. **Retroactive enforcement risk.** Does compliance with policy at publish-time protect against future policy changes? Specifically: if YouTube changes its AI-content policy in 2027, can it retroactively demonetize, age-restrict, or terminate videos that complied with the policy as it stood in 2026? Are there documented cases of retroactive enforcement following policy changes (the May 2025 disclosure rule, the July 2025 inauthentic content definition, the multi-language audio rollout)? What's the practical defensive posture — should AI-augmented creators *over*-disclose now to insulate against future tightening, or does over-disclosure create its own present-tense penalty?

**Output format:** A one-page operational checklist Parallax can run before each episode publishes (per-shot or per-element if needed), plus a "what to do if" appendix for the three most likely enforcement scenarios (false-positive AI flag, disclosure dispute, manual review). Distinguish clearly between documented YouTube policy, documented enforcement patterns, and creator-community speculation — confidence-rate each. Target depth: 5–8 pages, ~15 min reading.

## Prompt 4: Operational Risk Management for a Sub-100K Credibility-Driven YouTube Channel (May 2026)

This research is for a pre-launch YouTube channel that will eventually monetize and may attract attention (geopolitical analysis, occasional contested claims, cross-cultural angles). Sub-100K channels have meaningfully less recourse than mid-tier channels when things go sideways. The cost of channel hijacking, malicious copyright claims, demonetization without explanation, or termination is months-to-years of work erased. Pre-launch risk-management posture is much cheaper than reactive incident response.

**Filter out:** Generic "YouTube tips" content. Affiliate-marketed VPN and security product reviews. Outdated 2018–2022 advice that predates Made For Kids reform, the 2024 AI policy changes, and the 2025 Partner Program updates.

**Jurisdiction:** Tiger is US-based (NYU graduate, NYC area). Calibrate legal/business answers to US federal and NY state law. Flag where international distribution (EU GDPR, UK, Canada) would create meaningful additional obligations.

**Questions:**

1. **Account security in 2026.** What's the current best-practice security setup for solo creators? Specifically: 2FA flavors (SMS vs authenticator vs hardware key), hardware key recommendations for monetized creators, recovery email/phone configuration, brand account vs personal Google account ownership, who has access (assistant, editor), and the pre-launch hygiene checklist. What hijacking patterns are documented in 2025–2026? How do attackers typically get in (phishing, OAuth abuse, Google session theft)? Include backup-channel ownership best practice (the related "if your main channel is terminated" defense — was previously in Prompt 3 Q5).

2. **Copyright strikes for analytical/commentary content.** How do strikes work operationally vs Content ID claims vs fair-use claims? What's the dispute process, how long does each step take, and what's the success-rate distribution? Geopolitics content uses archival news footage / political speech / leader photos / map data — what's the realistic strike-risk profile for a Parallax-style channel? Are some content sources (AP, Reuters wire footage; Bloomberg; specific government channels) systematically more litigious? When are takedowns automated (Content ID match) vs manual (legal complaint)? What's the practical fair-use guidance for analytical commentary in 2026, and where is that guidance contested?

3. **Manual demonetization appeals.** When an episode gets demonetized (yellow icon), what's the appeal process and success rate by appeal type? What specific content/keywords trigger automated demonetization for geopolitics content in 2026 (post-Israel/Gaza 2023–2025, post-Ukraine, post-Trump/Iran 2026)? Is there documented practice on framing — does the same factual content get different treatment based on title/thumbnail/description framing? What's the realistic time cost of an appeal cycle?

4. **Channel termination and recovery.** What's the appeal process if a strike escalates to termination? Documented case studies of successful appeals (and unsuccessful ones). What preventive practices materially reduce the cost of a termination event — specifically: content backup off-platform (raw masters, narration, source files), audience-contact pathway held off-platform (newsletter, RSS), and any documented "warm restart" cases from terminated → new channel? (Backup-channel ownership specifically is covered in Q1 — focus this question on the recovery and audience-portability angle.)

5. **Legal/business setup as risk reduction (US-calibrated).** LLC vs sole prop for a US creator pre-monetization — when does it actually matter (liability shielding, tax treatment, sponsor contracting)? Contract templates for sponsors specific to analytical/educational content. Music licensing audit trail (the operational layer of what to keep on file in case of a license dispute). Copyright fair-use practical limits for the use of news clips in analytical commentary — what's the realistic envelope and where do claims typically come from? Flag where NY state law differs from federal default in ways that affect a creator (publicity rights, defamation thresholds, sponsorship disclosure).

6. **Insurance for sub-100K creators (lower priority — answer briefly).** Creator liability and errors & omissions options that exist for sub-100K creators in 2026 — are any actually worth their premium pre-monetization? Skip if the answer is "not yet, revisit at 50K subs"; expand only if there's a meaningful pre-launch case.

**Output format:** A pre-launch risk-management checklist (one page, completable in a day) plus a "what to do if X happens" runbook for the four most likely incident types (copyright strike, automated demonetization, account-security breach attempt, termination notice). Include realistic time-cost and money-cost ranges for each setup item so Tiger can prioritize within a finite pre-launch budget. Confidence-rate each recommendation. Target depth: 5–8 pages, ~15 min reading.

## Notes on running these

- **Tiger expects to filter heavily.** Generic creator-economy advice is the dominant signal in this space; ~70% of any Deep Research result will likely be filterable. The filter-out instructions in each prompt are doing real work — every prompt explicitly blocks the "go viral / hack the algorithm / 30 days / overnight success" register.
- **Each prompt has a calibrated reading length** (see the table at the top). Tier A and Tier B are larger reports (15–20 and 25–30 pages); Compliance and Risk are tight 5–8 page checklists. If Deep Research returns dramatically more than the target, the prompt was over-scoped.
- **Confidence ratings are non-negotiable.** A lot of operational advice is bro-science. The output should distinguish documented evidence from creator-community consensus from extrapolation. Three levels: High (documented YouTube policy or first-party data), Medium (documented case study from a credible comparable), Low (creator-community pattern with no documented case study).
- **Comparables are the spine.** Every claim should ideally be grounded in what specific channels in the comparable set actually did, not in generic creator advice. If the report can't ground a recommendation in a specific comparable's documented behavior, that's a flag — not necessarily a kill, but a lower-confidence signal.
- **Verify the few specific factual claims embedded in each prompt.** The Hindsight join date (July 2024), the CaspianReport timeline (~7 years to 100K), the Asianometry trajectory (2017 onward, slow growth then inflection) — these are starting hypotheses, not facts the prompts assert. Deep Research should verify and flag if any are wrong.

When each report returns, save to `project/research/2026-MM-<topic>.md` and add a stub entry in `project/RESEARCH_LOG.md` following the §13–17 pattern.
