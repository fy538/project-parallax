*Enable Research Mode (blue button, bottom-left) before pasting.*

I'm launching a bilingual (English/Chinese) video channel that analyzes contemporary geopolitics through historical analogy and philosophical frameworks. The tone is intellectually rigorous but narratively engaging — think "Hardcore History meets visual essays on current affairs." Content will appear on YouTube, Bilibili, TikTok/Douyin, and Patreon/Xiaohongshu.

Our working hypothesis for target audience: "educated concerned citizens who want cognitive tools — not just information — to make sense of an increasingly unstable world." These are generalists, not specialists. They read the Economist or follow geopolitics podcasts, but they're also reachable via more accessible formats (Kurzgesagt, Vox, Wendover-style visual essays).

Please research the following:

## 1. Audience Landscape — Who's Already Watching This Kind of Content?

- **Identify the top 10-15 YouTube channels** that overlap with our positioning (geopolitics + history + analytical depth). For each: subscriber count, typical view counts, content style, posting frequency, and what niche they occupy. Include both English and Chinese-language channels.
- Examples to include in analysis: CaspianReport, VisualPolitik, RealLifeLore, Wendover Productions, Kraut, Kings and Generals, PolyMatter, and any Chinese equivalents on Bilibili (观视频, 温铁军's channels, etc.)
- **What's the total addressable audience?** How large is the "geopolitics explainer" space on YouTube and Bilibili in 2025-2026? Any data on growth trends?

## 2. Audience Segmentation — Who Specifically Are We Reaching?

- What does research say about the demographics and psychographics of people who consume geopolitical/historical analysis content? (age, education, political leanings, media consumption habits)
- Is there evidence of a growing audience segment driven by **anxiety about global instability** — people seeking frameworks to understand deglobalization, great-power rivalry, institutional decay? What's driving this growth?
- How does this audience differ between English-speaking and Chinese-speaking markets? Different concerns, different reference points, different content consumption patterns?

## 3. Content Gap Analysis — What's Missing?

- What does the existing geopolitics content landscape do **poorly**? Where is the unmet demand?
- Specifically: is there a gap for content that combines **philosophical frameworks** (Stoicism, game theory, complex systems, Bayesian epistemology) with geopolitical analysis? Who, if anyone, is doing this?
- Is there a gap for **bilingual content** that bridges Western and Chinese analytical perspectives on the same events — not translating one for the other, but genuinely synthesizing both?

## 4. Platform Strategy

- For our content type (15-20 min analytical video essays), what's the optimal platform mix? How should content be adapted for YouTube vs. Bilibili vs. TikTok/Douyin vs. Patreon vs. Xiaohongshu?
- What posting frequency maximizes growth for analytical channels? (weekly vs. biweekly vs. monthly)
- What role do short-form clips play in driving traffic to long-form content in this niche?

## 5. Monetization and Community

- How do comparable channels monetize? (ad revenue, Patreon, sponsorship, merchandise, courses)
- What community-building strategies work for intellectually-oriented channels? (Discord, community posts, live streams, reading groups)
- What's realistic for subscriber growth in Year 1 given our positioning?

## 6. Risks and Contrarian Perspectives

- What are the strongest arguments that this kind of channel **won't work** or will struggle? (e.g., oversaturated market, algorithm doesn't favor depth, bilingual splits audience rather than doubling it)
- Are there cautionary tales — channels that tried something similar and failed? What went wrong?
- What do experienced YouTube strategists say about launching analytical channels in 2026?

Please provide citations for all data points and claims. I want to be able to verify everything.
