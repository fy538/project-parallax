# Practitioners, reception, platform rules and Remotion implementation for AI-illustrated narrated essays (2024 to Oct 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-11. Scope: who does what, how it is received, platform rules, and Remotion 4.x implementation. Model capabilities, prices and general editing vocabulary are deliberately excluded (covered by other researchers). "Creator's own account" and "third-party" are marked on each finding. Where a named creator's AI use could not be verified, they are listed under Gaps, not as examples.

---

## 1. Named examples: who is known for strong (or criticised) use of AI-generated or AI-assisted imagery in narrated video

### Takeaway
The verifiable, praised examples are almost all AI-native storytellers (Neural Viz, Gossip Goblin, cursejourney, The Archive In Between), one artist's visual essay (Alan Warburton's "The Wizard of AI"), and a handful of disclosed documentary uses. I could not verify a single established, respected narrated-essay YouTube channel that openly builds its picture from AI stills. The best-known flat-illustration explainer channel (Kurzgesagt) publicly commits to human-made work and was nonetheless misclassified as AI slop by YouTube in July 2026. The criticised examples cluster around photoreal fake-history, undisclosed "archival", and mass-produced narrated slideshows.

### Cited Findings

**Praised or accepted (with visible techniques)**

- **Neural Viz (Josh Wallace Kerrigan), YouTube, 2024 to 2026.** A one-person "Monoverse" of alien mockumentaries, cop-show and vox-pop parodies; episodes near-weekly, some eight minutes, "hundreds of thousands to millions of views". Techniques: mostly talking heads, one generated still per storyboard panel, performance transfer onto characters, deliberately degraded VHS/broadcast grade. Third-party profile dated 2 July 2026, built on his statements — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/neural-viz-ai-tv-universe/). A secondary summary of a WIRED profile gives 233,000 subscribers and says he writes, directs, edits and voices everything himself — [cnyes summary of WIRED](https://hao.cnyes.com/post/208831). (WIRED original could not be fetched.)
- **Neural Viz discloses the tool stack on every post** (creator's own account), e.g. "Written, directed, edited, sound designed, and performed by me. Made with: Google Gemini, Runway Act One, Luma Dream Machine, Kling, Veo 2, Elevenlabs, Udio, Premiere" — [Neural Viz on X](https://x.com/NeuralViz/status/1906512977177833715); a second post lists "Runway Act One, Sora, Kling, Elevenlabs, Suno, Premiere" — [Neural Viz on X](https://x.com/NeuralViz/status/1921732202934788551). Tool lists differ between secondary write-ups (one names Sora + Midjourney + Runway, another Nano Banana + Veo + Runway Act Two) because they describe different projects — [android.com.pl](https://android.com.pl/tech/981491-uniwersum-filmowe-ai-neural-viz/).
- **Gossip Goblin (Zack London), short-form animated stories.** Script first; character stills in Midjourney; Seedream to blend characters into environments; Veo 3 for dialogue scenes; Runway and HeyGen for performance/lip-sync; CapCut edit; camera movement done manually in the edit. Creator-reported, article dated 23 July 2026 — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/how-ai-creators-make-videos/).
- **cursejourney (Mike Chhay), "photos i found in the basement".** AI horror styled as found photographs: sepia, grain, still-first, then animated. Selected for AIGA Arizona's Best of 2025; the article reports over 140 million Instagram views for one piece — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/how-ai-creators-make-videos/).
- **The Archive In Between (anonymous "Curator").** Narrated sci-fi/horror anthology: all writing is human, imagery is AI-generated "in volume and then culled" ("sifting"); about four stories a week; full-time income from Patreon and book sales rather than ads — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/how-ai-creators-make-videos/). This is the closest verified analogue to "human-written narration over AI stills".
- **Kelly Boesch, AI films.** Image-first: composition-accurate still in Midjourney, then animated in Runway, Pika, Higgsfield or Veo 3 depending on the scene, then conventional edit, colour and sound; 17 years at IMAX before this — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/how-ai-creators-make-videos/).
- **Alan Warburton, "The Wizard of AI" (Nov 2023, slightly before the 2024 window but the canonical praised visual essay).** A roughly 20-minute, "99% AI-generated" narrated visual essay about AI, commissioned by Data as Culture at the Open Data Institute. AI use disclosed in credits and description, and the AI look is intentional because AI is the subject — [ODI](https://culture.theodi.org/the-wizard-of-ai/); classed as a "game-changer" by a documentary practitioner (24 May 2024) — [No Film School / Film Robots](https://nofilmschool.com/ai-in-documentary). Reception evidence beyond the commissioner's own account is thin.
- **Raoul Peck, "Orwell: 2+2=5" (2025 documentary).** Peck added an AI-generated absurd music video and a sequence about AI, made by Belgian filmmaker Vincent Lannoo, cut against real clips; he calls the result "totally unrealistic" and says he was "careful to stay transparent when we use AI, whether it's in image, sound, or edit"; he reports "People laugh at the AI parts". Interview dated 7 Oct 2025 (filmmaker's own account) — [IDA Documentary magazine, "The Synthesis"](https://documentary.org/column/digital-title-synthesis-raoul-peck-using-ai-critique-authoritarianism-orwell-225). One review singles out the AI material as an interesting choice, used to show how AI may serve disinformation — [De Facto Film Reviews](https://defactofilmreviews.com/?p=18769).
- **Disclosed documentary uses treated as acceptable by practitioners (May 2024 round-up):** "The Andy Warhol Diaries" (AI voice reading real diary text, disclosed in trailer, film and credits, estate permission); "Another Body" (deepfake face to protect a subject); "Eno" (generative editing) — [No Film School / Film Robots](https://nofilmschool.com/ai-in-documentary).
- **Killer Kings (Sky HISTORY, 2025; FirstLookTV with AI studio Gennie).** Six-part series "believed to be the first documentary TV series to air using fully AI-generated imagery"; reenactments built with Imagen, Veo 2 and Luma Ray 2. The "first" claim and the framing are the company's — [press release via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/13604949983319040). Gennie has since co-produced "Deep Blue CSI" with Woodcut Media (reported 13 Oct and 7 Nov 2025) — [Señal News](https://senalnews.com/en/interviews/cost-savings-and-big-creative-visions-gennie-shows-how-ai-is-transforming-content-creation) — and "Birds Aren't Real" with Phoenix Television (2 Feb 2026) — [VideoAge](https://www.videoageinternational.net/2026/02/02/news/gennie-phoenix-tv-partner-on-doc-birds-arent-real/). I found trade coverage only, no independent audience reception.

**Criticised**

- **"On This Day… 1776" (Primordial Soup / Darren Aronofsky, TIME Studios YouTube, launched late Jan 2026).** Weekly photoreal AI shorts with human SAG-AFTRA voice actors. Launch coverage describes a loud and unusually personal wave of disappointment from critics and filmmakers — [The Playlist, 30 Jan 2026](https://theplaylist.net/on-this-day-1776-trailer-darren-aronofskys-ai-animated-revolution-series-drops-and-the-backlash-arrives-right-on-schedule-20260130/); heise judged the images photorealistic but generic, the editing confused, with no recognisable dramaturgy, and reported commenters calling it typical AI slop — [heise](https://heise.de/-11161577); also [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/darren-aronofskys-ai-generated-series-faces-harsh-backlash-online/). A minority view asked what AI video's narrative strengths actually are — [Flicks](https://www.flicks.com.au/features/is-darren-aronofskys-ai-generated-drama-innovative-or-high-end-slop/).
- **"What Jennifer Did" (Netflix, 2024).** Photos of the real subject showing AI artefacts (morphed fingers, misshapen features), with no disclosure in the credits — [Futurism](https://futurism.com/netflix-true-crime-producer-ai); framed by 404 Media as creating "a false historical record", published just after the Archival Producers Alliance presented draft guidelines — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/netflix-doc-what-jennifer-did-uses-ai-images-to-create-false-historical-record/). An alternative reading is AI upscaling of real photos rather than fabrication — [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/news/netflix-ai-art-what-jennifer-did).
- **"Roadrunner" (2021).** Roughly 45 seconds of undisclosed AI Bourdain voice; strong backlash — [No Film School / Film Robots](https://nofilmschool.com/ai-in-documentary).
- **AI "POV" history videos (TikTok/YouTube creators Time Traveller POV and POV Lab, 2025).** First-person photoreal clips of waking up in Pompeii, the Black Death, Chernobyl; one drew 53 million views. Historians called them "amateurish" and potentially "dangerous", citing glazed windows and train tracks in the 1300s and a Chernobyl reactor modelled on post-disaster photos. Creators reply that the videos evoke atmosphere and "are not meant to be taken as pure historical fact". Secondary write-ups of BBC reporting; exact date not confirmed — [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/ai-generated-history-videos-are-going-viral-on-tiktok-but-how-accurate-are-they); [Mad Cornish Projectionist](https://www.madcornishprojectionist.co.uk/amateur-and-dangerous-historians-weigh-in-on-viral-ai-history-videos/).
- **"Boring History for Sleep" and sibling channels (Sleepless Historian, Boring History Bites, History Before Sleep, The Snoozetorian, Historian Sleepy, Dreamoria).** Multi-hour AI-narrated history with AI visuals; one video had 2.3 million views; Pete Kelly of History Time: "I absolutely hate it, primarily the fact that they're historically inaccurate". 404 Media report by Jason Koebler, summarised 3 Sept 2025 — [Slashdot](https://news.slashdot.org/story/25/09/03/2028206/ai-generated-boring-history-videos-are-flooding-youtube-drowning-out-real-history); [404 Media podcast](https://www.404media.co/podcast-ai-slop-is-drowning-out-human-youtubers/).
- **BBC Question Time (May 2026)** opened with AI-generated images of historical figures and told viewers so; still criticised on ethical and copyright grounds (single secondary source, lower reliability) — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/bbc-airs-ai-generated-historical-panel-on-question-time-6ebcfa99).

**Counter-examples: respected explainer channels that do not use AI imagery**

- **Kurzgesagt** published an "AI slop" video (first aired 7 Oct 2025) whose listing says Kurzgesagt videos will always remain human-made — [TheTVDB listing](https://thetvdb.com/series/kurzgesagt-in-a-nutshell/episodes/11383775); a summary notes they also say they do not want to be Luddites who never use AI — [duncan.dev](https://duncan.dev/link/kurzgesagt-on-ai-slop).
- **AI in Context (80,000 Hours)**, the highest-profile recent AI-topic essay channel: "We're Not Ready for Superintelligence" reached 8.9M views; its retrospective describes a cinematic, presenter-led look with studio shoots and "good graphics and editing" and does not mention AI-generated imagery — [EA Forum retrospective, 24 Dec 2025](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/RCRaBYSqBaMzHzTjF/retrospective-and-learnings-from-ai-in-context-s-first-two).

**Earlier slideshow wave (context)**

- AI still slideshows already boomed and faded once: Midjourney/Stable Diffusion slideshows ("X as an 80s dark fantasy film") drew millions of views from mid-December 2022, the trend "faded by February", and revived only when demonflyingfox combined Midjourney stills, ElevenLabs speech and D-ID talking heads in March 2023 — [Tuber's Digest, 7 May 2023](https://tubersdigest.substack.com/p/a-brief-history-of-ai-videos-on-youtube).

### Inferences
- The praised cases share three traits: a human-authored script and voice at the centre, a deliberately non-photoreal or openly artificial look, and upfront tool disclosure. The criticised cases share photoreal depiction of real history or real people, weak or no authorship signal, and volume.
- Every praised AI-native creator designs the world around what the tools do well (talking heads, stylised characters, grain or grading that unifies shots). None relies on a slow camera move over a single still; the still is a storyboard panel that becomes a performed, cut shot.
- The 2023 slideshow boom and bust suggests that stills-plus-ambient-audio has a short novelty half-life; what sustained audiences afterwards was authored character and narrative.
- For a flat, constructivist, philosophy/AI channel the nearest legitimate precedent is "The Wizard of AI" (AI imagery in an essay about AI, disclosed, intentional look) and "The Archive In Between" (human writing, AI imagery, reader-funded), not any mainstream essay channel.

### Gaps
- I ran targeted searches for AI imagery use by Voices of the Past, Kings and Generals, Epic History, Whatifalthist, Johnny Harris, Internet Historian, Kirby Ferguson, Wendigoon, MrBallen, Sabine Hossenfelder and Perun and found no citable confirmation for any of them, so none is listed as an example. This is absence of web-indexed evidence, not proof they do not use it; channel descriptions, pinned comments and Reddit threads are poorly indexed.
- No write-up was found of an essay channel using split screens, panel grids, cut-out animation or multiplane parallax specifically with AI stills. Visible-technique detail above comes from profiles, not shot-by-shot breakdowns.
- WIRED's Neural Viz profile and Variety's 2025 piece "AI-Generated Images Lead to Audiences' Distrust, Threaten Documentary" ([Variety](https://variety.com/2025/film/festivals/ai-generated-images-threaten-future-of-documentary-1236583466/)) were paywalled; only the Variety headline is known.
- No independent audience data for Killer Kings or other Gennie productions.

## 2. Documented workflows: steps, consistency methods, discard ratios, time and cost

### Takeaway
Documented pipelines converge on script, then storyboard, then one still per panel, then selective image-to-video, then a conventional edit, with heavy over-generation: published keep rates run from roughly 1 in 20 to 1 in 300. Solo AI-native creators report about 4 to 6 hours of work per finished minute at around $100 a month in subscriptions; hand-made comparators sit one to two orders of magnitude higher.

### Cited Findings

**Step order and consistency methods**

- **Neural Viz (creator-reported):** (1) full script in screenplay format with slug lines and camera blocking; (2) storyboard every shot and render one still per panel, mostly Midjourney, holding lighting and sight lines consistent across shots so cuts hold; (3) act the lines on webcam and map the performance onto characters with Runway Act-One; (4) Hedra for lip-sync; (5) ElevenLabs voices, sometimes layered over his own; (6) a conventional Premiere edit. Consistency comes from design choices: bulbous cartoon aliens avoid the uncanny valley, and VHS noise and soft focus hide artefacts. A character's verbal tic began as a software error he kept — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/neural-viz-ai-tv-universe/).
- **cursejourney's public seven-tool chain:** Midjourney base image; Magnific upscale before animation, to keep faces and details consistent; Photoshop for crop, colour and filters; Kling for most shots with Seedance reserved for "epic transformations and action scenes"; Premiere; Suno and ElevenLabs; Topaz upscale to 4K — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/how-ai-creators-make-videos/).
- **PJ Accetturo, Kalshi ad (2025 NBA Finals):** write a script; ask Gemini for a shot list with prompts, "5 prompts at a time — any more than that and the quality starts to slip"; paste into Veo 3; assemble in CapCut or Premiere (creator's own account, relayed by press) — [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/the-chaotic-kalshi-ad-during-the-nba-finals-was-made-with-ai-for-2000-the-guy-behind-the-clip-shared-how-he-made-it/).
- **WSJ (Joanna Stern and Jarrard Cole), three-minute AI film, 2025:** footage mostly Veo 2 and Veo 3 with some Runway Gen-4; prompts around 180 words, drafted with a Gemini thread loaded with prompting guidance; Stern said every good shot came with at least a few bad ones (creators' own posts) — [texxr quoting Stern and Cole](https://texxr.com/886281).
- **Shy Kids, "Air Head" (Sora, 2024):** post work included manual rotoscoping, painting out faces and strings that appeared on the balloon, recolouring the balloon in After Effects, colour correction, and retiming because the model tends toward slow motion — [TechCrunch, 27 Apr 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/27/creators-of-sora-powered-short-explain-ai-generated-videos-strengths-and-limitations); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/turns-out-the-viral-air-head-sora-video-wasnt-purely-the-work-of-ai-we-were-led-to-believe).
- **Alan Warburton, "The Wizard of AI":** Runway Gen-2 for 16:9 clips; Midjourney, Stable Diffusion and DALL-E 3 for stills; Pika for three-second loops; HeyGen for a talking head; Photoshop generative expand; Topaz Gigapixel upscale; "Adobe After Effects to put everything together". He regenerated clips made on 20 October again in early November because the tools had improved — [ODI](https://culture.theodi.org/the-wizard-of-ai/).
- **Raoul Peck** rejected the first short AI clips because the tools could not keep a shot or a face consistent, calling them not usable for a film at that stage — [IDA Documentary](https://documentary.org/column/digital-title-synthesis-raoul-peck-using-ai-critique-authoritarianism-orwell-225).
- **Hobbyist children's-story pipeline (Medium):** ChatGPT story, Midjourney illustrations, Kling 10-second clips from stills, DaVinci Resolve; character consistency handled by detailed character prompts and manually checking every generation — [Medium, Alex Braun](https://medium.com/@alex.braun.77/automating-a-childrens-story-for-youtube-using-ai-my-workflow-with-chatgpt-midjourney-kling-ai-da287b80d34b).

**Discard ratios (all self-reported)**

- Gossip Goblin: "probably 400 prompts / 1600 images" for one character set's faces; "about 150 generations" for one 90-second dialogue scene — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/how-ai-creators-make-videos/).
- Kalshi ad: 300 to 400 Veo 3 generations for 15 usable clips — [Public Gaming](https://www.publicgaming.com/news-categories/advertising/14510-heres-the-2-000-fully-ai-generated-ad-that-aired-during-the-nba-finals).
- WSJ film: about 1,000 clips generated for three minutes — [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/tech/you-wont-believe-this-movie-was-made-with-ai/).
- Air Head: a "300:1" ratio of generated to kept footage — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/turns-out-the-viral-air-head-sora-video-wasnt-purely-the-work-of-ai-we-were-led-to-believe).
- The Wizard of AI: "a few thousand images and video clips" generated, "hundreds" used — [ODI news](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/news/wizard-of-ai/).

**Time and cost**

- Neural Viz: about 12 hours and roughly $100 a month in subscriptions for a two-to-three-minute episode — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/neural-viz-ai-tv-universe/).
- Gossip Goblin: 12 to 14 hours per piece — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/how-ai-creators-make-videos/).
- POV Lab (Hogne): about eight hours per video — [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/ai-generated-history-videos-are-going-viral-on-tiktok-but-how-accurate-are-they).
- Kalshi ad: one person, two to three days, about $2,000 (self-reported, not an audited budget) — [Public Gaming](https://www.publicgaming.com/news-categories/advertising/14510-heres-the-2-000-fully-ai-generated-ad-that-aired-during-the-nba-finals).
- WSJ film: around $1,000 including free vendor credits per BGR — [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/tech/you-wont-believe-this-movie-was-made-with-ai/); a Korean summary says "thousands of dollars" — [tech42](https://www.tech42.co.kr/?p=102992). Sources conflict.
- The Wizard of AI: an "intense three-week period" for about 20 minutes — [ODI](https://culture.theodi.org/the-wizard-of-ai/).
- **Hand-made comparator, Kurzgesagt (own account, via transcript sites, undated):** "at least 1,200 hours for a single Kurzgesagt video" — [VoiceTube transcript](https://jp.voicetube.com/videos/123700); on average 200 panels illustrated in Adobe Illustrator, two to three illustrators for 8 to 12 weeks, then two to three animators for about 8 to 10 weeks, scripts taking from a few weeks to years — [LingQ transcript](https://www.lingq.com/zh-tw/%E7%B7%9A%E4%B8%8A%E5%AD%B8%E7%BF%92%E8%8B%B1%E8%AA%9E/courses/689474/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-120-4887128/).
- **Live-action comparator, AI in Context (own account):** direct costs about $50K and $64K per video; about 450 staff hours each; totals about $160K and $174K including staff time; 9 and 11 weeks per video; editing runs about 4.5 weeks with "200+ comments" per revision round; scripting is the stated bottleneck — [EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/RCRaBYSqBaMzHzTjF/retrospective-and-learnings-from-ai-in-context-s-first-two).

**Record-keeping recommended for documentary work**

- The Archival Producers Alliance (Sept 2024) recommends a cue sheet for every generative element recording "Prompts used, software version (including Terms & Conditions), and the date created", any reference material and its copyright status, and "Descriptions and timecodes for where GenAI materials appear", plus a temporary watermark on synthetic material during production — [APA Best Practices PDF](https://www.mishcon.com/download/apa-genai-best-practices).

### Inferences
- Normalised: Neural Viz is roughly 4 to 6 hours per finished minute; the Kalshi ad roughly 2 to 3 working days for 30 seconds; Kurzgesagt, if a typical video is around 10 minutes, is on the order of 100+ hours per finished minute. An 11-minute solo essay at the Neural Viz rate would be roughly 45 to 65 hours of picture work, excluding research and script.
- Keep rates are far lower for moving clips (about 4 to 5 percent for the Kalshi ad, about 0.3 percent for Air Head) than the still workflows imply. Budgeting stills generously and clips sparingly matches what practitioners do: the still is the unit of consistency, the clip is a selective upgrade.
- The storyboard-first habit (one still per panel with fixed lighting and eyeline) is the practitioners' actual answer to consistency; none of the documented solo creators reports a technical consistency trick beyond upscaling before animation and brute-force re-rolling.
- Regenerating earlier shots late in production (Warburton) is a real cost of fast-moving tools: a locked look in week one may be visibly weaker than week three's output.

### Gaps
- No documented workflow was found for a flat or constructivist illustrated style specifically; every detailed account is for cinematic, horror, or character-comedy looks.
- No published shot list was found that tags each shot as still, moving or graphic with proportions; mixes are described only qualitatively.
- No cost-per-finished-minute figure exists in any source; the per-minute numbers above are my arithmetic on self-reported totals.
- Gennie's interviews give no numbers on cost, team size or discard rate ([Señal News](https://senalnews.com/en/interviews/cost-savings-and-big-creative-visions-gennie-shows-how-ai-is-transforming-content-creation)).

## 3. Reception: how viewers respond to AI imagery in essay and documentary video

### Takeaway
Suspicion of AI lowers trust sharply even when the work is human-made, and disclosure does not remove the penalty; what it buys is protection from the far worse "caught hiding it" outcome. Evidence consistently separates accepted uses (stylised, clearly illustrative, disclosed, human voice and authorship foregrounded, AI as subject) from rejected ones (photoreal history, real people, fake archival, mass-produced narration). By 2026 the backlash is strong enough that human-made animation is routinely accused of being AI.

### Cited Findings

**Scale of the slop problem (context for viewer priors)**

- A 2025 Guardian analysis found "nearly one in 10" of the fastest-growing YouTube channels globally show only AI-generated content; after the Guardian's inquiry YouTube removed three channels and blocked two from revenue sharing — [Spokesman-Review / USA Today, 23 Aug 2026](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/aug/23/youtubers-worry-that-a-new-policy-will-lead-to-mor/). Search-surfaced secondary coverage (not opened) gives the underlying figure as nine of the 100 fastest-growing channels in July 2025, from Playboard data.
- Kapwing (late 2025): on a fresh account, 104 of the first 500 Shorts (21%) were AI-generated and 33% were "brainrot"; 278 of the top-100-per-country channels were AI-only, with 63 billion views, 221 million subscribers and an estimated $117 million a year — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/ai-generated-content-accounts-for-third-of-youtube-shorts-feed-kapwing-research-shows/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/110735-over-21-youtube-now-ai-slop-report.html). Kapwing sells video tools; treat as vendor research.

**Surveys and experiments**

- Raptive (July 2025, 3,000 US adults): when people suspect content is AI-generated they rate it 48% less trustworthy, 57% less authentic, with 60% lower emotional connection; the penalty applied even to human-written content believed to be AI. The test used articles, not video — [Raptive](https://raptive.com/blog/the-ai-stink-is-real-and-its-costing-brands/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/raptive-study-shows-ai-content-cuts-reader-trust-by-half/).
- Animoto (Sept 2025, 460 US participants): 82.6% had watched videos they suspected were AI-made; 36% said AI video reduced their trust in a brand; 77.9% trust videos featuring real people; cues for spotting AI were robotic gestures (67%), unnatural voices (55%) and lack of emotional tone (51%). Vendor survey — [Adweek wire](https://www.adweek.com/adweek-wire/83-of-consumers-can-spot-ai-videos-36-say-it-hurts-brand-trust-new-animoto-report/).
- Reuters Institute / YouGov (June to July 2025, six countries): public use of generative AI is rising but trust and comfort with AI in news remain low — [NewscastStudio, 16 Oct 2025](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2025/10/16/public-use-of-generative-ai-grows-but-trust-and-comfort-with-news-applications-remain-low); a secondhand report gives 12% accepting news created solely by AI versus 62% preferring human-made (verify against the original) — [Reporterzy.info](https://reporterzy.info/en/5307,journalism-in-the-age-of-ai-why-people-prefer-humans-over-machines).
- Australian research on generative AI in journalism (seven countries): comfort depended on several elements including "the medium itself – text, illustration, photos, videos were viewed differently", and most participants see human oversight as imperative — [Advanced Television, 17 July 2024](https://www.advanced-television.com/2024/07/17/report-concerns-over-generative-ai-in-news/). The follow-up report (April 2025) found only 25% of news-audience participants were confident they had encountered generative AI in journalism, with about 50% unsure — [ADM+S](https://www.admscentre.org.au/?p=28766).
- Bellaiche et al. (Cognitive Research, 2023): identical AI-made paintings labelled "human-created" were rated higher on liking, beauty, profundity and worth than when labelled "AI-created"; perceived effort and narrative moderated the liking and beauty effects — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/2023/08/new-psychology-research-reveals-why-people-prefer-human-created-artwork-to-ai-created-artwork-167429).
- Schilke and Reimann (OBHDP vol. 188, 2025), thirteen experiments: people who disclose AI use are trusted less than those who do not, across tasks including artistry; the mechanism is reduced perceived legitimacy, and positive attitudes to technology shrink but do not remove the penalty — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v188y2025ics0749597825000172.html); [University of Arizona](https://news.arizona.edu/employee-news/being-honest-about-using-ai-work-makes-people-trust-you-less-research-finds).

**Creator and newsroom incidents**

- Kurzgesagt (X post 31 July 2026): "YouTube's automatic AI detection tools wrongly think that our very much human-made videos are AI Slop"; the affected video was the channel's worst performer since 2013 despite above-average click-through and watch time — [Kotaku, 8 Aug 2026](https://kotaku.com/youtube-mistakenly-penalizes-popular-science-channel-kurzgesagt-for-ai-generated-slop-2000722702); [Creator Handbook, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/kurzgesagt-says-youtube-mistook-its-animation-for-ai-slop/). Creator Handbook's explanation (its inference, not YouTube's): a flat, clean vector style with a calm narrator and explanatory graphics is the look slop channels copy because it is cheap to imitate, so a detector built to catch the copies can catch the original.
- Hank Green (July to Aug 2026): viewers accused him of reading AI-written phrasing; he denied the line was AI but admitted over-reliance on AI-generated research notes, called it "careless", and said he would likely pause his main channel. The objection was to language, not visuals — [Kotaku, 31 July 2026](https://kotaku.com/famous-science-youtuber-admits-he-has-unhealthy-relationship-with-ai-after-facing-backlash-over-recent-video-2000720908); [Fortune, 4 Aug 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/08/04/did-hank-green-use-ai-chatgpt-youtube-social-media/).
- Wendigoon (X, Sept 2026) defending a long-time human animator: "ANY animated work online (or live action at this point) is immediately suspect. AI has ruined the appreciation for everything" — [Wendigoon on X](https://x.com/Wendigoon8/status/2100948109987557635).
- MrBeast withdrew an AI thumbnail tool after backlash: "I definitely missed the mark" — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/mrbeast-youtube-video-ai-thumbnail-tool-viewstats-2091432).
- El Confidencial ran a clearly labelled AI illustration; a comic artist called it "unethical" and cancelled his subscription; the editor concluded that the explanatory tweet "was not enough to convey our message" — [European Focus](https://europeanfocus.eu/?p=1705).
- The New Yorker ran a Sam Altman profile illustration by David Szauder labelled "Generated using A.I."; it reignited debate despite the artist's hybrid process using his own tools and archival imagery (date unclear, secondary source) — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/newsrooms-adopt-ai-illustrations-sparking-artistic-backlash-24bca6d7).
- AI in Context's AI 2027 video (no AI imagery reported): 97.3% of reactions were likes; praise focused on "production, research, and Aric's presenting" — [EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/RCRaBYSqBaMzHzTjF/retrospective-and-learnings-from-ai-in-context-s-first-two).

**What practitioners say separates accepted from rejected**

- Documentary practitioner Kristina Budelis (May 2024): accepted uses are transparent, consented to where real people are depicted, and suit the subject; rejected uses are undisclosed, cut corners, feel lazy or distracting, and erode audience trust (paraphrase); requests for fully generated B-roll or "archival" with no plan to disclose are what prompted the APA guidelines — [No Film School / Film Robots](https://nofilmschool.com/ai-in-documentary).
- IDA's "The Synthesis" column (28 July 2026) lists as a red flag a film that promises to take you somewhere you have "never gone before" with AI imagery, especially dream sequences that look like everyday "AI slop", while noting that "genuinely thoughtful, inventive, and critical works" exist — [IDA Documentary](https://www.documentary.org/column/synthesis-9-red-flags-look-ai-documentaries-2026).
- APA: "Synthetic material that is indistinguishable from primary sources risks being passed along… and is in danger of forever muddying the historical record" — [APA Best Practices PDF](https://www.mishcon.com/download/apa-genai-best-practices).
- Neural Viz: "Everything I do within these tools is a skill set that's been built up over a decade plus" — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/neural-viz-ai-tv-universe/).

### Inferences
- The two experimental results pull in different directions and both matter: a label lowers ratings of the artwork (Bellaiche) and disclosure lowers trust in the maker (Schilke and Reimann), yet every documented backlash that ended a project or forced an apology involved undisclosed or discovered AI. Disclosure is a cost paid to avoid a larger one.
- Bellaiche's moderators point to the practical mitigation: perceived effort and narrative. Showing authored structure (annotation, layout, motion design, a distinct recurring visual language) and a human voice is what signals effort; a slow push over a generic still signals the opposite.
- The Kurzgesagt incident is directly relevant to a flat, clean, narrated explainer made with AI stills: that look is now a prior for "slop" for both viewers and YouTube's classifier. Distinctive, less imitable style choices and visible human authorship are a defence on both fronts.
- Audience sensitivity in educational YouTube extends to language cadence (Hank Green), not just pictures. A philosophy/AI audience is plausibly more alert than average.
- AI as the essay's subject gives licence: the two best-received essay/documentary uses (Warburton, Peck) both use AI imagery to talk about AI or manipulation, and both keep it visibly unreal.

### Gaps
- No study was found that tests viewer response to disclosed, stylised AI illustration in long-form narrated video specifically; the quantitative evidence is on text, brand video, news and static art.
- No systematic comment or like/dislike analysis comparing AI-illustrated and human-illustrated essays was found; evidence is incident-level.
- Dislike counts are not public, so "dislike patterns" could not be evidenced beyond AI in Context's self-reported like ratio.
- The Variety 2025 article on AI images and audience distrust in documentary could not be read.

## 4. Platform rules: YouTube disclosure, monetisation, provenance labels (as of Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
Stylised, clearly unrealistic or animated AI imagery does not require YouTube's altered-content disclosure, and if disclosed the label sits only in the expanded description and does not affect recommendation or monetisation. The real exposure for a narrated essay built on AI stills is the "inauthentic content" monetisation policy, which names image slideshows and templated AI content, and the opaque automated detection that misfired on Kurzgesagt in 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Disclosure of altered or synthetic content (YouTube Help, current page, footer ©2026)**

- Disclosure is required for content that "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do", "Alters footage of a real event or place", or "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur"; the page also lists music that is the main focus of the video — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).
- Not required: clearly unrealistic content (examples include "an AI-generated missile animation in a fully animated video"), minor aesthetic edits, and production assistance such as generating or improving a script, outline, thumbnail, title or infographic; also upscaling, repair, and cloning one's own voice — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).
- Label placement: for photorealistic content "a label in the video player may also appear"; for non-photorealistic or animated content labels "may appear in the expanded description". Disclosure does not limit audience or affect monetisation eligibility — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).
- Enforcement: creators who consistently fail to disclose "may be subject to manual application of a label, or penalties from YouTube", including removal or suspension from the Partner Program — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).

**May 2026 change: automatic labels and more prominent placement**

- YouTube blog, 27 May 2026: "we're rolling out new internal signals to help identify AI-generated content"; if a creator does not say whether they used AI and systems detect significant photorealistic AI use, "we will now automatically apply a label". For long-form the label now sits directly below the player, above the description; for Shorts it is an overlay. Unrealistic, animated or slightly altered content keeps its disclosure in the expanded description. Labels are permanent for content made with YouTube's own tools (Veo, Dream Screen) and content with C2PA metadata indicating it is fully generative. "A disclosure label alone does not change how a video is recommended or whether it's eligible to earn money" — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/); summary — [TechWyse, 2 June 2026](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/youtube-automatic-ai-labels-disclosure-may-2026).
- YouTube has not said which technical signals the detection uses, and the blog does not mention SynthID — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/).

**Monetisation: "inauthentic content" (formerly "repetitious content")**

- Policy note dated 15 July 2025: "We're making a minor update to our 'repetitious content' policy to better clarify this includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced. We are also renaming this policy from 'repetitious content' to 'inauthentic content.'" "There is no change to our reused content policy" — [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).
- Not eligible to monetise, verbatim examples: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production… without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective"; content that "looks like it's made with a template" or "may feel repetitive to viewers after watching several videos in a row from the same channel" — [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).
- Eligible examples include the "same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different" and series with a distinct storyline, focus or concept per video — [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).
- The policy text never bans AI; the link to AI was an inference from timing — [Plagiarism Today, 8 July 2025](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/). No dated 2026 update note appears on the policy page — [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).

**2026 enforcement climate**

- Neal Mohan's 2026 letter (21 Jan 2026) names "AI slop" as a concern, says YouTube is "building on our established systems that have been very successful in combatting spam and clickbait, and reducing the spread of low-quality, repetitive content", and notes over 1 million channels used YouTube's AI creation tools daily in December — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/21/neal-mohans-2026-letter-to-creators-a-lot-of-ai-a-tiktok-shop-competitor-and-beautifully-produced-tv-to-compete-with-netflix/); [Variety Australia](https://au.variety.com/2026/digital/global/youtube-channels-using-ai-tools-reduce-slop-neal-mohan-letter-32190/).
- Reports of large AI-slop channel removals in early 2026 (one blog claims 16 channels, 4.7 billion views, about $10 million a year) come from marketing blogs and are unverified — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/); related coverage — [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/youtube-gets-tough-on-ai-slop-ruining-the-party-for-million-dollar-brain-rot-video-channels).
- False positives exist: Kurzgesagt says YouTube privately confirmed automated detection had classified its hand-animated work as low-quality AI content and suppressed its reach; YouTube made no public statement; YouTube began surveying some viewers in March 2026 on whether videos felt like AI slop; vidIQ noted a small channel would have no way to learn that an automated system was responsible — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/kurzgesagt-says-youtube-mistook-its-animation-for-ai-slop/).
- From 24 Aug 2026 YouTube counts a view as soon as playback starts rather than after 30 seconds, keeping "Engaged Views" as a separate metric; creators argue this rewards high-volume AI output — [Spokesman-Review / USA Today](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/aug/23/youtubers-worry-that-a-new-policy-will-lead-to-mor/).

**Provenance: C2PA, SynthID**

- YouTube may automatically label content containing C2PA metadata, and such labels cannot be removed by the creator — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).
- "Captured with a camera" (launched Oct 2024) appears only for unedited footage from devices or apps supporting C2PA 2.1 or higher; edits or non-C2PA tools break the chain of provenance (secondary coverage) — [Tubefilter, 16 Oct 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/10/16/youtube-transparency-disclosure-c2pa-cai-generative-ai/); [Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/youtube-introduces-new-label-to-verify-authentic-video-content/).
- Uploads exported from C2PA-compliant generative tools are expected to be labelled before processing finishes (secondary) — [TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/youtube-automatic-ai-labels-disclosure-may-2026).
- I found no evidence that YouTube reads SynthID watermarks for labelling; one secondary source states YouTube names C2PA, not SynthID, as the signal behind permanent labels — [c2paviewer](https://c2paviewer.com/articles/youtube-ai-labels-c2pa).

**EU AI Act (relevant if the channel has EU viewers; law-firm commentary, verify before relying)**

- Article 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026; the Commission's Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content is voluntary but the underlying obligations are law — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content); [ICTRecht](https://www.ictrecht.nl/en/blog/heads-up-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-effect).
- Deepfake labelling under Article 50(4) covers content that resembles real persons, and for evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional work the duty is reduced to appropriate disclosure rather than removed (commentary summaries; exact wording not checked against the Regulation) — [Greenberg Traurig, June 2026](https://gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/deepfakes-chatbots-ai-generated-text-european-commission-details-transparency-obligations-under-the-ai-act); [SRD Rechtsanwälte](https://www.srd-rechtsanwaelte.de/en/blog/labelling-requirements-ai-act-deepfakes).

**Voluntary norms followed by respected producers**

- APA (Sept 2024) suggested audience-facing methods: "Lower thirds, bugs, or other types of watermarking on the material itself"; "Visual vocabulary that alerts the audience to GenAI use, such as a unique frame around the material, change of aspect ratio, colorization"; narrator acknowledgement; top- or end-of-show language but not as "the only means of transparency" because films are excerpted; and end credits listing "GenAI software or tools used, including which version", the prompt author, and companies employed — [APA Best Practices PDF](https://www.mishcon.com/download/apa-genai-best-practices).
- Netflix partner guidance (late Aug 2025) expects partners to declare intended generative AI use and requires written approval for generating key story elements such as main characters or key visuals — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/netflix-generative-ai-use-content-production-guidelines/); [Kidscreen, 28 Aug 2025](https://kidscreen.com/2025/08/28/netflix-releases-guidelines-for-using-generative-ai-in-content-production).
- BBC guidance: any AI use in creating or presenting content "must be transparent and clear to the audience", ideally explaining how and why — [IBC](https://www.ibc.org/artificial-intelligence/news/bbc-updates-generative-ai-guidance/21587).
- AI-native creators credit tools per upload (Neural Viz's "Made with: …" lines) — [Neural Viz on X](https://x.com/NeuralViz/status/1906512977177833715); "The Wizard of AI" lists every tool and its role — [ODI](https://culture.theodi.org/the-wizard-of-ai/).

### Inferences
- A flat, constructivist AI illustration of an abstract idea falls in YouTube's "clearly unrealistic / animated" bucket, so the altered-content toggle is not mandatory. Any shot that depicts a real person, real place or real event realistically (for example an animated archival photograph) does trigger it. Ticking the box for a stylised film costs nothing in reach by YouTube's stated policy and places the label only in the expanded description.
- The monetisation risk is about format, not AI: stills with narration are safe only when the narration is original analysis and each episode has a distinct concept. An 11-minute original essay with human narration and bespoke graphics is well inside the "eligible" examples; the risk is the classifier, not the rule.
- Because detection is opaque and has misfired on flat vector explainers, practical protection is evidence of authorship: human narration, original script, varied non-templated layouts, and kept production records (the APA-style cue sheet doubles as an appeal file).
- C2PA metadata embedded in source stills by image generators will not normally survive a Remotion render to MP4, so the permanent C2PA label is unlikely to be triggered by the finished file; this is my inference and was not confirmed by any source.
- An on-screen or description credit line ("Illustrations generated with X, art-directed and composited by the author") plus tool credits matches the voluntary norm of the best-received practitioners.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube statement defines how stylised illustration is treated by the 2026 automated "AI slop" detection, or what signals it uses.
- Whether a US-based solo creator has any EU AI Act obligation for stylised illustration was not resolved; the sources are commentary on drafts and final texts I could not open.
- YouTube's own blog post for the 15 July 2025 change was not fetched; the wording above is from the live policy page.
- No source confirmed SynthID being used by YouTube for labelling as of Oct 2026.

## 5. Pacing evidence specific to YouTube essays

### Takeaway
There is no rigorous public dataset linking visual change rate to retention for essay videos. The usable evidence is one widely cited practitioner reversal (MrBeast slowing from 38 to 23 cuts per minute while views rose), one essay channel's published retention figures, and vendor rules of thumb that disagree by a factor of ten.

### Cited Findings
- MrBeast (3 March 2024) posted that over the past year he had slowed his videos down, focused on storytelling, let scenes breathe, yelled less and made longer videos: "And our views have skyrocketed!"; "My fellow YouTubers lets get rid of the ultra fast paced/overstim era of content"; "It doesn't even work." Tubefilter's check found 38 cuts in a sampled minute of a March 2023 video versus 23 in a newer one, with longer, wider shots, and cautioned that the view growth cannot be attributed to editing alone — [Tubefilter, 4 March 2024](https://tubefilter.com/2024/03/04/mrbeast-editing-style-number-of-cuts-per-video/).
- Nearly 45% of YouTube viewing now happens on TVs, up 15% from 2020, which Passionfruit argues makes longer videos with room to breathe more viable — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/youtube-editing-mrbeast-54202/).
- AI in Context (own analytics, 24 Dec 2025): average view duration was 29% of the video for the AI 2027 essay (8.9M views, about 1.4M watch hours) and 35% for its second video; the team says the second video's story "trails off" in the second half; editing feedback included "this cut feels fast"; lessons ranked as important were topic choice, cinematic visuals, strong hooks, story structure and emotional weight, while upload frequency and curiosity-gap titles were ranked less important — [EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/RCRaBYSqBaMzHzTjF/retrospective-and-learnings-from-ai-in-context-s-first-two).
- Kurzgesagt's flagged video underperformed drastically despite above-average click-through and watch time, showing that reach can decouple from retention when classification intervenes — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/kurzgesagt-says-youtube-mistook-its-animation-for-ai-slop/).
- Kurzgesagt illustrates on average 200 panels per video — [LingQ transcript](https://www.lingq.com/zh-tw/%E7%B7%9A%E4%B8%8A%E5%AD%B8%E7%BF%92%E8%8B%B1%E8%AA%9E/courses/689474/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-120-4887128/).
- Buffer reported that adding pattern interrupts (angle changes, B-roll, on-screen graphics) raised its audience retention; a single before-and-after, not a controlled test — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/hub/youtube/retention).
- Vendor rules of thumb conflict: a visual change every 10 to 15 seconds early in the video — [monitoryt](https://monitoryt.com/blog/editing-for-retention); every 10 to 20 seconds in the first three minutes then 25 to 40 seconds — [Increditors](https://increditors.com/video-pacing-youtube-retention-science/); a pattern interrupt every 60 to 90 seconds past minute eight — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/advanced-retention-editing-cutting-patterns-that-keep-viewers-past-minute-8). None cites underlying data.
- Retention benchmarks also conflict: vidIQ puts a realistic target for a 6 to 9 minute education video near 30% average percentage viewed — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/); another source says 40 to 50% for 10 to 15 minute videos — [prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/blog/youtube-retention-benchmarks-2026). YouTube publishes no official benchmark.
- Academic adjacent: Guo, Kim and Rubin (ACM Learning at Scale 2014, 6.9 million edX sessions) found shorter videos more engaging, and informal talking-head and Khan-style tablet-drawing videos more engaging than slides or recorded lectures — [paper PDF](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf).
- The 2023 AI slideshow trend drew millions of views then faded within about two months — [Tuber's Digest](https://tubersdigest.substack.com/p/a-brief-history-of-ai-videos-on-youtube).

### Inferences
- If a typical Kurzgesagt video is roughly 10 minutes, 200 panels implies a new illustrated panel about every 3 seconds, before counting animation within panels. That is the density benchmark for the flat-illustration explainer genre, and it is far denser than any still-per-beat essay.
- The project's current episode (53 manifest segments over about 11 minutes, per the project brief) averages one segment every 12 to 13 seconds. That sits inside the vendor rules of thumb but well below the Kurzgesagt panel rate; within-segment change (reveals, annotation, layer motion) has to carry the difference.
- The MrBeast evidence argues against raising cut rate for its own sake; the Guo finding that hand-drawn build-up outperforms static slides suggests progressive construction on screen is the more defensible form of "visual change" for an explanatory essay.
- For a 35 to 40 minute essay, AI in Context's 29 to 35% average view duration with a team and a six-figure budget is a realistic ceiling reference; an 11-minute video should be judged against the 30 to 50% band, with wide uncertainty.

### Gaps
- No creator-published dataset relating B-roll density, shot length or on-screen variety to retention in essays was found. Claims of specific intervals are unsupported.
- No evidence was found on whether AI stills specifically hold retention differently from stock footage or hand illustration.
- Kurzgesagt's typical runtime was not confirmed in the sources read, so the panel-rate arithmetic is approximate.

## 6. Remotion 4.x implementation (project pinned at 4.0.457)

### Takeaway
Everything required (multi-clip grids, rapid still sequences, layered parallax, looping regions, custom transitions, SVG draw-on annotation) is buildable at 4.0.457 with `<OffthreadVideo>` or `@remotion/media`'s `<Video>`, `<Series>`, `<Loop>`, `@remotion/transitions` and `@remotion/paths`. Several conveniences (`premountFor` directly on `<Img>` and `<Video>`, `freeze`, `trimBefore`, crop props, `Loop` playbackRate) need 4.0.462 to 4.0.535. Remotion 5.0 was still unreleased when checked.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Grid or split screen of several clips, and performance**

- Three video components exist. Recommended for new code is `<Video>` from `@remotion/media` (Mediabunny + WebCodecs, draws to a `<canvas>`, frame-perfect, partial download, loopable, fastest render, requires CORS, falls back to `<OffthreadVideo>` for unsupported codecs). `<OffthreadVideo>` (Rust + FFmpeg, frame-perfect, broadest codec list, no CORS, not loopable, must download the whole file). `<Html5Video>` (the former `<Video>` from `remotion`; not frame-perfect) — [Remotion: Video vs OffthreadVideo](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/video-vs-offthreadvideo).
- "Use `<Video>` from `@remotion/media` for the best rendering performance"; `<Html5Video>` and `<OffthreadVideo>` "are not optimized" — [Remotion: Performance](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/performance).
- During render `<OffthreadVideo>` extracts the exact frame with FFmpeg and shows it in an `<img>`; in preview it is a `<video>` element — [Remotion: OffthreadVideo](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/offthreadvideo).
- With native video tags, "Chrome may throttle video tags if the page is heavy. If too many video tags get rendered simultaneously, a timeout may occur"; with `<OffthreadVideo>` "more videos can be displayed simultaneously as Chrome will not apply throttling" (older docs wording, still mirrored) — [Remotion v3 docs: Video vs OffthreadVideo](https://v3.remotion.dev/docs/video-vs-offthreadvideo).
- Flicker docs list "Many `<Html5Video>` tags" as a cause of stutter and advise switching to a frame-accurate tag — [Remotion: Flickering](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/flickering).
- At high concurrency "Chrome might decide to not load some `<Html5Video />`s which can lead to a timeout error"; workaround is to reduce concurrency; default `delayRender` timeout is 30 seconds; per-component `delayRenderTimeoutInMilliseconds` exists from 4.0.140 — [Remotion: Debugging timeouts](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/timeout).
- `<OffthreadVideo>` frame cache: since 4.0 the default cache is half of available system memory at render start; override with `--offthreadvideo-cache-size-in-bytes` — [Remotion: OffthreadVideo cache size](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/options/offthreadvideo-cache-size-in-bytes).
- SIGKILL during render is most likely memory; fixes are lowering the OffthreadVideo cache, lowering concurrency, and upgrading (memory improvements noted through 4.0.171) — [Remotion: SIGKILL](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/troubleshooting/sigkill).
- `npx remotion benchmark` tests several `--concurrency` values in one run — [Remotion: benchmark CLI](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/cli/benchmark).
- `<OffthreadVideo>` props relevant here: `muted`, `transparent` (4.0.0; PNG extraction, slower), `toneMapped` (4.0.117; set `false` to save colour-conversion time), `trimBefore`/`trimAfter` (4.0.319, replacing `startFrom`/`endAt`), `playbackRate`. `durationInFrames` on the tag needs 4.0.530 — [Remotion: OffthreadVideo](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/offthreadvideo).
- `@remotion/media` `<Video>` props by version: `playbackRate` 4.0.354; `onError` 4.0.404; `objectFit` 4.0.442 (CSS `object-fit` is not supported, use the prop); `from`/`durationInFrames` 4.0.446. Newer than the pin: `effects` 4.0.464, `premountFor` 4.0.495, crop props 4.0.500, `maxCanvasSinkFrameSize` 4.0.530. `loop`, `muted`, `trimBefore` are unversioned on the page — [Remotion: media Video](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/media/video).
- `useRemotionEnvironment()` can select a different component for preview and render — [Remotion: Video vs OffthreadVideo](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/video-vs-offthreadvideo).
- GPU-accelerated content includes video decoding, `transform`, `box-shadow`, `text-shadow`, gradients, `filter: blur()` and `drop-shadow()`; "in headless mode, Chromium disables the GPU, leading to a significant slowdown"; enable with `--gl` — [Remotion: GPU](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gpu). In v5 the renderer will default to `angle` — [Remotion: v5 migration](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/5-0-migration).
- Output-side costs: PNG frames render slower than JPEG; higher resolution is slower; `--scale` reduces it — [Remotion: Performance](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/performance).

**(b) Rapid sequence of stills cut to narration**

- `<Series>` chains scenes so each plays after the previous one; `<Series.Sequence durationInFrames offset>`; a negative `offset` overlaps the previous scene; only the last sequence may be `Infinity`; `premountFor` on `Series.Sequence` from 4.0.140. `freeze` (4.0.476), `trimBefore` (4.0.497) and `playbackRate` (4.0.528) are newer than the pin — [Remotion: Series](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/series).
- `<Img>` guarantees the image is loaded before the frame renders; `maxRetries` default 2; unhandled failure cancels the render in 4.x; Chrome's maximum image size is 2^29 pixels; use `staticFile()` for `public/` assets. `premountFor` directly on `<Img>` needs 4.0.497 — [Remotion: Img](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/img).
- Premounting mounts a component early so media can load; it "works in the Player and Studio but has no effect during rendering"; available from 4.0.140 on `<Sequence>`; default is 0 seconds in v4 and 2 seconds in v5; `defaultPremountInSeconds` needs 4.0.535 — [Remotion: Premounting](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/player/premounting).
- All motion must derive from `useCurrentFrame()`; render tabs "don't share state"; do not rely on frame order; use `random()` for randomness — [Remotion: Flickering](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/flickering).

**(c) Multiplane parallax from transparent layers**

- `interpolate()` with `extrapolateLeft`/`extrapolateRight: 'clamp'` maps frames to values; `interpolateTranslate()` produces CSS translate values and accepts pixel units only — [Remotion: interpolate](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/interpolate); [Remotion: interpolateTranslate](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/interpolate-translate).
- `transform` is GPU-accelerated — [Remotion: GPU](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gpu).
- Avoid CSS `background-image` and `mask-image` for layers because Remotion cannot know when they have loaded; use `<Img>` inside `<AbsoluteFill>`; if `mask-image` is unavoidable, render an off-screen `<Img>` with the same `src` so it is downloaded first — [Remotion: background-image troubleshooting](https://convert.remotion.dev/docs/troubleshooting/background-image); [Remotion: Flickering](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/flickering).

**(d) Masked or looping regions over a still**

- `<Loop durationInFrames times layout style>`; loops nest and cascade; `Loop.useLoop()` (4.0.142) returns the current iteration; `playbackRate` and premount props on `<Loop>` need 4.0.528 — [Remotion: Loop](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/loop).
- `<OffthreadVideo>` has no `loop` prop and holds its last frame when it ends; to loop, read the duration (Mediabunny `computeDuration()`), wrap in `<Loop durationInFrames={Math.floor(duration * fps)}>`, and gate with `delayRender()`; the docs supply a `LoopableOffthreadVideo` pattern that uses `<Html5Video loop>` in preview — [Remotion: OffthreadVideo](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/offthreadvideo).
- `@remotion/media` `<Video>` supports `loop` directly — [Remotion: Video vs OffthreadVideo](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/video-vs-offthreadvideo).
- Transparent overlays: `<OffthreadVideo transparent>` is required for alpha and "slightly reduces the performance of the render"; `@remotion/media` `<Video>` is recommended for transparent videos and needs no prop; alpha sources are WebM VP8/VP9 with `yuva420p`, or ProRes 4444 — [Remotion: Transparent videos](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transparent-videos).

**(e) Transitions with `@remotion/transitions`, including custom presentations**

- `<TransitionSeries>` (4.0.59) takes `<TransitionSeries.Sequence>`, `<TransitionSeries.Transition presentation timing>` and `<TransitionSeries.Overlay durationInFrames offset>` (4.0.415; draws on top of a cut without changing timing). Default presentation is `slide()`; `fade()` and `wipe()` are shown; timings are `linearTiming()` and `springTiming()` — [Remotion: TransitionSeries](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transitions/transitionseries).
- Transitions shorten total duration because scenes overlap: 40 + 60 frames with a 30-frame transition is 70 frames. Rules: a transition cannot be longer than an adjacent sequence; two transitions, two overlays, or a transition and an overlay cannot be adjacent. `layout="none"` is deprecated and throws in v5. `trimBefore` on `TransitionSeries.Sequence` needs 4.0.497; `durationInFrames` on the wrapper needs 4.0.533 — [Remotion: TransitionSeries](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transitions/transitionseries).
- Custom presentation: a function returning `{component, props}` of type `TransitionPresentation`; the component receives `children`, `presentationDirection` (`"entering"` or `"exiting"`), `presentationProgress` (0 to 1) and `passedProps`; `presentationDurationInFrames` from 4.0.153. The docs' star-wipe example applies a `clipPath` only on the entering scene and generates a stable clip id with `useState(() => String(random(null)))` — [Remotion: Custom presentations](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transitions/presentations/custom).

**(f) Drawing annotations with SVG path animation**

- `evolvePath(progress, path)` from `@remotion/paths` returns `{strokeDasharray, strokeDashoffset}` to spread onto a `<path>`; progress 0 is invisible, 1 fully drawn. Pitfall: progress above 1 un-draws the start and below 0 draws from the end, so clamp the interpolation — [Remotion: evolvePath](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/paths/evolve-path).
- In v5, `getPointAtLength()` and `getTangentAtLength()` will return `null` past the end of the path — [Remotion: v5 migration](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/5-0-migration).

**(g) Templates, showcases, community resources**

- Official Agent Skills: `npx skills add remotion-dev/skills`; skills include `/remotion-best-practices`, `/remotion-markup`, `/remotion-render`, `/remotion-captions`, `/remotion-maps`, `/remotion-upgrade` — [Remotion: Agent Skills](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/skills).
- Official "Prompt to Motion Graphics" SaaS starter (`npx create-video@latest --prompt-to-motion-graphics`) — [Remotion template page](https://www.remotion.dev/templates/prompt-to-motion-graphics).

Planned v5 changes: premount default 2 seconds; `pauseWhenBuffering` defaults to `true` on `<Html5Video>`/`<OffthreadVideo>`; renderer GL defaults to `angle`; colour space defaults to `bt709`; `TransitionSeries` `layout="none"` removed; `@remotion/light-leaks` stops receiving 5.x releases — [Remotion: v5 migration](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/5-0-migration).

**Illustrative sketches (my own code, written against the APIs cited above for 4.0.457; not copied from the docs and not run)**

```tsx
// (a) 2x2 grid of clips with staggered entrances. Each tile is a positioned box,
// so the default absolute-fill Sequence fills the tile and can be premounted.
const clips = ['a.mp4', 'b.mp4', 'c.mp4', 'd.mp4'];
<AbsoluteFill>
  <div style={{position: 'absolute', left: 48, top: 48, right: 48, bottom: 48,
    display: 'grid', gridTemplateColumns: '1fr 1fr', gridTemplateRows: '1fr 1fr', gap: 12}}>
    {clips.map((c, i) => (
      <div key={c} style={{position: 'relative', overflow: 'hidden'}}>
        <Sequence from={i * 8} premountFor={30}>
          <OffthreadVideo muted src={staticFile(c)}
            style={{width: '100%', height: '100%', objectFit: 'cover'}} />
        </Sequence>
      </div>
    ))}
  </div>
</AbsoluteFill>

// (b) Stills hard-cut to narration. Derive frame boundaries from cumulative
// seconds so rounding error does not accumulate across cuts.
<Series>
  {cuts.map((c) => {
    const start = Math.round(c.startSec * fps);
    const end = Math.round(c.endSec * fps);
    return (
      <Series.Sequence key={c.id} durationInFrames={end - start} premountFor={fps}>
        <Img src={staticFile(c.src)}
          style={{width: '100%', height: '100%', objectFit: 'cover'}} />
      </Series.Sequence>
    );
  })}
</Series>

// (c) Multiplane parallax: one progress value, per-layer depth multiplier.
const t = interpolate(frame, [0, durationInFrames], [0, 1],
  {extrapolateLeft: 'clamp', extrapolateRight: 'clamp'});
{layers.map((l) => (
  <Img key={l.src} src={staticFile(l.src)} style={{
    position: 'absolute', left: '-5%', top: '-5%', width: '110%', height: '110%',
    transform: `translate3d(${-t * l.depth * 80}px, 0, 0) scale(${1 + t * l.depth * 0.04})`,
  }} />
))}

// (d) Looping clip confined to a region of a still (geometry-only clip, no image mask).
// Loop length should equal the clip length in frames (here a 3 s clip at 30 fps).
<AbsoluteFill>
  <Img src={staticFile('street.png')} />
  <div style={{position: 'absolute', left: 1120, top: 300, width: 420, height: 360,
    overflow: 'hidden'}}>
    <Loop durationInFrames={90}>
      <OffthreadVideo muted src={staticFile('flag-loop.mp4')}
        style={{width: '100%', height: '100%', objectFit: 'cover'}} />
    </Loop>
  </div>
</AbsoluteFill>

// (f) Draw-on annotation over an image.
const p = interpolate(frame, [20, 50], [0, 1],
  {extrapolateLeft: 'clamp', extrapolateRight: 'clamp'});
const {strokeDasharray, strokeDashoffset} = evolvePath(p, d);
<svg viewBox="0 0 1920 1080" style={{position: 'absolute', inset: 0}}>
  <path d={d} fill="none" stroke="#C4A747" strokeWidth={6}
    strokeDasharray={strokeDasharray} strokeDashoffset={strokeDashoffset} />
</svg>
```

### Inferences
- For a grid at 4.0.457, `<OffthreadVideo muted>` per tile is the conservative choice (frame-perfect, no Chrome throttling, local files so the whole-file download is trivial). `@remotion/media` `<Video>` should render faster and loops natively, but at the pin it lacks `premountFor` and crop props, and it draws to canvas, so layout must use its `objectFit` prop.
- Render cost of a grid scales with tile count times source resolution, because each tile needs one decoded frame per output frame and the frame cache holds full-size frames. Pre-rendering each AI clip to the tile's display size (for example 960x540 for a 2x2 grid) and trimming to the used range should cut memory and decode time substantially; pre-compositing the entire grid to a single clip removes the problem but gives up per-tile timing control. The docs' general advice to replace expensive effects "with precomputed images" supports the same trade.
- If memory pressure appears (SIGKILL), the documented order is: lower the OffthreadVideo cache, then lower concurrency, then upgrade.
- Because premounting does nothing at render time, render correctness for stills and clips depends entirely on `<Img>` and the video tags' built-in `delayRender`; premounting only improves Studio scrubbing.
- A hard cut costs nothing in duration, but every `TransitionSeries` transition shortens the timeline by its length. For narration-locked essays either compensate each adjacent sequence or use `TransitionSeries.Overlay` (4.0.415, available at the pin) for effects that sit on a cut without moving it.
- For masks, geometry-only CSS (`overflow: hidden`, `clip-path` with inline shapes) or inline SVG `<clipPath>` avoids the asset-loading race that makes `mask-image` unsafe. I found no Remotion documentation on `mix-blend-mode`; test it with a single `npx remotion still` against the Studio preview before relying on it, and give the blending parent an opaque background.
- Worth upgrading from 4.0.457 for: `freeze` and `trimBefore` on sequences (hold-frame and jump-cut patterns), `premountFor` on `<Img>` and `<Video>`, crop props for split-screen wipes, `Loop` playbackRate, and `defaultPremountInSeconds`.

### Gaps
- No Remotion benchmark or issue was found that quantifies render time or memory for N simultaneous videos; the scaling statement above is reasoning, not measurement. Related issues exist about OffthreadVideo renders hanging or failing under limited RAM (#3883, #3865) — [mirror of remotion-dev/remotion issues](https://git.tdem.in/remotion-dev/remotion/issues/3883) — without a documented multi-video threshold.
- No official or community template for a narrated essay, parallax, Ken Burns, image collage or split screen was found; the showcase page content is loaded dynamically and could not be listed.
- The full list of built-in presentations beyond `slide`, `fade` and `wipe`, and the other `@remotion/paths` helpers, were not confirmed from the pages fetched.
- The version in which `@remotion/media` was first published is not stated on its docs page (earliest versioned prop is 4.0.354).
- `calculateMetadata` for deriving cut timings from narration data was not fetched in this pass.

## 7. Guidance on mixing illustration styles with real footage or photographs in one film

### Takeaway
Published guidance is consistent: let the subject dictate the medium, keep real archival material in its original form, mark any shift of medium with a clear and repeatable visual signal, and treat a change of medium as a deliberate event rather than a convenience. The strongest documented uses make the switch itself carry meaning.

### Cited Findings
- **Keep primary sources in their original form.** APA: filmmakers should consider "maintaining the original form and/or medium of primary source material (i.e. still photo vs. moving image, black & white vs. color, aspect ratio, etc.), or to alert the audience if they do otherwise"; "converting a photograph to a moving image could imply that a moving image camera was at an event when it was not"; altering a photograph's aspect ratio with generative fill "may introduce new people, buildings, landscapes, or even empty space… that could change its context and meaning" — [APA Best Practices PDF, Sept 2024](https://www.mishcon.com/download/apa-genai-best-practices).
- **Signal the generated medium with a consistent visual vocabulary.** APA suggests "a unique frame around the material, change of aspect ratio, colorization", lower thirds or bugs on the material itself, and narrator acknowledgement — [APA Best Practices PDF](https://www.mishcon.com/download/apa-genai-best-practices).
- **Treat generation like re-enactment.** APA places generative imagery "on a continuum" with re-enactment but at "greater magnitude because they often require little time and expense", and asks for "the same intentionality" as a traditional re-creation — [APA Best Practices PDF](https://www.mishcon.com/download/apa-genai-best-practices).
- **Animation is a tool, not the point.** Keith Maitland on "Tower" (2016): "it is a storytelling tool, and it is not the point of the film"; the film is animated for its first hour and conventional for its last half hour, and the cut from the rotoscoped actress to the real Claire Wilson is the moment the events feel real — [IDA Documentary, 23 Aug 2017](https://documentary.org/feature/life-animated-how-doc-makers-heighten-perception-and-solve-problems-through-animation).
- **Derive the illustration style from the source material.** Jessica Yu's "In the Realms of the Unreal" animated only elements found in Henry Darger's own artwork, building puppet pieces to "maintain the collage-like quality of the original paintings" — [IDA Documentary](https://documentary.org/feature/life-animated-how-doc-makers-heighten-perception-and-solve-problems-through-animation).
- **Set the look with boards first; audiences accept mixed media.** Mindbomb Films (graphics for "Blackfish", "Weiner", "Jodorowsky's Dune"): "boards are needed" before production moves forward; audiences now "just accept it" when documentaries include animation and motion graphics — [IDA Documentary](https://documentary.org/feature/life-animated-how-doc-makers-heighten-perception-and-solve-problems-through-animation).
- **Subject dictates form.** Practitioners at DOK Leipzig's AniDox:Lab conceded that there seems to be a rubric for when animation is deemed apt, but that ultimately the film's subject matter should dictate the form (paraphrase from search summary; article not opened) — [IDA Documentary, DOK Leipzig report](https://www.documentary.org/online-feature/docs-meet-animation-dok-leipzig).
- **Scholarly frame.** Annabelle Honess Roe's "Animated Documentary" (Palgrave, 2013) names three functions of animation in non-fiction: mimetic substitution, non-mimetic substitution and evocation, arguing animation shows what live action cannot reach — [Springer / Palgrave](https://link.springer.com/978-1-137-01745-1).
- **Make generated material visibly unreal when it sits beside real footage.** Raoul Peck cut an absurd AI sequence against real clips and describes it as "totally unrealistic"; viewers laugh at the AI parts — [IDA Documentary](https://documentary.org/column/digital-title-synthesis-raoul-peck-using-ai-critique-authoritarianism-orwell-225).
- **Unify disparate generated shots with a grade or texture.** Neural Viz uses a worn broadcast grade (VHS noise, soft focus) and consistent lighting and sight lines across stills so cuts hold — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/neural-viz-ai-tv-universe/); cursejourney unifies with sepia and grain — [The Daring Creatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/how-ai-creators-make-videos/).
- **Classify generated material as illustration by default.** A newsroom commentary recommends keeping generated material separate from verified photographs, field video and authenticated submissions, with a disclosure trail recording why it was used and what it is meant to represent (opinion piece, lower authority) — [IPS News](https://ipsnews.net/business/?p=248598).
- **Audiences judge media differently.** Comfort with AI depends on the medium: "text, illustration, photos, videos were viewed differently" — [Advanced Television, 17 July 2024](https://www.advanced-television.com/2024/07/17/report-concerns-over-generative-ai-in-news/).

### Inferences
- For a flat, constructivist illustrated essay that also includes archival photographs and a real government notice, the documented practice implies two separate registers that never blend: real documents shown as documents (original aspect, original colour, framed and attributed, not AI-animated or outpainted), and illustration that is unmistakably illustration. The frame or treatment around the real material is the "visual vocabulary" APA describes, applied in reverse.
- A change of medium helps when it marks a change of epistemic status (idea to evidence, thought experiment to record) and is repeated consistently so viewers learn the code. It breaks the world when the same kind of content appears in two media for no reason, or when a real photograph is restyled or animated so that it reads as part of the illustrated world.
- Animating an archival photograph with image-to-video is the specific move the APA warns against, and on YouTube it is also the move that turns an exempt stylised film into one requiring the altered-content disclosure.
- The Tower example suggests a single, late switch from illustration to the real thing can be the strongest beat in a film; sparing use preserves that effect.
- Deriving the illustration palette and texture from the real source material (Yu) is a documented way to make two media feel like one film without disguising either.

### Gaps
- No published guidance was found that addresses mixing AI-generated illustration specifically with real photographs in a YouTube essay; the guidance above comes from documentary and animated-documentary practice and is applied by analogy.
- Honess Roe's primary text was not read; the three-function summary comes from publisher and secondary descriptions.
- No audience study was found testing whether viewers are more or less tolerant of medium switches when one medium is AI-generated.
